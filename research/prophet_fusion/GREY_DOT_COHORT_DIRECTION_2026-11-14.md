## Direction

1. Treat G0 as the early washout lane, not an unconditional replacement for T1/T2. Keep a continuation/relative-strength lane for leaders that never provide a qualifying washout. Compare equal attention/exposure budgets and retain unmatched events.
2. Test market timing first, cohort selection second, stock selection third. A pooled advantage that disappears within the same date can motivate a market-date hypothesis but does not prove sector or stock selection. Sector timing must also beat simpler same-date/equal-exposure cohort alternatives.
3. Represent volatility level, direction and recent retreat separately. A high-but-easing VIX is not a low-but-rising VIX; VVIX measures options-implied volatility of VIX forward prices, not a realized standard deviation of VIX changes. Never substitute one silently. The incumbent VVIX/VIX ratio is partly driven by its VIX denominator; separate MA slopes avoid treating that mechanical ratio as independent corroboration.
4. Treat washout -> reduced selling -> broader repair -> persistence as an ordered hypothesis, not a discovered law. Compare the proposed sequence against the same final state and the same delay without the earlier sequence. Otherwise a complicated state story may only be a slower momentum signal.
5. Estimate behavioral traits from pre-event histories using Stock Identity, with shrinking own-history estimates toward peer priors and only matured earlier episodes. The Chairman's examples are hypotheses, not permanent labels for named stocks. Stable compounders, cyclical risk assets and crypto require separate eligible populations and clocks.
6. Explain changes in observed success rates before fitting a router: separate the frequency of market/cohort states from within-state success. Preserve the outcome definition and weighting; a descriptive decomposition is not causality.
7. Explain 63-session outcomes on exactly the same entries using the 0-21 and 21-63 gross-return segments, matched market/cohort references, and complete/censored path counts. Do not replace the frozen primary horizon because a later one looks better. A no-undercut probability cannot increase with a longer horizon on the same fully observed population, whereas positive terminal-return frequency may.

## The three questions must have different comparisons

**Market-date timing.** Does the common market state identify better or safer dates
for an otherwise fixed opportunity population? Use time/episode-blocked comparisons,
matched exposure and simple market/risk baselines. Date fixed effects would absorb a
pure VIX/VVIX date-level predictor entirely; they cannot be its primary test. Thousands
of stocks on one panic date are not thousands of independent panic observations.

**Cohort rotation.** At the same market date, does one dated sector/theme cohort offer
better absolute and relative opportunity than the eligible alternatives? Use within-date
cohort comparisons, equal attention/exposure budgets, dated membership and leave-target-out
breadth. Relative defensive leadership while all cohorts fall is not automatically a
profitable long entry. 'Tech versus staples' is a candidate comparison, not a timeless
risk-on/off definition. Price-relative rotation is not proof of a specific capital flow.

**Within-cohort selection.** On the same date and in the same cohort, which names add
beyond that common move? Only this third question should prioritize stock beta,
behavioral traits, catalysts, earnings and other stock-specific evidence. It cannot
rescue a failed market/cohort timing claim by selecting today's remembered winners.

The screenshots' pooled-to-within-day shrinkage is therefore not a reason to discard
the washout idea. It is a reason to assign the putative effect to the correct question
and test it against the correct alternative. The 0.2pp within-day observation is not
itself a completed market-date test or an independently verified event-level result.
