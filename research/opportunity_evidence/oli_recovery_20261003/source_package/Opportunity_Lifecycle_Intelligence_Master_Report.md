**Opportunity Lifecycle Intelligence (OLI) should be the governed, current-landmark interpretation of an opportunity across its native evidence, episode, strategy, and user-state owners.** It should explain phase and change, estimate transition-specific outcomes where qualified, distinguish present entry from holding, and preserve every accountable origin and subsequent decision.

## 5. External research synthesis

### 5.1 What the market literature supports—and what it does not

**Technical structure.** Lo, Mamaysky and Wang found that systematically recognized technical patterns can alter conditional return distributions in their historical U.S. sample. That supports testing explicit, reproducible constructions rather than dismissing all technical geometry. It does not establish executable profit, causal early recognition, a particular breakout rule, or current Mastermind performance. [X01]

**Momentum and proximity to highs.** Jegadeesh and Titman's evidence concerns intermediate-horizon winner/loser continuation; George and Hwang show that proximity to the 52-week high contains information beyond familiar momentum measures. These results contradict a universal assumption that a stock near its high is automatically unattractive. They do not establish that every extended stock is a good entry, or that a monthly effect transfers to a five-minute signal. [X02, X03]

**Volume and maturation.** Lee and Swaminathan show that volume conditions momentum behavior and subsequent reversal patterns. The relevant research implication is interaction and lifecycle heterogeneity, not “high volume confirms everything.” Volume-derived transforms share information with the price move and must not be counted as independent witnesses. [X04]

**Information diffusion and catalysts.** Hong, Lim and Stein's findings are consistent with slower diffusion under lower analyst coverage and asymmetric treatment of bad news. The useful hypothesis is that catalyst age, qualified revisions and observed price incorporation may matter at different landmarks. A price move does not reveal investors' private information or prove a causal narrative. [X05]

**Group effects.** Moskowitz and Grinblatt find an important industry component in momentum. Zaremba and coauthors report breadth-related predictability across market and industry portfolios in many countries, with implementation-cost caveats. Both motivate strong group controls. Neither licenses today's theme memberships as historical facts or establishes a calibrated local-subtheme entry probability. [X06, X10]

**Regime and tail risk.** Daniel and Moskowitz document momentum crashes concentrated in particular market conditions. This supports conditional risk analysis, but their portfolio mechanism is not a universal sell rule for an individual long winner. A regime variable must improve the particular transition or holding decision after controls, not merely sound plausible during a drawdown. [X07]

**Earnings and execution.** Ng, Rusticus and Verdi connect post-earnings drift to transaction costs and show why apparent predictability need not equal realizable profit. Earnings continuation should be tested from a qualified, executable decision time, with expectation comparability, spread, delay, and the full original event population retained. [X08]

**Options as a witness.** Pan and Poteshman's predictive evidence uses a specialized measure of buyer-initiated opening option volume. Generic premium turnover lacks those semantics. The paper does not justify reading institutional intent from an unsigned feed, or treating estimated dealer positioning as observed inventory. [X09]

**Compression, fakeouts and reclaim.** This review did not establish a transferable, calibrated law for the commission's exact compression, failed-breakout or re-entry definitions. Those remain registered transition hypotheses. Pattern terminology, vendor chart annotations and selected examples are not substitutes for causal detection, full failure retention and out-of-sample comparison.

### 5.2 Why transitions and current landmarks are a defensible modeling problem

Landmark prediction estimates outcomes conditional on being eligible and observed at a specified decision time. It is a natural methodological precedent for asking what remains after development or deterioration without replacing the origin question. Landmarking 2.0 also illustrates the tradeoff between a relatively direct landmark approach and richer models of longitudinal covariates. These are statistical methods, not evidence of equity alpha. [X11, X29]

Competing-risk and multi-state methods distinguish mutually exclusive first events from subsequent transitions. For OLI, reaching invalidation first, producing confirmation first, and expiring are not interchangeable negatives. Cause-specific hazards and cumulative event probabilities answer different questions; a subdistribution model should not be relabeled an instantaneous cause probability. [X12, X13]

Hidden semi-Markov models explicitly represent duration, which could matter when an old armed setup differs from a newly armed one. Bayesian online change-point methods offer a causal run-length representation. Neither justifies hindsight-smoothed phase labels in a live decision. The first challenger should add elapsed duration to a transparent hazard model before adopting hidden states. [X21, X22]

### 5.3 Models, uncertainty and decisions

Gu, Kelly and Xiu provide finance-specific evidence that nonlinear interactions can improve risk-premium prediction in their setting. TFT provides a multi-horizon architecture separating static, historically observed, and genuinely known-future inputs. These support a later challenger ladder, not a presumption that sequence models beat a source-correct baseline for OLI. An earnings date known today may be a known-future input; future earnings content is not. [X23, X24]

Proper scoring rules reward honest predictive distributions. Censored-event scoring requires appropriate treatment of censoring; unresolved outcomes cannot simply be scored as failures or omitted. Adaptive conformal inference targets long-run coverage under distribution shift, while selective prediction studies the risk–coverage tradeoff. None of these automatically supplies conditional calibration for every stock, phase, regime or selected top-pick slice. [X14, X15, X27, X28]

POMDP theory is a useful conceptual language for decisions under partially observed state. Optimal stopping work shows how entry, exit, deadlines and costs can interact under specified stochastic models. Leung and Li's mean-reverting-spread assumptions are not a general law for equity leadership. OLI should first compare a small frozen set of admissible policies; an opaque reinforcement-learning trader is not the first deliverable. [X25, X26]

### 5.4 Search control is part of the hypothesis

White's Reality Check, Hansen's superior-predictive-ability test, and Sullivan, Timmermann and White's technical-rule study address the danger of selecting a favorable rule from many candidates. Harvey, Liu and Zhu emphasize the evidentiary consequences of extensive factor search. Backtest-overfitting diagnostics add useful selection-risk information, but do not replace chronological, point-in-time and prospective validation. OLI's trial budget must include label definitions, clocks, horizons, thresholds, policies, and feature families—not only final fitted models. [X16–X20]

### 5.5 Research conclusion

Published evidence supports **conditional mechanisms worth testing**, not the full OLI hypothesis as an established fact. The scientifically strongest starting point is causal technical definitions plus price/relative-strength/volatility controls, coherent event targets, common-origin policy comparisons, and explicit incremental family tests. The strongest product claim available before those tests is improved interpretability, consistency and accountability—not improved investment returns.

**Discovery versus entry?** Several products explicitly distinguish screening/watch conditions from entries or alerts. Mastermind should match this through its existing two-queue architecture, not claim the distinction as a new invention. [P01, P04, P06, P07]

**Confirmation cost?** Confirmation conditions are documented, especially by TrendSpider. The reviewed pages do not establish a common, calibrated economic cost-of-waiting analysis over original opportunities, including never-confirmers. That is a proposed research and product advantage, not a proven exclusive feature. [P19]

**Remaining opportunity and management?** Targets, stops, zones, estimates and risk modes provide relevant neighboring capabilities. The reviewed documentation does not establish the exact OLI bundle of origin-preserving current-landmark payoff distributions, remaining adverse-path risk, and distinct fresh-entry versus existing-position assessment. This bundle is the testable product thesis, not a claim that no institution has equivalent custom analytics. [P07, P08, P14, P16]

**Reset and re-entry?** Configurable alert restart is documented. That is not sufficient evidence of immutable failed-episode retention, independently accountable re-arm forecasts, or calibrated repeated-failure risk. Mastermind should make those laws observable in its product and evaluation record. [P12]

**Probabilities or scores?** The reviewed pages do not provide a uniform calibration protocol across products. A proprietary rating, target zone, simulated P&L or vendor-reported accuracy is not interchangeable with a horizon-specific calibrated event probability. Do not import vendor marketing numbers as Mastermind's benchmark.

### 6.4 Defensible advantage to pursue

The proposed advantage is **one consistent, source-qualified answer across discovery, entry, holding, reset and return visits, with immutable decision history and independently earned statistical claims**. Its ingredients exist in Mastermind, but the integrated capability is not yet proven. Competitive success should be measured through completed user tasks, correct transitions, source/degradation honesty, reduced reconstruction burden, and eventually prospective decision value—not a larger feature count.

## 7. Lifecycle ontology v1

### 7.1 Smallest useful representation

**Proposal:** retain each owner's native state and add a versioned, strategy-specific interpretation profile. Do not replace native enums with one global ordinal lifecycle. For the first technical profile, a small coarse stage set is sufficient: **FORMING, ARMED, TRIGGERED, CONFIRMED, TERMINAL**. A mapping is admitted only when the source can prove its predicates. Earnings and Cycle strategies may retain different native phases rather than being forced into a technical-breakout vocabulary.

This coarse profile is for interpretation and risk-set construction, not a second canonical state writer. A TOI native `EXTENDED` state remains visible as such; the profile must not erase it by displaying only `CONFIRMED`. Unsupported mappings are null with a reason. A state is economically useful only if it changes the forecast question, permitted action set, or next observation—not because it makes a historical chart look orderly.

| Axis | Question | Proposed representation |
|---|---|---|
| Opportunity phase | What is developing under this species/strategy? | Native owner state + optional admitted coarse stage + transition receipt |
| Entry actionability | Can a fresh entry be considered under this exact strategy now? | B4/native verdict, reasons, quote basis, validity interval; no locally recomputed permission |
| Actual Plan/position | Has the user saved, entered, exited, or resolved anything? | Existing private Plan/position reference and native state |
| Management assessment | What needs attention for this existing plan? | Native policy assessment; observational flags and qualified forecasts, not an exit command |
| Thesis integrity | Which technical, catalyst, group or regime claim remains supported? | Typed component assessments, contradictions and unknowns; no blanket “price down = broken thesis” |
| Path and maturity | How far has price progressed, and what remains uncertain? | Measured path descriptors plus separately qualified forecast distributions |
| Time | Which observation, decision, prediction and expiry clocks apply? | Explicit clock and policy references, elapsed duration, remaining valid horizon |
| Evidence and data | How strong and current is each statement? | Native scientific status, source quality, missingness, calibration receipt and authority ceiling |

The screen need not display eight equally prominent badges. Phase, current-entry status and Plan state belong in the first glance; the remaining axes explain them. This preserves the commission's four fundamental lifecycle distinctions while avoiding a Cartesian-product enum. [C00, I04, I20, I22, I30]

### 7.2 Classify the disputed words

**FORMING** is a species-qualified opportunity phase: necessary ingredients exist, but the lawful activation predicate is not satisfied. **ARMED/READY** means a known trigger and invalidation/expiry definition exist; it is not a buy instruction. **TRIGGERED** is both an observed event and the start of an activated technical phase. **CONFIRMED** means a registered additional evidence predicate was met; it must carry when that became known and what waiting cost was paid.

**ENTER** is a decision/action, not an asset state. **MANAGE/DELIVERING** describes a plan-specific task or assessment, not a new-entry recommendation. **EXTENDED/MATURE** should normally be path/maturity context; it can remain a native technical state where the owner defines it, but never universally means sell. **EXHAUSTED/DETERIORATING** requires a named observation or forecast target, not an impression from a large historical move.

**RESET/PULLBACK** is an observed development; **RE-ARM** is an owner-governed transition; **RE-ENTRY** is a new decision at an accountable landmark, sometimes on a new canonical episode. **INVALIDATED, RESOLVED, EXPIRED and RETRACTED** are terminal episode dispositions where supported by the native owner. **FAKEOUT/FAILED** is a registered outcome or diagnostic: it need not be identical to economic-thesis invalidation. **DEAD MONEY** is a horizon- and policy-specific time/opportunity-cost outcome, not a universal market phase. **INSUFFICIENT DATA/AMBIGUOUS** is an epistemic condition; **ABSTAIN** is the resulting policy choice when appropriate.

### 7.3 Allowed and forbidden transitions

| From / development | Lawful treatment | Forbidden shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Forming → Armed | Registered ingredients plus frozen trigger, invalidation and expiry become available | Armed because the model assigned a high uncalibrated score |
| Armed → Triggered | Native trigger event at its actual knowledge/executable clock | Backdating to the price extremum or unfinished higher-timeframe bar |
| Triggered → Confirmed | Named confirmation predicate with elapsed-time and price-cost receipt | Labeling only eventual winners confirmed and dropping failed starts |
| Any live phase → invalidated/expired | Owner transition with typed cause and time | Missing data or a red candle treated as economic invalidation |
| Active → reset observation | Append development; retain original origin and policy | Silently moving the original anchor or stop to rescue performance |
| Terminal → potential new opportunity | New admitted anchor/generation or other owner-approved economic episode; link prior history | Reopening a terminal episode by overwriting the failed outcome |
| Extended existing plan + unavailable new entry | Preserve both assessments | “Extended” automatically closes the position, or “Manage” opens another |
| Correction / retraction | Append lineage; retain as-run and corrected-as-known views | Repainting the originally published prediction as if corrected facts were then known |

Direct forming-to-triggered observation can occur when a source first sees a gap through a threshold. It must be represented as first observed already triggered, with missing earlier observation history—not fictional evidence that the system previously armed the setup. Stage skipping is therefore permitted only as an honest observation history, not as backfilled monitoring success.

### 7.4 Transition explanation contract

Every displayed change must answer: **which owner changed which field, what newly admissible evidence caused it, what remained unchanged, what contradicted the change, and whether the implication is observational, predictive or actionable**. A text generator may summarize this structured diff. It may not invent a cause that the owner receipt does not support.

## 8. Episode identity and re-entry law

### 8.1 Current constraints are implementation law

Current Prophet B1 uses an owner-minted identity of the form `pe:<security_id>:<identity_epoch>:<anchor_token>:<generation>`. The canonical anchor includes kind, time, price and basis; the data receipt provides provenance rather than a new identity on every refresh. Ordinary source keys are immutable, unknown anchor kinds are suppressed, and a different anchor cannot replace an already active episode. The opening time cannot precede knowledge of the anchor. [I21, I22]

OLI must carry a **native-reference bundle**, not invent a global replacement episode key. Before B1 admission, a forming TOI occurrence or Radar nomination may still be shown under its native identity with `prophet_episode_ref = null` and the exact absence reason. Absence of a B1 episode does not mean no market opportunity exists; it means that particular canonical strategy/population contract has not admitted it.

### 8.2 Same episode versus new episode

**Same opportunity, new landmark:** ordinary price development, a pullback still inside the registered thesis, additional confirmation, or an update that does not change the governing structural/economic anchor. Append an assessment; do not modify the original forecast.

**New episode required:** the prior owner episode has reached a terminal disposition and a materially new, admitted anchor or strategy-defined economic origin qualifies under the owner. Link `rearm_of` or an equivalent native relationship. Under current B1 code, merely resubmitting the same old anchor is not a new opening path. OLI must not evade this by perturbing a price or timestamp to create a different hash. [I22]

**New thesis while an episode remains active:** a catalyst or strategy change may justify an additional assessment, but current B1's one-active rule still applies. A requirement for genuinely simultaneous independent economic episodes needs an explicit B1 owner amendment and downstream migration study. OLI cannot grant that capability through a projection.

### 8.3 What persists and what can reset

Persist security/issuer lineage, original evidence and forecast, source-key custody, original entry/Plan policy, all failed starts, completed outcome, costs and prior failure count. A lawful new episode may establish a new anchor, trigger, invalidation, horizon, forecast and model version. It does not inherit the previous episode's favorable performance selectively or erase its unfavorable performance.

Re-arm qualification should specify geometry, duration and evidence rather than a universal cooling-off number. Examples for research include a completed pullback and reclaim of an owner-defined level, restoration of a predeclared risk/reward geometry, or a genuinely new source-qualified catalyst. These are candidate mechanisms, not admitted detectors. Repeated-failure count must be calculated from the then-known retained history, not only the successfully repaired episodes.

### 8.4 Hindsight-laundering attack tests

A valid implementation must reject a terminal-to-active overwrite, the same event with changed bytes, ticker/date surrogate IDs, an anchor known after the decision cut, and a failed episode later relabeled as a successful long-term thesis. It must distinguish an ordinary new observation from an explicit correction command. Replaying a prefix must reproduce the originally published prefix even after later corrections arrive; a corrected historical view is a separately labeled query.

A user can deliberately open a new strategy plan after a loss. The UI must show that it is new, retain the earlier loss, and require an explicit new plan decision. No automatic plan transfer is implied.

A provider's historical timestamp is not proof that Mastermind captured the observation then. ### 9.2 Prevent false confluence

Attach a `dependence_group` to evidence at its native semantic family. Ten transformations of the same price history do not supply ten confirmations. Establish incremental value by removing a family, replacing it with a matched simpler proxy, and comparing the same model capacity and candidate population. A family can improve explanation or missingness awareness without improving forecasts; those are different acceptance claims.

Where optional evidence is absent, preserve the base eligible forecast only if its registered missingness policy permits that case. Do not fill absent options or catalysts with neutral zero. A model may use a predeclared availability indicator, but it must survive source-era and missingness-only controls; otherwise it may be learning the data pipeline rather than the market.

## 10. Transition-prediction design

### 10.1 Unit, risk set and executable time

Let `i` denote an owner-qualified opportunity/occurrence, `s` a registered strategy or species, `l` an accountable landmark, and `F_l` all admissible information at that cut. The predictive unit is `(native_ref, strategy/species version, landmark definition, decision cut)`, not an unqualified ticker-day and not every refreshed UI row.

A forecast states its **risk set**. An armed-to-trigger question includes all armed cases, including those that invalidate or expire first. A triggered-to-confirmed question includes all lawful triggers, not just eventual confirmers. A holding forecast conditions on an actual or explicitly simulated eligible entry under a fixed policy. It cannot be compared directly with the original discovery cohort without accounting for that selection.

The first executable time is determined by source knowledge, completed-bar law, allowed session, processing latency and quote/fill availability. Event time and executable time are different fields. A late historical reconstruction cannot be labeled an early live prediction.

### 10.2 First-event targets

| Transition question | Target and competing dispositions | Primary evaluation object |
|---|---|---|
| Forming → Armed/Trigger | Time to registered readiness or trigger; pre-trigger invalidation and expiry retained | Cumulative event incidence, time calibration, useful coverage and false preparation burden |
| Armed → Trigger | Lawful trigger before invalidation/expiry; gap-through and no-fill distinct | Activation distribution and executable activation coverage |
| Triggered → Confirmed | Confirmation before specified failure/invalidation or expiry | Confirmation incidence, delay, false-start rate and signed confirmation premium |
| Triggered/Confirmed → realized path | Net return/path distribution from actual executable landmark | Proper predictive score; paired policy value separately |
| Existing Plan → milestone/deterioration | Strategy-native realization, risk breach, thesis change and time stop | Remaining horizon risk, milestone timing, tail outcomes under fixed entry |
| Extended/reset → re-arm | Qualified reset and newly admissible geometry before terminal expiry | New accountable opportunity yield and repeated-failure risk |
| Any phase → invalidation | Technical, catalyst, group, regime causes, with unknown knowledge separate | Cause-specific event law and calibrated aggregate risk |

“Fakeout” must be defined before labeling. It may be a diagnostic such as a return through the breakout boundary, while a deeper stop remains unhit. Such overlapping diagnostics cannot be added as if they were mutually exclusive first-event probabilities. When multiple causes coincide, the registered outcome ontology needs either an explicit joint category, a resolution rule supported by finer evidence, or an unresolved category. Arbitrary priority must not masquerade as observed chronology.

### 10.3 Transparent first model

A proposed initial model is a discrete-time competing-risk hazard with a no-event category. For mutually exclusive event types `k` and future interval `u`:

```text
h_k(u | F_l) = P(first event is type k in interval u | no prior event, F_l)
S(0) = 1
S(u) = S(u-1) × [1 - sum_k h_k(u | F_l)]
F_k(H | F_l) = sum_{u=1..H} S(u-1) × h_k(u | F_l)
```

Use a multinomial parameterization or equivalent constrained construction so total first-event probability never exceeds one. The horizon forecast at landmark `l` cannot use realized future covariates. It uses current admissible covariates, registered duration effects, and only genuinely known-future calendar facts. New observations create a new forecast at a later landmark; they do not retroactively improve the old prediction. Competing-risk literature supplies the methodological basis, while these targets and interfaces are OLI design proposals. [X12, X13]

### 10.4 Model ladder and stop rules

1. **Deterministic owner states and base rates.** Establish causal correctness, label accounting and useful display before fitting.
2. **Regularized logistic/discrete-hazard/additive models.** Include elapsed duration, simple price/RS/volatility, and a small number of registered interactions.
3. **Hierarchical partial pooling and calibrated boosting.** Admit only if sparse groups or nonlinear interactions provide measured incremental benefit with the same information and search budget.
4. **Multi-state, latent-duration, change-point, sequence or graph challengers.** Require a specific residual failure of simpler models, sufficient independent evidence, faithful explanation tests, and bounded compute/operational cost.

The ladder is a proposed selection procedure, not a claim that every rung is necessary. Hidden-state models must use filtered probabilities at decision time; retrospective smoothing is a diagnostic view only. An attention plot is not proof of causal importance. A complex model that adds no economically material, robust benefit is rejected even if its aggregate fit looks marginally better.

## 11. Remaining-opportunity framework

### 11.1 The object is a conditional distribution, not a score

Define remaining opportunity as **the distribution of feasible future reward, adverse path, invalidation and time from a specified current executable landmark, under a specified strategy and information set**. Keep the asset-path forecast separate from any policy-dependent payoff distribution. Neither is “distance to analyst target,” inverse RSI, or the percentage of a future top that has not yet happened.

The proposed output vector has six parts: future net-return quantiles; adverse-excursion quantiles; favorable-excursion quantiles; target/invalidation/expiry incidence; time-to-realization distribution; and uncertainty/coverage. An unqualified head is null, not a model-looking number.

### 11.2 Descriptors versus forecasts

| Measured as of the landmark | Estimated only after qualification |
|---|---|
| Return since immutable origin | Distribution of future return from the current executable quote |
| Distance from original trigger and current invalidation | Probability of invalidation within the declared remaining horizon |
| Extension normalized by an explicitly frozen or current volatility scale | Remaining favorable/adverse excursion distribution |
| Giveback from the peak observed so far | Probability of continued leadership versus deterioration |
| Signed price premium paid for confirmation | Expected economic value of waiting versus entering now |
| Elapsed sessions and remaining registered expiry | Time to realization, time in dead money and uncertainty |

Call the first group **progress since origin / extension**, not “percent of total opportunity consumed,” unless the denominator is an explicit frozen plan objective and labeled as such. The eventual total move is unknown at the landmark. Recomputing a historical origin-volatility scale with later data is also forbidden.

### 11.3 Price and payoff basis

For a long-only executable forecast, an illustrative accounting convention is:

```text
net_return(l, t) = (exit_bid(t) + eligible_distributions(l,t) - entry_ask(l))
                   / entry_ask(l) - non_spread_costs(l,t)
```

This is an accounting specification, not a fitted model. The price/adjustment and distribution convention must avoid double-counting dividends or corporate actions. A midpoint-plus-spread model is an alternative, not an extra spread charge on top of bid/ask accounting. Borrow, financing, short-sale constraints and option Greeks/expiry require their own qualified contracts before extending the first long-only equity slice.

MFE is an upper-path excursion diagnostic; a strategy does not capture the maximum merely because it was observed later. MAE from entry is not running-peak drawdown. Report each with its own denominator and sampling resolution. A future high/low inside a coarse bar may establish an excursion bound without establishing an executable sequence or fill.

### 11.4 Confirmation and waiting

A signed confirmation premium compares the first lawful executable price with the later confirmation price for the same opportunity. It can be negative: waiting can improve price or avoid a failed entry. The actual value-of-waiting experiment must retain cases that never confirm and cash while waiting, rather than compare successful early trades with successful late trades from different populations.

Use two separate rulers. The **forecast ruler** asks about a stated horizon from each landmark. The **policy ruler** compares complete policies over a common origin-to-expiry accounting window. Giving every late entry a fresh full holding window can change the economic question and should be a separate registered study, not an unnoticed advantage.

### 11.5 Four different judgments

A strong company can have a weak current entry. An excellent original call can have little remaining opportunity. An unattractive fresh entry can coexist with an appropriate existing hold because position constraints, costs and strategy differ. However, the underlying future price path from today is not improved by having bought earlier. Original P&L is historical attribution, not a reason to assign a more favorable future asset forecast to an existing holder. Preserve that distinction to avoid encoding sunk-cost reasoning into “management intelligence.”

## 12. Management-intelligence framework

### 12.1 Freeze the governing contract at entry

Freeze the actual decision/fill reference, strategy/version, entry source basis, original thesis, invalidation law, expiry, sizing/risk owner reference, initial forecast and applicable management policy. Subsequent evidence may update forecasts and attention flags, but it cannot rewrite what the original trade promised. A policy amendment is an explicit new decision with lineage, not a silently changed stop or horizon. This extends the strategy-native hold law already present in R6. [I20]

### 12.2 What management should observe

Track technical invalidation separately from ordinary volatility; catalyst facts separately from price interpretation; peer/breadth deterioration separately from the subject's return; and macro context separately from the plan's authorized response. Measure progress toward the registered thesis, time remaining, current adverse-path risk, liquidity changes, and source quality. A stale source means the assessment may be unavailable; it does not certify either safety or failure.

**Hidden fragility** should be a registered conditional-risk hypothesis, not an evocative label attached to every losing chart. Phase 22's rising-real-yield and weak equal-weight breadth interaction remains that existing owner's prospective question. OLI must neither retune it after viewing the earlier forensic set nor make it a live veto without promotion. [I23]

### 12.3 Entry and management must be experimentally separated

Use a staged factorial design. First vary entry policy while holding management fixed. Next vary management while holding the entry cohort and execution rule fixed. Only then test a separately registered entry-by-management interaction. This can distinguish a late-entry problem from a deterioration-during-hold problem. An apparent “better management” system that quietly selects easier entries has not answered the question.

The first management product should support **observe, review, and explain**. Suggested review conditions can include a source-qualified thesis change or approaching expiry. They must not automatically trim, sell, add, move stops or re-enter. Any future action policy requires its own feasible portfolio/risk evaluation, consumer-specific authority and rollback.

### 12.4 Time stops and opportunity cost

A time stop is strategy-native: failure to achieve a registered milestone by a defined deadline, not an ex-post observation that another stock did better. Evaluate it against the same opportunity set with cash and feasible alternative-use assumptions clearly separated. Equal-opportunity simulations are not investable portfolio performance; overlapping positions and capital constraints require the existing portfolio owner.

## 13. Characteristic horizon and regime integration

### 13.1 Six clocks, not one timeframe dropdown

| Clock | Role | Admission requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Observation grain | Raw bar/event sampling | Exact vendor, session, exchange close and adjustment semantics |
| Anchor/session grammar | Where windows start and end | Named calendar/version; early closes and cross-market sessions explicit |
| Filter memory | How much past information affects a feature | Fixed causal recipe; no hidden dependence on future bars |
| Prediction horizon | Future quantity being estimated | Registered endpoint and clock; no off-horizon verdict |
| Episode/plan expiry | When the original opportunity or mandate ends | Native strategy law, preserved across later forecasts |
| Management/review cadence | When a new assessment is appended | Predeclared scheduled or event-driven landmarks, deduplicated and accountable |

TOI owns broad data/clock admission, and Temporal Grain retains its characteristic-time study. A smoother chart or attractive motivating example does not establish a better decision clock. Derman's intrinsic-time formulation is a conceptual precedent, not validation of a stock-specific best timeframe. [I07, I25, I26, X30]

### 13.2 Proposed adaptive-horizon experiment

Start with the incumbent fixed-clock policy. Add a causal duration/volatility descriptor without changing the horizon. Only then test a small predeclared clock-selection rule derived on permitted development data and frozen before validation. Compare it against fixed-clock and equal-compute alternatives, preserving the same origin population and information availability. Instrument-disjoint and regime-separated tests should follow the Temporal Grain owner's accepted sequence.

Potential clocks include completed sessions, elapsed trading minutes, catalyst-relative time and a source-qualified volatility/activity clock. A forecast horizon, lifecycle expiry and management cadence may differ. Faster monitoring does not justify a shorter original thesis or more independent observations.

### 13.3 Cross-market boundary

The first empirical vertical should remain within an actually qualified market/sleeve/session. U.S. RTH evidence must not silently authorize China, Hong Kong or Canada. Each market needs its own exchange calendar, sessions, corporate-action basis, instrument and currency identity, liquidity/price-limit rules where relevant, rights, and historical eligibility. The architecture can share interfaces while its admitted population remains narrow. A unified product is not a universal scientific permission.

## 14. Research and statistical protocol

### 14.1 Three distinct acceptance claims

**Semantic/engineering value:** can the system reproduce source truth, preserve identity and explain changes consistently? **Predictive value:** do the new heads forecast the registered outcomes better than strong same-information baselines? **Decision value:** does a feasible policy using them improve a declared economic objective after costs, waiting, failures and coverage? A pass in one category does not imply a pass in the others.

### 14.2 Population and denominator law

Freeze the population rule before outcomes: market, security types, historical eligibility, source era, session, species, anchor admissibility, and observation-start rule. Retain every nominated/forming/armed/triggered opportunity and its disposition, including suppressed, passed-over, unpriced, stale, never-triggered, never-confirmed, expired and failed cases. Do not backfill current members into a historical universe.

TOI W0 starts its forward evidence episode at trigger. Therefore **pre-trigger research has a real denominator requirement**: determine which existing nomination, near-miss, suppression or evaluation owner can retain the first qualifying forming observation. Extend that owner only where its current contract lacks the necessary reference and timestamps. Do not fabricate B1 episodes for unanchored candidates or introduce a separate OLI event store. [I04, I22]

Report the accounting identity at each step: observed inputs = mapped + suppressed; mapped = qualified + unqualified under named reasons; target-eligible = resolved + administratively unfinished + unresolved/invalid measurement dispositions. These are separate denominators, not filters quietly applied to the final win rate.

### 14.3 Registered landmarks

Choose a bounded landmark schedule before outcomes, such as first forming observation, first lawful trigger, registered confirmation, fixed strategy review intervals, and independently defined material changes. Deduplicate identical assessment keys. Event-driven landmarks must have a causal trigger independent of the future outcome. Store the original and every subsequent prediction through the existing prediction/outcome owner.

A price path that generated 100 five-minute refreshes remains one dependent opportunity history, not 100 independent successes. Explain whether estimates are episode-weighted, date-weighted, landmark-weighted or user-decision-weighted. The default policy comparison should prevent long-lived episodes from winning simply by contributing more rows.

### 14.4 Chronological development and validation

The proposed development design has an outer chronological evaluation split and inner chronological training/tuning/calibration partitions. Split whole decision dates, then enforce episode and label-information intervals. Purge training examples whose outcome interval overlaps a validation/test information boundary. Express embargo in the owner's trading-session clock, not blindly in row counts. Training labels must have matured before fitting.

Feature scaling, imputation, universe selection, interactions, threshold tuning, model selection and calibrator fitting all occur inside the appropriate training partition. Freeze the final candidate and calibrator before the untouched test. After the historical test is used, it becomes development evidence for a later iteration; the next confirmation needs a new unexposed period or prospective epoch.

Do not invent fixed historical start/end dates before the data owner establishes usable source eras. The registration must fill exact dates, horizons and admissible source-generation ranges before a target outcome read. A default 70/15/15 row split is not an acceptable substitute.

### 14.5 Baselines and ablations

The mandatory controls are the exact current static Buy/Wait/Extended implementation, TOI's two queues, existing Prophet admission/entry policy, simple price/RS/volatility models, same-information transparent logistic/hazard models, deterministic rule policies, and a useful abstain/cash reference. Freeze versions and actual input populations; do not weaken the incumbent retrospectively.

Separate **richer information** from **richer modeling** using a crossed comparison: baseline information with simple and complex models, then added family information with both. Run leave-family-out, matched-proxy, source-era, missingness-only, and current-only versus PIT-admitted sensitivity checks. A model cannot claim incremental catalyst value merely because its catalyst cohort has better data or larger, more liquid firms.

### 14.6 Endpoints and economically meaningful gates

For transition forecasts, predeclare a proper event/distribution loss plus calibration and coverage diagnostics. For an entry policy, the proposed primary is a **date-weighted paired full-opportunity net outcome difference**, measured over the common origin-to-expiry window with declared cash while waiting. Supporting views include episode-weighted and fill-conditional outcomes, severe-loss incidence, retained large-winner contribution, MAE/MFE, turnover, time to payoff, and cost sensitivity. They are not interchangeable primary endpoints. [I20]

A formal promotion decision must precommit: economic minimum improvement `delta`, harmful-endpoint non-inferiority margin `m`, minimum useful coverage `c`, acceptable uncertainty, exact multiplicity family, and the formal look schedule. Derive these from the consumer's loss budget and permitted pilot variance/power analysis, not from protected outcomes. This report does not invent universal numerical thresholds and falsely label the study registered. Filling these numbers, with owner approval before outcomes, is an explicit wave acceptance deliverable.

Existing study gates remain binding. Phase 22's months/episodes/effective-name floors and Top Anatomy's maturity and one-look law cannot be replaced with a convenient OLI sample threshold. A large row count cannot compensate for insufficient independent dates, names or adverse events. [I23, I24]

### 14.7 Dependence and uncertainty

Report rows, native occurrences, B1 episodes, issuer counts, distinct decision dates/months, event counts, and concentration-based effective-N separately. The exact block length is selected on permitted development evidence or fixed by the incumbent ruler, never optimized to make a protected result significant.

Sparse regime/cause cells should use shrinkage or remain unestimated. A narrow confidence interval from incorrectly independent repeated rows is a defect, not high certainty. ### 14.8 Censoring, ambiguity and execution

Administrative horizon immaturity can be right-censored under a declared event-model convention. A delisting is an economically meaningful event with a recovery/return treatment, not automatically harmless censoring. A data gap is measurement uncertainty, not a zero return. A halt can prevent execution even when a model forecasts a favorable eventual path. If a target and invalidation occur in one coarse OHLC bar, chronology is unresolved unless qualified finer data resolves it; an adverse-first convention is a sensitivity bound, not an observed fact.

Replay must use the appropriate quote side or an explicit spread/slippage model, session permissions, latency, limit-order fill rules and capacity assumptions. No fill is a policy outcome. Unknown prices are not cash. Current L1 quotes do not prove historical queue execution or L2 capacity. Preserve the exact adjustment convention across features, targets and execution accounting. [I07, I20]

### 14.9 Calibration and changing risk

Report calibration at declared horizons and on meaningful selected slices, not only pooled across all rows. Include event-probability reliability, proper scores, interval coverage and width, and risk–coverage behavior where abstention is used. Confidence in a source, probability of an outcome and model uncertainty are separate quantities. A score becomes a probability only with a defined outcome, population, horizon, model and calibration receipt.

Use proper scoring and censoring-aware methodology as applicable; do not claim an average coverage guarantee provides conditional calibration in a thin regime or among today's top picks.
