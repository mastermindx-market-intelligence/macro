**The expected outer US lock now matches.** `control_plane/locks.py:51–62` resolves `data/locks/book:autonomous.lock`; `113–149` opens the persistent file and uses nonblocking exclusive `fcntl.flock`. First-run counterparts also hold it; daily marking holds the corresponding per-book lock through mark and evaluation (`1295–1360`). US settlement holds the US lock, and Asia settlement acquires both regional locks in fixed order (`680–750`). Manual Brain handlers also take their respective outer book lock (`main.py:389–432` and analogous regional handlers).

This narrows the earlier generic concurrency concern for those inspected callers. It does **not** establish that every low-level/external writer participates. `paper_account.py:406–429` additionally uses a different, nested lock at each book's `.paper_transaction.lock`. The helper does not take that inner lock or regional outer locks, and it takes its US lock only after health. Source pathname agreement and the observed bind are positive evidence, not a new cross-process contention test or an inventory of all loaded module identities.

**Startup performs intended writes before health.** `main.py:248–365` validates authoritative authentication, records startup/governance events, starts the scheduler, and may start first-run daemon threads. Scheduler initialization starts paused, removes retired jobs, runs `preflight_pending_target` for all three active books, and resumes only when each book is compatible, empty, or successfully quarantined (`scheduler.py:1605–1687, 1882–1900`). The paper preflight recovers any WAL first and atomically quarantines incompatible queue bytes under the inner lock, with digest/audit evidence (`paper_account.py:2712–2870`). Failed quarantine refuses startup; successful quarantine is an intended safety outcome.

Queue quarantine and its audit records survive code rollback because runtime data is outside the helper's code restore scope. US account/fill hashes do not detect a queue-only change. Do not undo this protection by restoring incompatible executable intent automatically. A release proof should distinguish unchanged authority artifacts from explicitly permitted, receipted startup effects.

The daily mark is also not byte-read-only: it accrues cash and writes account mark metadata/NAV (`scheduler.py:1317–1360`; `paper_account.py:303–330, 3158–3247`). ## Bounded defects and smallest validations

### A. First-run history read errors can fall through to a new Brain thread

The US/China/HK first-run checks return early for nonempty readable NAV history, but catch a read/path exception and continue toward availability checks and thread launch (`scheduler.py:2015–2050, 2106–2141, 2148–2183`). An unreadable established history is therefore not treated as unknown state that forbids first-run initialization.

**Small source correction, in separately owned scheduler scope:** distinguish genuinely absent/empty first-run state from an inability to inspect existing history; the latter should refuse launch and retain a visible reason. A focused regression should inject a history-read failure and verify no runner thread/model entry is invoked, with existing-readable and genuinely-new controlled cases. The current helper-only release can first establish that the actual historical books' guard inputs are readable; this finding is not permission to edit the scheduler or claim a live first-run incident.

### B. Ordinary `mark` can recover a WAL after its receipt guard

Inside the inner paper lock, `mark` checks receipts at `paper_account.py:3141`, then `_load_account` at `3153` can replay an interrupted target settlement and create a receipt (`182–192, 1250`). There is no second ownership check before account/NAV writes (`3170, 3204, 3244`). This is a source-supported counterexample to the ordinary-mark guard when no receipt existed before recovery.

Current daily-mark callers have additional protection: union account pre-reads can recover first (`scheduler.py:1266`); the per-book loop checks receipts (`1309`); cash accrual loads/recovers before checking receipts itself (`paper_account.py:312–318`); and another account read precedes `mark`. Those protections prevent equating the direct function counterexample with a demonstrated scheduler incident.

**Small source correction, in separately owned account scope:** recover first, then verify receipt ownership under the same inner lock before ordinary writes. Preserve receipt-driven marks. The decisive regression uses a prepared target-settlement WAL with no receipt yet, proves recovery leaves its receipt, and proves an ordinary mark cannot write a new NAV/account projection past it; retain successful ordinary/no-WAL and authorized receipt-driven controls. No test or repair was executed here.

- Keep exact source/restore scope, backup success, rollback health and permitted runtime-data effects visible. A zero return or `started` status cannot replace those receipts.
- Bind the prospective source base, effective service/interpreter and expected lock paths. The parent reports `origin/master` advanced beyond the reviewed three pins, so these hashes cannot silently qualify a later bundle.
