## 4. Research conclusions that govern the build

The primary sources below motivate mechanisms and falsifiers; none validates a newly proposed Mastermind rule. No new empirical forecast or capital policy has passed promotion in this turn.

### 4.1 A run-up creates vulnerability, not a universal sell signal

Greenwood, Shleifer and You find that strong industry run-ups raise crash risk, while the run-up alone does not imply unusually low average subsequent returns. Run-up path, volatility, turnover and issuance add information [S1]. A 2026 long-history extension also emphasizes that booms can lead to unusually large gains as well as losses [S2]. These are not validations of a one-to-three-session exit rule.

Design consequence: do not fire a red warning merely because leaders made new highs or became extended. Research the interaction between concentrated advance, worsening participation/stress and subsequent observed failure. Include strong continuing advances as negative controls. Test whether any new interaction improves on a simple price-break baseline after costs.

### 4.2 Crowding and liquidity can amplify a modest trigger

Greenwood and Thesmar define fragility in terms of concentrated ownership and correlated demand/liquidity shocks; their evidence concerns volatility and comovement, not certainty about downward direction [S3]. BIS analysis of August 2024 distinguishes initial news from amplification by leverage, margin increases and unwinds, and records rapid stabilization afterward [S4].

Design consequence: label precursor, trigger and amplifier separately. Options/gamma/crowding estimates may corroborate a measured break, but inferred dealer inventories and incomplete positioning must remain uncertain. A valid price/breadth shock observation must not wait for an options dataset that lacks sufficient history. Nor should a warning remain permanently severe when a short-lived unwind repairs.

### 4.3 Different risk channels cannot be replaced by one volatility gauge

BIS market-condition work finds only loose comovement between market-specific stress indicators and VIX; it is explicit that some early-warning findings are in-sample and concern vulnerability rather than exact event timing [S5]. New York Fed analysis distinguishes trading liquidity from the ability to raise funding and explains their feedback under stress [S6].

Design consequence: preserve distinct rates, credit, market-depth/price-impact, funding and equity-volatility channels. Calm equity volatility cannot cancel measured credit/rates/breadth damage. A central-bank balance-sheet proxy must not be relabeled as current executable market depth. Country-specific funding and calendar conventions remain local.

### 4.4 Defensive rotation is a conditional hypothesis, not a two-stock rule

WMT/COST and the Oct 8 observation motivate a short-window hypothesis, not an accepted standalone predictor. Compare broad staples and defensive-basket relative strength against SPY, QQQ and the affected leader cohort over fixed 3/5/10/20-session windows. Distinguish absolute gains, relative outperformance, industry-specific earnings/sales effects and actually measured fund flows. XLU is a rate-sensitive channel, not a proxy for every defensive stock.

Healthy broadening means replacement leaders emerge while participation improves. Defensive escape is a different candidate interaction: defensive relative strength plus weakening breadth/stress and failure of prior leaders. Correlated manifestations of the same fact—WMT, COST, XLP and a staples score—must not count as four independent confirmations. Promotion requires an incremental, point-in-time test beyond each simpler input.

### 4.5 Recovery must not be the inverse of the entry warning

Daniel and Moskowitz's momentum crashes often occur in high-volatility rebounds after market declines, involving the short side/past losers [S7]. This is not the same phenomenon as long leaders rolling over at a top. Moreira and Muir provide a useful volatility-managed comparison strategy, not a license to import exact allocation coefficients into this product [S8].

Design consequence: separate capital-protection effectiveness from repair/re-entry effectiveness and test them jointly. A falling stress number, price bounce or announced support is not proof of durable repair. Conversely, a one-way defensive system that misses every rebound is not successful. Compare to simple trend/volatility policies, realistic cash returns, execution delay, turnover and missed upside.

### 4.6 Online change detection can improve reaction without pretending to predict news

CUSUM accumulates small shifts; NIST describes its sensitivity to smaller mean changes [S9]. Bayesian online changepoint detection estimates the current run-length distribution using only observations already available [S10]. Both require model assumptions and tuning; neither source establishes an equity-crash signal.

Design consequence: begin with transparent level/velocity/persistence and failed-break rules. A fixed CUSUM or online changepoint model is a challenger only after simple baselines and data clocks are correct. It may indicate a distributional change, not invent a calibrated crash probability or an economic cause. Do not add a second streaming infrastructure merely to host it.

### 4.7 Selection bias is a first-class failure mode

Backtest-overfitting research cautions against selecting financial strategies from many tried configurations and trusting an ordinary holdout after repeated selection [S11]. The program already has a specific replay/live parity invalidator (#7632), insufficient point-in-time membership, and rejected probability/capital-policy findings.

Design consequence: preserve every trial, freeze transforms/splits/targets before reserved outcomes, use canonical replay semantics, keep overlapping windows from posing as independent episodes, report uncertainty and leave-one-episode sensitivity, and preserve null findings. Oct 8 is a forensic acceptance case, not a parameter-selection set to be advertised as out-of-sample success.

### 8.4 Evaluation

Predeclare candidate count, fit window, evaluation splits and acceptance thresholds before reserved outcomes. Use expanding/walk-forward evaluation with label-overlap protection; episode/block uncertainty; realistic costs/turnover/cash returns; leave-one-crisis and country/era checks. For probability models add calibration/Brier/log loss and exact independent episode counts. For policy candidates report drawdown/tail loss and opportunity cost jointly, compared with an exposure-matched baseline.

A candidate fails promotion if its benefit vanishes with correct source clocks, is carried by one episode, does not improve over simple controls, requires unavailable membership, or wins solely by holding permanently less risk without acceptable opportunity cost. A failed forecast candidate may still justify a descriptive visibility or input-quality repair; these decisions stay separate.

### 8.5 Prospective learning

Use the existing first-writer, model-fingerprinted issued record and evaluation owner. A changed model starts a separately identifiable evidence cohort. Preserve prior-model results; do not pool them into a new apparent track record. Outcome maturity is a real time dependency, not something a longer ChatGPT turn can accelerate.
