## 1. What this wave changes

A defense or long-cycle program thesis should not begin and end with the amount customers announce they may spend. The first task is to identify the business's contractual claim, the costs and capital it must commit, the conditions for getting paid, and the portion of that activity belonging to shareholders. Physical importance does not answer those questions.

This wave adds a second dimension to the earlier aerospace risk-owner framework: a company may have substantial prospective demand but limited currently committed revenue; it may have firm revenue but highly uncertain profit; or it may have recognized profit while financing a large amount of unfinished or unbilled work. None is inherently an investment verdict. Each requires a different model, forecast horizon and falsifier.

The useful user outcome is an explanation of why two companies described as defense beneficiaries can have different earnings and cash trajectories. The machine must distinguish commercial certainty, cost-risk allocation, program phase, funding timing, accounting adjustments and ownership. It must not turn these differences into an invented universal backlog score or allow narrative enthusiasm to originate trades.

## 2. Backlog is a definition before it is an indicator

General Dynamics reports firm backlog separately from estimated potential contract value. In the compared quarters, firm backlog rises by $5,658 million while potential falls by $7,197 million, so combined estimated value declines by $1,539 million. Those are reconciled stock changes; they do not reveal how much potential converted to firm work or how much was canceled, revalued or newly recognized. The apparent trend depends on which object the analyst selects. [W5-S01]

Northrop excludes unexercised options and unawarded IDIQ orders. Leidos includes certain expected options and sole-source IDIQ task orders in unfunded backlog. Leonardo DRS highlights funded backlog. Fincantieri has firm and soft categories, and Rheinmetall's definition includes expected framework call-offs. These are legitimate issuer-defined disclosures, not a common measurement scale. [W5-S03, S08, S15, S17, S18]

The proposed framework therefore stores source-native measures first. A company comparison requires a documented reconciliation; a shared display label is insufficient. A funded percentage can help explain that company's disclosed financing coverage, but must not automatically rank it against another company whose denominator includes different options, currencies, products or customers. The research does not assign probability weights to unfunded work merely because a model needs a number.

A contract ceiling is also not a customer purchase at that ceiling. The FAR indefinite-quantity description distinguishes the stated minimum from possible future ordering. This establishes a reference distinction, not a finding about the clauses in every actual contract or jurisdiction. [W5-S24]

### Independent commercial dimensions

| Dimension | Question to preserve | What does not answer it |
|---|---|---|

| Funding | What funding is committed, authorized or still conditional for this scope? | A multi-year maximum |
| Scope | Which business, customer, work package and reporting perimeter? | Group-level defense label |
| Effectiveness | Have financing, approvals, option exercise or other stated conditions occurred? | A signed announcement alone |

| Recognition | What revenue and profit belong in the reporting period? | A physical delivery count alone |

| Duration | Which time window and cumulative/discrete basis applies? | Backlog divided by one year's sales |

The dimensions are an analytical view, not another lifecycle or intake service. They should be represented through existing evidence and relationship owners with the source's wording intact. Some arrangements will have missing data. Unknown is preferable to a fictitious progression through standardized stages.

## 3. Revenue visibility is not a cash-flow duration estimate

Northrop's approximately 35% expected recognition within twelve months and 55% within twenty-four months are cumulative forecasts. A compatible disjoint view is 35% in months 1-12, 20% in months 13-24 and 45% later. Adding 35% and 55% as separate buckets would double count. Recognition is not cash collection, completion or customer acceptance. [W5-S03]

A simple backlog-to-sales ratio should consequently be labeled a ratio, not guaranteed years of economically profitable work. The denominator may include services or businesses absent from the numerator; options may be contingent; currency and acquisitions may differ. Even a correctly measured revenue schedule still needs cost and payment schedules before discounting.

Large awards can make order growth lumpy. Saab's identified order accounts for about 68.7% of its current-quarter bookings. Removing that one award from the numerator does not create a normalized growth rate unless the comparative period and the broader award population are treated consistently. A concentrated award can be valuable, but it also changes exposure to timing, execution and customer decisions. [W5-S10]

The research needs both the stock of obligations and the flows that change it: new orders, recognition, amendments, currency, portfolio movements and withdrawals. Without the bridge, label the residual unexplained. Do not infer a cancellation simply because a combined total declines, or a new economic win when a conditional instrument becomes an already-counted firm obligation.

## 4. Contract type determines who captures a cost change

Public contract definitions make a useful starting distinction. Firm-fixed-price work exposes the contractor to actual-cost changes. Cost reimbursement is bounded by allowability, authorization and cost limits. A fixed-fee contract does not make the fee grow automatically with actual costs. Fixed-price incentive work shares cost variation until its ceiling binds. Actual incorporated clauses, jurisdiction, changes in scope and applicable versions remain controlling. This is economic research, not legal adjudication. [W5-S20-S23]

### A financial sensitivity with three arrangements

Assume a fixed selling price of 120 for the first arrangement. The incentive arrangement has target cost 100, target profit 20, contractor cost-share 20% and a price ceiling of 132. The reimbursable example pays an unchanged fee of 10; all modeled costs are assumed allowable and authorized within a cost limit of 150.

| Actual cost | Firm-fixed-price profit | Incentive profit | Cost-plus-fixed-fee profit |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 90 | 30 | 22 | 10 |
| 100 | 20 | 20 | 10 |
| 115 | 5 | 17 | 10 |
| 120 | 0 | 12 | 10 |
| 145 | -25 | -13 | 10 |

The incentive model first computes price as actual cost plus target profit plus the contractor's share of the difference between target and actual cost, then applies the price ceiling. In this example, the ceiling starts binding at cost 115. A ceiling on price is not a ceiling on the contractor's loss. These are chosen parameters, not estimates of any real program.

The cost-plus example also illustrates a denominator effect. Fee profit remains 10 as reimbursed costs increase, so percentage margin can fall without a fall in dollar fee. That does not mean the business has no risk: unallowable expenditure, unavailable funding, disallowed cost, penalties, delayed payment, overhead and future competitiveness remain outside this deliberately narrow illustration.

The proposed sector model cannot infer attractiveness from a higher margin alone. A lower-margin business with less downside exposure may have different capital and risk characteristics. Conversely, a fixed-price franchise may capture valuable productivity but bear a disproportionate tail loss. The research must compare the relevant return and risk, not reward one contract label unconditionally.

## 5. Productivity does not automatically become supplier profit

Consider an illustrative service delivering the same accepted outcome. Initially it uses 100,000 hours, charges 150 per hour and incurs labor cost of 100 per hour: revenue is 15 million, labor cost 10 million, contribution 5 million. A new process reduces hours by 20% but costs 1 million for tools and support.

With an unchanged fixed output price, contribution rises to 6 million. Under an hourly arrangement that bills only actual hours, revenue falls to 12 million and contribution becomes 3 million. This assumes no staff redeployment, other capacity utilization or new contracts, and no price change. It does not suggest billing unperformed work.

The same productivity improvement can create customer value while reducing the provider's near-term revenue. A supplier investment thesis therefore needs to specify retention of savings through output pricing, shared-savings terms, increased transaction throughput, winning additional work or redeploying scarce staff. A narrative about automation or AI is not enough.

The model also needs the full cost of delivery: implementation, support, software fees, security requirements where relevant, and the expenditure to maintain customer service. Contract labels alone do not establish software economics, recurring gross margins or scaled paid adoption. Business scopes at Leidos, CACI and L3Harris provide investigation anchors, not proof that the full issuer is a pure software or defense exposure. [W5-S12, S15, S16]

This distinction connects Wave 5 to the broader Industrials program. Any contractor or service provider can face the same question: who keeps the benefit of better execution? The shared template should support that question without embedding an industry-specific assumption that productivity always increases the supplier's margin.

## 6. Cash timing changes value even when lifetime profit is identical

In the following hypothetical contract, receipts total 120 and fulfillment cash costs total 100. The cost schedule at times 0, 1, 2 and 3 is 20, 30, 30 and 20. Discounting uses 10%; taxes, intra-period cash peaks, financing charges and default are omitted.

| Payment schedule | Receipts at t=0,1,2,3[,4] | Lifetime net cash | Peak period-end net funding | NPV |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Earlier payments | 30, 30, 30, 30 | 20 | 0 | 17.51 |
| Later payments | 0, 10, 30, 80 | 20 | 40 | 6.90 |
| Last receipt delayed one more period | 0, 10, 30, 0, 80 | 20 | 60 | 1.43 |

The first schedule does not imply no financing is ever required inside a period; that cannot be inferred from period-end netting. Nor are advances free profit. They are valuable funding conditional on completing obligations. The calculation establishes sensitivity to payment timing, not the terms of any issuer's contracts.

HII's increase in net contract assets demonstrates why this question belongs in the financial model. It combines higher contract assets and lower liabilities and is attributed primarily to billing timing. That balance-sheet movement cannot simply be treated as the entire operating-cash-flow change; taxes, payables, other working capital and noncash adjustments also matter. [W5-S06]

BAE's cash improvement includes advances, while its later overlapping multi-year targets are unchanged. The evidence motivates investigating timing and durability, but does not supply a complete schedule proving all the cash improvement is merely pulled forward. Likewise, Rheinmetall's strong revenue and profit alongside negative cash does not alone distinguish valuable growth investment from a problematic obligation. Both require subsequent conversion and full capital analysis. [W5-S07, S08]

The proposed product should show the customer-funding balance, the expected cost to fulfill the related obligations and the next verifiable payment condition. It must not reward positive working capital mechanically or penalize all advance-funded growth as low quality.

## 7. Known program losses: neither erase them nor charge them twice

A program charge may make an otherwise attractive reported quarter look weak; removing it mechanically may make an economically poor contract look attractive. Our research needs the change in estimated lifetime economics and the timing of both recognition and cash.

For a hypothetical fixed contract priced at 120, suppose the latest total cost estimate is 135, of which 40 has been paid. Collections to date are 50. Lifetime net cash is negative 15; net cash to date is positive 10; remaining receipts of 70 less remaining costs of 95 imply future net cash of negative 25. Historical plus future cash correctly reconciles to negative 15.

If a model already includes the remaining 95 of fulfillment costs, subtracting the full known 15 loss again from future cash would double count. But excluding the loss from an earnings metric also does not make the unfavorable cash obligation disappear. The example does not implement a particular accounting standard's loss-provision entries; it is a cash consistency test.

The valuation should use a coherent boundary: either explicit forecast cash including obligations, or an appropriately reconciled liability adjustment that excludes the same cost from the operating forecast. A provision balance is not automatically cash, and its movement is not automatically a payment. Evidence must separate revised estimates, settlements, payments and recoveries.

A large comparison-base charge can also make subsequent reported growth look exceptional without a matching improvement in recurring economics. Northrop's divestiture comparison, HII's contract adjustments and BWXT's fewer favorable adjustments demonstrate that the analyst must isolate the actual source of change rather than treat every margin move as operating learning. [W5-S02, S05, S13]

## 8. A guidance upgrade must be decomposed before it becomes a thesis

Lockheed's midpoint arithmetic is unusually clear. Operating cash remains 9,300 million; planned capex falls from 2,650 to 2,200 million; FCF rises from 6,650 to 7,100 million. The 450 million cash-flow midpoint increase matches the capex reduction, not an increase in the operating-cash midpoint. Meanwhile, sales midpoint rises 2,000 million and segment-profit midpoint only 50 million. These are descriptive comparisons, not a full earnings attribution or market-consensus surprise. [W5-S04]

A lower capital budget can reflect efficiency, revised timing, changed scope or future underinvestment. The disclosure of a lower amount does not settle which explanation is correct. The research must ask what capability or obligation still needs funding, whether later expenditure increases, and whether the same output remains achievable. It should not automatically treat the change as either good or bad.

Northrop raises its EPS range while leaving the segment operating-income range unchanged, and separately reports a significant quarterly tax change. This means an EPS revision is not interchangeable with an operating-profit revision. It does not establish that every dollar of revised guidance comes from taxes; a complete bridge remains a further research obligation. [W5-S02]

The current wave has an issuer-attributed expectations example: Rheinmetall's preliminary revenue was below the market figure it cited despite year-over-year growth. That is a dated reference with a publisher and limitation. It is not a substitute for a broad external consensus panel. No claim about subsequent stock response is made. [W5-S09]

The shared dossier should distinguish reported results, management's forecast, attributed external expectations, a retained licensed consensus vintage and our own scenario. A missing forecast source remains missing. A favorable business development cannot be called underappreciated simply because the analyst finds it persuasive.

## 9. Financing and capital allocation belong in the quality analysis

CACI explicitly supplies operating cash both before and after its receivables-purchase arrangement. Quarterly GAAP operating cash grows approximately 142.5%; excluding that arrangement, it grows approximately 67.4%. Both improve, but they describe different amounts. Its own reconciliation allows the distinction without alleging accounting impropriety or assuming every receivable sale is economically identical. [W5-S16]

Fincantieri's disclosed adjusted-debt reduction is 555 million; the difference between actual and equity-effect-excluded current debt is 478 million. The residual reduction excluding that effect is 77 million. This is a bridge based on disclosed alternatives, not proof that the residual comes entirely from recurring operating improvement. The 891 million supplier-financing balance also needs separate visibility. Adding it to adjusted net debt creates a clearly labeled liquidity sensitivity, not a restatement of the issuer's accounting debt. [W5-S18]

A funding transaction can genuinely reduce financial risk while diluting existing shareholders or increasing dependence on a facility. The economic question is the resulting claim per share, future financing flexibility and returns on the capital deployed. Improving a leverage ratio does not by itself establish organic deleveraging or value creation.

Acquisitions and internal reorganizations require effective dates. BWXT's post-quarter acquisition must not be included in June historical sales. Saab's business transfer does not create new external revenue. Parent and listed-subsidiary exposures, including Leonardo DRS, cannot be added without understanding ownership and noncontrolling interests. [W5-S11, S13, S17]

These principles extend beyond defense. They are essential to industrial consolidators, contractors, distributors and equipment owners. The shared template should expose capital and scope changes alongside the narrative instead of treating them as distant accounting footnotes.

## 11. Twelve rerating hypotheses and falsifiers

| Hypothesis | Evidence that would support it | Strong counterargument or falsifier |
|---|---|---|
| W5-H01 Conditional demand becomes economically firm | Funded/order conversion with matching scope and timing | Same amount merely relabeled or added twice |
| W5-H02 Cost risk is repriced | Accepted new terms improve retained contribution | Better headline price offset by wider scope or costs |
| W5-H03 Execution improves through the cycle | Cohort cost-to-complete and accepted output improve | Gains primarily estimate releases or favorable mix |
| W5-H04 Production duration becomes more profitable | Incremental capacity earns cash returns after investment | Growth extends low-margin obligations or idle assets |
| W5-H05 Customer funding reduces capital burden | Durable payment terms with profitable fulfillment | Advances reverse or future cost obligations rise |
| W5-H06 Known losses run off predictably | Cash outflows and remaining obligations match the reserve thesis | Additional charges, refunds or unmodeled claims |
| W5-H07 Service productivity is retained | Pricing/contract design converts savings into provider contribution | Hourly billings decline without replacement work |
| W5-H08 Portfolio simplification improves returns | Completed transactions reduce costs and capital burden | Stranded overhead, expensive acquisition or dilution |
| W5-H09 Cash quality improves | Billing, collection and full capital needs reconcile over time | Financing, tax timing or deferred capex explains the gain |
| W5-H10 Program concentration risk declines | Broader funded contribution across independent obligations | Backlog expansion dominated by one award or correlated customer |
| W5-H11 Profit uncertainty decreases | Forecast errors and unfavorable revisions shrink in held-out cohorts | Historical restatement or selective winners explain improvement |
| W5-H12 Price expectations lag the economic change | Dated external estimates and a consistent price/valuation panel | Change already anticipated or broad factors explain returns |

These are candidate research mechanisms, not instructions to buy or short a company. A large customer can offer long-duration work while retaining bargaining power; qualification can protect incumbency while requiring ongoing investment. Higher certainty, higher margins, longer duration and faster growth are not interchangeable. Each has different valuation implications and must be supported on its own terms.

Avoid attaching a premium multiple to an industry label. For program-heavy businesses, value the remaining obligations and future awarded cohorts with coherent costs, financing and capital. For services, test retention and contribution without assuming every recurring contract is high quality. Reconcile corporate costs, financing, tax, pension differences and minority interests before comparing shareholder values. Never subtract a recognized loss twice or count an expected follow-on contract as both existing backlog and a new growth opportunity.

## 12. Data feasibility and validation design

Public releases and filings provide business-level accounting, selected commitments and management explanations. They rarely provide complete clauses, task-order funding histories, contract-level margin, detailed acceptance events, full cost-to-complete distributions or customer-specific adoption. That coverage boundary must be visible. A source claiming maturity or production readiness is not independent proof of customer acceptance, nor permission to infer technical capability.

The next validation work should use dated disclosure vintages and report actual missingness. Separate thesis explanation from financial-forecast improvement, and both from stock-return prediction. Hold out time periods and programs rather than optimize a score on the same examples used to invent it. Include unfavorable charges, delayed projects and canceled opportunities, not only successful incumbents.

Candidate tests compare naive headline order/revenue growth against properly defined funding, cost and cash features. Test whether an inference survives removing acquisition growth, gains, tax changes and financing effects. A correct source-normalization rule can reduce mistakes without creating predictive alpha; that useful capability should be reported honestly. Current results contain no empirical backtest, calibrated transition probabilities or accepted trading rule.

The GAO sample provides a warning about long schedules and maturity uncertainty, not a company-level score. Do not map aggregate audit findings to a particular listed supplier's earnings without a supported business/program relationship. Actual clauses also need the correct version: the base FAR and overhaul guide can number similar material differently. The index therefore separates effective reference dates from source publication dates. [W5-S19, S26]

Native source retention, licensing and current data publication must use the approved existing owners. This public research packet is original methodology and selected editorial examples. It is not permission to place a paid full-fidelity current research snapshot in public static files. The eventual view should show missing inputs, source limitations and unresolved conflicts rather than silently complete the story.
