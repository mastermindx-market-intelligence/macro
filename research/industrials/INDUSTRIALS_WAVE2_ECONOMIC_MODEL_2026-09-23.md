## 1. Decisions produced by this wave

The useful division is not simply automation versus electrification. Research should distinguish **production-linked equipment, long-cycle project equipment, and paid software/service cohorts**, then connect them to the businesses that actually earn the revenue. A single issuer can occupy all three.

A second decision corrects the first-wave funnel. Commercial commitment, physical progress, accounting recognition and cash collection are four separate measurements. They often influence each other but are not interchangeable steps in a universal sequence. Powell recognizes most relevant revenue over time; Vertiv shows substantial customer-funded working-capital movements. A factory shipment, an accounting sale and an advance cash receipt cannot be displayed as the same realization event. [W2-S13, W2-S20]

Third, business exposure and economic sensitivity require different representations. A reported segment share can support an accounting allocation; it cannot establish a narrower product's share, the company's incremental profit sensitivity, or the stock's return sensitivity. Fourth, valuation work must price the **capital and risk required to produce growth**, not attach a fashionable multiple to a product label. Fifth, a thesis should include its strongest discriminating counterexample before becoming a product research module.

The proposed delivery to the user is a dossier that explains what changed, whose economics are affected, how that might reach earnings and cash, which expectations would need to change, and what evidence would overturn the interpretation. Citation density without that explanation would not complete the user job.

## 2. Three demand rhythms, not one industrial cycle

### 2.1 Production-linked equipment and replenishment

The proposed model begins at the customer's actual activity: production, machine utilization, replacement needs and investment returns. Supplier orders may reflect those forces, but may also reflect distributor inventory, changing lead times or purchasing timing. A causal interpretation requires evidence on more than the supplier's sales growth.

Use a reconciled inventory identity where the necessary data exist: ending channel inventory equals opening inventory plus sell-in minus sell-through, with returns and perimeter adjustments explicit. An increase in supplier sell-in without stronger sell-through is a different observation from customer consumption. Where channel stock is unavailable, state the limitation; do not create a synthetic inventory explanation from a slowing growth rate.

The recovery hypothesis should progress from broad customer activity to actual paid orders, region/end-market breadth and margin/cash response. The opposite hypothesis is that replenishment, currency or a narrow customer cohort explains the improvement. Neither hypothesis is resolved by the word automation. SMC's volume/currency bridge and Keyence's local-currency regional disclosures provide useful evidence structures, not interchangeable measures of factory utilization. [W2-S17, W2-S21]

A business can be healthy while its stock disappoints. An improving order rate may already have been anticipated, a more profitable product mix may normalize, or the market's discount rate may rise. The research should not infer an expectations gap without an actual dated expectations reference.

### 2.2 Long-cycle electrical projects

Here the paying customer's project economics and ability to execute matter alongside demand. The proposed research model distinguishes the announced project, customer funding, equipment specification, firm commercial commitment, manufacturing progress, site readiness, commissioning and actual use. Not every project follows the same legal or accounting sequence; the data should preserve source-native stages.

Investigate the contract's price/cost allocation, cancellation terms, progress payments, change orders, customer concentration, delivery responsibilities and cost-estimate revisions. A large backlog can be attractive, ordinary or problematic depending on these terms. Expanding the plant to fulfill it can create durable capacity or a costly fixed-cost burden. Advance payments may reduce funding needs while creating obligations that must still be performed.

The important economic distinction is between demand for more equipment and the supplier's ability to capture value from delivering it. A constrained buyer may postpone the whole project. A supplier may incur ramp costs before receiving mature factory margins. A fixed-price contract may lock in sales without locking in input costs. These are questions to resolve from disclosures and contract evidence, not reasons to assume every backlog is weak.

### 2.3 Paid software and installed-base services

A recurring-revenue hypothesis begins with a specific paid contract, not a segment name. Separate perpetual licenses and maintenance, subscriptions, usage charges, embedded control software, engineered integration, field services and outcome-linked contracts. Growth in one does not prove growth in the others.

For an actual recurring cohort, start with opening recurring revenue, retained revenue after churn/contraction, expansion, and new customers, plus currency, acquisitions and definition changes. A net retention ratio needs the original cohort denominator; total revenue growth cannot substitute. Model customer acquisition, implementation effort, support obligations and contract duration before assigning software-like economics.

An installed equipment base may create replacement and service opportunities without automatically creating a subscription. Research the conversion from installed asset to active use, eligible service event, paid attachment, renewal and contribution after service costs. An announced modernization offering is evidence of a proposition, not evidence of adoption or attractive economics. Schneider's launch and ABB's monitoring options therefore create research questions rather than automatic recurring-revenue assertions. [W2-S07, W2-S24]

## 3. The four realization measurements

| Measurement | Question | Examples of admissible observations | Invalid shortcut |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial commitment | What has the customer actually committed to? | Dated reservation, accepted order, contract, RPO, backlog under its stated definition | Treating every announcement as an order |
| Physical progress | What has been made, delivered, installed or operated? | Manufacturing milestone, shipment, commissioning, measured use | Treating accounting revenue as commissioned capacity |
| Accounting recognition | What revenue, cost and profit belong in this period? | Point-in-time or over-time recognition; cost-to-complete revision | Assuming recognition occurs only on delivery |
| Cash collection and investment | Who funded the work and what remains to be spent? | Advance, milestone payment, collection, inventory, capex, refund | Treating cash received as completed economic profit |

This is an analytical distinction, not a proposed replacement lifecycle service. Existing native owners retain their source records and identities. One project can have evidence on all four axes, each with its own date, quantity, unit and uncertainty. A summary can connect them only when the common project/business binding is supported.

**Example of a negative case:** a supplier receives an advance, begins custom manufacture and recognizes some over-time revenue before delivery. The product must not show three separate customer wins, three units of final demand, or full customer acceptance. Nor should it assume the advance is an accounting irregularity. The correct output explains the funded work, recognition basis and remaining obligations.

## 6. Exposure quality without invented purity

A future exposure record should answer four separate questions: what the company demonstrably does; how much of a matching financial denominator it represents; how much incremental profit might change under a scenario; and whether the stock's behavior responds to it. These require different evidence and must not share one confidence label.

Source-backed accounting arithmetic illustrates the distinction. Siemens' disclosed software amount is approximately 36.3% of Digital Industries revenue for the matched quarter. That is **not** 36.3% of Siemens group revenue, EDA revenue, ARR or industrial-cloud subscriptions. The Smart Infrastructure service subset is approximately 19.7% of that segment; it is not necessarily all recurring. [W2-S05; derived ratios, rounded]

Likewise, nVent's Systems Protection represents approximately 72.9% of its reported quarterly group sales. That percentage is not a data-center or liquid-cooling exposure estimate. Rockwell's Software & Control share of reported group sales is approximately 32.5%; it cannot be relabeled recurring-software purity. [W2-S03, W2-S14; derived ratios]

Where a source states an application but supplies no numerator, preserve qualitative evidence and an explicit undisclosed share. A null share is not zero. A source-supported range should identify how it was estimated, not look like a disclosed point value. Multiple overlapping themes are non-additive unless a reconciled allocation establishes otherwise.

Marginal sensitivity is a separate analytical object. A small business could contribute disproportionately to profit growth if its incremental margin is high; a large revenue business could consume investment. The appropriate scenario therefore includes incremental revenue, variable costs, fixed costs, tax and capital needs. It does not infer a price-return coefficient from a revenue weight.

## 7. Earnings models the research should actually build

### 7.1 Volume, price, mix and costs

Do not start with a single revenue-growth input when the available evidence supports a bridge. Use quantities, realized price, product/customer mix, currency and portfolio scope as separate terms, with source-defined arithmetic. Reported percentage contributions may be rounded or calculated using sequential bases; do not force an exact identity that the source does not promise.

A simple **hypothetical** model demonstrates why revenue and profit can diverge. Begin with sales of 100, variable costs of 65 and fixed operating costs of 15, giving operating profit of 20. Volume increases 10%, price 2%, variable cost per unit 3%, and fixed costs rise to 17. Revenue becomes 112.2, variable costs 73.645 and operating profit 21.555. Sales grow 12.2%, profit grows 7.8% and operating margin falls to approximately 19.2%. No company forecast is implied.

The inverse can also occur: a lower-cost structure or favorable mix can improve profit with modest sales. The research must determine whether that change is sustainable or a favorable comparison. Eaton's sequential/annual margin contrast and Siemens' severance-adjusted comparison demonstrate why both the comparison axis and adjustment basis are necessary. [W2-S01–02, W2-S05]

### 7.2 Projects and estimate changes

For an over-time contract, retain the contract value, performance obligations, progress measure, incurred costs, estimated remaining cost and revision history where disclosed. Revenue recognition does not independently verify site readiness or customer commissioning. Favorable estimate revisions may reflect good execution, changed scope or prior conservatism; repeated reliance on them is a research question, not automatically misconduct or evidence of a superior moat.

A proposed project scenario should stress delayed materials, reduced labor productivity, price escalation, customer deferral and disputes. Identify who bears each cost contractually. Backlog duration and conversion are not enough without profit and cash sensitivity. Avoid turning a monetary backlog-to-sales ratio into a literal delivery lead time.

### 7.3 Software and service cohorts

The model should distinguish growth of paid users/sites/assets from price, expansion, renewal and acquisitions. A broader platform can improve cross-selling, but it can also increase integration costs. Neither outcome is established by a renamed segment. Gross retention, net retention, contract length and actual service margin are often missing; those gaps should remain visible rather than be replaced by a sector-average assumption.

Business quality should be tested across both adoption and slowdown conditions. Does a customer renew because the product remains useful? Are services essential support or discretionary projects? Does greater scale lower support cost per customer, or do difficult installations consume skilled labor? The relevant evidence is cohort economics and customer outcomes, not only bookings.

## 8. Cash, customer funding and the cost of growth

Vertiv's reported cash components reconcile: net working-capital changes add $451.0 million to cash from operations, and operating cash less capex and capitalized software reconciles to its reported adjusted free cash flow. That reconciliation verifies arithmetic only. Removing one favorable funding component while holding the associated inventory, receivables and payables unchanged does not produce a credible normalized business. [W2-S13]

Research should show both the reported bridge and the economic interpretation. Customer funding can be valuable when orders are durable and fulfillment is profitable. It can reverse when new advances slow, existing orders are delivered, or refunds are required. Cash associated with performance obligations must therefore be considered with expected fulfillment costs, not assigned a blanket quality penalty or bonus.

A proposed stress case asks what happens when new orders slow but prior orders continue converting. It should change collections, inventory purchases, supplier payments, customer advances and earnings consistently. An independent cash normalization needs assumptions about the whole operating cycle. The simple arithmetic of subtracting all working-capital movements may be displayed as a decomposition, not as a forecast of sustainable free cash flow.

SMC's inventory-availability proposition poses a different question: which inventory is necessary to achieve valuable service levels, and what is excessive? A high inventory ratio can be part of a customer proposition, yet still require capital and carry obsolescence risk. The model should test availability, repeat business and the return on capital supporting that service, rather than apply a universal low-inventory preference. [W2-S18]

**Hypothetical capital example.** Two businesses grow sales from 100 to 120 at a 20% operating margin and 25% tax rate. Both increase after-tax operating profit from 15 to 18. If one needs 5 of net new operating capital and the other needs 20, cash after that reinvestment is 13 versus negative 2, under the simplified assumptions. Negative growth cash is not automatically value destruction; the key questions are the subsequent returns, duration and funding risk. The example assumes maintenance investment offsets depreciation and excludes acquisitions and financing.

This distinction belongs in the research product because it directly affects the user's interpretation of growth. A business that requires heavy capital to meet attractive demand may still be worthwhile, but it is not economically identical to a business that can meet the same growth using existing capacity.

## 9. Valuation: what can rerate, and what would need to be true

### 9.1 Four distinct valuation questions

Separate current economic performance, expected future performance, the amount of uncertainty, and the price paid for that future. Management guidance is not consensus. Our scenario is not uniquely implied market belief. An observed multiple change is not a clean causal measure of sentiment or quality. A useful dossier keeps these layers inspectable.

A near-term earnings revision may result from volume, margin, currency, interest, tax, share count or a comparison-base change. A longer-duration valuation change might require a higher through-cycle margin floor, a longer profitable runway, greater retention, improved capital returns or reduced project risk. The proposed hypotheses should specify which mechanism is supposed to operate; the system should not call every favorable quarterly result a structural rerating.

The current wave has no matched price/consensus panel. Therefore no company-level statement that an opportunity is cheap, expensive, underappreciated or already fully priced is established. That is a data/evaluation limitation, not a reason to omit valuation architecture.

### 9.2 A transparent stable-state sensitivity

For a deliberately simplified stable business, let next-period after-tax operating profit be NOPAT1, sustainable growth be g, the return on incremental invested capital be R, and the cost of capital be r. Assume growth requires reinvestment at rate g/R, the forecast is internally consistent, and g is below r. Then:

`EV / NOPAT1 = (1 - g/R) / (r - g)`.

This follows from distributing NOPAT after the reinvestment required for growth and discounting a stable growing cash stream. It is a teaching/sensitivity model, not a current industrial target multiple. Average historical ROIC cannot automatically substitute for the return on new investment; near-term ramp costs, acquisitions, changing margins and non-operating items require explicit modeling.

| Hypothetical stable case | Growth | Incremental return | Cost of capital | EV / next-period NOPAT |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 3.0% | 12.0% | 9.0% | 12.50x |
| B | 3.0% | 24.0% | 9.0% | 14.58x |
| C | 3.0% | 24.0% | 10.5% | 11.67x |

B differs from A because it reinvests less to support the same growth. C demonstrates that improved economics can coexist with a lower valuation when the discount rate changes. These inputs are chosen illustrations, not estimated current market parameters. The calculation is invalid at g greater than or equal to r and is not suitable for indefinite growth with implausible competitive returns.

### 9.3 Valuation by economic model

For short-cycle equipment, compare normalized earnings at plausible utilization and reinvestment rather than blindly annualize the strongest quarter. For project equipment, value the profitability and conversion of obligations along with returns on expansion, not backlog dollars alone. For software/service cohorts, model retention and contribution after selling, integration and support. For integrated groups, reconcile the parts to corporate costs and capital before claiming a sum-of-parts discount.

Reported segment Profit, adjusted EBITA, EBIT, EBITDA and free cash flow must retain their definitions. Siemens' Profit-to-EBIT reconciliation and Emerson/nVent's segment-to-group costs show why multiplying segment profit and forgetting the corporate bridge overstates comparable operating earnings. [W2-S05, W2-S10, W2-S14]

A complete enterprise-to-equity bridge should specify operating value, corporate obligations, cash needed in operations, excess financial assets, debt, leases, pensions and noncontrolling interests as applicable. Do not value a finance subsidiary using industrial operating assumptions or subtract its funding debt from an industrial-only valuation. Do not add buyback yield again when its per-share benefit is already included in the earnings scenario.

### 9.4 Evidence required for a rerating thesis

| Proposed mechanism | Evidence that would support the mechanism | Evidence that should weaken it |
|---|---|---|
| More durable earnings | Paid retention, installed-base use, repeat service and supported through-cycle profitability | Recurring labels depend on one-time engineering or customer concentration |
| Better incremental returns | Profitable capacity conversion and cash after consistent reinvestment | Expansion costs or acquisition capital dominate the incremental profit |
| A higher margin floor | Low-demand comparison with sustained operating improvements | Favorable price/cost timing, mix or adjustment bases explain the improvement |
| A longer profitable runway | Funded customer demand and repeatable delivery economics | Repeated deferrals, weak customer returns or loss-making obligations |
| Reduced execution risk | Reliable project milestones, cost estimates, collection and service performance | Unrecognized liabilities, persistent rework or deteriorating collections |
| Productive portfolio change | Completed ownership and credible post-integration economics | Unclosed deals or assumed synergies supply the entire case |

None of these rows is an automatic positive signal. The research should state which mechanism is under investigation and the observable condition that would change the conclusion.

## 10. Physical detail earns its place through economics

There are three useful levels of physical detail: the component specification, its place inside a purchasable system, and the customer outcome. The system should not blur them. Eaton's integrated-power offering illustrates overlapping purchase boundaries; the CDU table demonstrates that a capacity rating depends on operating conditions. [W2-S25–26]

A component map is useful when it changes the estimate of content, substitutability, qualification, installed cost, capacity or service burden. It is less useful when it merely expands the number of nodes. A supplier selling a complete enclosure may incorporate third-party devices; knowing the contained brands does not allow their revenues to be added again to the customer's total spend.

For a bottleneck hypothesis, require the constrained product/configuration, evidenced lead-time or capacity condition, qualification barrier, substitute, economic beneficiary, affected cost/price contract and time horizon. A critical component need not be scarce. Announced capacity need not be qualified. A design that reduces component count may improve customer economics while reducing supplier content. All three possibilities must remain available to the analysis.

The proposed research should explicitly test whether scarcity is transient or generates durable power. Customer qualification and support requirements could sustain an advantage after supply normalizes; insourcing or substitution could weaken it. Neither conclusion follows from technology complexity alone.

## 11. Research observations, scenarios and evaluations

### 11.1 Comparison keys

Before a numerical change is calculated, match the legal/business scope, fiscal interval, measure definition, unit, currency treatment, accounting basis, seasonal adjustment, portfolio perimeter and source vintage. An exact match is not always possible. In that case show the raw observations and explain the mismatch instead of generating a misleading change.

A record needs both the reported value and any derived value. It should carry the original denominator, the arithmetic or methodology for the derivation, source locators and explicit exclusions. A ratio with rounded inputs should display appropriate precision. A customer name or product label should not acquire a live security identity through string similarity.

### 11.2 Candidate tests of incremental intelligence

The later empirical study should compare this economic model against simpler alternatives, not just recount successful companies. Proposed baselines include a broad industry classification, supplier sales growth alone, price momentum alone, and a model using only general market/industry factors. The richer model should have to demonstrate an improvement for a declared task.

Tasks should remain separate. Explaining contemporaneous earnings, forecasting the next earnings revision, detecting a thesis failure and predicting a stock return are different evaluation problems. An explanatory success does not prove return predictiveness. A return correlation does not prove the proposed operational mechanism.

Freeze the eligible universe, source availability and horizon before observing outcomes. Retain acquired, delisted and failed businesses where feasible. Use publication-time financial estimates rather than later consensus; preserve revised filings and macro vintages. Split development and held-out evaluation by time or genuinely independent cohorts. Avoid selecting whichever horizon makes the same story look successful.

Useful falsification tests include removing currency/acquisition growth, replacing reported margins with consistently adjusted measures, checking customer concentration, and testing whether the interpretation survives the next comparable disclosure. A proposed order-based forecast should be challenged against end-market demand and channel conditions; a cash-quality thesis should be challenged against the full working-capital cycle.

### 11.3 What is not yet available

No licensed point-in-time consensus panel, matched current valuation dataset or source-complete customer/product-revenue census has been recovered. No empirical backtest, return attribution or forecasting-calibration result is claimed. Native source retention, rights, current security binding and product admission are distinct later requirements. The new SMC presentation resolves its specific quarterly read gap; it does not resolve these broader data needs.

## 12. Product workflow and reusable-template requirements

The proposed user workflow is **Theme Tracker → industrial research slice → mechanism and business scope → evidence changes → earnings/cash explanation → qualified expectations/scenarios → falsifiers → existing company workflow**. Preserve the current page and analytical owners. Do not add an independent rank, signal, identity, graph, evidence, forecast or publication service.

The top of a dossier should answer a user's actual question in plain language: what is driving this business now, what makes that interpretation uncertain, and which next observation would matter? Then allow inspection of the supporting structure. A graph is optional; the correct period, definition and business scope are not.

A short-cycle module should emphasize matched order/demand/inventory evidence. A project module should show commercial commitment, recognition, delivery and cash separately. A recurring module should show paid cohorts and contract economics where known. Each can share the same source drawer and company navigation without sharing a meaningless universal score.

A valuation view should expose assumptions and unavailable inputs. It should not quietly replace missing consensus with the model's own summary or turn a hypothetical sensitivity into a price target. Research coverage, confidence that a statement was made, confidence in the economic inference, and investment attractiveness are different concepts.

The existing GMI evidence owner, K1 source/clock bindings, Data OS identity and relationship/forecast owners remain the integration destinations. The Robotics proposal is a reusable design reference, not proof that its proposed native extension has been enrolled. ## 14. Frontier after this wave

The important result is a researched distinction between several superficially similar growth stories, with business scopes, comparison hazards, economic models and falsifiers sufficiently concrete to constrain future implementation. The 28 slices are not a global company census. The 13-company cohort is not a basket. The valuation examples are not current fair values. The proposed tests are not shipped software.

Remaining Wave 2 obligations include narrower product/customer revenue evidence, a broader specialist and regional cohort, retained native source bytes and rights, consistent multi-period cash/capital data and genuine expectations inputs. Schneider's detailed presentation and ABB's unread visual tables remain bounded source gaps; their primary headline releases are usable. These gaps should be revisited only when the next question needs them, not repeatedly polled.

The next major research unit should test these principles in **machinery, rental, distribution and transport**, where customer replacement economics, dealer inventory, finance exposure, fleet investment and network utilization can dominate. The specific first comparison is machinery replacement demand versus rental-fleet economics, using matched customer, operating and capital data and adverse-cycle counterexamples. Fable's build handoff remains held until the sector research, data feasibility and written integration design are mature.
