## 1. Five deltas worth preserving

### Delta A — revenue exposure can understate profit relevance

Rockwell's June 2026 Form 10-Q reports Software & Control sales of **$751 million** against enterprise sales of **$2,313 million**, and segment operating earnings of **$261 million** against **$550 million** across the three reported segment operating-earnings amounts. Software & Control operating earnings increased **$62 million** year over year while enterprise operating profit increased **$98 million**. Reproducible calculations therefore give approximately:

| Reported contribution | Derived result |
|---|---:|
| Software & Control share of enterprise sales | **32.47%** |
| Software & Control share of disclosed aggregate segment operating earnings | **47.45%** |
| Software & Control share of the enterprise operating-profit increase | **63.27%** |

Source W2X-S01: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1024478/000102447826000030/rok-20260630.htm — quarter/nine months ended 30 June 2026; segment tables and corporate reconciliation.

Historical business/channel context comes from W2X-S02: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1024478/000102447825000116/rok-20250930.htm — FY2025 Form 10-K. Independent distributors accounted for 65% of fiscal-year sales; distributor sell-in therefore must not be silently promoted to observed end-user consumption.

The three contribution percentages have different denominators. The 63.27% figure is **not** proof that Software & Control is 63% of Rockwell's economic value, pure software, a 63% organic incremental margin, or a 63% stock beta. A thematic system should retain revenue contribution, reported profit contribution and incremental profit contribution as separate dated fields. Stock sensitivity remains a separately validated empirical object.

### Delta B — constraints can pull orders forward and drive substitution

FANUC management states that longer delivery times can cause customers to order earlier. It also discusses customers adopting competitors' products because of delays while separately discussing component procurement constraints and FANUC's own available production capacity. These are management explanations, not independently measured causal magnitudes.

W2X-S03: https://www.fanuc.co.jp/en/ir/announce/pdf/2026/qasummary202606_e.pdf — 31 July 2026 Q&A, pages 1–2.

W2X-S04: https://www.fanuc.co.jp/en/ir/announce/pdf/2026/reference202606_e.pdf — 31 July 2026 first-quarter presentation, product sales/orders.

The rounded monetary figures in W2X-S04 yield approximate order-to-sales ratios of **1.571 for FA**, **1.019 for Robots**, and **1.220 for the group**. These are period monetary ratios; they are not lead times, unit order growth, capacity utilization, manufacturing yield or universal shortage severity.

The research model should therefore separate **underlying customer demand**, **order timing**, **qualified/deliverable output**, and **competitive retention**. A long lead time can raise near-term orders while worsening future competitive economics. Easing the constraint can reduce early ordering without meaning end demand collapsed. A better falsifier is: orders weaken after lead times normalize while customer activity and later deliveries remain stronger than the order decline implies.

### Delta C — cash labels can reverse the directional conclusion

Legrand's H1 presentation reports its pre-working-capital cash measure at **€980.6 million versus €862.8 million**, net operating cash at **€568.6 million versus €578.0 million**, and free cash flow at **€488.3 million versus €501.6 million**. The corresponding changes are approximately **+13.65%, −1.63%, and −2.65%**. Increased working-capital cash use was approximately **€127.2 million**, greater than the approximately **€117.8 million** improvement in pre-working-capital cash.

W2X-S05: https://www.legrand.com/sites/default/files/Documents_PDF_Legrand/Finance/2026/6m/Legrand_Slideshow_H12026_EN%20_1785247986.pdf — 29 July 2026, printed pages 11, 23 and 41; relied-upon tables/screens were visually inspected in the prior research turn.

The same presentation states that datacenters represented **32% of group revenue in H1 2026**. That is a period-specific revenue denominator, not segment profit, recurring revenue, a broader transition category or stock-price sensitivity.

The product requirement is formula-aware normalization: preserve the issuer-native cash label, formula, period and reconciliation. Higher inventory/receivables can support profitable growth, reflect timing, or indicate worsening conversion; favorable customer advances can fund work before obligations are fulfilled. Neither cash sign is self-interpreting.

### Delta D — one issuer can contain opposite business cycles

Yaskawa's March–May 2026 quarter shows Motion Control operating profit increasing by approximately **¥2.525 billion** while Robotics operating profit decreased by approximately **¥4.118 billion**. System Engineering increased roughly **¥0.893 billion**; the group operating-profit change was approximately **−¥2.017 billion**. The rounded business components sum to **−¥2.019 billion**, leaving a **¥2 million rounding residual**, consistent with the source's rounding convention. Preserve that residual rather than altering a source number to force exactness.

The same calculations yield approximately **21.52%** growth in Motion Control external revenue, **1.97%** in Robotics external revenue, and **38.91%** growth in group net operating cash. Management also attributes effects to ERP implementation and European restructuring; those explanations remain attributed.

W2X-S06: https://www.yaskawa-global.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/20260710_en.pdf — 10 July 2026, period 1 March–31 May 2026; cash and segment tables.

W2X-S07: https://www.yaskawa-global.com/company/profile/business — current product/business descriptions, observed 23 September 2026; capability descriptions are not quantified customer revenue or adoption.

The design implication is that business-level states must coexist inside one issuer. The March–May period also must not be silently aligned to April–June peers.

### Delta E — reverse valuation is non-unique

A deliberately hypothetical five-year FCFF experiment complements the canonical stable-state sensitivity. It uses no current company price or forecast. All scenarios begin with revenue of 100 and the same 8% annual revenue-growth path:

| Hypothetical case | Economic difference | Modeled EV | Terminal-value share |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| A — baseline | 20% operating margin, baseline capital needs, 9% required return | **254.55** | **77.28%** |
| B — more capital for same growth | Higher capex/WC; lower terminal return | **211.77** | **81.28%** |
| C — higher margin/capital return | Margin rises toward 25%; higher terminal return | **339.20** | **79.74%** |
| D — higher required return | Baseline operating assumptions; 11% required return | **189.51** | **71.09%** |

The same sales growth supports materially different values because profit capture, reinvestment and required return differ. Under case B's more capital-intensive assumptions, approximately **12.8919%** annual revenue growth solves to the same modeled EV as case A's 8% growth. Therefore a market price does not uniquely reveal `the growth rate the market expects` unless other assumptions have been constrained. Reverse valuation should present an **assumption set consistent with price**, not a unique recovered market belief.

The accompanying supplemental calculation JSON preserves the input/output values. These are pedagogical research fixtures, not price targets.

## 2. Additional scope/version findings

**Ownership changes need effective dates.** Eaton's June filing records Boyd Thermal as acquired on 12 March 2026 for **$9.55 billion net of cash** and reported within Electrical Global; the Mobility transaction remained forward/conditional in that filing. W2X-S08: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1551182/000155118226000030/etn-20260630.htm. Acquired exposure is not organic share gain, and an announced separation must not remove current ownership prematurely.

**Segment taxonomies are versioned.** Emerson's November 2025 filing introduces a five-segment FY2026 presentation and recast periods; its June 2026 10-Q carries that perimeter. W2X-S09: https://ir.emerson.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000032604-25-000094/emr-20251120.htm and W2X-S10: https://ir.emerson.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000032604-26-000043/emr-20260630.htm. Historical research should retain the applicable taxonomy and recast status rather than projecting today's names backward over unrecast observations.

**Workflow/capability evidence is not adoption evidence.** Emerson's Roche collaboration, Siemens SIMATIC catalog and ABB business-area descriptions establish named workflow/capability/scope. They do not establish customer ROI, paid recurring revenue, installed base, market share or scaled adoption. W2X-S11: https://www.emerson.com/en/corporate/news/2025/emerson-modality-library-innovation-life-sciences-collab-roche ; W2X-S12: https://www.siemens.com/en-us/products/simatic/ ; W2X-S13: https://new.abb.com/about/our-businesses .

**Consensus discovery is not consensus data.** Schneider's IR portal exposes consensus-document links, including metadata for an **8 April 2026 version 1.1 pre-Q1 artifact**, but the numerical PDF was not recovered in the prior research turn. W2X-S14: https://www.se.com/ww/en/about-us/investor-relations/share-information/analyst-coverage-and-consensus/ and W2X-S15: https://www.se.com/us/en/download/document/Pre-Q1_2026_release_consensus/ .
