## 1. Audit conclusion: three different jobs, three different proof burdens

The six earlier waves provide a substantial economic vocabulary and source-backed examples. They do not yet constitute a complete global sector dataset or a validated investment signal. The next stage must improve decision readiness, not inflate the number of research pages.

**Job A: understand the business.** A reader should identify what the business supplies, who pays, which activity creates demand, who bears contractual risks, and which capital is required. We have multiple grounded examples and explicit counterexamples. Narrow product/customer profit exposure, global specialists and several commercial-service categories remain incomplete.

**Job B: explain or forecast a financial change.** This requires aligned periods, business perimeters, operating drivers and an earnings-to-cash bridge. We have useful comparative observations and illustrative models. Calibrated customer cohorts, actual contract parameters, longitudinal cost/capital histories and genuinely out-of-sample forecasts are much thinner. An explanation constructed after results is not a successful ex-ante forecast.

**Job C: identify an expectations gap or stock mispricing.** This additionally requires a dated independent expectation, compatible actuals, security identity, market data, corporate actions and event confounders. Wave 7 advances this by recovering an actual pre-results AWP poll for SGS. One historical aggregate is not a complete revision panel. No price reaction, abnormal return, valuation advantage or executable strategy is established.

These distinctions should be visible in the eventual dossier. A useful explanation can be available while the expectations section is explicitly limited. Missing market evidence should not hide sound business research; equally, a strong narrative must not masquerade as a verified opportunity.

## 3. Testing and certification: initial activity and active programs are different

UL's filing distinguishes certification testing, ongoing certification, other testing/services and software. Its 2025 ongoing-certification revenue was $1,006 million, approximately 33.0% of total revenue. Initial certification testing was $851 million, approximately 27.9%. Those category denominators are disclosed; they are not customer attachment or retention rates. [W7-S01]

The proposed economic model has two connected populations: new customer products undergoing evaluation, and previously certified products still participating in paid programs. An initial evaluation can lead to later inspections, monitoring, audits or label-related activity, but the filing says frequency varies with product, production and compliance history. Some usage-based charges and distinct label obligations also matter. The correct representation is not an unchanging annuity attached to every historical certification.

For analysis, maintain source-defined active programs rather than infer them from certificates issued, brand customers or a count of test reports. A product can be retired, redesigned, moved between sites, superseded or subjected to different requirements. Similar names do not establish the same continuing economic unit. A repeat visit and a new certificate are not interchangeable units of demand.

An illustrative identifiability example begins with 1,000 programs, charging 100 per active program. World A retains 950 and admits 50 new programs. World B retains 700 and admits 300. Both end with 1,000 and revenue of 100,000, but the new-program requirement is six times greater in World B. Aggregate service revenue alone cannot recover retention. Neither world describes UL's actual cohorts.

The thesis question becomes whether new-product activity replenishes or expands profitable ongoing relationships at acceptable acquisition, testing, oversight and capital costs. Useful counterevidence includes more initial work without later paid participation, fee growth offset by exits, lab investment preceding weak utilization, or a change in standards creating alternative providers. Recurrence should be demonstrated through paid activity and costs, not inferred from a compliance label.

## 4. Accreditation creates eligibility, not guaranteed scarcity rent

Intertek's September accreditation announcement explicitly links expanded certification-body capacity to greater customer choice. The evidence establishes qualified market access and a wider service proposition, not paid demand, exclusive rights, a commercial win or attractive pricing. [W7-S08]

Our proposed map should connect the relevant product/application, method or scheme, eligible provider, customer acceptance and actual engagement. Accreditation scope, physical laboratory capability, available appointment capacity, quality history and commercial terms are separate observations. Scope can vary by site and method; a group-level logo does not prove every subsidiary is eligible for every task.

The moat hypothesis should ask why customers prefer the provider and whether that preference produces cash after supporting expertise, equipment and quality assurance. Possible mechanisms to investigate include trusted acceptance, reduced duplication, turnaround reliability, multi-location coverage and customer workflow integration. These are research hypotheses; the present source set does not quantify switching costs or monopoly power.

There is a trust constraint on the investment model itself. More billable throughput is not necessarily a desirable improvement if reliability deteriorates or customers stop accepting results. A future efficiency assessment should preserve error/rework, complaints and acceptance evidence alongside throughput when those data are available. An unavailable quality series must remain a gap, not an assumed constant.

## 5. Specialist inspection and expert consulting do not share one cycle

MISTRAS describes investment to address temporarily constrained aerospace-and-defense in-lab capacity. That is a management statement about its business, not a measured global shortage. Its end markets, on-site work and in-lab work can have different demand timing and resource requirements. [W7-S09]

An inspection program may be linked to customer shutdowns, asset use, construction milestones or a particular technical requirement. Expert consulting can instead depend on innovation programs, failure investigations and disputes. The common feature is specialized knowledge; that does not imply the same contract duration or revenue recurrence.

Exponent's Q2 total revenue increased approximately 20.9%, but revenue before reimbursements increased approximately 12.0%. EBITDA divided by net revenue is approximately 28.70%; divided by gross revenue it is approximately 24.90%. The denominator difference is substantive, not a rounding error. [W7-S11; derived]

The proposed research therefore uses staff capacity, realized billing, project mix, reimbursed expenses, quality and collection rather than applying a software-style subscription model. Aggregate utilization cannot establish each additional project's margin, nor does strong demand prove that scarce expert capacity scales without further hiring and support. Cross-company profitability comparisons need the same denominator and treatment of compensation.

This is also a useful cross-sector connection. A technical-service provider can have customers in healthcare, energy and consumer electronics while its own economics remain professional services. The customer's sector is an exposure dimension, not automatic reclassification of all supplier revenue or a claim to the customer's growth rate.

## 6. Better operations can coexist with accounting and cash distractions

UL's Q2 2026 net-income increase includes a material software-business disposal gain. Its H1 cash bridge is more informative about the investment burden: operating cash increased by $78 million, capital expenditure by $45 million and free cash flow by $33 million. This does not isolate sustainable cash, but it prevents operating improvement from being treated as costless. [W7-S02; derived]

SGS reports CHF260 million free cash flow and 25% growth on a basis excluding CHF80 million of prior-year headquarters proceeds. Treating that reported percentage as exact implies a prior ex-disposal comparator of 208 and, conditionally, 288 including the disposal. That reconstructed all-in comparison would be approximately -9.7%, not +25%. It is explicitly conditional because the image containing the complete table was not retrieved and rounding or other basis changes could matter. The authoritative observation here is the stated exclusion, not a newly verified whole-statement reconstruction. [W7-S03]

Bureau Veritas separately reports positive adjusted operating-profit growth and negative reported operating-profit growth, and different reported/organic cash changes. Those facts do not choose a normalized number for us; they identify the reconciliation that must be completed. Planned exits also change how forward growth should be interpreted. [W7-S06]

These examples support a common rule: operating performance, portfolio transactions, cash realization and capital allocation require distinct explanations. The system should not mechanically subtract every one-off or trust every adjusted label. Preserve the company's bridge, determine which costs and obligations remain, and make the proposed normalization inspectable.

## 8. A separate management-guidance case: Exponent

On April 30, Exponent guided Q2 EBITDA to 27.0%-27.8% of revenue before reimbursements. Its July 30 release reported a rounded 28.7%, approximately 90 basis points above the prior upper bound. That is a same-basis comparison with a dated management range. It is not an analyst-consensus beat. [W7-S10, W7-S11]

The revenue guide was expressed as high-single-digit growth. We preserve the phrase rather than inventing a point estimate or numerical range. Actual net revenue growth can be reported alongside it, but a precisely quantified revenue surprise is unavailable from that wording alone.

This pair is a development example, not a held-out success. The present research selected it after observing the data. A complete intervening-disclosure census was not performed, so the April range is identified as that dated reference, not conclusively the last forecast available before July results. These restrictions are important because the Wave 6 Pentair case already showed how a preliminary disclosure can change the relevant reference point.

The two examples answer different questions and must retain distinct statement roles in the shared template. A poll aggregate, management guidance, a house scenario and a price-implied scenario are not substitutes for each other. Combining them under one unlabeled expected-value field would undo the domain research.

## 9. An acquisition event can invalidate a clean rerating example

Intertek's July 31 results describe a recommended prospective EQT acquisition and an offer value including a dividend already paid. An observed price move over that period could reflect transaction terms, completion probability or timing, as well as ordinary business information. The release does not establish completed ownership. [W7-S07]

This is a reason to flag the event, not delete the company from the research because it complicates the narrative. For an operating-rerating study, either identify a suitably uncontaminated event window or preserve the deal as a separate explanation. For a total-return calculation, a dividend included in quoted consideration must not also be counted a second time.

More generally, corporate actions, divestitures, buybacks, capital raises and index changes can coexist with earnings news. A residual multiple change is not automatically sentiment, quality or proof that our industrial mechanism was discovered by the market. The evaluation should test alternative explanations rather than celebrate a price chart that happens to fit the story.

## 11. Validation design: test useful questions before investment advantage

This audit selects three separate evaluation targets. First, test whether a dossier correctly explains disclosed economics without invalid joins or invented exposure. Second, test whether a frozen operational hypothesis predicts a subsequent business measurement better than a declared baseline. Only then test incremental information about prices or returns with compatible expectations and market inputs.

The SGS and Exponent examples are development cases. They are not independent validation samples, and one favorable metric must not become a measured hit rate. Before examining new outcomes, freeze an eligible event set and horizon, record all eligible events including failures and missing inputs, and specify which exclusions are mandatory for measurement compatibility rather than inconvenient results.

For operational forecasts, useful baselines might include the latest same-basis management forecast, an admitted external estimate, a simple matched-season prior, or unchanged margins. Their applicability differs by business and available information. Measure absolute error on a defined scale, direction where meaningful, coverage/refusal frequency, and uncertainty calibration only when numerical probabilities are actually supplied. Percentage errors with near-zero or negative denominators require different handling.

For return research, add market/industry factors, overlapping announcements, corporate actions, reporting-time alignment and a predeclared return horizon. An event reaction, several-month estimate revision and multi-year cash-return change are different targets. Do not choose the horizon after seeing which makes the thesis look best. A trade implementation would need its own accepted evaluation and cost/risk rules; nothing here grants it.

Data-quality failures should be visible outputs. Distinguish missing actuals, unsupported identity, unknown publication timing, unavailable history, rights restriction and an adverse investment result. Counting missing rows as successes or quietly dropping failed companies would manufacture quality. Existing owners should preserve these distinctions; this research does not create another evaluation authority.
