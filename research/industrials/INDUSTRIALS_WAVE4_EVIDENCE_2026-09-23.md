## W4-S01 - Rolls-Royce TotalCare contract proposition
Source: https://www.rolls-royce.com/products-and-services/civil-aerospace/services/totalcare.aspx
Period: undated service description observed 23 September 2026. Locator: risk transfer and service coverage.

TotalCare describes transferring time-on-wing and shop-visit cost risk to Rolls-Royce. This establishes a contractual business-model distinction, not a universal rate, coverage term or margin for every engine. More maintenance activity can increase the obligated provider's costs. The offering page does not establish a particular customer's signed terms, paid adoption or realized returns.

## W4-S02 - Rolls-Royce H1 2026
Source: https://www.rolls-royce.com/investors/results-reports-and-presentations/financial-results/rr-holdings-plc-2026-half-year-results.aspx
Published 30 July 2026; six months ended June. Locators: Civil Aerospace, guidance and cash reconciliation.

Civil Aerospace underlying profit was GBP1,567 million. Net contractual/operational improvements were GBP497 million: GBP372 million of contract catch-ups and GBP125 million of onerous provision releases. Gross benefits of GBP574 million were offset by GBP77 million of charges. Separately, LTSA revenue catch-ups were GBP356 million. Large-engine flying hours rose 4%. Management expected a lower second-half contribution from contractual improvements. The different catch-up measures cannot be merged, mechanically annualized or treated as immediate cash receipts.

## W4-S03 - Rolls-Royce H1 2020 adverse case
Source: https://www.rolls-royce.com/media/press-releases/2020/27-08-2020-rr-holdings-plc-2020-half-year-results.aspx
Published 27 August 2020; historical first-half outcome, not current conditions.

Large-engine flying hours fell 47%; group free cash outflow was approximately GBP2.8 billion. The company identified GBP1.1 billion of adverse impact from ceasing invoice discounting alongside reduced activity and other working-capital effects. This is evidence that installed-base services can suffer a usage/liquidity shock, not an estimate that all the cash decline came from hours or a transferable present-day cash sensitivity.

## W4-S04 - GE Aerospace Q2 2026 results
Source: https://www.geaerospace.com/news/press-releases/ge-aerospace-announces-second-quarter-2026-results
Published 16 July 2026; quarter ended June. Locator: Commercial Engines & Services.

CES revenue grew 27%, profit grew 20%, and margin fell 160 basis points. Management attributed pressure to installation-engine growth, investment and inflation. Announced durability benefits and later production cutovers remain forward statements, not measured improvement across the active fleet. Higher delivery volume and a growing service opportunity do not establish the same immediate margin outcome.

## W4-S05 - GE Aerospace June 2026 Form 10-Q
Source: https://www.geaerospace.com/sites/default/files/geaerospace_webcast_10q_07162026.pdf
Filed/released with July 16 results. Locators: printed pages 5 and 22, PDF indices 4 and 21; screenshots inspected.

Equipment and service remaining performance obligations were USD32,085 million and USD178,705 million. Note 8 separately reports LTSA revenue recognition of USD5,468 million and billings of USD5,208 million, plus net unfavorable estimate changes of USD42 million that include a USD118 million reversal of earlier tariff charges. RPO is not profit or current cash, and the nested estimate components must not be adjusted twice. Certain engineering costs are capitalized and amortized by unit; investment does not disappear because recognition is deferred.

## W4-S06 - Safran H1 2026
Source: https://www.safran-group.com/pressroom/safran-reports-its-first-half-2026-results-2026-07-28
Published 28 July 2026; six months ended June. Locators: civil aftermarket, propulsion and adjusted/consolidated reconciliation.

Civil aftermarket services rose 40.4% in USD, led by LEAP rate-per-flight-hour contracts, while spare-parts sales rose 27.9%. These are different revenue models. LEAP deliveries were 1,030. Adjusted revenue/recurring operating income were EUR17,571/3,237 million, versus consolidated EUR17,245/2,636 million. The release describes adverse mix from installation deliveries and service-contract activity within propulsion. Do not treat all service growth as spare-parts economics or mix adjusted and consolidated denominators.

## W4-S07 - CFM joint-venture identity
Source: https://www.cfmaeroengines.com/about
Undated corporate description observed 23 September 2026. Locator: company ownership.

CFM is a 50/50 joint company of GE Aerospace and Safran Aircraft Engines. A physical CFM production statistic reported by both parents is not two independent sets of engines. The ownership percentage is not either parent's total-company revenue exposure. Issuer financial participation and unique physical-event counting require separate treatment.

## W4-S09 - RTX June 2026 Form 10-Q
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/101829/000010182926000027/rtx-20260630.htm
Period ended 30 June 2026. Locator: Note 16 powder-metal matter and operating-cash discussion.

The historical USD2.9 billion charge is net of Pratt's 51% program share. Remaining accrued customer compensation fell to USD0.4 billion from USD0.7 billion, primarily through credits issued. ## W4-S11 - Airbus H1 2026

## W4-S18 - Lufthansa Technik H1 2026

## W4-S19 - IATA July 2026 passenger demand
Source: https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/08-31-air-passenger-demand-grows-july/
Published 31 August 2026; July observations. Locator: RPK, ASK and methodology.

Passenger kilometers grew 0.2% year over year and available seat kilometers 0.3%. Load factor was 85.2%. These measures combine distance, capacity and passenger use; they are not engine flying hours or cycles. Populations, estimates and revisions must be retained before an engine-maintenance inference.

## W4-S21 - Ryanair/CFM material and maintenance arrangement
Source: https://corporate.ryanair.com/news/ryanair-and-cfm-agree-multi-year-multi-billion-dollar-engine-material-services-agreement/
Published 10 February 2026. Locator: memorandum and planned maintenance shops.

The release describes a memorandum for spare-engine material support and Ryanair's planned internal maintenance facilities from 2029. The customer intends to obtain relevant spare material from CFM. Internalizing maintenance labor therefore need not eliminate OEM parts revenue. Preserve the memorandum and future-facility status; neither is proof of operating capacity or realized savings today.

## W4-S22 - FAA Parts Manufacturer Approval
Source: https://www.faa.gov/aircraft/air_cert/design_approvals/pma
Official regulator description observed 23 September 2026. Locator: approval meaning and DRS lookup.

PMA is a combined design and production approval for modification or replacement articles. Approved eligibility is part/product specific. Approval does not establish realized sales, universal interchangeability, airline or lessor commercial acceptance, or attractive manufacturer economics. This is regulatory context, not engineering guidance or an assessment of any particular part's airworthiness.

## W4-S26 - AerSale Q2 2026
Source: https://ir.aersale.com/news-releases/news-release-details/aersale-reports-second-quarter-2026-results
Published 6 August 2026; quarter ended June. Locator: equipment sales, leasing, USM and MRO.

Revenue fell to USD70.9 million from USD107.4 million. Management primarily attributes the decline to no flight-equipment sales and lower used-serviceable-material volume, partly offset by leasing and MRO growth. Whole-asset sale timing and recurring service or lease activity require different models. A customer's choice to operate, lease, sell or disassemble an asset changes material availability and cannot be counted as several independent supplies of the same asset.

## Research implications and remaining limits

The evidence supports distinguishing contract risk, physical usage, maintenance completion, accounting changes, cash and ownership. It does not establish current stock mispricing or a complete global aerospace census. Flight-hour cohorts, contract terms, shop-level turnaround/works scopes, realized part substitutions, lifecycle capital and point-in-time consensus remain incomplete. Headline corporate statistics cannot fill those gaps. The next artifact will develop explicit analytical models, hypothetical counterexamples and product acceptance requirements; Fable handoff remains withheld.
