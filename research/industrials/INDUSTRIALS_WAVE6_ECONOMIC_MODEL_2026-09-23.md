## 1. What this wave changes

The proposed Industrials system must distinguish a recurring customer need from recurring revenue, and recurring revenue from durable distributable cash. A relationship with the building owner, a maintained equipment unit, a repeated consumables purchase, an outsourced facility function and an availability contract are different economic exposures. A source mentioning service cannot justify assigning all of them one valuation model.

The principal question is now: which scarce capability or service does the customer need, who authorizes and funds it, how does the provider get paid, what must the provider keep spending, and how reliably does the resulting cash recur? This perspective extends the earlier project-risk and installed-base models without duplicating them. It adds retention, local density, verification of customer savings and the possibility that an efficiency benefit reduces the supplier's own volume revenue.

The sampled evidence contains both favorable and adverse cases. It is deliberately not a collection of businesses chosen because their latest margins improved. The intended product should help an investor distinguish a strong demand environment from an attractive business model and both from an attractively priced security. The final distinction still requires dated prices and expectations; it cannot be inferred from the operational disclosures alone.

## 2. Start with the customer's decision, not the supplier's label

For a building, separate the payer, asset owner, occupier, facilities manager, installer, equipment manufacturer and maintenance provider. These may be the same entity or different entities. The research must establish the actual relationship before assuming that the party able to approve investment also receives the savings. A technically superior installation can remain economically unattractive to the decision-maker if its benefit arrives elsewhere or after the relevant planning horizon.

Different jobs produce different spending decisions. A failure requiring immediate restoration is not the same as a discretionary modernization. A replacement needed for capacity differs from one justified by energy savings. A new construction project depends on financing, tenant demand, project readiness and construction execution; an existing building's maintenance decision can have a different set of dependencies. These are proposed research dimensions, not a claim that any category is recession-proof.

Research should record the alternatives the customer actually has: maintain the old system, repair it, replace a subsystem, upgrade controls, replace the entire installation, outsource the operation, or defer discretionary work. Identify the cost of downtime, the useful life of the alternative, the required installation interruption, financing and residual obligations. A simple equipment-price comparison is inadequate when installation, commissioning and ongoing service determine most of the customer's economics.

For environmental infrastructure, identify whether the customer pays for throughput, a collected stop, a tested sample, a treatment outcome, an available facility or a combination. Physical consumption can fall while the customer continues valuing reliability. Conversely, a supposedly essential system can still lose profitable volume or face an expensive change in operating conditions. The model must say which channel is under investigation.

## 5. Modular construction: productivity must cover the factory

Comfort Systems' filing establishes both a large customer-funding contribution and material buildings investment for modular growth. [W6-S01] The inference to test is not simply that modular work saves labor. It is whether those savings exceed facility capital, fixed operating support, inventory, logistics and the consequences of an underfilled factory.

An original sensitivity assumes investment of 10 million, eight years of use, no terminal value, an annual saving of 5 million at full utilization and 2 million of annual fixed support. The required return is 9%. Savings are defined after variable execution costs; tax, financing and further expansion are excluded.

| Hypothetical utilization | Annual net savings, million | Investment NPV, million |
|---|---:|---:|
| 100% | 3.00 | 6.60 |
| 60% | 1.00 | -4.47 |

The break-even utilization is approximately 76.13% under these assumptions. This is not a measured utilization requirement for Comfort Systems or a universal modular-factory hurdle. It demonstrates how the same process improvement can create or destroy value depending on capital utilization.

The practical research should follow booked work into manufacturable designs, procurement, labor hours, transport, on-site integration and acceptance. Ask whether customer designs are repeatable enough to use the facility efficiently; whether savings are retained in contract price; whether a delayed site strands completed modules; and whether billings fund inventories before cash is collected. Each question needs its own evidence rather than an automatic productivity score.

Compare a strong volume period with a less favorable one. A margin floor is credible only if the economics survive plausible underutilization and changed project mix. Customer advances help financing, but must remain connected to remaining performance obligations. A change in contract liabilities on the balance sheet need not equal the cash-flow-statement movement when other reconciling items exist; unexplained differences remain unallocated.

## 6. Recurrence has several forms, and costs recur too

Otis's Service growth coexists with margin compression; its reported increase in segment profit divided by its increase in sales is approximately 8.05%, not its 23.2% average segment margin. [W6-S04; derived] Neither number establishes the contribution of a newly retained maintenance account, because the segment includes mix and other effects. This is a warning against substituting consolidated growth for cohort economics.

Veralto reports a different form of recurrence: repeated goods and leases, with service/software described as immaterial. [W6-S24] Our proposed demand model therefore distinguishes scheduled subscriptions, frequently consumed products, replacement parts, maintenance commitments and recurring-but-discretionary repairs. Their relationships to usage, failure, price, attachment and cancellation are different.

A proposed installed-base reconciliation begins with supported units under contract, subtracts losses and retirements, and adds converted installations, third-party wins and acquisitions. Do not use the total installed population as the maintained population, or ending maintained units as a time-weighted revenue denominator. Currency, mix and scope changes must remain separate from price per retained unit.

A ten-year hypothetical account model pays contribution of 30 at year-end initially, with subsequent cash surviving at an annual renewal probability. There is no terminal value. Acquisition cost and central overhead are excluded and would have to be deducted to derive economic value.

| Hypothetical annual contribution | Renewal probability | Present value at 9% |
|---|---:|---:|
| 30 | 95% | 160.09 |
| 30 | 90% | 134.64 |
| 35 | 90% | 157.08 |

Even a contribution increase from 30 to 35 does not fully offset the assumed retention deterioration. This is a sensitivity, not a fitted retention estimate or proof that price caused churn. The model treats the first payment as certain and independent renewal probabilities thereafter; it excludes partial losses, renegotiation, cohort heterogeneity and competitive responses.

For the real research, measure the full obligation. More covered breakdowns can raise service labor and material costs; fewer callbacks may reduce revenue on event-paid work while improving a fixed-fee contract. Evaluate travel, dispatch, standby capacity, spares, warranty work and customer credits where relevant. A remote-monitoring offering is only evidence of a capability until paid use, lower costs or retained revenue are established.

## 7. Owner-direct relationships are not recurring revenue percentages

Limbach's Owner Direct Relationships label describes the commercial relationship, not a guarantee of subscription-like economics. Its disclosed sales growth was overwhelmingly acquired, and its updated revenue and adjusted-EBITDA guidance moved in opposite directions. Its guided free-cash formula excludes specified investment and working-capital changes. [W6-S19]

The broader design lesson is to keep three independent attributes: who the customer is, how revenue is earned, and how much capital and risk are needed to earn it. A direct relationship can create valuable knowledge, cross-selling or lower acquisition costs, but those remain hypotheses until measured. A direct customer can still defer a project, negotiate aggressively, or create a difficult fulfillment obligation.

This also changes how to evaluate recurring demand through order books. Comfort excludes qualifying short service commitments from remaining obligations. [W6-S01-S02] A smaller reported RPO is therefore not sufficient to infer less service demand. Conversely, a broad bookings series may not remove later cancellations. Source-native definitions must survive into the consumer's explanation.

The proposed economics are customer-level: paid recurring contribution plus separately priced project opportunities, less selling/support costs and required capital. Do not apply a recurring multiple to the customer's entire lifetime relationship or assume every repeat project is contractually secured. Compare the business before and after acquisitions on matched scope before attributing a new margin or recurrence profile to organic strategic progress.

## 8. Retrofit demand needs verified savings and a willing payer

DOE's methodology emphasizes baseline conditions and adjustment planning. [W6-S20] Our proposed research translates that into three separate observations: engineering consumption, counterfactual savings and the financial benefit accruing to the decision-maker.

In a hypothetical example, old energy use is 1,200 units, use after installation is 1,000 and the correctly adjusted no-project baseline is 1,050. The raw reduction is 200, but the modeled attributable saving is only 50. If the tariff rises from 0.15 to 0.25, the bill rises from 180 to 250 even though the current counterfactual bill is 262.5. Avoided cost is 12.5. Higher bills do not by themselves prove failed efficiency, and a large raw consumption decline does not prove a large equipment effect.

The baseline in this example is stipulated, not statistically estimated. Actual M&V requires an accepted method, suitable metering, occupancy/workload/weather adjustments, data quality and treatment of changed equipment use. An unavailable baseline cannot be manufactured from a supplier's headline saving claim. Keep projected, measured and independently verified results separate.

A second, independent investment sensitivity assumes installation cost of 2 million, annual gross savings of 0.4 million and incremental maintenance of 0.05 million. The required return is 8%, and there is no terminal value.

| Hypothetical decision constraint | NPV, million |
|---|---:|
| Ten years, all net benefit captured | 0.349 |
| Only five years of benefit | -0.603 |
| Ten years, payer captures 30% of net benefit | -1.295 |
| Ten years, initial cost overruns by 25% | -0.151 |

These cases explain why a positive engineering proposition may not become an immediate order. Horizon, implementation cost and allocation of benefits can matter more than the advertised energy saving. The 30% case deliberately holds the initial cost with the payer while allocating net benefit; it does not claim that actual leases use that arrangement.

Investigate funding, installation disruption, acceptance, savings sharing and performance guarantees. Determine who pays for a shortfall and which circumstances permit baseline adjustment. A financed offer can remove the initial cash hurdle while introducing interest, counterparty risk or new obligations. The research must model those economics, not assume financing turns an unprofitable investment into a profitable one.

## 9. Route density has a capacity boundary

Recurring collection or field visits are not pure recurring gross profit. A proposed route model separates the number of economically compatible stops, their prices, time and disposal/consumables cost, travel, and fixed route resources. Customer count alone cannot represent density without geography and workload.

An intentionally simplified daily model charges 12 per stop, incurs variable cost of 6 and fixed cost of 1,200 per active route. A route can serve 300 stops. Shared overhead, taxes, fleet capital, geography and overtime alternatives are omitted.

| Stops | Routes required by the toy rule | Contribution |
|---|---:|---:|
| 250 | 1 | 300 |
| 275 | 1 | 450 |
| 300 | 1 | 600 |
| 301 | 2 | -594 |
| 500 | 2 | 600 |

This is not an optimized routing algorithm or a recommended operating response to one extra customer. A real provider can redesign routes, outsource, use overtime, alter service frequency or reject work. The discontinuity demonstrates why spare capacity and network configuration matter. Smooth revenue growth cannot automatically be assigned a smooth incremental margin.

Now raise the price by 5% but lose 10% of the original 250 stops. Revenue becomes 2,835 rather than 3,000 and contribution 285 rather than 300. Price improved, but the modeled total economics deteriorated. This is not a measured price elasticity. It defines a discriminating hypothesis: does realized pricing cover inflation without reducing density or service economics excessively?

Republic's price, yield and volume percentages have different disclosed total-versus-related-business denominators. [W6-S11] Do not combine them indiscriminately or invert rounded percentages into an exact exposure estimate. Capture renewal timing, discounts, surcharges and lost business alongside headline price.

## 10. Water conservation can reduce a supplier's revenue

A water-service proposition may be valuable because it improves reliability, quality or resource efficiency. Those customer benefits need not align with a provider paid solely for throughput. The proposed research must establish whether compensation is volumetric, availability-based, performance-based or mixed; the announced name of a partnership does not supply those terms.

Consider a hypothetical operation with baseline volume of one million cubic meters, variable cost of 1.2 per cubic meter, fixed support of 0.9 million and maintenance of 0.3 million. A volumetric price of 3 produces 0.6 million of cash contribution. An alternative fee of 1 million plus 2 per cubic meter produces the same baseline revenue and contribution.

| Hypothetical payment arrangement | Baseline contribution, million | Contribution at 30% lower volume |
|---|---:|---:|
| Volume only | 0.60 | 0.06 |
| Availability plus volume | 0.60 | 0.36 |

The alternative allocation reduces but does not eliminate exposure. Initial investment, taxes, financing, performance penalties and nonpayment are excluded. No actual Xylem, Veolia or Ameresco contract is assumed to use these terms.

Xylem's Dow project has an intended future operating date; its exact commercial economics were not disclosed in the reviewed announcement. [W6-S14] Treat it as evidence of a customer/workflow and a future execution obligation, not a known stream of present service revenue. Date and stage are useful intelligence, not administrative metadata.

Water also requires separation between equipment economics, repeat consumables, project delivery and owned operations. Xylem's broad product and service classifications yield derived gross margins of approximately 44.03% and 26.89%. [W6-S13] That comparison does not rank their investment quality, but it invalidates a universal assumption that a service label implies higher margin. Veolia's different growth figures with and without projects further demonstrate the importance of scope. [W6-S26]

## 11. Essential services still consume capital and face commodity effects

In waste research, distinguish collection fees, disposal/treatment, recovered-material sales and episodic environmental work. The system should ask which part of profit depends on contracted service and which depends on a commodity spread. The source evidence for Clean Harbors explicitly separates these operating channels and describes a new contract with a multi-year ramp. [W6-S25] A long contract should not be divided evenly across its stated duration without evidence of the volume and pricing schedule.

A disposal asset also needs a lifecycle analysis: initial capital, maintenance, capacity consumed, site development, environmental obligations and the eventual cost of closure or restoration. The research should not assume these outflows disappear because they are deferred or classified differently. It should also avoid deducting an obligation twice when the forecast cash flows already include it.

WM's cash reconciliation provides a concrete comparison. The half-year increase in FCF is 731 million: 474 from higher operating cash, 131 from lower business capex, 152 from lower sustainability-growth capex, and minus 26 from lower proceeds. [W6-S12; derived] This is a bridge, not a determination of the sustainable growth rate. Each spending change requires investigation of what was completed, deferred or no longer required.

Reclassifying an expense can alter an EBITDA presentation without the same change in underlying economic cost. Current comparisons must use the recast series rather than attach a valuation multiple to a discontinuity. Likewise, a company's FCF variant excluding selected growth investment should remain labeled; it is not automatically cash distributable while the growth forecast still relies on that investment.

ABM's receivables agreement is another boundary: disclosed facility capacity does not prove use. [W6-S18] Do not mechanically subtract the full facility size from CFO. Read actual draws, accounting treatment and timing before quantifying the effect. A financing choice can genuinely reduce costs without proving a corresponding improvement in customer operating economics.

## 12. Expectations must be reconstructed at the decision date

Pentair provides a practical chronology. April's Q2 adjusted EPS midpoint was 1.485; the July 14 preliminary value was approximately 1.12; the July 28 reported result was 1.14. The last is 23.23% below the April midpoint but 1.79% above the preliminary number. These are comparisons to management statements, not consensus surprises. [W6-S15-S17; derived]

A July 28 event study that uses April guidance as the latest available expectation would misstate the information change. This is true even if the arithmetic is flawless and all three figures came from authoritative releases. Preserve the status of each figure, the publication date and the horizon it forecasts. Day-level dates alone do not determine whether the market could use the information before an intraday price observation.

Guidance comparisons also need dimensions other than EPS. Limbach's higher sales and lower profit ranges illustrate a change in implied economics, while ABM's higher EPS midpoint and lower segment-margin range show that a per-share upgrade is not necessarily a margin upgrade. [W6-S18-S19] Do not collapse tax, financing, share count, acquisition perimeter and operating profit into a single optimism score.

Portfolio changes reinforce the temporal requirement. Carrier's NORESCO sale and Xylem's partial Sensus sale have specific closing and geographic scopes. [W6-S08, W6-S21] Historical participation remains valid for the prior period; current exposure must reflect the evidenced change. A new company name or an old catalog cannot settle current issuer ownership by itself.

## 13. Rerating hypotheses and falsifiers

These twelve proposals identify what future research must demonstrate. They are not accepted forecasts, calibrated scores, security rankings or trading triggers.

| Hypothesis | Positive evidence to seek | Strong falsifier |
|---|---|---|
| H6-01: Modular execution creates a higher profit floor | Utilization-adjusted savings and returns after facility capital | Good margins vanish when the factory is less full |
| H6-02: Service attachment lengthens earnings duration | Retained paid cohorts with stable fulfillment economics | Renewals or contribution fall despite growing installed units |
| H6-03: Modernization supports valuable repeat work | Customer-approved upgrade economics and repeated contribution | Deferred projects or low-return competitive bids dominate |
| H6-04: Price creates value rather than destroying density | Realized price covers cost without excessive churn | Customer losses erase the contribution increase |
| H6-05: Retrofit demand has a fundable payback | Verified counterfactual savings accrue to the payer | Short horizon, disruption or split benefits prevent adoption |
| H6-06: Water-service contracts align incentives | Pricing preserves required returns while improving outcomes | Conservation or changed quality creates an uneconomic obligation |
| H6-07: Repeat consumables support durable growth | Active installed use and retained share of repeat purchases | Equipment exists but use or attachment declines |
| H6-08: Local networks gain operating leverage | More compatible work per existing resource | Added coverage requires disproportionate capacity |
| H6-09: Project mix improves risk-adjusted earnings | Better contracts, fewer adverse revisions and cash acceptance | Changes mostly reflect selective exclusions or gains |
| H6-10: Essential assets earn attractive lifecycle returns | Cash covers maintenance, growth and residual obligations | Excluded capex or delayed liabilities explains apparent quality |
| H6-11: Portfolio changes improve shareholder economics | Completed scope change and return on total invested capital | Acquisition-led growth dilutes contribution or requires too much capital |
| H6-12: Expectations lag an economic change | Time-valid forecasts and prices corroborate the mismatch | The market already incorporated the information |

A thesis can be rejected without implying that the whole company is poor. Preserve failed cases and the evidence that invalidated them. A structural rerating requires more than one favorable quarter, and a change in fair economic value does not automatically imply a change in the prevailing price-to-earnings multiple.

## 14. Data feasibility and product integration

The first usable workflow should answer a narrow investor question from existing pages: select a research slice, understand its payment/economic model, inspect the relevant business and change, follow the evidence, test a scenario, and return to the existing company workflow. The main screen should contain synthesis and an explicit falsifier, not force the user to reconstruct the thesis from dozens of citations.

Use independent modules for contract execution, installed-base service, repeat consumables, retrofit verification, local-network contribution and asset lifecycle. Shared navigation and evidence drawers do not require an identical economic score. A producer must return missing inputs honestly; the browser must not fabricate retention, service purity, unobserved contract terms or an implied valuation.

Construction spending needs a defined boundary. Census value put in place is not new awards, and its series are revised. [W6-S22-S23] Do not sum downstream project value and embedded supplier revenues into an inflated market, or turn nominal growth into physical volume without the necessary deflator. Different geographic and institutional samples cannot be relabeled the same market.

Existing native GMI evidence, Data OS identity, K1 clock/reference bindings, relationship and forecast owners retain authority. This research adds no canonical product, customer, contract, score, store, queue or publication owner. Shared-template custody and accepted interfaces must be refreshed before implementation. Public editorial research is not permission to publish the private current full-fidelity intelligence payload or redistribute raw source documents.

## 16. Boundary and next research

This wave supplies a differentiated economic model across 17 source-native groups, not exhaustive sector research. Specific service-cohort data, detailed contract obligations, route geography, maintenance capital, asset liabilities and source rights remain unresolved. The indexed-only Pentair preliminary release still needs native retention. All previous-wave caveats remain obligations for the consolidated audit.

The next highest-leverage step is a cross-sector coverage and evidence-feasibility audit, followed by a matched longitudinal expectations/valuation study. Build an explicit map of which industrial research jobs now have usable evidence and which still depend on broad labels. Prioritize missing specialist and testing/inspection coverage, customer/profit exposure, and point-in-time estimates by the decision they unlock. Test improving and deteriorating cases under comparable definitions rather than acquiring another broad collection of positive headlines.

Only after those gaps are resolved or explicitly bounded should the final native-owner integration specification and Fable implementation handoff be assembled. The template may continue independently, but this research must not duplicate its architecture or silently assume its proposed interfaces are already live. No implementation, deployment, empirical return claim or final handoff is made at this boundary.
