## 1. The decision this research changes

Aerospace should not be represented as one delivery cycle followed by uniformly profitable aftermarket growth. The useful analytical unit is a particular business, engine or aircraft cohort, and commercial arrangement. The investor must know who receives revenue, who owns the equipment, who holds the maintenance obligation, who performs the work, and who pays when durability or costs disappoint.

The most important distinction is between earning more for performing additional work and having to perform additional work under an already-priced service obligation. Rolls-Royce's TotalCare proposition explicitly transfers maintenance-cost and time-on-wing risk. The same deterioration in an engine's reliability can therefore affect the contract owner, independent repair provider, spare-parts supplier, lessor and airline differently. The contractual role cannot be inferred from a label such as aftermarket. [W4-S01, W4-S09]

A second decision is to make economic duration conditional. A large installed fleet may support years of activity, but its value depends on active use, retirements, contract attachment, the mix of repair work, competition, customer solvency and the capital needed to serve it. A recurring billing mechanism is not a guarantee of recurring profit or cash.

Third, headline growth and accounting margins need more than a comparison of percentages. Contract estimates, pass-through revenue, acquisitions, intercompany eliminations, noncontrolling interests and definition changes can materially alter what those percentages represent. These are not reasons to reject company reporting; they are reasons to understand the reporting before constructing a valuation thesis. [W4-S02, W4-S15, W4-S25]

The proposed user outcome is an aerospace dossier that explains the source of economic improvement, the party capturing it, the investment required, and the conditions under which the thesis fails. A supply-chain graphic is helpful only when it makes those relationships clearer.

## 2. Seven commercial roles to retain

A single company can occupy several of these roles. They are source-scoped research descriptions, not new globally authoritative entities.

| Role | Economic question | Evidence needed before valuation |
|---|---|---|
| Aircraft operator | Can the aircraft produce enough value after fuel, maintenance, financing and downtime? | Active fleet, schedules, unit costs, maintenance obligations and liquidity |
| Engine or aircraft original-equipment manufacturer | Does the program create attractive lifetime cash after development, production and support? | Cohort deliveries, learning/ramp costs, guarantees, capital and future service economics |
| Hourly-service contract owner | Do fees cover maintenance and other obligations over the contract life? | Actual terms, covered usage, costs, escalation, exclusions, credits and risk sharing |
| Maintenance performer | Does completed work earn attractive contribution after labor, material, facilities and licensing? | Workscope, admissions, completions, turnaround, pricing, costs and rights |
| Parts intellectual-property or approval holder | Can an eligible product retain price and content against alternatives? | Product eligibility, customer acceptance, price, share, substitution and required investment |
| Distributor or used-material provider | Does availability create contribution after purchase, repair, inventory and credit? | Provenance, condition, inventory age, realized sales and working capital |
| Asset owner or lessor | Do lease cash flows and disposal proceeds compensate for purchase, funding and residual risk? | Contract rent, maintenance settlements, debt, customer credit, downtime and exit value |

The payer and the physical performer need not be the same legal entity. Risk-sharing partners may participate in revenue and costs without appearing as the sole seller. A maintenance provider can buy parts from the manufacturer against whom it competes for labor services. An airline can internalize maintenance while retaining an OEM material agreement. [W4-S07, W4-S21]

This suggests a specific research representation: each relationship should preserve the paying party, obligation holder, performing party, product/program, contract basis, effective period and source. Where the source does not disclose a role or term, the relationship remains incomplete. Do not invent a complete commercial graph from an announcement.

## 4. Fleet activity is not one variable

The proposed fleet model begins with distinct populations: delivered, owned, managed, installed, active, parked, in maintenance, spare and retired. An engine can move between aircraft and maintenance without becoming a newly produced engine. An order for a future aircraft is not part of the operating fleet. A lessor's total portfolio can combine owned, managed and ordered assets. [W4-S24]

Preserve the measurement unit. Passenger kilometers measure people transported over distance. Aircraft flight hours measure time flown by an aircraft; engine hours are a different population; cycles measure events and can create a different maintenance burden from elapsed hours. Capacity and load factor are not a substitute for any of these. IATA's traffic series is a useful demand context, but not a direct engine-maintenance input without a supported bridge. [W4-S19, W4-S20]

For each cohort, investigate entry into service, active count, usage per active unit, age since the last major visit, relevant life limits, upgrade status and retirement decisions. The source may support only a subset. Do not estimate a precise fleet-age distribution from average age, or a precise next-visit schedule from aggregate hours.

A future forecasting model should maintain separate uncertainty in usage, maintenance incidence, workscope and revenue capture. A ten-percent change in passenger traffic need not create a ten-percent change in parts sales. Different routes, aircraft types, parked capacity, airline inventories and contract structures can change the relationship.

The product should show whether a measurement is global, a regional sample, an issuer's served fleet or a single program. Those populations cannot be compared as though they were identical. Growth in one network's completed visits could reflect share gain, expansion, backlog processing or a different mix rather than a universal increase in maintenance demand.

## 5. The durability paradox - a reproducible economic counterexample

Consider a hypothetical fleet of 1,000 engines, each producing 3,000 billable engine hours per year. An hourly-service provider charges 300 per hour, incurs 40 per hour of other support cost and pays 1.2 million per visit. The baseline interval between visits is 6,000 hours. A separate event-paid provider charges 1.5 million per visit and incurs the same visit cost.

The following is a long-run renewal-rate proxy, using total hours divided by the interval. It is not an actual one-year visit forecast, recognized accounting profit, or a simulation of a finite fleet's age distribution. It holds annual billable hours fixed to isolate the contractual effect.

| Hypothetical condition | Visit-equivalents | Hourly provider contribution, millions | Event-paid provider contribution, millions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Baseline: 6,000-hour interval | 500.0 | 180.0 | 150.0 |
| Improved durability: 9,000 hours | 333.3 | 380.0 | 100.0 |
| Worse durability: 4,000 hours | 750.0 | -120.0 | 225.0 |

The hourly provider's billings remain 900 million in all three cases. Its recurring billing looks unchanged while expected renewal cost changes sharply. The event-paid provider earns less when fewer visits are needed, assuming it has enough available capacity, customers still pay and its unit costs do not change.

This does not establish that real-world providers have these prices, costs or coverage. It demonstrates that the same physical improvement can have opposite direct effects depending on contractual role. In reality, better durability might also increase active hours, reduce compensation, improve retention or alter contract pricing. Worse durability might reduce billable hours or overwhelm shop capacity, so the fixed-hours stress case must not be promoted to a complete forecast.

The break-even fee in this simplified hourly model is `visit_cost / hours_between_visits + other_cost_per_hour`: 240 at baseline, approximately 173.33 with the longer interval and 340 with the shorter interval. A usable valuation scenario must therefore connect contract rate and cost to actual durability assumptions rather than attach a generic recurring-revenue multiple.

## 6. Maintenance demand, shop output and working capital

A service backlog is not completed work. A capacity analysis should distinguish requests, admitted engines, work in progress, completed visits, invoiced work, collections and released aircraft. Each boundary needs its own quantity, date and source.

A simple steady-state illustration uses 200 annual shop completions. At 90 days of turnaround, average work in progress is approximately 49.3 engines; at 150 days it is 82.2. The difference is 32.9 engines. Valued illustratively at 2 million per unit, that is 65.8 million of additional assets in the maintenance system. This is a Little's-law calculation, not proof that the MRO provider owns or finances every unit.

The identity only applies to a consistently defined steady-state boundary. Using admissions from one shop, completions from another and calendar time from a third would not constitute a valid calculation. A sudden disruption requires a dynamic queue and age distribution, not simply a new steady-state ratio.

Likewise, 750 admissions and capacity for 600 completions would add 150 units to the backlog in the period, before cancellations or transfers. The optimistic event-paid case in the preceding section cannot assume every demanded visit is simultaneously completed under that constraint. Inventory, spare-engine leasing and customer downtime may rise while completed revenue and cash lag.

This produces a better capacity-expansion thesis: new resources must turn into qualified, productive completions with attractive contribution and acceptable working capital. An announced facility or equipment purchase is not that proof. License access, trained labor, material availability, process qualification and customer acceptance can each remain limiting.

## 7. Original-equipment growth can depress current margins while creating value

The economic logic of an installation cohort needs an explicit lifetime model. Early losses, customer support, development and production investment can precede attractive service cash. That possibility does not justify unlimited losses or assume every installed unit becomes a profitable service customer.

Our finite-life hypothetical begins with a net initial cost of 100 and ten annual net service cash payments of 25 in years 3 through 12, discounted at 10%. There is no terminal value. Net present value is 26.95. The annual service amount is assumed to be after the operating and reinvestment costs required to earn it; it is not revenue or EBITDA.

| Hypothetical variation | Net present value |
|---|---:|
| Baseline | 26.95 |
| Same ten payments delayed to years 5-14 | 4.92 |
| Annual net service cash reduced from 25 to 20 | 1.56 |
| Initial net cost increased from 100 to 130 | -3.05 |

The results demonstrate why production scale, service timing and economic capture must be studied together. A large fleet can be valuable but much less valuable if its cash is delayed. A small recurring reduction in net contribution can absorb most of an apparently attractive initial value. Costs hidden in a development asset or license still enter the economic calculation.

The assumption that the entire stream shifts two years without shortening is favorable relative to a case where the customer retires the asset on the original date. An actual program model must decide whether delay extends life, compresses the service window or causes cancellation. It must also reflect competition, retention and risk-sharing partners before attributing the whole stream to one issuer.

For current reporting, GE's installation-versus-service mix is useful evidence of the distinction; it is not a calibration of this hypothetical cohort. [W4-S04] For financial planning, show both current margins and the separately justified expected value of new cohorts. Neither should be used to conceal the other.

## 8. Contract estimates: information, not automatic recurring profit

The Rolls-Royce disclosure makes separate categories observable: contractual profit effects, revenue catch-ups, onerous provisions, invoiced receipts and cash-flow movements. The arithmetic of GBP574 million less GBP77 million, or GBP372 million plus GBP125 million, reconciles to the same GBP497 million total. Relative to Civil Aerospace underlying profit, that total is approximately 31.7%. This is a disclosed-component comparison, not an assertion that the balance is a fully normalized profit measure. [W4-S02]

A forecast revision can contain real information about a contract's future economics. It can also accelerate recognition of a benefit into the current reporting period. Annualizing the current effect without considering future recognition would therefore risk counting the same improvement twice. Automatically discarding all estimate changes would lose potentially valuable information. The appropriate research output explains the change and separates its current recognition from its future cash implications.

A generic cost-to-cost illustration makes the timing distinction visible. With contract consideration of 1,000, incurred cost of 400 and expected total cost of 800, cumulative revenue is 500. Reducing expected total cost to 700 raises cumulative revenue to 571.43, a 71.43 catch-up without an assumed new payment. Raising expected total cost to 900 instead lowers cumulative revenue to 444.44. This is not a representation of every aerospace contract's accounting method. Loss-making contracts and onerous provisions require additional treatment outside this simple example.

A naive adjustment that removes a revenue catch-up only from profit but leaves the same revenue denominator can also create a misleading margin. Research should retain the original report, the proposed adjustment to each line, the effect on remaining recognition, and the reason the adjustment is appropriate. Unknowns remain unknown.

GE's net estimate-change disclosure contains a favorable reversal within a net unfavorable amount. The implied remainder is a derived residual, not a newly reported standalone category. Do not add the reversal again after using the net number. [W4-S05]

## 9. Cash claims and obligations require their own bridge

Billing, recognized revenue and cash collection are separate. Collections can reflect past services, advances, credits or payment schedules. A net contract liability is not necessarily cash freely available for shareholder distribution; it can fund future obligations. A smaller compensation accrual is not necessarily an equal cash payment or an economic improvement.

RTX's remediation disclosure is an important adverse example because program share, customer credits, partner recoveries and timing all matter. The research question is not simply whether the accrual declined. It is what obligation remains, what has been settled, through which instrument, and who ultimately bears the cost. [W4-S09]

The historical Rolls-Royce 2020 observation is another challenge to the simplistic defensiveness claim. It is a realized usage and liquidity shock, not a current forecast or a basis for copying a historical cash sensitivity into today's fleet. Financing-policy changes and working capital were part of the outcome alongside flying hours. [W4-S03]

For future models, construct a complete bridge: opening contract assets/liabilities; billings; cash; recognized work; estimate changes; currency and perimeter; transfers, credits and settlements; closing balances. The required data will not always be disclosed. A partial bridge should state its residual rather than invent a financing explanation.

Cash quality analysis should also ask what spending was deferred or excluded. A positive quarter after a weak prior period may be a genuine recovery, seasonal collection or the timing of large obligations. A six-month total and a quarter cannot silently substitute for each other.

## 10. Maintenance rights are capital too

StandardAero adds a useful test of the full investment burden: its new adjusted cash-flow guidance excludes intangible purchases, while its reporting describes license-related assets and an unpaid acquisition of intangible assets. Those definitions can be useful for management's stated purpose without representing all the capital required to obtain the economic rights. [W4-S25]

The research should distinguish a facility that can perform work physically from the rights, access, approvals and customer relationships needed to perform that work commercially. Costs may take the form of upfront licenses, milestone obligations, royalties, tooling, training, spare stock or development. Each affects value through a different timing pattern.

A hypothetical license costing 40 upfront and producing incremental net cash of 10 annually for five years has an NPV of approximately -1.10 at a 9% required return. Positive annual contribution and an adjusted measure excluding amortization do not by themselves establish an attractive investment. Conversely, a license could be valuable if it enables a longer, larger or lower-risk cash stream. The answer requires the terms and resulting business, not an automatic penalty for intangibles.

An unpaid liability also has not yet become a cash outflow in the cash-flow statement. It still represents a future economic claim that a valuation must consider. Do not subtract it as both current cash spending and a future liability settlement. Preserve recognition, payment dates and obligations separately.

## 11. Apparent margin expansion can be a denominator change

In a hypothetical service business, revenue of 100 and operating cost of 80 yield profit of 20 and a 20% margin. Removing 60 of zero-margin pass-through revenue and its associated cost produces revenue of 40, cost of 20 and the same profit of 20, but a 50% margin. Nothing in this isolated example improves absolute profit.

StandardAero explicitly identifies pass-through removal as one contributor to its margin change. It also reports operational improvements; the denominator explanation should not be used to erase them. The research obligation is to separate both effects, not choose whichever narrative is more appealing. [W4-S25]

The same discipline applies across peers. MTU's OEM category includes spare parts. TransDigm's defined EBITDA is not another company's EBIT. Safran's adjusted and consolidated results use distinct financial bases. Lufthansa Technik's business mix and one-time items matter. A ranking assembled from those headline margins without reconciliation would look precise but compare different economic quantities. [W4-S08, W4-S16, W4-S06, W4-S18]

Useful peer comparison begins with a matched operating role and consistent measurement. Where adjustment is impossible, show parallel source-native measures and explicitly decline to rank them as equivalent.

## 12. Parts scarcity, alternatives and customer choices

The proposed moat analysis should separate technical criticality from ownership of the financial benefit. A critical part may be sold under constrained pricing. A provider may have an approved alternative but lack customer acceptance or economical production. A distributor may create availability value without owning the underlying intellectual property.

FAA PMA supplies a bounded regulatory fact: approval applies to eligible articles and products. It is not blanket permission to replace every part or proof that a particular airline, lessor or contract will accept an alternative. The research needs source-supported eligibility, commercial acceptance, actual adoption, development cost and price competition. [W4-S22]

Used material adds another customer choice. Keeping an aircraft in service can support recurring maintenance demand while reducing the near-term supply of material from disassembly. Retiring it can release material but remove future operating demand. These are competing channels to investigate, not an assumed fixed elasticity. Whole-asset sales, leases, repaired components and disassembled material must not be counted as simultaneous independent uses of the same asset. AerSale's mix highlights why revenue timing and operating model matter. [W4-S26]

Ryanair's planned maintenance arrangement illustrates a related distinction: bringing labor and shop capability inside the customer does not automatically eliminate OEM material purchases. The announcement remains a future arrangement rather than observed realized economics. [W4-S21]

The dossier should therefore name the mechanism of any claimed supplier displacement: loss of labor revenue, loss of spare material, price renegotiation, changed workscope, internalized inventory or a different asset-use decision. A generic 'disruption' label is inadequate.

## 13. Corporate growth and shareholder value

An original-equipment, service or parts thesis ultimately reaches shareholders through the correct legal and financial scope. Joint-venture percentages, segment profits and consolidated cash do not automatically belong wholly to one listed security.

CFM's physical output must be counted once even when both parents report it. HEICO's consolidated results require corporate and noncontrolling-interest reconciliation. AAR's reporting changes require versioned segment history. Announced acquisitions must retain their actual completion state. [W4-S07, W4-S15, W4-S13, W4-S23]

HEICO's operating cash less physical capex is approximately USD761.802 million for the cited nine-month period; subtracting the disclosed acquisition spending gives a partial cash subtotal of negative USD256.362 million. That is not the company's total free-cash definition, a verdict on the acquisitions, or proof of distress. It shows that an acquisition-dependent growth model cannot be described as requiring only plant capital. Other investing flows, financing and shareholder claims remain outside this partial calculation. [W4-S15]

For capital allocation, evaluate purchase price, acquired cash-generating capacity, integration cost, financing, dilution, contingent consideration and the counterfactual use of capital. EPS accretion can coexist with inadequate economic returns. A restructuring or transaction announcement does not instantly change historical exposure or current consolidation.

## 14. Aircraft and engine leasing are different from service subscriptions

A lease creates exposure to customer payment, asset availability, financing and future value, not simply an annuity detached from the aircraft. Maintenance-related receipts can depend on contract and asset transitions. Realized disposal gains are different from recurring rent and should be understood relative to carrying values and retained fleet economics.

AerCap's guidance explicitly includes first-half gains but excludes additional gains in the remaining half. That asymmetry matters when comparing guidance vintages or an analyst forecast; it is not appropriate to assume identical disposal activity is embedded throughout the year. Its mixed portfolio count is also not a count of currently leased, owned aircraft alone. [W4-S24]

A lessor model should connect purchase and funding costs, lease rate, maintenance settlements, transition downtime, credit losses, residual value, taxes and capital recycling. Comparison with an asset-light parts supplier needs a different capital framework. Comparing margins alone ignores what each company must own or finance to earn them.

Potential scarcity benefits also need adverse scenarios. Delayed new aircraft can support demand for existing assets, but operating disruptions, customer liquidity or unexpectedly high repair costs can impair the same portfolio. The dossier should expose both channels and identify which is evidenced rather than assert that constrained supply always raises shareholder value.

## 16. Data acquisition and validation design

The next data work should proceed by question rather than buying an undifferentiated aviation dataset. For cohort economics, seek active units, engine type, usage, service attachment and revision history. For shop economics, seek comparable workscopes, admissions, output, turnaround, cost and cash. For alternatives, seek eligible parts, actually accepted substitutions, observed prices and customer usage. For valuation, seek dated forecasts and prices with licensing that supports the intended product.

Public reports demonstrate some concepts but do not supply a complete contract-level history. Missing underlying series should be recorded explicitly. Do not fill a paid-consensus gap with management guidance or an LLM summary. A source title or scheduled release is not available numerical data: AAR's next announced reporting date is later than this research cutoff. [W4-S14]

A proposed historical evaluation should freeze each input at its availability time. Preserve revised filings, changed contract estimates, acquisition effects, changes in segments, and failed programs. Test separate outcomes: explaining current earnings, improving future cash estimates, detecting thesis failure, and predicting returns. Success on one does not establish another.

For incremental value, compare the proposed models with simpler baselines using prior financial trends, broad aviation activity and price factors. Hold out periods or cohorts rather than optimize every decision after seeing results. Stress the result by removing exceptional charges and favorable reversals consistently, changing measurement windows, and evaluating the losers as carefully as the winners.

No such empirical evaluation has been completed in this wave. The local calculations and authored rejection fixtures only establish that the proposed arithmetic and measurement rules are internally coherent for the selected cases.

## 17. Proposed product workflow

The existing Theme Tracker remains the entry surface. The reader selects an aerospace research slice, sees a plain-language economic mechanism, examines source-backed business scope and operating changes, and opens the relevant company workflow. The dossier should answer why the observation matters, not simply offer a larger table of citations.

A contract-role module should show who earns, performs and bears risk. A fleet module should distinguish active use, installation and future orders. A maintenance module should separate demand, output and capital tied up. A financial module should reconcile reporting measures, contract revisions and cash. A valuation module should show explicit scenarios, missing expectations and falsifiers.

The shared evidence drawer should preserve source period, publication date, scope, unit, reporting basis and correction state. An unavailable contract, unresolved identity or missing forecast must produce a visibly limited answer rather than an apparently complete one. Research confidence that a statement was made remains separate from confidence that a forecast will occur.

The implementation must compose through existing GMI evidence, Data OS identity, K1, relationship/forecast and authenticated-publication owners. The Robotics template is a design reference, not proof that its proposed interfaces are production-ready. No new graph, identity master, scoring system, persistent curation queue or public current full-fidelity payload is authorized by this research.

Before an eventual build, refresh source custody and shared-template interfaces. Preserve existing basket membership, entry, sizing, ranking and execution policies. Research observations and hypothetical arithmetic cannot originate or size trades. Browser acceptance must later show the actual user journey and its negative/degraded states; a source file or passing local script does not demonstrate that outcome.
