## 4. What changes in the economic conclusions

**Paired adjustments.** A reduction in an operating expense can be accompanied by an equal reduction in non-operating income. A claimed improvement in operating leverage therefore requires inspecting both locations. A researcher may calculate a clearly labeled paired sensitivity, but may neither present it as the issuer's accepted non-GAAP metric nor remove only the gain while leaving its matched expense. Removing an equal expense/gain pair leaves pretax income unchanged.

**Cash conversion.** Revenue and earnings describe different processes from cash collection. The relevant question is whether funding tied up in receivables supports profitable work or reflects deterioration. One six-month cash comparison does not calibrate a durable working-capital ratio. A later filing can strengthen today's explanation while remaining unavailable to an earlier historical view.

**Business scope.** A sharp decline in one business can dominate several improving businesses. Segment contributions explain where a reported change resides, not which narrow theme or customer caused it. Corporate items remain part of the bridge. Refunds, reorganizations and announced acquisitions cannot disappear from the interpretation.

**Information timing.** The first public warning and the final result are different disclosures about the same fiscal outcome. A later finalization can be favorable relative to a preview and unfavorable relative to much older guidance. Neither comparison identifies the external expectation immediately before the event. Historical replay requires knowledge eligibility, not merely a fiscal endpoint.

These findings refine the selected first vertical; they do not reopen prior completed source sweeps or establish return predictability.
