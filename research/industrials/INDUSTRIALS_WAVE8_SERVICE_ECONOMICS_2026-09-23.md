## 1. What this wave changes

The initial Industrials research was strongest on physical equipment and projects. The human-capital and outsourcing extension requires another distinction: who supplies labor, who owes benefits or service performance, who temporarily holds money, and who has the right to retain productivity gains. A low factory-capital requirement does not settle working-capital, customer-acquisition, claims or liquidity economics.

The proposed investment question is not whether a business has recurring fees. It is whether the retained fee and related economics can produce durable cash after the necessary people, technology, risk obligations and financing. A service can be necessary to the customer but competitively priced. A customer can stay while the number of paid workers or transactions falls. A company can improve operations while an old comparison charge makes the reported improvement look much larger. These are distinct hypotheses to investigate, not reasons to reject service businesses.

The research also closes part of the specialist-transport gap. Container shipping and airports supply counterexamples to treating all transport as domestic freight: effective vessel capacity can vary without a change in the fleet, while airport commissioning changes both the accounting and capital cycle. The selected examples do not complete a global airline, airport-concession, tanker, dry-bulk or transport-equipment census.

The tangible outputs are a business-role map, explicit profit/cash mechanisms, a new external-expectations comparison, an existing-owner historical-data discovery, and testable product constraints. Sector classification remains with the current identity/taxonomy owners. Research membership is not permission to alter live basket constituents.

## 3. Payroll is a service business and a balance-sheet business

ADP's disclosure makes two economic measurements available: retained operating service economics and the net client-funds investment strategy. Its PEO benefits pass-through subtraction yields a much narrower retained-revenue measure than total PEO sales. The interest strategy includes both earnings and associated financing, not just the headline interest line. These source arithmetic results are reproduced in the script. [S01]

The proposed model separates payroll service fees from income earned on temporarily held client money. The service model asks about paid customers, eligible paid workers, pricing, service costs, implementation, customer-acquisition spending and retention. The funds model asks about balance scale, investment duration, liquidity matching, yield resets and financing cost. They interact, but they are not the same source of profit growth.

A stable client relationship does not guarantee stable paid employee counts. Stronger wages can increase money passing through the system without proportionately increasing service fees. Calendar timing can change cash held at a reporting date. A source reporting average balances is answering a different question from one reporting quarter-end balances. These are proposed measurement rules; actual sensitivity requires the customer and portfolio data.

### An interest-rate counterexample

An illustrative portfolio holds 100 of client principal. Initially 80% is fixed at 2% and 20% earns a short rate of 5%, producing 2.6 of annualized interest. At the next measurement, 20 percentage points of the portfolio have matured and are reinvested at 4%; the remaining old fixed portion is 60%, while the 20% short portion also earns 4%. Annualized interest rises to 2.8 even though the short rate fell from 5% to 4%.

This is a deliberately simplified portfolio snapshot, not realized annual earnings. It excludes intra-period timing, balance changes, credit losses, liquidity needs, taxes and financing. It demonstrates why a lower policy or market rate does not identify the immediate earnings change of a portfolio with older vintages. The real research needs maturity schedules and net funding economics before making that inference.

The principal is still 100 owed to clients in both scenarios. It is not added to corporate excess cash. Counting both the principal as equity value and the income produced by retaining it would misstate ownership. Likewise, gross client assets less one selected obligation do not provide a complete solvency assessment. Liquidity and the full liability boundary must be understood, not guessed from two headline numbers.

### Valuation implications

Model core service cash and the rate-sensitive investment component separately before combining them. A rerating thesis could concern better retained service economics, stronger customer longevity, improved liquidity management or a changing investment yield. Each requires a different falsifier. A favorable rate vintage is not automatically a permanent improvement in software quality, and its entire benefit should not be capitalized indefinitely at a recurring-service multiple.

Paychex adds a chronology and normalization requirement: some current guidance components improved without changing the total-growth ranges, while shrinking adjustments explain part of the difference between GAAP and adjusted profit growth. The older quarter also crossed a changing acquisition comparison. No one reported growth number isolates organic operational improvement. [S02,S03]

## 4. PEO revenue is not comparable to pure payroll subscription revenue

A professional employer organization may combine administration, benefits and other obligations. The precise risk retained depends on the actual arrangements. Our dataset should preserve the disclosed revenue and costs rather than label all gross flows as subscription economics.

TriNet supplies a useful reported bridge. Insurance contribution increased by 39, while professional-service revenue fell 13 and interest fell 6; the remaining cost savings were 3. The arithmetic reconciles the 23 increase in pretax income. The serviced population declined. These figures are source-based decompositions, not claims about the causes of improved insurance costs or a forecast of future claims. [S04]

The research must next distinguish repricing, population mix, claims experience, estimate development, recoveries and cost control where disclosures allow. Lower costs today are not automatically a recurring technology benefit. A changing workforce can alter revenue and risk at the same time. End-of-period workers, average paid workers and the population exposed to a claim may not be identical.

For valuation, the administration fee and insurance-risk contribution should be analyzed separately and then reconciled to shared costs and capital. A blanket software margin applied to gross PEO revenue would be wrong. So would treating all insurance revenue as valueless pass-through when the company actually retains economic risk or contribution. The correct model follows the issuer's contract and financial definitions rather than choosing whichever presentation produces the highest apparent margin.

## 5. Staffing can grow revenue without growing its economic spread

A staffing supplier's useful unit is not necessarily a client account. It may be an accepted hour of work, a completed placement or a managed recruiting outcome. The bill rate, loaded labor cost, recruiting expense, customer credit and timing of wage funding determine different economics.

Manpower's reported profit change can be reconciled into 16.6 of additional gross profit, 32.0 of lower selling/administrative costs and the absence of an 88.7 prior impairment. That bridge should not be advertised as pure structural productivity, particularly when other portfolio and adjusted-item effects remain. Robert Half illustrates that sequential improvement can coexist with a weaker year-over-year level. [S05,S06,S09]

### Wage pass-through and repricing lag

Consider 100,000 annual hours billed at 30, with wage 22, other hourly labor burden 3 and fixed recruiting/administration expense of 200,000. Revenue is 3,000,000; gross profit is 500,000; contribution is 300,000.

A 10% wage increase adds 2.2 per hour. If the entire amount is immediately passed through in the bill rate, absolute contribution remains 300,000, but the gross margin percentage falls. Revenue growth in that example is not volume growth, and percentage-margin compression does not mean the absolute spread fell.

If repricing occurs after three months, average annual billing is 31.65 while wage plus burden is 27.2 throughout the year. Contribution falls to 245,000. The timing mismatch costs 55,000 even though the new rate eventually recovers the full same-dollar wage change. This model assumes constant hours and equal activity through the year; it excludes wage-linked burden increases, turnover, overtime, customer losses and credit costs. Those exclusions make it a narrow sensitivity, not a forecast.

Security contracts may expose a similar timing question, but their service obligations and deployment costs need their own data. Securitas' portfolio-adjusted organic rate is not its unadjusted organic rate, and technology/solutions growth does not identify software subscription revenue. Selecting the most flattering rate would hide the business being exited. [S10]

### The funding requirement

On 3,000,000 of annual credit revenue, fifteen additional collection days tie up approximately 123,288 in receivables under a uniform-sales approximation. Relative to the modeled 300,000 contribution, this is material. It is not automatically a recurring expense: it is extra capital that may require financing and faces collection risk.

A faster recovery can increase receivables and wage funding before cash arrives. A contraction can release working capital even as economic profit deteriorates. The product should explain this directional difference instead of labeling every cash inflow as a quality improvement.

Permanent placement requires a different model: placement completion, fee realization, cancellation or guarantee terms and recruiter cost, rather than assuming that the supplier funds the placed employee's ongoing payroll. Contract staffing, permanent recruitment and consulting should therefore remain separate even inside one issuer.

## 6. Outsourcing productivity requires a commercial transition model

An automation announcement is not a realized revenue or profit measure. Our proposed model asks which unit the customer buys and how the transition changes billable activity, retained pricing, service quality, labor, tools, and migration investment.

A hypothetical operation completes one million transactions at a price of 5 and variable cost of 3, with fixed support costs of 500,000. Contribution is 1.5 million. Automation saves 1 per transaction but adds a tool cost of 0.3. The provider gives the customer a 0.4 price concession and spends 400,000 on migration.

Steady annual contribution improves to 1.8 million, but first-year contribution is only 1.4 million. At 20% lower volume, first-year contribution falls to 940,000 with the same fixed support and migration cost. The unit process is more efficient in all the automated cases, yet the shareholder outcome depends on volume, pricing and the transition.

The recurring concession break-even is 0.7 per transaction under the assumptions. For the first year at one million transactions, the migration investment reduces that break-even to 0.3. This model omits tax, financing, customer acquisition, quality penalties, depreciation and additional retention effects. It is not an issuer forecast and does not establish that any particular AI product generates the assumed saving.

Concentrix gives a related reporting lesson. Its quarter's free cash improved while the half-year comparison weakened. Its factoring adjustment is sign-sensitive. A reduction of earlier financing can suppress reported current cash, so reversing the change may add to an adjusted measure; it is incorrect to subtract every factoring label mechanically. Retain both reported and adjusted measures and the disclosed formula. [S13]

TP supplies a source-quality hold. Its issuer-distributed text contains conflicting language about restructuring inclusion. The official page did not expose the underlying financial report in the available rendered view. We should not choose an adjustment merely to force a clean peer margin. Its offshore and service-mix discussion is useful attributed evidence, but a complete normalized-profit comparison remains held. [S14]

The possible rerating mechanism is demonstrably more durable contribution after the customer receives its share of savings and the provider pays to migrate. The strongest counterargument is that automation reduces billable demand or enables price concessions faster than operating costs fall. Both must be visible before an attractive technology narrative becomes an investment conclusion.

## 7. Cash-management services and ownership changes

Cash-management outsourcing should be analyzed through the provider's share of the customer's work, serviced locations, contracts, equipment and unit costs. Growth in outsourced services does not logically require growth in total cash use: share gains can offset a declining addressable activity pool, but not indefinitely without limits. That is a hypothesis requiring customer and population data, not a claim about today's penetration.

Brink's distinguishes different service lines and describes a future strategic combination. Contract pricing, ownership of equipment and required deployment capital still determine the economic value of the recurring revenue. The announced transaction must remain separate from completed ownership; consideration including debt cannot be treated as an equity-only purchase price. [S11,S12]

A business transition should record the effective date, acquired scope, consideration, financing, integration obligations and minority claims before rebuilding theme exposure. A closing probability is not assumed here. The research does not grant authority to alter a live issuer map or publish pro forma exposure as historical fact.

## 8. Specialist transport: capacity, throughput and the start of depreciation

For a hypothetical fleet with 100,000 slots, a 90% load factor and a 40-day round trip, annual effective loaded slots are 821,250. A 50-day round trip reduces them to 657,000, a 20% decline without removing a single vessel. The calculation holds every other assumption fixed. It does not model actual shipping schedules, route decisions or operational vulnerabilities.

This creates a financial research question: is a favorable rate environment supported by durable customer demand, or by temporarily reduced effective supply that may normalize? New fleet delivery, utilization, fuel, leasing and voyage costs then affect how revenue reaches cash. A period of strong profit is not automatically a higher through-cycle return on capital.

Maersk's reported rate, volume and revenue headlines use scopes that do not exactly multiply into the stated revenue change. The discrepancy remains unallocated rather than being labeled invented mix or foreign-exchange effects. Terminal storage income can behave differently from productive moves. The selected source supports separate shipping, terminal and logistics questions, not a single transport coefficient. [S15]

Airports introduce a different capital transition. Fraport attributes part of its weaker net profit to the end of construction-interest capitalization after commissioning. That does not mean cash interest suddenly appeared only when it reached the income statement. [S16]

An illustrative capital-cycle reconciliation holds total cash interest at 30. Before commissioning, EBITDA is 100, depreciation 20 and expensed interest 10, with 20 interest capitalized. Pretax profit is 70. After commissioning, EBITDA rises to 110, depreciation to 35 and expensed interest to 30; pretax profit falls to 45. Meanwhile, assumed asset spending excluding interest falls from 120 to 40, raising a simple pretax cash proxy from -50 to +40. The model omits tax, working capital, financing flows and concession obligations; it is not cash available to equity.

The analyst must follow the entire project investment, not assume either the profit decline or the improved cash proxy alone establishes value creation. Passenger growth, fee realization, commercial concessions, required upgrades and finite operating rights remain separate research inputs. Airport commercial and wider maritime coverage remain incomplete.

## 9. A new external-expectations case and a better data-recovery route

Randstad's issuer-hosted analyst table labels its Q2 aggregate as updated and published July 9, ahead of the July 22 release. Reported revenue exceeds the aggregate by about 1.71%, and underlying EBITA by 4.00%, on the stated underlying/ex-exceptional comparison. These are retrospective arithmetic observations, not investment outcomes. [S07,S08]

Two limits matter. First, each consensus line is averaged independently. Mean revenue multiplied by mean margin need not equal mean profit; that alone is not evidence of an error. Second, the mutable page carries newer call information beside the older table label. Retrieval today is not an immutable receipt proving precisely which values were available to every investor before the event. Original contributor times, revisions and a complete intervening-announcement search remain outstanding.

Actual statutory net income must not be compared against the table's adjusted net-income estimate. Likewise, being above the maximum of a small analyst sample is not a calibrated tail probability or proof of a positive price response. The case was selected after results and belongs in development, not the held-out evaluation set.
