## 1. Research decision and intended user outcome

Industrials will be organized first around how businesses earn money and how expectations change. A physical bill of materials is useful for some questions but is not the universal sector model. The research must connect economically funded customer demand to profitable delivery, cash conversion, reinvestment, durable earning power, and valuation. A shortage is neither necessary nor sufficient for an attractive investment thesis.

The intended investor journey is to select a granular industrial subtheme, understand its current operating drivers, identify the particular businesses exposed, distinguish evidence from forecasts, inspect what could change the thesis, and return to Mastermind's existing company and market workflows. The machine's job is to preserve business scope, economic mechanism, dates, measurement definitions, uncertainty, and historical revisions without promoting research hypotheses into trading authority.

The end state is not a larger ticker list or a single attractiveness score. It is an explainable research dossier that answers: What creates demand? Who captures the economic profit? What has changed? Which expectations would have to change? What assumptions are required by a valuation scenario? What would falsify the thesis?

This first wave resolves the organizing architecture and pressure-tests it against a deliberately varied sample of public disclosures. It does not establish current stock cheapness, a complete global company census, empirical predictive power, or accepted source admission. Those remain explicit subsequent obligations.

## 3. A multi-axis ontology, not one tree

A company can sell several products, serve several demand themes, and occupy several economic cycles at once. Keep these independent dimensions connected through evidenced business scope:

| Dimension | Question it answers | Must not be confused with |
|---|---|---|
| Industry classification | What conventional industry contains the business? | Its only economic exposure |
| Economic archetype | How does it earn, reinvest, and generate cash? | A tradable basket |
| Demand theme | Which customer spending or usage change matters? | Every dollar of company revenue |
| Product/capability | What does the business supply or perform? | A verified customer contract |
| End market | Who uses or funds the output? | Production geography |
| Business/segment | Which operating unit has the exposure? | The entire issuer |
| Research slice | Which related questions are investigated together? | Authorized live constituents/weights |
| Cycle state | What changed in demand, capacity, inventory, or margins? | A stock-return forecast |
| Expectations/valuation | What assumptions are forecast or implied? | A reported fact |
| Physical dependency | What must be available to deliver? | Automatic pricing power or profits |

Revenue exposure, profit exposure, marginal growth contribution, and stock-price sensitivity are different measurements. A small business may drive a large share of incremental profit, but that does not justify inventing its contribution or treating its revenue share as stock beta. Overlapping themes are non-additive unless a reconciled allocation explicitly establishes otherwise.

## 4. From business improvement to stock-price change

### 4.1 Separate the accounting identity from the forecast

For positive, consistently defined earnings per share E and matching price/earnings multiple M:

`P1 / P0 = (E1 / E0) * (M1 / M0)`.

This is an identity, not evidence that earnings caused the observed price change. A purely hypothetical 20% EPS increase and 25% multiple decline produce `1.20 * 0.75 - 1 = -10%` price return. Dividends are separate; repurchase effects already reflected in per-share earnings must not be added again as a second mechanical return contribution. Changing from trailing EPS to a different forward horizon invalidates a like-for-like decomposition. Negative or unstable earnings require a different valuation lens.

For a forward-multiple analysis, freeze the expected fiscal horizon and estimate vintage. Distinguish estimate revisions from the mechanical roll into the next forecast year. A multiple residual is not a clean causal estimate of sentiment, discount rates, or quality.

### 4.2 Growth has a reinvestment cost

The valuation methodology in S20 explicitly connects growth, reinvestment, and returns on capital. Our proposed industrial model therefore asks how much capital and operating support are required to earn additional profit, not just how quickly revenue can rise.

Illustration, not a company forecast: with a stable 6% profit-growth assumption and a simplified sustainable incremental return of 20%, the required reinvestment rate is 30%; at a 10% return it is 60%. Two businesses with the same headline growth can consequently have different distributable cash. The illustration assumes the simplified growth-return-reinvestment relationship applies; it is not a substitute for explicit capacity lags, changing margins, or acquisition accounting.

Value scenarios must connect revenue, margins, tax, working capital, maintenance and growth capital, and a coherent discount rate. Do not assume indefinite growth with no reinvestment. Do not compare industrial enterprise value including all captive-finance debt against only industrial operating profit. Model the finance business and its funding consistently. Lease, pension, minority-interest, and non-operating-asset treatment must be explicit before peer comparisons.

### 4.3 Seven rerating mechanisms to investigate

| Mechanism | Evidence needed | What would undermine it |
|---|---|---|
| R01 A higher through-cycle earnings floor | Comparable downturn evidence, durable cost/productivity changes, retained customers | Margins fall back when utilization normalizes |
| R02 A longer profitable growth runway | Paid adoption, funded projects, capacity conversion, returns on new capital | Announced demand never becomes attractive deliveries |
| R03 More durable revenue/profit | Retention, installed-base usage, genuine service or software economics | Recurring labels hide projects, churn, or loss-making obligations |
| R04 Reduced execution uncertainty | Delivery history, fewer overruns, reliable cash milestones | New growth increases operational tail risks |
| R05 Improved capital efficiency | Sustainable cash conversion and returns with consistent capital accounting | Temporary deposits, inventory liquidation, or underinvestment explain cash |
| R06 Reduced financial/portfolio complexity | Completed business changes, deleveraging, credible business-level economics | Announced transactions do not close or destroy value |
| R07 Changed market discounting | Dated market prices, rates, peer factors, expectations and positioning | A company story is merely a broad factor move |

These are conditional hypotheses, not claims that any current issuer deserves a higher multiple. Management guidance, external consensus, a house forecast, and a reverse-valuation scenario remain separate. An implied scenario is one set of assumptions consistent with a price, not a uniquely recovered market belief.

## 6. What the comparative research establishes — and does not

The evidence establishes the necessity of differentiated measurement. It does not establish which stocks will outperform. In this sample, organic demand, orders, margins, and cash do not move uniformly; business-level differences are large enough that a sector aggregate cannot stand in for each thesis.

Four proposed analytical distinctions emerge:

**Demand acceleration versus profitable conversion.** Orders are a commitment/flow measurement under a particular definition. Revenue is recognized delivery or service. Margin concerns the economics of that delivery. Cash adds billing, collections, deposits, inventory, and capital needs. Each transition needs evidence; none is implied by the prior stage's headline strength. S01, S03, S07, S10, S11.

**Cyclical improvement versus business-quality change.** Higher volume can improve fixed-cost absorption without proving a higher long-run margin floor. A high-multiple quality thesis requires persistence through different demand conditions, not one favorable quarter. Portfolio changes, prior charges, and gains must be isolated before claiming structural improvement. S02, S06, S13, S16.

**Recurring revenue versus recurring economic profit.** Installed-base service, software subscriptions, and regular industrial supply differ in gross margin, service liability, customer retention, working capital, and capital intensity. A recurring label alone does not establish common valuation economics. S02, S04, S09, S12, S15.

**Economic level versus momentum versus expectations.** A PMI above its neutral threshold can coexist with deceleration; industrial output and a survey can diverge. Likewise, positive business growth can disappoint a more optimistic forecast. The missing expectations/valuation evidence is not supplied by fundamental momentum alone. S17, S18, and the identity in Section 4.

## 7. A causal research protocol for every subtheme

Every completed subtheme dossier should contain an explicit mechanism, exposure boundaries, dated observations, competing explanations, a valuation sensitivity, and a falsifier. The following protocol is proposed for analysis, not a new state machine.

### 7.1 Demand transmission

Begin with the paying customer's economics. Separate the need for a product from willingness and ability to pay. Track project announcement, budget, financing, firm award, delivery, commissioning, utilization, and ongoing service where relevant. Not every contract follows identical stages; retain the issuer's terminology and legal/economic scope rather than forcing a universal funnel.

For an AI-infrastructure hypothesis, the relevant industrial question is not simply whether AI demand rises. It is whether financed projects progress into orders and profitable deliveries for a particular business, at a particular content level and time. Identify which vendor benefits at each stage. Avoid adding a component's value to the integrated equipment containing it and calling the sum an addressable market.

For an automation-recovery hypothesis, distinguish the customer's production, distributor inventory, replenishment orders, and underlying installed equipment needs. For a recurring-service hypothesis, test installed activity, retention, realized price, density, cost-to-serve, and required upkeep. These are different causal questions even when they involve the same industrial site.

### 7.2 Profit capture

For each hypothesized beneficiary, ask whether contract terms permit price changes, whether costs rise first, whether capacity can actually deliver, whether substitutes reduce content, and whether the customer captures the savings. Qualified scarcity is only one possible source of economic power. Reliability, workflow integration, route density, service response, and customer switching costs are other mechanisms to verify, not generic labels to award.

Supply easing can help downstream volume while hurting upstream scarcity pricing. Conversely, a shortage can raise a supplier's opportunity but prevent it from shipping. Each proposed dependency therefore needs direction, affected business, time horizon, contractual channel, substitute, and evidence. There is no universal positive shortage coefficient.

### 7.3 Cash and capital

Reconcile earnings with cash from operations and then the relevant capital burden. Customer advances are financing for obligations, not costless recurring profit. Reduced inventory can be healthy efficiency or temporary liquidation. Reduced capital expenditure can be the end of a build program or underinvestment. A larger fleet can generate more revenue without improving unit economics. Separate those explanations through balance-sheet and operating evidence.

Use multi-period comparisons when seasonal timing is material. Record definitions of reported free cash flow, acquisitions, equipment-sale proceeds, leases, and financial receivables. Retain GAAP/IFRS measures alongside reconciled analytical measures rather than replacing inconvenient numbers with adjustments.

### 7.4 Expectations and rerating

Before using words such as underappreciated, expensive, cheap, or surprise, require a dated reference. A useful dossier should state: the currently observed economic fact; management's current guidance; the external forecast source and vintage if licensed; the house scenario; and the valuation assumptions. Missing consensus remains missing, not replaced by management commentary or an LLM summary.

Estimate-revision breadth, duration, dispersion, and forecast accuracy are separate candidates for study. Current price strength is not evidence that the exact industrial mechanism is priced correctly; weakness is not evidence of neglect. Decompose peer/factor effects and maintain uncertainty about causality.

| Relation | Business-specific mechanism, direction and horizon; no automatic security/trade linkage |
| Correction | Exact superseded record, reason, effective period, preserved historical version |

### Examples of non-comparable measurements

A twelve-month rolling order average is not a quarter's orders. A stock of backlog is not a flow of revenue. A manufacturer order book is not automatically comparable to an EPC contractor's estimated MSA backlog. Book-to-bill is not a delivery lead time. Factory capacity is not qualified output. Announced spending is not a funded award. An ARR measure is not the entire software-and-control segment. A guidance change is not an actual result. Reported acquisition growth is not organic share gain. A geographic sales disclosure is not a factory location. A fuel surcharge is not equivalent to underlying freight yield. A share of revenue is not a valuation weight.

Missing denominators remain unknown. No percent exposure should be invented from a catalog mention, partnership, or customer logo. Numerical ranges should not imply precision unsupported by the source. Confidence is multidimensional: strong proof that a statement was made can coexist with weak proof that its forecast will happen.

## 10. Historical validation plan before predictive claims

The later validation wave must test the economics-first design against simpler baselines, not merely illustrate it with winners. Freeze the eligible universe and source availability as of each historical date, including delisted, acquired, and failed companies where available. Keep original publication timestamps, vintage estimates, corporate actions, and revisable macro releases.

Measure whether subtheme evidence adds explanatory or predictive value beyond price momentum, broad industry exposure, size, quality, rates, and the market. Distinguish contemporaneous explanation, forecast improvement, and a deployable investment rule. A successful narrative after the event does not demonstrate a signal.

Use separate training/development and genuinely held-out periods or cohorts; preserve failed hypotheses; avoid choosing horizons after seeing winners. Test level versus change, earnings growth versus estimate revisions, and price return versus total return. Predeclare horizon families such as event reactions, several-month estimate revisions, and multi-year business-quality changes, then study them separately. These are proposed evaluation choices, not validated timings.

Relevant error measures include forecast calibration and absolute error, false-positive thesis transitions, missed reversals, stale evidence, incorrect business mapping, and robustness to source revisions. Trading performance, capacity, implementation costs, and sizing belong to their accepted downstream validation owners and are not authorized here.

Current gaps: no licensed point-in-time consensus panel has been recovered; no matched current valuation panel has been built; no empirical return attribution or backtest has been run. No company is ranked for attractiveness by this wave.

The reusable template should support different economic modules rather than require every theme to fill a bottleneck/BOM dashboard. The proposed Industrials dossier begins with a readable thesis mechanism, then shows business-level exposure, changes in operational evidence, earnings/cash implications, expectations/valuation scenarios when qualified, and falsifiers.

A reader should be able to open an industrial slice, identify the economic driver, compare a small set of correctly scoped businesses, inspect the source and period, and return to a company page. The view must say when valuation or company identity is unavailable rather than presenting a fully composed-looking answer.

Candidate visual modules are an earnings-to-cash bridge, an order-to-delivery view, a business-by-end-market matrix, a cycle comparison panel, an expectations-versus-house-scenario table, and a conditional physical dependency map. These are proposed designs, not generated UI, accepted score changes, or browser proof. A graph is optional; an accurate, accessible table and concise synthesis can be more useful.

All modules should consume existing owners and preserve legacy timing/entry/basket outputs. Real-source admission and current detailed research publication require the existing approved private owner and authenticated transport. The shared template's current code custody, source rights, and interface maturity must be refreshed before implementation. No parallel publisher or public full-fidelity payload is proposed.
