# Skew methodology-parity — 2026-09-23

This note says what the parity receipt measured. It does not change the skew formula, and it does not choose what to do next. The companion receipt is `research/MARKET_ONTOLOGY_F03_SKEW_PARITY_RECEIPT_2026-09-23.md`. The run used the full legacy ledger (12,375 polygon_gex rows) and the local ThetaData store. It was not a sample. Both sides use the same formula: the implied volatility of the 25-delta out-of-the-money put minus the implied volatility of the 50-delta at-the-money call, at the expiry nearest 30 days.

## What the numbers say

Of the 12,375 legacy keys, 3,965 can be priced from the store and 8,410 cannot. The six classes add up to 12,375: 2,875 weekend dates, 124 roots with no store directory, 5,411 dates the root's file does not contain, 0 keys with no usable tenor, 0 other failures, and 3,965 compared. The 2,875 weekend rows match the earlier overlap count. The other 5,535 weekday keys that the overlap audit called "no chain" split into 124 missing roots and 5,411 missing dates. No stored chain failed because the expiry was unusable, and no recompute raised an error.

On the 3,965 keys both sides price, the sign agrees on 2,392 and flips on 1,563. Ten keys have a zero on at least one side. Those three buckets add up to 3,965. Sign agreement, defined as the 2,392 same-sign keys divided by 3,965, is 60.3 percent. The absolute gap has a p50 of 0.0379, a p90 of 0.167, and a maximum of 3.9529. Those are the same headline figures as the overlap receipt. The largest leftover between the stored skew gap and the put-minus-call identity, on any one key, is 0.0002, which is ledger rounding.

The absolute gap is not one broken leg of the formula. Each compared key is assigned to one explanation: a tenor mismatch of more than 5 days first, otherwise a spot mismatch of more than 2 percent, otherwise whichever implied-volatility leg moved more. On that rule, spot accounts for 46.4 percent of the absolute gap (1,679 keys), the put leg 29.1 percent (1,052 keys), the call leg 24.3 percent (1,230 keys), and tenor 0.2 percent (4 keys). The spot flag is on 1,680 keys and the tenor flag is on 4 keys. One key has both flags and is counted under tenor. Separately, the raw size of the two implied-volatility moves is 53.8 percent put and 46.2 percent call. Both legs move. The engine does not return the strike it picked, so this receipt cannot say which strike produced the extreme prints.

The gap is small on the four index ETFs and large in the tail of everything else. SPY, QQQ, IWM, and DIA together are 104 compared keys: 91 match, 13 flip, none zero, sign agreement 87.5 percent, absolute-gap p50 0.0107 and maximum 0.0959. The other 3,861 compared keys are 2,301 match, 1,550 flip, and 10 zero, sign agreement 59.6 percent, p50 0.0393 and maximum 3.9529. That second bucket is not a pure single-stock sample. Any underlying that is not one of those four ETFs is in it, including sector funds. More strikes and more open interest shrink the typical gap and do not fix the sign. The lowest legacy strike-count quartile (N=1,000) has p50 0.0552 and holds the maximum of 3.9529. The highest (N=985) has p50 0.0269 and maximum 0.973. Sign agreement is 63.2 percent in the lowest strike quartile (632 of 1,000) and 61.0 percent in the highest (601 of 985). The lowest stored-open-interest quartile (N=991) agrees 64.9 percent (643 match) and holds the same maximum. The highest (N=991) agrees 57.8 percent (573 match) with p50 0.0301. One compared key had no stored open interest. Its absolute gap is 0.319 and its sign flips. Volume splits the same way: the lowest bin (N=992) agrees 64.9 percent, and the highest (N=991) agrees 58.6 percent. The three largest gaps are MQ on 2026-06-30 (absolute gap 3.9529, spot 4.18 versus 4.06, so the spot flag is on), MQ on 2026-06-25 (3.5095, spots 3.97 versus 3.95, spot flag off, put implied volatility 0.46 versus 3.99), and MQ on 2026-06-22 (3.2061, spots 3.78 versus 3.76, spot flag off, put implied volatility 0.41 versus 3.64). Tenor matches on all twenty of the worst keys. The tail is a level break in the put implied volatility on a few low-priced names, not an expiry mismatch.

## Ruling proposal

The lane does not pick. The seat has three options.

**(i) Keep the display, and add one source-break sentence on the site card.** For: the sign flips on 1,563 of 3,965 compared keys (39.4 percent), so a reader who treats the ledger as one series will often see the direction change when the source changes. The four index ETFs are the calmer case (13 flips out of 104, absolute-gap p50 0.0107), and a single sentence can say the history is two sources without pretending those names moved. Against: a sentence does not repair the series. Spot differs by more than 2 percent on 1,680 of the 3,965 compared keys, and that group carries 46.4 percent of the absolute gap. The card would still plot a mixed history. The typical gap (p50 0.0379) is much smaller than the maximum (3.9529), so one sentence has to cover both the index names and the MQ tail or it will mislead one of them.

**(ii) Backfill the ledger's polygon_gex rows with a ThetaData recompute on every key the store can price, so the covered history is one source.** The 3,965 compared keys can be backfilled. They are the keys where the store has the root and the date and the same formula returns a skew. The other 8,410 keys cannot be backfilled, and the reason is specific. 2,875 are weekend dates. The store does not carry a weekend chain, and recomputing a nearby weekday would be a different key, not a fill of the weekend row. 124 keys name a root that has no end-of-day directory in the store. 5,411 keys name a root the store has, but that calendar day is not in the root's 2026 file. Zero keys failed because the stored chain had no live expiry, and zero failed for any other reason. For: those 3,965 rows would then be one source, and the identity leftover of 0.0002 shows the recompute is the same formula, not a new definition. Against: the ledger would still be mixed unless the 8,410 rows are dropped or left marked. Dropping them deletes the weekend history and 5,411 weekday keys. Keeping them leaves two sources in one file. The backfill also changes history on purpose: 1,563 compared keys would flip sign, and 1,680 would move to a spot more than 2 percent away. Index history would move less (13 flips out of 104) than the rest (1,550 flips out of 3,861).

**(iii) Change the construction, for example an open-interest-weighted strike or a fixed moneyness rule.** For: both implied-volatility legs move (53.8 percent of the absolute leg movement is the put, 46.2 percent is the call), so the gap is not a single broken side of the subtraction. The worst prints are extreme put levels on low-priced names, and a different strike rule might avoid a bad contract if the current rule is landing on one. The maximum gap of 3.9529 sits in the lowest strike-count quartile (N=1,000) and the lowest open-interest quartile (N=991), which is where a fragile strike pick would show up. Against: tenor is not the cause. Only 4 of 3,965 compared keys differ by more than 5 days, and that is 0.2 percent of the absolute gap. Every one of the twenty largest gaps has the same tenor on both sides. Spot, which a strike rule does not choose, already accounts for 46.4 percent of the absolute gap. Sign agreement does not get better as the chain gets larger: the highest open-interest quartile agrees 57.8 percent (573 of 991), which is worse than the lowest quartile at 64.9 percent (643 of 991). Two of the three largest gaps, MQ on 2026-06-25 and MQ on 2026-06-22, are not spot mismatches and are not tenor mismatches. Their ThetaData put implied volatility is several times the legacy print (3.99 versus 0.46, and 3.64 versus 0.41). The engine does not return the strike, so this audit cannot tell a bad 25-delta contract from a bad implied-volatility field. Changing the construction on that evidence would rewrite the definition without showing that the new rule removes the tail.

Skew stays display-tier. Nothing in this audit promotes it to a scored signal. The lane takes no decision.
