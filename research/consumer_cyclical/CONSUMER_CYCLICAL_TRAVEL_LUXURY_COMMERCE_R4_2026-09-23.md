R3 separated demand recovery from inventory normalization, capacity/cost repair and financing effects. ## 2. Travel needs a consumption cohort, not just a quarterly sales comparison

**Research proposal.** Model each permitted observation against booking time, intended service time, cancellation or modification time, consumed service time, payment time and settlement time. A booking is an intention under contractual terms; consumption is a different event. A cancellation and replacement reservation should not become two independent completed trips. Room nights, guests, bookings, cruise days and available capacity require distinct units.

For an eventual cohort analysis, let `B[b,s,x]` be reservations made in booking period `b` for service period `s`, within a source-defined population `x`. At an explicit information cutoff, record what remains booked, what has canceled, what has been modified and what has actually been consumed. A projection of eventual consumption requires a separately estimated completion process. It must not silently use the final cancellation outcome when replaying an earlier date.

The same-quarter ratio of recognized revenue to newly booked gross value is therefore not automatically a contractual commission rate. It can change when the booking window lengthens, when an older cohort travels, when travel products change, or when the accounting/payment model changes. A matched estimate would align the gross value and the retained revenue of the same service cohort, under the same currency, tax, cancellation and incentive definitions. Those joined data have not been acquired in this tranche.

Booking's annual filing distinguishes reservation-time gross bookings from revenue generally recognized at check-in. Its merchant revenue descriptions include net commissions or transaction amounts, payment-related fees and relevant incentive effects. This is not proof that the platform owns the full physical inventory or recognizes the full hotel bill as its revenue. [R4-S06]

A simple hypothetical illustrates the danger. Suppose consumed gross travel is 100 and retained revenue is 15 in both periods, but newly booked future travel rises from 100 to 120. Dividing current retained revenue by new bookings gives 15% and 12.5%, although the stipulated cohort monetization is unchanged. This does not prove that any observed company ratio is explained entirely by timing; it demonstrates the missing identification condition.

The proposed early-demand question is consequently more specific than “bookings accelerated”: did comparable forward cohorts improve at similar lead times, did cancellations remain controlled, and will those cohorts produce contribution after fulfillment? A longer booking window alone is neither a forecast of stronger final travel nor evidence that customers have become more affluent.

## 3. Four economic roles along the travel chain

| Research role | Principal economic question | Key alternative explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Physical capacity operator | How much contribution is earned from each available unit after delivery costs and capital requirements? | Added capacity, product mix or higher costs can separate sales from returns. |
| Property owner | What remains after property operations, maintenance, financing and contractual payments? | A strong hotel EBITDA observation can coexist with a large reinvestment requirement. |
| Brand, manager or franchisor | Which contractual fees grow, and can owners sustain the system that generates them? | Conversions, card agreements and reimbursed costs can grow without matching same-property room demand. |
| Booking/distribution/payment business | What retained contribution follows from completed demand after acquisition, servicing, payment and refund costs? | Booking timing, payment migration and working capital can change ratios without the assumed demand improvement. |

These are proposed views over existing owners, not new canonical sectors or a universal merchant classification. An issuer can span several roles. An agency reservation platform, an inventory-owning merchant, and a payment facilitator must not be equated solely because each uses the word “merchant.”

For a hypothetical trip, the hotel bill can be reported in an owner's revenue, contribute to the brand's system activity, appear in the platform's gross bookings, and generate a separate payment fee. Summing those amounts would overstate the consumer's expenditure. Similarly, three companies discussing the same underlying travel cycle do not provide three statistically independent demand observations. Actual relationship edges still need source evidence; the illustration does not establish a specific Marriott–Host–Booking contract.

A productive research map should expose both beneficiaries and constraints. More fulfilled stays could support operator contribution and brand fees, but the effect depends on price, channel costs, labor, rent, financing and fee terms. Additional distribution volume can be valuable even with a lower percentage margin. Higher room prices can raise fee dollars while also reducing conversion for certain travelers. Direction must be conditional on the measured business and the proposed mechanism.

## 4. Capacity: higher activity and higher yield need not mean better unit contribution

Royal Caribbean's June-quarter filing reports revenue of $4,832 million versus $4,538 million and available passenger cruise days of 13,572,396 versus 12,942,385. Adjusted EBITDA was $1,830 million versus $1,851 million; the reported per-capacity figure fell from $143.00 to $134.84. Its Net Yields increased from $283.56 to $288.95. The capacity convention uses double occupancy; reported occupancy of 110.2% is therefore not an impossible physical-utilization observation. [R4-S01]

**Research interpretation.** This is a mixed control: growing revenue and a favorable named yield measure do not establish better contribution per available unit. The source's Net Yields definition is not “profit after every operating cost.” The research consumer should retain the measure, its definition, and the adverse contribution observation rather than decide which one to discard.

The proposed capacity model separates fleet or property additions, same-asset volume, paid occupancy, itinerary or room mix, net realized pricing, ancillary contribution, operating costs and capital intensity. Comparisons should identify unavailable units, scheduled maintenance, cancellations and whether the observed population changed. It is not sufficient to divide company revenue by a headline capacity count from a different period.

A capacity-expansion hypothesis requires a return-on-incremental-capital question. Suppose capacity grows 10% while contribution per unit falls 5%; total contribution grows 4.5% under those assumptions. That may or may not cover the added capital and financing. Neither “positive total growth” nor “negative unit growth” alone decides the investment case.

The proposed early opportunity sequence is: forward comparable demand improves; retained price or contribution improves without excessive support; capacity is added at acceptable economics; and the resulting cash supports the common-equity claim. Falsifiers include discount-dependent occupancy, rising fulfillment costs, maintenance deferral, a surge of competing capacity, or unfavorable financing. No stage has been empirically calibrated here.

## 5. Hotel brands and property owners can improve through different mechanisms

Marriott's August release reports worldwide comparable constant-currency RevPAR growth of 3.4%. Franchise and base management fees rose 14%; the explanation includes co-branded card fees, rooms growth and RevPAR. Conversions represented 40% of first-half openings. The updated annual outlook includes a partial-year effect from newly executed card agreements. Cost reimbursement revenue and reimbursed expense are separately reported and are not exactly equal. [R4-S02]

Marriott's annual filing describes revenue-based base management fees and profit-related incentive fees, including owner-return hurdles in relevant contracts. It also discusses contract investment and financing or guarantee exposure. [R4-S03]

**Proposed inference discipline.** Net brand-room growth is not synonymous with net new physical hotel capacity. A conversion can increase branded distribution while leaving the building already in the market. A signed development pipeline is not a completed income-producing asset. Card-contract economics are not the same as hotel demand, even though both can support parent earnings. An asset-light model can still require capital and depend on financially viable owners.

The research therefore proposes separate fee bridges: existing-system operating activity; added or lost rooms; conversions; contractual rates and other agreements; incentive eligibility; and contract acquisition/amortization costs. Parent fee growth should be reconciled to owner economics where evidence permits. A brand whose operators cannot maintain properties or finance required investment has a different durability question from a purely mathematical fee-growth forecast.

| May 6, 2026 | 2.0%–3.0% | 5,925–5,985 | Primary search-index evidence only; then-negotiated card terms were excluded. |
| August 3, 2026 | 3.0%–3.5% | 6,025–6,055 | Direct release read; includes a partial-year effect of new card terms. |

These are management ranges, not analyst consensus or market-implied expectations. The May page's direct read failed; its exact source and partial-access status remain visible. The fee-midpoint arithmetic is not an attribution model. Better travel assumptions and new contractual economics must not be collapsed into “demand alone raised guidance.” [R4-S02, R4-S04]

Host Hotels supplies a property-owner comparison. Its release reports comparable hotel RevPAR up 7.0%, comparable hotel EBITDA of $497 million versus $461 million and margin of 31.9% versus 31.3%. First-half capital expenditure was $243 million, including $138 million of renewal and replacement. The comparable-property population has its own eligibility and acquisition/disposition adjustments. [R4-S08]

The positive operating evidence should remain positive. It is not erased merely because maintenance exists. But quarter EBITDA must not be reduced by a first-half capital number and labeled quarterly free cash flow. The proposed property view requires matched periods, corporate costs, financing, taxes and actual maintenance/expansion allocations. Host remains a cross-sector exposure reference, not an automatic Consumer Cyclical constituent.

## 6. Platforms: payment migration can improve dollars while lowering a margin percentage

Booking's June-quarter filing reports merchant gross-booking mix of 73% versus 69% and states that payment-facilitation incremental revenue exceeded associated incremental variable expenses over the trailing year. It also says the greater merchant expense burden can reduce operating margins. Deferred merchant bookings of $10.1 billion include estimated supplier amounts and future company revenue, with most amounts subject to cancellation refunds. A 25-for-1 share split became effective April 2, 2026, with retrospective share-data adjustment. [R4-S05]

**Research conclusion.** Percentage margin, retained dollars and capital risk need separate views. A hypothetical business with revenue 100, cost 60 and profit 40 adds a service with revenue 20 and cost 16. Profit rises to 44 while the margin falls from 40% to 36.67%. This arithmetic shows why a lower margin can accompany a beneficial incremental service. It does not establish full economic profitability after every fixed cost or future risk.

The proposed platform model should distinguish repeat demand from acquisition spending; an app-based booking from a booking actually sourced without paid acquisition; fee income from reimbursed supplier value; and payment facilitation from taking inventory risk. Cohort retention is valuable only when measured with its support, servicing, refund and capital costs. Aggregate marketing/revenue ratios are not enough to establish a customer-acquisition advantage.

The share-split observation creates a separate historical requirement: prices, per-share estimates and share counts must use compatible corporate-action bases. An adjusted current filing must not be spliced into an unadjusted historical series. This program does not create an alternative identity or corporate-action registry; it consumes the existing owner.

Expedia's June filing reports first-half operating cash flow of $5,409 million, including a $4,998 million cash-flow contribution from changes in deferred merchant bookings. Its revenue/gross-bookings ratio includes trivago revenue without associated gross bookings. Its reported segments and accounting models are separate views, not interchangeable economic cohorts. [R4-S07]

Subtracting those two cash-flow figures gives $411 million, but **that is only a partial bridge**, not normalized operating cash flow, free cash flow or earnings. Other working-capital, tax, noncash and investment effects remain. A seasonal inflow that belongs partly to future service providers cannot all be treated as distributable owner cash. Conversely, recurring negative working capital can have genuine financing value when it is stable and appropriately risk-managed; the research must estimate its durable portion rather than either capitalize or discard it wholesale.

## 7. An actual historical counterexample: monetization can rise during demand collapse

Expedia's February 11, 2021 release, describing Q4 2020, reported room nights stayed down 61%, lodging revenue down 58% and revenue per room night up 6%. The release attributes the latter partly to product mix and Vrbo's merchant-of-record transition. Annual company-defined free cash flow was negative $4,631 million versus positive $1,607 million in 2019. [R4-S16]

This is a dated historical counterexample, not an estimate of how likely another shock is. It directly challenges the proposed shortcut “higher monetization per unit means demand and cash quality are improving.” A ratio can rise because the population of surviving transactions changes while total activity collapses. The example also cautions against treating a structural accounting/payment change as a stable time-series coefficient.

The evidence is not an investment backtest. No price response, trading rule, out-of-sample result or causal effect has been estimated. The next historical work should include ordinary slowdowns, false dawns and refinancing episodes, not calibrate the entire sector to one extreme year. Companies sharing the same shock should be clustered rather than counted as many independent events.

For production research, a useful event narrative would explicitly distinguish a better unit metric from the loss of total demand. The decision-support product should show the counterevidence near the thesis, not hide it in an appendix. A persuasive story is not a substitute for the population and timing conditions that make the metric meaningful.

## 8. Luxury: destination, price, product mix and constrained supply are different variables

Hermès' H1 press release reports total revenue of €8,163 million, up 1.6% reported and 6.1% at constant currency. Its Japan destination line is down 2.1% reported but up 11.0% at constant currency. The table is explicitly sales by destination. The release also reports an additional leather-goods workshop opening and later planned workshops, not a series of qualified unit output or unused capacity. The geographical table was inspected visually in the official PDF. [R4-S09]

**Research interpretation.** A destination observation cannot establish the nationality or residence of the buyer. Opposite signs under reported and constant currency are not necessarily a data conflict. Neither a workshop opening nor a product sellout proves a measured global shortage or identifies the amount of demand left unsatisfied.

The proposed luxury model separates customer-cohort willingness to pay, full-price repeat behavior, product desirability, distribution discipline, geographic arbitrage and controlled capacity growth. Sales can be constrained by allocation as well as demand. An illustrative relation `fulfilled units = min(available qualified units, willing purchases)` is only a conceptual starting point: substitution, timing, channels and unmet demand are not observed from sales alone.

This matters for rerating hypotheses. Scarcity that protects an experience or brand may support long-duration cash generation, but expansion can weaken perceived exclusivity or change the customer mix. Conversely, cautious expansion can make reported growth understate an unobserved demand opportunity. Neither direction can be inferred from a corporate narrative. The research needs realized full-price sales, product/channel mix, retention, quality, inventory age and the actual economics of added capacity where sources permit.

LVMH's H1 disclosure shows group organic growth of 2%, reported growth of minus 3%, and different business-group trajectories. Fashion & Leather Goods generated €18,146 million of revenue and €6,195 million of recurring operating profit against group totals of €38,644 million and €8,691 million. Its own recurring operating profit declined 7%. “Organic” is a constant-perimeter/currency measure, not a unit-volume or price realization measure. [R4-S10]

The corresponding revenue and recurring-profit fractions are approximately 47.0% and 71.3%. These are different denominators, not a universal purity score. Negative other/elimination contributions must be preserved when reconciling group profit. Brand-level economics remain unknown where the group does not disclose them.

A proposed luxury exposure map should therefore distinguish the actual business group, brand relationship, product role, destination and measured financial basis. It should not assign every subsidiary the parent's margin or infer buyer residence from the location of the sale. A theme can have improving jewelry evidence while a larger earnings contributor remains under pressure; the system should explain concentration rather than vote by brand count.

## 9. Global commerce: lower marketing expense can be a reclassification

Alibaba's June-quarter release describes a revised reporting structure and recast comparisons. Its customer-management revenue declined 7%, versus growth of 1% on a stated like-for-like basis excluding the development-program effect. The company explains that relevant subsidies previously in sales and marketing now reduce revenue; excluding share-based compensation, the marketing/revenue ratio fell from 21.3% to 17.6%. E-commerce adjusted EBITA was RMB39,749 million versus RMB39,988 million, while consolidated adjusted EBITA was RMB27,329 million versus RMB38,844 million. [R4-S11]

**Research conclusion.** A lower recorded marketing ratio is not sufficient evidence of cheaper customer acquisition or a weaker subsidy burden. The relevant economic support must be tracked once wherever it is recorded. Changes in cloud, other investments or corporate costs should not automatically become evidence about household commerce demand. A new segment definition also requires a vintage, not a silent backfill of newly reported distinctions into earlier information sets.

A hypothetical isolates the accounting issue. Revenue 100, other costs 60 and promotional expense 10 produce operating profit 30. Moving the same support into contra-revenue gives revenue 90, other costs 60 and promotional expense zero: profit remains 30. The marketing/revenue ratio improves from 10% to zero while economic support is unchanged. A model that adds back the revenue reduction and also removes the old expense would count the same adjustment twice.

The proposed commerce analysis should follow gross paid demand, fulfilled orders, returns, customer and seller incentives, advertising, logistics, payment and service costs, and retained contribution. Growth purchased with subsidies can be rational investment if later retention and contribution justify it; it should be tested rather than automatically celebrated or rejected. The unit of evaluation should separate new-customer acquisition from repeated transactions by subsidized existing customers.

PDD's release reports RMB57,637 million of online marketing services and other revenue and RMB54,721 million of transaction services, totaling RMB112,358 million. Those reporting categories do not supply a domestic-versus-Temu financial partition in the inspected release. [R4-S12]

An undisclosed geographic or platform allocation must remain unmeasured. Labels such as transaction revenue, advertising revenue and international-commerce exposure cannot be substituted for one another. Nor should transaction revenue be divided by a fabricated GMV denominator to generate a take-rate estimate. The source identifies how revenue is reported; more detailed exposure needs additional lawful evidence.

## 11. Cross-family synthesis: shared grammar, different economic equations

The proposed common grammar is now sufficiently concrete to guide research without pretending that one formula fits every subtheme:

`demand population -> activity/commitment -> fulfillment -> economic right -> retained contribution -> capital and financing -> common-equity cash -> dated expectations -> separately validated market response`

R1–R3 supplied housing, brands, restaurants, autos and durables. R4 adds time-limited capacity, service cohorts, contractual fee layers, luxury demand censoring and commerce incentive accounting. The common grammar is a read/composition requirement over existing owners, not a new graph or universal consumer score.

| Candidate research slice | Mechanism to test | Leading evidence to seek | Disconfirming evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Travel demand becoming cash | Comparable cohorts complete at acceptable retained economics | Forward service-date curves, controlled cancellations, contribution | Longer lead time only, refunds, supplier failure, higher servicing cost |
| Capital-light brand expansion | Distribution and contractual rights grow with healthy operators | Openings versus conversions, owner returns, fee durability | Unsigned pipeline, terminations, unfunded maintenance, costly guarantees |
| Repeat platform demand | Retention reduces fully loaded acquisition burden | Cohort repeat contribution and paid-source mix | App growth without direct sourcing, subsidies, refunds and payment risk |
| Profitable capacity growth | Incremental units earn an adequate return | Unit contribution and matched capital commitments | Occupancy purchased through discounting, cost inflation, idle expansion |
| Controlled luxury expansion | Desirability and qualified supply scale without dilution | Full-price behavior, quality, source-specific output | Inventory aging, geographic arbitrage mistaken for new customers |
| Commerce monetization quality | Fulfilled demand generates sustainable seller and platform economics | Aligned fees, incentives, retention, fulfillment contribution | Expense relocation, seller stress, unsupported GMV or geographic estimates |

These are research slices, not new enrolled canonical themes. Their connection to actual company securities remains governed by existing identity and basket owners. Naming a useful mechanism does not authorize a graph edge, weight, rank or trade.

The research priority is to find cases where a material economic transition can be measured, not simply companies that already fit a compelling story. A strong candidate has a defined exposed business, an observable change, a plausible but falsifiable transmission path, a source-supported denominator and a prospective outcome that would resolve the hypothesis. Missing evidence lowers the strength of the conclusion; it need not remove the company from research discovery.

## 12. Valuation: translate mechanisms into explicit assumptions

For capacity owners, the proposed valuation bridge starts with sustainable contribution after necessary maintenance, taxes and capital requirements, then reconciles financing and common-equity claims. Compare incremental returns with the cost and timing of expansion rather than extrapolate a peak occupancy percentage. The analysis must specify what happens to cash if the next cohort cancels or competitors add supply.

For hotel brands and other contractual businesses, analyze the fee base, duration, renewal/termination terms, operator viability, required contract investment and related capital commitments. Fee growth can deserve a different valuation hypothesis when it becomes more durable, but “asset-light” is not itself a complete cash-flow model. Avoid deducting the same capital cost both through amortization-based earnings adjustments and a cash-flow line without reconciliation.

For platforms, model retained contribution by service/customer cohort, cash settlement and refund exposure, investment in repeat demand and the cost of maintaining distribution. A temporary booking float should not be capitalized as permanent excess cash. A durable working-capital advantage should not be discarded automatically either; test its behavior under both growth and contraction.

For luxury, the critical assumptions include realized pricing, full-price volumes, mix, qualified growth capacity, customer recurrence and the risk that expansion weakens the very scarcity being valued. For diversified commerce, separate cash-generating operating roles from investment-intensive roles and respect shareholder, subsidiary and geographic rights. Segment profit alone is not distributable parent cash.

These are proposed valuation inputs, not completed issuer valuations. No market-price series, current multiple distribution, implied-return estimate or point-in-time analyst consensus was acquired. Management guidance can supply one dated expectation but cannot substitute for what the market expected. The product must distinguish “business improved,” “management increased its outlook,” “valuation requires a stronger outcome,” and “the security responded.”

## 13. Historical and prospective research design before any predictive promotion

The first study should test interpretation, not trading. Select a diverse set of positive, mixed and negative disclosures; freeze their information cutoffs; and ask whether the dossier identifies the correct scope, arithmetic, operating mechanism and strongest contrary evidence. Include the RCL contribution example, the Marriott contract/RevPAR distinction, Booking's margin-dollar distinction, Expedia's cash timing, Hermès' currency/destination case, Alibaba's subsidy reclassification and the 2020 historical counterexample.

A separate predictive study could then test predeclared hypotheses. Examples are whether matched completed-travel and contribution acceleration contains incremental information beyond sector price momentum and earnings revisions, or whether apparent revenue improvement dominated by timing/reclassification has weaker subsequent operating confirmation. Those are questions, not asserted effects. Baselines, horizons, eligible populations and disconfirmation rules must be frozen before observing results.

The study must preserve filing and source publication times, fiscal calendars, corporate actions, changed reporting segments and point-in-time comparable cohorts. Historical data repeated in a later filing is not automatically what the system knew earlier. A new method should be evaluated on more than the examples that motivated it, including missing inputs and outcomes that contradict the narrative.

Cluster correlated events, shared corporate groups and common macro shocks. Include failed or acquired companies when the target universe requires them. Separate source error, interpretation error, horizon mismatch and a genuinely incorrect economic hypothesis. Do not repair a failed forecast by retroactively changing its claimed horizon or relabeling the original signal.

Outcome learning should distinguish cash/earnings confirmation from security returns. A correct business forecast can still be a poor investment at the purchase valuation; a rising stock does not by itself validate the proposed causal explanation. Existing Evaluation and authority owners must decide any promotion. This research grants no rank, gate, entry, sizing or trading authority.

## 14. Product contribution and acceptance examples

The proposed product remains the existing earnings-inflection dossier within the Theme Tracker/detail workflow. It should expose a short useful conclusion, a business-specific contribution explanation, measured exposure, material disagreement and the next observable condition. The deeper source view supports that intelligence rather than replacing it.

An investor should be able to ask why travel beneficiaries disagree and receive a scope-aware answer: the capacity operator has one unit-contribution picture, the franchisor has a contract/fee picture, and the platform has a reservation/fulfillment/cost picture. The system should not silently merge those observations into a single green theme score.

The first acceptance examples should include positive as well as adverse facts. Host's improved comparable contribution must not disappear under generic maintenance warnings. Booking's stated positive incremental variable contribution must not become an unqualified margin-deterioration label. RCL's unit-contribution decline must remain visible alongside rising revenue and named yield. Alibaba's marketing reclassification must not produce a false efficiency claim. Hermès' destination/currency distinction must survive a country drill-down.

A proposed visible workflow is:

`existing theme view -> selected economic slice -> measured company/business exposure -> change in operating economics -> dated expectation and contradiction -> watch condition -> existing company/market workflow`

No new public full-fidelity payload, collector, publisher, identity store, correction plane, relationship authority or automated trade route is introduced. Existing paid transport and source rights remain governing. Shared-template or adjacent-session implementation is not claimed deployed by this research.
