## 5. Seven economic models and their useful outputs

**Purchase and replenishment.** Distinguish end demand from manufacturer shipments and channel rebuilding. Decompose comparable activity, price/mix, openings/acquisitions, returns and currency where disclosed. The useful conclusion is whether demand and recurring retained contribution are improving together; an unexplained sales increase is insufficient.

**Installed-asset replacement.** Organize repair, upgrade, new/used replacement, postponement and retirement around a defined installed population. Financing modifies feasible choices, but an older asset population is not a scheduled sales forecast. This model's output is a conditional replacement mechanism and the observations that could validate it.

**Location and capacity conversion.** Compare activity and contribution on compatible capacity bases, separating mature units, new cohorts, closures, conversions and acquisitions. Connect expansion to the capital required and the time before returns emerge. Total contribution and contribution per available unit may move differently without contradiction.

**Fees and franchise economics.** Trace system spending to royalties, rent, service fees and other parent income. Retain the operator's labor, capex and financing burden. A stronger parent fee stream supported by weak operators may not be durable; successful operator growth is not automatically attributable parent cash.

**Access and finite services.** Trace acquisition, entitlement, delivered service, customer outcome, renewal or successful completion. Visits can add costs without new admission revenue, or add profitable ancillary activity. Finishing a finite educational service must not be classified as unwanted churn simply because subscription duration falls.

**Platform intermediation.** Separate gross activity, recognized net revenue, take economics, payment/fulfillment obligations, acquisition incentives and repeat contribution. A lower percentage margin can accompany higher profit dollars; a reclassified subsidy can lower a recorded expense ratio without improving economics.

**Financial claims.** Connect operating income/cash to common shareholders after debt, leases, minority interests, financing businesses and shares. Keep issuer-defined adjusted measures separate from GAAP and from house sensitivity analysis. Growth, capital allocation and financing are different explanations even when all change per-share outcomes.

These are domain interpretation models, not seven new software services. Any implemented calculation belongs in its existing approved financial or F04 composition owner. Research identifies what to calculate and why; provenance supports but does not replace the explanation.

## 6. Demand, rerating and valuation across those models

The sector research should expose simultaneous dimensions rather than force them into one state:
1. Household/customer capacity and willingness to spend.
2. Category or replacement cycle.
3. Competitive position and business-model economics.
4. Recurring contribution, funding and reinvestment.
5. Dated management/market expectations.
6. Valuation assumptions and separately governed market confirmation.

These are proposed research dimensions, not newly calibrated scores. Different income, financing, geography and customer cohorts can behave differently; a national aggregate cannot be allocated to a company's customers without evidence.

Candidate rerating mechanisms include profitable conversion before trailing earnings recover; inventory and cost normalization; improved full-price/repeat purchasing; better returns on new units; more durable contractual fees; reduced funding risk; and upward revision to sustainable cash. Every hypothesis names the target business, transmission variable, lag, offsets and falsifier.

For example, an affordability improvement is not automatically a builder profit improvement if the builder pays for the offer. A brand shipment recovery is not full-price consumer absorption. More recurring access revenue does not prove healthier engagement or customer outcomes. These are questions preserved from R1–R9, not new claims about today's economy.

Valuation must charge growth for required investment. R9's stationary illustration, M=(1-g/R)/(r-g), is a transparent assumption model, not an issuer fair value. Actual analysis needs finite competitive duration, changing incremental returns, capital/working-capital and financing. Trailing P/E, enterprise multiples, yield and reverse valuation answer different questions and require consistent equity/enterprise claims.

Separate the original plan, current expectations and actual results. R9's DHI, Target and lululemon histories are exposed development cases. Annual return endpoints cannot be earned using later annual releases; dividend-reinvested wealth indices cannot serve as raw stock prices for P/E decomposition. No historical example becomes calibrated alpha.

Market confirmation consumes current eligible price, leadership, participation, entry and outcome objects from their owners. An economic explanation can be improving while entry remains poor. No model summary, sentiment, or graph path acquires rank, size, gate, originate or entry authority.
