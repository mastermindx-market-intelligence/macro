## 1. The new economic distinction

R1–R4 established company exposure, growth decomposition, financing and the conversion of purchases into cash. R5 closes an important gap: recurring access and finite services do not have the same customer economics. A billed gym account, a park admission entitlement, an actual visit, a course registration and a completed qualification are different economic objects. Repeated billing is not proof of repeated use, and repeated use is not automatically incremental revenue. A successful customer exit can be the intended outcome of education rather than commercial failure.

The proposed analytical chain is **eligible population -> acquired customer -> contractual entitlement -> delivered service -> customer outcome -> renewal or successful exit -> retained cash after service and capital costs**. This is an economic view composed from existing owners, not a new lifecycle authority.

The job is to identify where a business creates a valuable repeat relationship, can serve it economically and can reinvest at acceptable returns. Merely detecting that revenue is recurring would not do that. Nor would it distinguish an early inflection from a change in billing, customer mix or the footprint being compared.

Five companies provide the new primary-source comparisons: Planet Fitness, Life Time, Vail Resorts, Six Flags and Strategic Education. They are research cases, not a basket. Their customers, contract terms, geography, capacity and fiscal calendars differ. No causal comparison of price tiers or customer migration is inferred from differences in their results.

## 2. Planet Fitness — recurring fees, advertising throughput and equipment

### Evidence

The August 6 release reports Q2 revenue of $365.2 million versus $340.9 million. Franchise revenue increased $16.1 million; $10.1 million was added advertising-fund revenue with an equal $10.1 million expense increase. Same-club sales rose 1.7%; that metric uses monthly dues billed at eligible clubs, not visits or every revenue stream. Equipment revenue increased 4.1% while segment adjusted EBITDA declined 8.0%. [R5-S01]

The 2025 annual filing distinguishes royalties, owned clubs and equipment. It reports approximately $5.3 billion of system sales, but only $1.324 billion of consolidated revenue. Its corporate-club unit economics and illustrative royalty adjustment are not a disclosed census of actual franchisee returns. [R5-S02]

### Interpretation and investment research

About 41.6% of the reported $24.3 million revenue increase is represented by the $10.1 million advertising-fund increase. That is an arithmetic attribution to one disclosed bridge item, not an estimate of organic revenue or underlying demand. The equal incremental expense means the item does not contribute that same amount to the disclosed profit bridge. Advertising could still create future value; the data do not establish the return on that spending.

The proposed research needs three connected but separate businesses. The franchise parent earns contractual royalties and fees. The operator collects dues and pays staff, rent, advertising, equipment and reinvestment costs. The equipment seller may benefit from openings or refreshes while the operator bears the capital burden. Do not count a refresh requirement as independent consumer demand.

The useful re-rating hypothesis is that better customer acquisition, renewal and operator returns can support sustained network expansion and durable parent fees. A nominal dues increase alone is not enough. A network growing through weak operators can impair later openings, refurbishment and royalty collections even while near-term parent fees rise.

Evidence to test next: actual contract-version economics, payment failures, membership transitions, operator cash after required reinvestment, closures/transfers and new-club maturation. Do not copy a royalty-adjusted corporate margin onto all franchisees. No member-level retention data or franchisee cash census was acquired.

## 3. Life Time — value per membership, physical capacity and property finance

### Evidence

The July 30 release reports Q2 revenue growth of 13.7%, period-end center-membership growth of 1.2%, revenue per average center membership of $993 versus $888, and adjusted EBITDA margin of 28.5% versus 27.7%. Management identifies customer-mix changes, including fewer low-dues qualified memberships. The revenue-per-membership denominator is a defined period average, not the quarter-end account count; its numerator also differs from total corporate revenue. [R5-S03]

The annual filing does not equate individual people with memberships: junior members can belong within an existing membership. It says new centers have on average taken three to four years to reach expected performance. Comparable-center eligibility after roughly a year therefore cannot be treated as full maturity. [R5-S05]

The June filing's six-month cash bridge is $408.351 million operating cash, less $523.276 million capital expenditure net of construction reimbursements, plus $200.191 million sale-leaseback proceeds, producing the company's $85.266 million free-cash-flow measure. New lease obligations accompany the property transactions. [R5-S04]

### Interpretation and investment research

This is a positive operating comparison, not merely a warning case: the disclosed adjusted margin improved. Its causes and durability remain separate research questions. Price, mix, ancillary purchases, new centers and cohort behavior cannot be recovered by multiplying two differently scoped headline growth rates.

The period cash difference before sale-leasebacks is negative $114.925 million. Adding the reported property proceeds reproduces the company's measure. Neither number should silently replace the other. The former includes growth investment and does not by itself establish weak mature-center economics. The latter includes monetization of property and is not proof that operations alone funded expansion. The useful analysis reconciles both, with future rent, maintenance, modernization and financing shown explicitly.

The re-rating hypothesis is **capacity-constrained premium service with improving contribution and productive expansion**, not simply more members. If experience quality depends on capacity per customer, a business might deliberately reduce low-contribution access while growing revenue. That can be economically rational, but the result must be tested against later renewals, experience quality, price elasticity and site returns.

A steady-state EBITDA yield on opening cost would omit ramp losses, maintenance, taxes and lease structure. The proposed site-cohort view follows opening date, invested capital, cumulative cash, transfers/closures, maturation and remaining obligations. New-center counts alone cannot prove successful replication.

No matched customer-survival curves, capacity utilization by time of day or site-cohort cash histories were acquired. Management's explanation is not an independently estimated causal effect.

## 4. Vail — selling an entitlement is not delivering a visit

### Evidence

The June 8 report says North American pass sales for the 2026/2027 season through May 26 were approximately 10% lower in units, 8% lower in its days-sold measure and 5% lower in sales dollars. The dollars include sales/admissions taxes. Days sold assigns eight days to unlimited passes and actual purchased access days to frequency products; it is not observed visitation. The company separately describes geographic and product differences and uncertainty. [R5-S06]

The September 4 notice schedules full-year results for September 28. That publication is beyond this research cutoff and is not treated as available evidence. A website link to an upcoming webcast does not establish that the result has occurred. [R5-S07]

### Interpretation and investment research

The approximate ratio 0.95/0.90 implies a 5.56% increase in tax-inclusive sales dollars per sold unit for this matched sales-window comparison. It does not identify pure pricing, net revenue, usage or per-customer profitability. Product mix can change the entitlement sold; the assigned eight-day quantity is an estimate in a reporting convention, not a fixed delivery obligation or forecast of actual visits.

The proposed opportunity hypothesis is a **renewal and conversion inflection before the service season**, with a matched sales cutoff, comparable product scope and later fulfillment evidence. A better early sales window can reflect timing shifts rather than incremental season demand. A weak window can recover later, but that is an unproven possibility, not a forecast.

Prepayment reallocates risk. It may improve near-term revenue visibility, while the operator still bears delivery costs, ancillary-revenue exposure, refunds or credits under the actual contract and future customer renewal risk. Poor conditions can reduce usage without proportionately reducing already committed fees; stronger visitation can raise service costs without equal incremental access revenue. Neither means that delivering a poor customer experience creates durable value.

Needed evidence: entitlement versions, comparable sale windows, product/region cohorts, refunds and credits, realized operating availability, actual visitation and ancillary contribution. Weather-sensitive comparisons require defined operating days and geography, not an after-the-fact universal weather excuse. A causal weather adjustment and pass-to-renewal model remain research tasks, not implemented features.

## 5. Six Flags — scope changes and repeated-use economics

### Evidence

The August 6 release compares Q2 reported revenue of $864.919 million with $930.390 million, but its same-park figures are $864.461 million versus $844.236 million. The retained-portfolio comparison shows revenue growth of 2.4% and adjusted EBITDA growth of about 7%, while per-visit spending declines modestly. Same-park reporting excludes specified sold and closed parks. [R5-S08]

The filing describes initial membership terms and multi-use products recognized using estimated uses; those estimates can be revised during the season. Memberships beyond the initial 12 months are recognized straight-line. The recurring label therefore does not imply one recognition rule across all tenures. [R5-S09]

### Interpretation and investment research

The correct conclusion is neither that all growth vanished nor that the whole historical estate recovered. There is evidence of improved operating performance in the retained portfolio. Its relationship to the common shareholder still requires disposition proceeds, lost future earnings, remaining debt, obligations and capital allocation.

The same-park cash/earnings comparison must retain its membership set and as-of date. It cannot be projected backward as the original investable portfolio without disclosure. Conversely, an aggregate decline caused partly by a smaller estate cannot be treated as a clean decline in every remaining park's demand.

Repeated pass use creates a second denominator problem. More visits from the same sold passes can lower recognized admission revenue per visit without a new reduction in the price customers paid. If the extra visit produces ancillary contribution greater than variable delivery cost and does not displace a more profitable visit, it may increase total contribution. If capacity becomes crowded or costs exceed attachment economics, it may not.

For a hypothetical pass cohort, let incremental contribution per extra visit equal ancillary margin less service cost and any displacement cost. A fall in admission revenue per visit alone does not determine the sign. Recognition-estimate changes also need separation from a new payment or a genuine demand revision.

The research hypothesis is **profitable repeat engagement on a defined retained estate**. Required falsifiers include rising service costs, deterioration in customer experience, renewal weakness, expensive benefit expansion and capital needs inconsistent with reported earnings. Per-visit spending should never be the sole success objective.

## 6. Strategic Education — access, active learning and successful completion

### Evidence

The July 29 release reports 81 Workforce Edge corporate agreements whose employers collectively have approximately 4.020 million employees. It separately reports Sophia average-subscriber growth of about 32% and revenue growth of 26.7%. US higher-education enrollment was down 0.5% while segment revenue increased 2.3%. These are different populations and business models. [R5-S10]

The quarterly filing says an enrollment attributed to an employer relationship remains attributed until graduation or withdrawal even if employment changes or the partnership expires. It also describes a funding-per-credit measure reported one quarter in arrears. Neither label can be silently interpreted as a current payer contract or current-period funding observation. [R5-S11]

The annual filing describes both course/term pricing and a fixed 12-week FlexPath billing period. Tuition is recognized over instruction and net of specified refunds/discounts. It also identifies restricted advance tuition in Australia/New Zealand. [R5-S12]

### Interpretation and investment research

Employer reach is an addressable population, not enrollment, paid utilization, current subsidy or acquired customers. The conversion funnel should distinguish agreement coverage, eligibility, actual starts, instruction, continuing participation, completion and subsequent outcomes. A school and its distribution/benefit service can touch the same learner; adding their labeled reach or enrollment without scope reconciliation would manufacture demand.

Using the rounded Sophia growth rates, revenue per average subscriber changes by approximately 1.267/1.32 - 1 = negative 4.02%. This is an approximate aggregate yield calculation, not evidence that list prices fell or cohort value deteriorated. Timing, plan mix and tenure need examination. A finite learning job also makes endless retention the wrong default objective.

An education business can legitimately lose active customers through successful completion. A smaller enrolled base could reflect weaker recruitment, withdrawals, faster completion, transfer or combinations. Without flows, there is no single interpretation. The proposed economics should consider delivered value, collectible net tuition, instructional/support cost, acquisition cost and the ability to attract the next suitable cohort. Faster completion can reduce tuition duration while strengthening the service's value proposition; the net business effect needs evidence.

The scope warning extends to external outcomes. The official IPEDS search text describes graduation-rate cohorts as full-time, first-time undergraduate entrants. That population is not automatically representative of all adult, transfer, part-time or subscription-course learners. The direct documentation page failed to open in this session; only its indexed official description was inspected. No institution-level outcomes corpus or linkage was acquired. [R5-S13]

The re-rating hypothesis is **affordable, effective completion with repeat distribution and sustainable contribution**, not maximal billed duration. Needed evidence includes cohort starts and exits by reason, discount/payer mix, cash realization, outcomes measured on the correct population and the cost of serving each programme. Company statements about educational quality are not substitutes for verified outcomes.

## 7. A common cohort grammar with different economic meanings

The following is proposed research logic, not a production schema or a new identity system.

**Population.** Separate people, accounts, memberships, locations, entitlements, visits, registrations and completed services. Store the company's definition and its validity interval. Where joint counts are unavailable, avoid deriving them from marginal percentages.

**Transitions.** A stock reconciliation can use opening population plus additions, reactivations and acquired transfers, less withdrawals, successful completions, closures/disposals and other exits. Every residual remains visible. Repeated visits are events, not additions to the customer population. On-hold accounts are neither automatically lost customers nor active service users.

**Time.** Preserve purchase, entitlement, billing, service, recognition, cash, disclosure and correction times. Calendar-aligned fiscal periods are insufficient when selling windows, school terms or operating seasons differ. A changed estimate about prior service is not a new sale.

**Economics.** Follow the contractual receiver of each payment and the provider of each service. Revenue allocated to an advertising fund is not a second royalty. An equipment sale can transfer capital burden inside a network. A property transaction can supply expansion cash while creating lease obligations. An employer relationship can remain a reporting attribution after the live contract ends.

**Outcome.** Define success before measuring retention. A useful gym relationship may recur indefinitely; a qualification is intended to be completed. Neither billing inertia nor forced duration is evidence of a superior product. The system should surface customer-outcome evidence as part of durability, not silently reward non-use or delayed completion.

A practical proposed contribution identity is:

`cohort contribution = retained access/instruction revenue + ancillary contribution - service/support costs - acquisition costs - required reinvestment`

The allocation boundary matters. Costs assigned to a site, central platform or parent need not be included twice. A customer-level metric must not be reported if only company-level data exists.

A lifetime-value scenario can sum probability-weighted future contribution less acquisition and capital required. It must expose retention/completion assumptions, discounting and allocation uncertainty. No customer lifetime value is estimated for these companies in this report; no indefinite survival assumption is fitted to a single quarter.

## 8. Re-rating mechanisms and early evidence to test

### A. Existing-customer value improves without economic damage

Potential leading observations include a properly scoped change in renewals, collectible dues, product attachment or engagement. Confirmation requires contribution after the related service cost and evidence that future retention is not being traded away. Price increases, customer mix and a narrower eligibility population remain competing explanations.

### B. A repeatable unit model earns returns during expansion

Openings create value only when cumulative cash and defensible later contribution justify the capital and ramp. Compare like formats, opening cohorts and lease structures. A location entering the comparable base does not become mature by definition. Weak cohorts and closed locations must remain in the historical denominator.

### C. Pre-commitment improves risk-adjusted cash visibility

Advance pass or tuition commitments can improve visibility, but their economic value depends on cancellation, refund, fulfillment and renewal obligations. Growing cash collected is neither automatically profit nor automatically poor quality. The correct question is which obligations remain and how they respond to service or demand shocks.

### D. Better delivery increases contribution, not just activity

Extra use can increase attachment and satisfaction while adding cost. Attribution requires matched service population and capacity. The model should test the sign and persistence of incremental contribution, not apply a blanket engagement bonus.

### E. Distribution creates effective acquisition rather than reported reach

Employer networks, franchise systems and platform distribution can reduce acquisition friction. They can also create an impressive addressable count with little actual conversion. Test starts, outcomes, contribution and contract renewals. A reporting attribution that survives a contract is not a current revenue guarantee.

### F. Financial risk or capital intensity genuinely falls

A sale-leaseback, debt extension or portfolio disposal changes the mix of assets and obligations. Analyze the whole claim and future rent/service burden. A reduction in reported debt without accounting for new obligations does not establish lower economic risk. Cost of capital and valuation expansion remain hypotheses requiring dated market evidence.

These mechanisms are condition-dependent and can coexist. No common consumer score or stock-ranking authority is proposed. Research salience, expected business outcomes, valuation and entry quality remain separate.

## 9. Proposed falsification and learning

The structured companion contains 20 prospective interpretation cases. They address billing/use, membership/person counts, advertising throughput, equipment/operator asymmetry, financial-claim changes, average/end-period denominators, maturation, property cash, assigned access days, unavailable future results, retained-portfolio scope, estimated uses, renewals, employer reach, attribution carry-forward, average-subscriber yield, successful exits, restricted cash, outcome population and unsupported forecasting. They are not implemented tests.

Later validation should have three separate stages. First, blinded analysts and deterministic checks test whether the correct source scope and economic explanation are recovered. Second, predeclared prospective studies test business outcomes using evidence that was available at the time. Third, the existing investment-evaluation owner tests whether any candidate adds value beyond simple earnings, sector and market baselines after costs and multiple-testing control. None of these stages was executed here.

An interpretation may be correct yet have no predictive edge. A predictive business variable may still be priced in. A backtest using only today's surviving sites or members may be misleading. Stop and retain uncertainty when the required history, counterfactual or observation window is unavailable.

## 10. What can honestly be built from this research

The accompanying cross-family synthesis selects an evidence-backed economic-change dossier, not an automatic Consumer Cyclical stock selector. Public filings can support observed units, business scope, selected cash bridges and explicitly attributed explanations. They generally do not provide individual retention, customer switching, site-cohort returns or real-time purchase intent at the desired granularity.

The first product should make that distinction useful. Its top view should say what changed, why that matters economically and what condition would change the assessment. Supporting views should show measured exposure, the correct numerator/denominator, competing explanations and exact source history. Missing material data should alter the explanation, not disappear into a confidence score.

Source admission and paid publication must use existing owners. The original study files are not live graph evidence. Public source access is not blanket redistribution permission; no licensed membership, geolocation, learning or payments dataset was purchased or acquired. The sources below were read through the web tool, not through a deployed Mastermind collector.
