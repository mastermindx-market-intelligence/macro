## 3. Planet Fitness: earnings per share are a shareholder measure, not a demand measure

The Q2 adjusted reconciliation reports income of 68,449 versus 72,568 in USD thousands, adjusted diluted shares of 77,462 versus 84,398 in thousands, and adjusted EPS of $0.88 versus $0.86 [S1]. The adjusted basis assumes exchange of outstanding Holdings units; the GAAP diluted-share denominator is different [S2].

From the displayed income and share inputs:

| Derived quantity | Result |
|---|---:|
| Adjusted income change | -5.6761% |
| Adjusted diluted share-count change | -8.2182% |
| Current income divided by matching shares | $0.883646 |
| Prior income divided by matching shares | $0.859831 |
| EPS growth from those displayed inputs | +2.7698% |
| EPS growth using the rounded published cents | +2.3256% |

These calculations answer different precision questions. Both quotients are compatible with the displayed cent-rounded EPS. The package preserves the published EPS rather than overwriting it with a reconstructed number or treating the different growth rates as evidence of an issuer error.

The exact identity is:

`EPS_new / EPS_old = (income_new / income_old) × (shares_old / shares_new)`.

Income declines but shares decline more, producing a higher per-share quotient. This is not an argument that repurchases are inherently bad or that the improvement is fictitious. It is an argument for attributing the improvement correctly. An investor evaluating operating momentum needs the numerator; an investor evaluating the common-share claim also needs the denominator and the financing supporting any repurchase.

The proposed dossier should therefore separate an operating explanation from the per-share bridge. It should not let a favorable EPS direction validate customer demand or franchisee returns. Conversely, it should not discard a genuine per-share benefit merely because it is not organic demand growth.

### What would establish a durable improvement?

Three distinct hypotheses remain open. First, an operating recovery would require comparable recurring contribution and actual demand measures to improve. Second, a capital-allocation thesis would require an assessment of the purchase price, future cash generation, funding cost and competing uses of capital. Third, a risk thesis would require evidence that the changed obligations are affordable across plausible downside cases.

The present arithmetic establishes none of those future outcomes. It provides a decomposition that tells the subsequent research where to look. It also prevents mixing an adjusted all-units-exchanged numerator with a different GAAP denominator, which could create a precise-looking but economically inconsistent ratio.

## 4. A past successful year is not a current forecast-validation result

Planet Fitness's initial 2025 same-club-sales outlook was approximately 5–6% [S3]. The subsequent annual release reported 6.7% for that year and introduced a 4–5% outlook for 2026 [S4]. The August 2026 release then projected approximately 1% for the current full year [S1].

This is useful history because it includes an actual outcome and separately dated forward expectations. The 2025 outcome exceeded the initial range. That does not show that the later 2026 outlook will be achieved, that every intervening forecast was accurate, or that shareholders earned a particular return.

The source definition measures billed membership dues in the eligible club population [S3]. The comparable-series label must travel with that meaning. It is not a visit count, an independent measure of member health, or a directly observed customer-switching rate.

The research should distinguish two statements:

- “A prior-year outcome exceeded a specified earlier management range.”
- “Management subsequently lowered a different year's expected comparable growth.”

Both can be true. Neither is an analyst-consensus surprise. They should not be fused into a generic management-quality score from two observations.

This history also explains why the final sector system needs target-specific expectations. Annual targets and reporting events have different roles. An earnings release can update a future-year or current-year expectation without correcting the document that contained the previous expectation. The incumbent guidance and revision owners must preserve that distinction; R8 does not implement a competing history.

### Forward EPS guidance can change for a different reason than operating guidance

The August release describes approximately 6% adjusted EPS growth versus a prior 4%, alongside approximately 3% lower adjusted income versus the earlier 2% decline assumption, and a share assumption of about 77 million versus 79 million [S1]. These are approximate company-guidance descriptions, not enough to reconstruct a unique high-precision earnings forecast.

The material inference is qualitative: a more favorable per-share outlook need not indicate a more favorable income outlook. The future dossier should show both changes and their denominators. It should not convert approximate percentage guidance into fictitious exact forecast points or treat a share assumption as a completed repurchase.

## 5. Life Time: the same cash metric changes direction across horizons

The selected releases report the following cash bridge in USD thousands [S5]:

| Interval | Operating cash | Capital expenditure, net | Net sale-leaseback proceeds | Company-defined free cash flow |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Q2 2026 | 209,558 | 263,260 | 200,191 | 146,489 |
| Q2 2025 | 195,698 | 222,004 | 138,771 | 112,465 |
| H1 2026 | 408,351 | 523,276 | 200,191 | 85,266 |
| H1 2025 | 379,554 | 364,486 | 138,771 | 153,839 |

Every row satisfies `operating cash − capital expenditure + net proceeds = company-defined free cash flow`. The Q2 measure increases by 30.2530%; the H1 measure decreases by 44.5745%. This is a horizon difference, not an arithmetic contradiction.

Subtracting Q2 from H1 gives first-quarter CFO, net capital spending and the company cash measure. Those derived figures match the independently inspected Q1 release: respectively 198,793, 260,016 and -61,223 for 2026; and 183,856, 142,482 and 41,374 for 2025 [S6]. This is a useful cross-document check. It is not an independent audit because the disclosures share the same issuer and accounting process.

The first-half change decomposes exactly:

`-129,993 before additional property proceeds + 61,420 additional property proceeds = -68,573 change in company-defined free cash flow`.

The pre-sale-leaseback subtotal is not normalized owner cash either. Its capital deduction includes investment in expansion. R8 deliberately shows the company's metric, the subtotal excluding property proceeds, and the capital allocation beside one another instead of asserting that one is the uniquely correct measure of value.

### The investment question becomes more precise

The relevant hypothesis is not merely “cash fell, therefore growth is bad.” It is whether the additional investment produces sufficient incremental cash after the time, risk and obligations required to build and operate the assets. A company can rationally invest more than current operating cash; the evidence owed is on the expected and subsequently realized returns.

Nor should property proceeds be capitalized as recurring operating growth. Asset monetization changes the funding and ownership of future obligations. A forecast must choose a consistent treatment of rent, leases and terminal assets. Deducting a lease burden twice, or counting the same property value in both sale proceeds and terminal ownership, would distort common-equity value.

The next discriminating data would be center-opening cohorts, cumulative investment, maturity curves, lease obligations, and cohort-level contribution or cash where actually disclosed. Consolidated growth alone cannot manufacture those allocations.

## 7. How the two cases sharpen Consumer Cyclical re-rating research

A useful valuation thesis needs to distinguish the expected operating cash stream from how much of that stream reaches each share and at what risk. The following research structure uses the two cases without turning either into a recommendation.

**Operating improvement:** comparable demand, contribution and capital productivity improve on a coherent basis. Confirm with matched observations and contrary evidence; do not infer from EPS alone.

**Capital-allocation improvement:** the same business can produce a different common-share outcome after repurchases, dilution, debt or asset monetization. Evaluate the future costs and claims, not just the current numerical benefit.

**Investment-phase change:** capital requirements can precede the revenue or cash associated with new capacity. Study the cohort rather than annualizing a temporarily favorable or unfavorable quarter.

**Expectation change:** a new observation matters relative to a dated expectation with matching scope. Management guidance, analyst consensus and price-implied expectations are separate data.

**Valuation change:** a better business can coexist with a lower security price if the required return, risk or starting valuation changes. This requires actual market data before assigning historical or forecast returns.

For illustration only, applying the familiar positive-EPS price identity to the reconstructed PLNT EPS ratio gives a break-even multiple decline of approximately 2.6951%. That is the multiple contraction which mathematically offsets the 2.7698% EPS increase in this hypothetical identity. No observed price, actual market multiple or return is used. The quarterly adjusted denominator is not silently promoted to sustainable annual earnings.

The next empirical work should investigate whether these decompositions add information beyond simpler comparable-growth, earnings-revision and market baselines. It must predefine the outcome, horizon, data available at the time, comparison group and adverse cases before examining favorable examples. R8 supplies interpretable inputs and failure modes; it does not establish alpha.
