# Consumer Cyclical R9 — Historical Expectations, Shareholder Outcomes and Valuation

**Research cutoff:** September 23, 2026. **Operation:** `gmi-consumer-cyclical-research-20260923-sol-001`.
**Carrier:** Macro PR #7804, `sol/consumer-cyclical-research-20260923`.
**Status:** original research, curated historical observations, executed research arithmetic and a proposed future evaluation protocol. Not a native product, source admission, approved investment model, blind test, preregistered study or Fable commission.

## 1. The material advance

R1–R8 established economic models, selected company cases, a written dossier specification and a delivery-plan candidate. R9 adds observed historical shareholder outcomes and dated expectation histories. The central question is now testable in a narrower form: does a change in business economics tell us something useful about the next business outcome, the expectations investors must revise, or the return available after the information could actually be used?

These are three different questions. This report does not manufacture a strategy return by attaching an annual earnings number, published later, to an earlier annual share-price interval. It uses three developmental case families: Target's inventory/profit transition, D.R. Horton's housing cycle, and lululemon's changing FY2024 expectations. Target is an explicitly contextual retail-mechanism comparison, not an admission into Consumer Cyclical membership. The other examples are also research selections, not approved baskets.

The new evidence rejects several shortcuts: positive comparable sales do not ensure profit growth; negative year-over-year earnings do not preclude a strong shareholder outcome; outperforming the broad market does not prove stock selection within a leading industry; and exceeding a recently reduced forecast does not mean the original thesis was realized. It does not establish how frequently those failures occur, a reliable forecasting coefficient, or a causal explanation for a stock move.

## 2. Target: test the promised recovery against the correct target

On May 18, 2022, Target described a Q2 operating-margin outlook centered around 5.3% and a full-year outlook centered around 6%. The June 7 inventory response changed Q2's center to approximately 2% and described the second half around 6%. The August Q2 release retained that second-half expectation. Those are approximate centers with unspecified ranges, not probability means or exact interval boundaries. [T01–T03]

Actual Q2 operating margin was 1.2%. More importantly, the half-year outcome must be constructed from matched amounts rather than an average of quarterly percentages:

| Target FY2022, USD millions | Full year | First half | Derived second half |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Total revenue | 109,120 | 51,207 | 57,913 |
| Operating income | 3,848 | 1,667 | 2,181 |

The derived H2 margin is `2181 / 57913 = 3.76599%`. It is approximately 2.234 percentage points below the June/August 6% center. This is a comparison with those dated expectations, not a formal claim that the outcome lies outside an unspecified forecast interval. It uses total revenue in the denominator, not a gross-margin denominator based on merchandise sales. [T03,T05]

The later forecast must remain visible. On November 16, Target reduced its Q4 margin expectation to a wide range centered around 3%; the subsequent Q4 amounts, 1,159 operating income and 31,395 revenue, produce approximately 3.69167%. A Q4 result above that later center cannot retroactively satisfy the earlier H2 ambition. Nor should the earlier H2 shortfall erase the improvement from the Q2 trough. [T04,T05]

For the full FY2022, comparable sales rose 2.2% while operating income fell 57%. FY2023 brought the opposite sign combination: comparable sales fell 3.7%, while operating income rose from 3,848 to 5,707 million, approximately 48.3%. The FY2023 release identifies lower markdown, inventory, freight and fulfillment costs among the improvement mechanisms; FY2023 also included a 53rd week. These observations do not imply that discretionary demand recovered. [T05,T06]

### Research consequence

Inventory correction has at least two potentially investable transitions, not one: the costly clearing of past decisions and the economics of replenishing under a new demand pattern. A smaller inventory balance may reflect productive cleanup, lost demand, reduced service availability, or a portfolio change. To distinguish them, the next observation set should include comparable activity, markdown dependence, available assortment, gross contribution, carrying costs and cash needed for replenishment.

Our proposed hypothesis is that a credible contribution recovery can precede renewed comparable-sales growth. The falsifier is a recovery explained only by a weak comparison base, short-lived cost relief or deferred spending that impairs future service. Conversely, an investor waiting for every top-line measure to turn positive could miss a genuine cost-and-capital transition. This is a hypothesis motivated by an observed case, not a validated entry rule.

For the product, the output should be a conditional explanation: the business is below its earlier recovery ambition, but an identified cost mechanism is improving. It should state which part of the economics improved, what remains weak, and which new evidence would change the interpretation. The underlying statements must stay separate from a management promise and from an analyst or market expectation.

## 3. D.R. Horton: trailing earnings and shareholder outcomes can move in opposite directions

D.R. Horton's FY2022 diluted EPS increased from $11.41 to $16.51, approximately 44.70%. Its FY2023 EPS then fell to $13.82, approximately 16.29%. These are disclosed annual GAAP per-share observations, not estimates that were all available at the beginning of the corresponding year. [D01,D05]

The annual-report stock-performance table provides dividend-reinvested wealth indices. Dividing consecutive matched endpoints gives:

| Fiscal interval, issuer-reported September 30 endpoints | EPS change | DHI total return | S&P 500 total return | Homebuilding benchmark total return |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FY2022 | +44.70% | −18.90% | −15.47% | −20.79% |
| FY2023 | −16.29% | +61.13% | +21.62% | +63.23% |

The source graph is normalized to $100 on September 30, 2018 and assumes reinvested dividends. Results are calculated from its rounded displayed levels; they are annual contextual comparisons, not event-window returns. The sector comparator is the disclosed S&P 1500 Homebuilding Index, not a custom portfolio excluding DHI. FY2023 DHI lagged that comparator by about 2.10 percentage points even while strongly outperforming the broad market. Relative terminal wealth was about 1.28% lower; that is a different calculation from the percentage-point return difference. [D06]

The final EPS announcements occurred on November 9, 2022 and November 7, 2023, after their respective September return endpoints. Using either completed annual EPS number as a signal for the already-finished interval would introduce future information. The annual comparisons demonstrate divergence, not that our system could have traded it. [D01,D05]

### The genuinely earlier observation candidates

The November 2022 release already showed a weak forward order picture despite strong delivered earnings: Q4 net orders were 13,582 and cancellations 32%. The January 2023 update reported 13,382 orders and 27% cancellations. These are observations about new commitments, not the same economic population as the homes already closed. [D01,D02]

In April, net orders rose sequentially to 23,142, or about 73%; management discussed normal spring seasonality alongside adjusted pricing and incentives. Quarterly EPS was still $2.73 versus $4.03 a year earlier. July's report showed 22,879 net orders versus 16,693 a year earlier and cancellations of 18%. The improving order evidence is real; attributing it entirely to independent category demand rather than seasonality, pricing or a low base is not supported. [D03,D04]

The candidate leading mechanism is conversion after an affordability reset. To research it properly, compare orders by season, cancellation and completion risk, the economics of financing incentives, community availability, backlog conversion and capital turns. A volume recovery purchased with excessive concessions could benefit consumers without producing adequate equity returns. A builder with appropriate lots, liquidity and operational capacity may respond differently from a highly constrained competitor.

This suggests a useful separation between cyclical leadership and company selection. A housing group can rerate collectively while the selected issuer fails to outperform its own industry. The system should report both. Correlated builder observations are not independent confirmations, and a shared macro repricing should not be counted once as a rates signal and again as several unrelated company discoveries.

### One real historical valuation anchor, with a narrow claim

The annual report quotes a closing price of $122.32 on November 13, 2023. Dividing by the already announced FY2023 EPS of $13.82 yields a retrospectively reconstructed trailing ratio of approximately 8.85 times. This is one dated observation, not a current quote, a full point-in-time valuation distribution or proof of executable liquidity. [D05,D06]

An apparently low trailing ratio does not establish cheapness at a cyclical earnings peak, and a rising ratio can be consistent with a falling trailing denominator and an improving expected future. Determining which interpretation applies requires dated forward assumptions and consistent price/share/earnings treatment. The dividend-reinvested performance graph cannot be divided by EPS to claim a historical P/E expansion or contraction. Price return and total return must remain distinct.

## 4. lululemon: the forecast version changes the answer

All annual revenue observations below target FY2024 ending February 2, 2025. The reporting calendar contains 53 weeks, so reported growth and growth excluding the extra week are different measurements.

| Publication | Source-issued FY2024 revenue range, USD millions | Source-issued EPS range |
|---|---:|---:|
| March 21, 2024 | 10,700–10,800 | 14.00–14.20 |
| June 5, 2024 | 10,700–10,800 | 14.27–14.47 |
| August 29, 2024 | 10,375–10,475 | 13.95–14.15 |
| December 5, 2024 | 10,452–10,487 | 14.08–14.16 |

These are management guidance versions, not analyst consensus. Stated assumptions, including share-repurchase exclusions in relevant releases, travel with them; the EPS columns must not be treated as a pure operating forecast. The August revenue reduction moved both original bounds down $325 million. [L01–L04]

January 13, 2025 adds an important horizon trap: the company updated Q4 revenue to $3.560–$3.580 billion, not directly to a new annual range. Previously reported nine-month revenue of $6,976.629 million plus that quarter range produces an explicitly **derived** annual range of $10,536.629–$10,556.629 million. It must retain two source dependencies and must not be presented as an issuer-issued annual forecast. [L04,L05]

Actual annual revenue, released March 27, 2025, was $10,588.126 million. It was $111.874 million below the original lower bound, but $113.126 million above August's upper bound and $101.126 million above December's. It exceeded the January-derived upper bound by $31.497 million, which also equals the Q4 revenue excess over January's upper bound. These comparisons are compatible because they answer different questions. A midpoint is an arithmetic summary, not an inferred probability-weighted expectation. [L06]

### Business growth did not imply a positive fiscal-year shareholder return

FY2024 GAAP EPS was $14.64 versus $12.20, up 20%; using the prior year's adjusted $12.77 base instead gives approximately 14.64% growth. That second calculation is specifically a comparison to a prior adjusted base, not a newly audited normalization of both years. [L06]

The annual stock-performance table shows lululemon wealth falling from 199.69 at January 28, 2024 to 173.02 at February 2, 2025: approximately −13.36%. Over those same fiscal endpoints the S&P 500 rose approximately 23.50%, and the disclosed apparel/accessories/luxury comparator rose approximately 6.10%. The table assumes reinvestment of dividends, if any, and attributes its underlying data to Bloomberg. We use only the bounded published observations; no market-feed rights are inferred. [L07]

The annual actual was announced after the return interval ended. The result cannot prove that selling or buying at any one earnings release would have worked. Nor does it identify the exact contribution of expectations, discount rates, positioning or company news to the return. It demonstrates why a framework based only on realized growth would be incomplete.

### Research consequence

The proposed brand-recovery model should track the distance from the initial plan, the direction of more recent revisions, actual commercial performance and the price required to own those prospects. A company can be recovering relative to a lowered plan while remaining below the original expansion thesis. The customer-facing explanation should make both visible rather than choose whichever version supports a desired verdict.

For an early opportunity, investigate whether full-price demand, product acceptance and repeat purchase are improving before consolidated growth visibly accelerates. Separate distribution changes, international expansion, currency and calendar effects. Candidate market confirmation is another observation, not permission for the business thesis to originate a trade. A failed recovery case is as important as a successful one because it defines the conditions under which an apparently favorable revision is not durable.

## 5. Valuation: connect growth to the investment that funds it

A valuation module should expose an economic argument, not attach the same revenue multiple to every subtheme. The following stationary model is an original worked analytical sensitivity, not a valuation of any researched issuer.

Assume positive next-period after-tax operating profit, constant long-run growth `g`, a constant return `R` on incremental invested capital, and a constant discount rate `r`. All growth requires net reinvestment equal to `g/R` of operating profit; remaining profit becomes cash to all capital providers. With `0 <= g < r` and `g < R`, the enterprise-value multiple of next-period after-tax operating profit is:

`M = (1 - g/R) / (r - g)`

This follows by discounting a perpetually growing cash stream after its required reinvestment. It excludes a transition phase, finite competitive runway, changing returns, financing claims and unusual accounting. It is not appropriate for loss-making early-stage businesses without a separately modeled path to sustainable cash.

At an assumed 10% discount rate:

| Assumed incremental return | 3% growth | 6% growth |
|---|---:|---:|
| 8% | 8.93× | 6.25× |
| 10% | 10.00× | 10.00× |
| 20% | 12.14× | 17.50× |

The result is economically informative: in this model faster growth supports more value only when the incremental return exceeds the discount rate. Growth at the discount rate is value-neutral at a fixed next-period profit base. Growth below it consumes value. The derivative is `(1 - r/R)/(r-g)^2`, which makes the sign explicit.

These are controlled comparisons with next-period profit held fixed. They do not imply that every high-growth business should have a larger observed multiple, that accounting ROE equals incremental economic return, or that reducing investment necessarily benefits an actual company. A project can need an initial loss period, and maintenance can be mislabeled. The assumptions must be judged before the formula is used.

A reverse question is equally useful. Given `M`, `r` and `R`, the model implies:

`g = (M*r - 1) / (M - 1/R)`

At a hypothetical 18× multiple, 10% discount rate and 20% incremental return, the implied perpetual growth is approximately 6.154%. That is a demanding long-run assumption to examine, not evidence that a real stock is expensive. When the denominator is zero the inverse may be unidentified or inconsistent; when the solution violates the model's domain it must not be reported as a valid implied growth rate.

### What the eventual product should show

Use a short operating model whose assumptions have identifiable evidence: category activity, exposure, realized contribution, continuing investment, asset turns, cash conversion and financial claims. Separate a cyclical recovery in normalized earnings from an extension of competitive duration and from a reduction in funding risk. They can all raise equity value but require different confirmation.

For builders, the key sensitivity might be margin against capital turns and lots committed. For expanding centers, it might be mature-site contribution against opening investment and lease obligations. For brands, it may be repeat full-price economics and distribution investment. For platforms, gross transaction volume must first become retained contribution after acquisition and service costs. Apply consistent treatment of leases, captive finance and minority ownership rather than subtracting the same claim twice.

The proposed view should show which assumptions an observed price requires, which are measured, which are hypothesized and which are unavailable. It should not convert an unmeasured variable into a precise fair value. A sensitivity surface is useful when it reveals the assumptions that matter; a single price target is not required for the first descriptive dossier.

## 6. The empirical question must be fixed before the next outcome is seen

All examples in this report are already examined developmental cases. They cannot become a held-out test by writing a protocol afterward. The following design is **proposed**, not registered, running, or accepted by the evaluation owner.

Separate three evaluation levels. First, interpretation: does the system bind the right target, period, unit, source and mathematical relationship and produce a useful explanation? Second, business prediction: do saved hypotheses improve the forecast of the next two reporting quarters or the actual for a stated fiscal target? Third, investment usefulness: does the information improve a specified decision after publication, execution costs and existing market/sector signals? Success at one level cannot be promoted automatically to the next.

### Proposed first shadow panel

Use PLNT, LTH, LULU and DHI for an engineering and interpretation shadow panel, covering the next four complete reported quarters for each after a separately accepted start. Freeze this set before those outcomes, rather than adding the company whose next release looks easiest. It is a convenience panel of known cases, not a representative sector sample or sufficient evidence of investment alpha.

A broader investment study requires the existing accepted issuer universe and historical membership definitions, including unavailable and delisted observations. The full sector mission still requires international coverage. A four-issuer panel can identify extraction and decision failures, but cannot validate the many subthemes researched in R1–R5.

### Information and execution clocks

Save the actual analysis revision and the available inputs before measuring its future outcome. Separate source acceptance, independently evidenced public availability, actual system observation and served publication. For a date-only source, require a later calendar day and a confirmed regular session rather than fabricating an intraday timestamp. Missing historical acquisition evidence cannot be repaired by retroactively stamping a current backfill.

For the proposed investment labels, entry is the first regular-session open strictly after the saved analysis and all required inputs became usable. Exit is the close of the specified completed regular session, counting the entry session as one. Use the relevant exchange calendar, not calendar-day addition. The primary horizon is 21 sessions, the secondary 63; 1 and 5 are diagnostic, and 126 is explicitly exploratory. The longer horizon does not turn a short-term product into a medium-term recommendation.

Do not choose the best horizon after seeing results, count an overnight announcement move as return earned by a later reader, or treat today's reconstructed model as actually served historical output. Historical replay and prospective operation need separate labels.

### Outcomes, baselines and dependence

For a range forecast, compare the actual with both bounds and preserve the target's size, interval and definition. Do not score an unspecified approximate center as an interval miss. Treat updates to one annual target as related versions, not independent forecasts with independent realized outcomes. Keep management forecast changes, model forecasts and analyst consensus separate.

For investment labels, obtain matched total returns in a common currency for the security, broad market and accepted industry benchmark. Preserve fees, spread, market impact assumptions, delayed execution, corporate actions and listing identity. A percentage-point excess return is not the relative-wealth ratio. Do not interchange adjusted prices, total-return series and raw prices or assume an ETF perfectly replicates an index.

Compare the full model with a frozen simpler baseline: the same eligible events with the existing price/sector context and basic earnings or comparable-activity variables but without the new economic-mechanism features. Use pre-event inputs only. Feature ablations should ask whether contribution, reinvestment or expectation versions add information, not whether a complicated model fits the developmental examples.

Estimate uncertainty with dependence preserved across issuers, shared calendar shocks and overlapping holding periods. Report results by company and mechanism as well as aggregate; a broad market rally is not many independent discoveries. Choose one primary comparison before observing it and account for additional exploratory comparisons. No effect size, probability or success threshold is fitted from the three cases in this report.

Missing data is an outcome of the study's feasibility, not permission to remove inconvenient observations. Report eligible, covered, abstained, unavailable and realized-outcome counts. Retain failures, delistings and abnormal events with the appropriate labels. A claim that the new system adds investable value requires accepted out-of-sample evidence and real cost/data checks, not merely a positive mean over a tiny panel.

## 7. Product and research decisions this supports

The economic-change dossier should preserve three views side by side: the present business level, the direction of the latest comparable evidence, and the distance from a named prior expectation. The market and valuation context remains separate. This avoids a single score where a weak current level automatically overrules an improving trajectory, or a positive trajectory erases a broken original plan.

The proposed next-observation conditions should be specific. In retail cleanup, ask whether replenishment and contribution improve without renewed excess inventory. In housing conversion, ask whether seasonal orders and cancellations improve after accounting for incentives and required capital. In a brand reset, ask whether the revised plan is being exceeded through repeat full-price demand rather than another timing or basis change. In an expansion model, ask whether the new cohort is earning sufficient cash for the investment required.

These conditions belong in the existing workflow and existing evaluation ownership. This report does not add a new forecast store, lifecycle, metric registry or ranking authority. The first descriptive PLNT/LTH/LULU delivery plan remains intact; research associations must not become automatic theme membership or trade authority.

The evidence also establishes what we cannot yet claim: a current fair value, a validated early-runner detector, causal attribution of annual returns, sector-wide forecast accuracy, or an accepted production source path. Those are explicit remaining objectives, not reasons to substitute another generic company-summary series.
