# Consumer Cyclical R3 — Autos, Financing, Aftermarket and Household Durables

**Research cutoff:** September 23, 2026. **Operation:** `gmi-consumer-cyclical-research-20260923-sol-001`.
**Carrier:** Macro PR #7804, `sol/consumer-cyclical-research-20260923`.
**Status:** principal research and proposed analytical requirements. Not an accepted product specification, production schema, investment recommendation, validated forecast or Fable commission.

## 1. The capability this research is meant to unlock

A useful Consumer Cyclical system should distinguish a demand recovery from an inventory adjustment, a financing subsidy, a reduction in loss-making output, a favorable accounting comparison, or a change in who owns the earnings. R3 develops that distinction for vehicles, replacement parts, appliances and home furnishings. It complements R1's taxonomy and R2's housing, brands and restaurant research without changing their files or restarting their investigations.

The investor's job is to discover an economically meaningful change early, understand which business benefits, and identify the next observation that would confirm or invalidate the interpretation. The machine's job is to preserve the population, contract, inventory stage, financial claim and information date behind that explanation. The proposed advantage is a maintained explanation of the mechanism, not another generic consumer score or a static list of automotive stocks.

This tranche examines six listed issuer groups: Ford, AutoNation, AutoZone, Whirlpool, Ethan Allen and Williams-Sonoma. Ford Credit is studied as a separate financing business within the Ford group, not as an independent confirmation of vehicle demand. The selection is for analytical coverage, not basket admission or investment preference. It is intentionally not a complete global census: Asian manufacturers, European brands, suppliers, battery economics, tires, repair operators and additional appliance businesses remain future coverage.

The research asks a sharper question than whether earnings rose:

> What changed in demand, conversion, delivered contribution, capital at risk and the shareholder claim — and which of those changes could persist?

The following architecture is proposed research. Equations express accounting identities or explicitly hypothetical models, not fitted predictors. Source facts are confined to identified evidence capsules; management explanations remain attributed claims. No historical stock-return study, current valuation distribution, live ingestion or browser acceptance was performed.

## 2. Replace the single sales funnel with linked economic stages

The proposed representation follows an asset through several stages:

`household need → repair/replace/upgrade choice → order and financing → factory or supplier shipment → channel inventory → delivery and use → maintenance/resale → retirement`

A parallel financial path follows:

`payment commitment → retained product margin → fees or financed receivable → funding and losses → cash available to the relevant equity claim`

These paths interact, but they are not interchangeable. A financed sale can recognize merchandise revenue today while leaving a lender exposed for years. A deposit can provide cash before delivery while remaining an obligation. A shipment can improve manufacturer sales without demonstrating equivalent consumer absorption. A used-asset price decline can make an item cheaper for one buyer while reducing another buyer's trade-in equity.

The existing Theme Graph and evidence owners should retain the source-bound relationships; the financial owner should retain amounts and formulas; existing relationship owners should retain proposed transmission; F04 and current page builders should compose the explanation. R3 does not authorize a durable-goods graph, new financial ledger, metric registry, correction service or publisher.

### Proposed research lenses

| Lens | Distinct question | Principal confounder |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Installed asset base | What stock can fail, require maintenance or be replaced? | An average age conceals different age and usage distributions. |
| Purchase conversion | Can a willing customer complete a transaction? | Longer terms, subsidies or relaxed credit can imitate better affordability. |
| Production and channel | Where is inventory accumulating or being liquidated? | Unmatched manufacturer and retailer populations do not form an inventory identity. |
| Retained contribution | What remains after product, logistics, selling and support costs? | Mix shifts and internal transfers can change ratios without comparable demand. |
| Credit and residual risk | Who owns repayment and resale uncertainty? | Fee income, interest spread, charge-offs and residual depreciation are different economics. |
| Capital and equity | How much capital is needed, and which claim receives the residual? | Financing debt, restricted cash, minority interests and preferred claims are easily double counted. |
| Expectations | What previously knowable assumptions have changed? | A better result against an easy comparison is not automatically a positive surprise. |

The same company may appear in several lenses. One consolidated relationship is not several independent pieces of evidence. A legal ownership change should update the affected interest without erasing separately evidenced contracts that survive it.

## 3. Evidence capsules: what the primary sources actually establish

### 3.1 Ford: loss reduction need not be demand-led

Ford's June-quarter filing reports Model e wholesales of 28,000 versus 60,000, revenue of $1.026 billion versus $2.357 billion, and EBIT of minus $919 million versus minus $1.329 billion. The reported EBIT margin worsened from minus 56.4% to minus 89.6%. Management attributes part of the improvement to lower losses on earlier products and a one-time supply-agreement adjustment. The filing also warns that wholesale units can include unconsolidated affiliates and deferred-revenue transactions. Ford Credit EBT rose from $645 million to $757 million, while its U.S. retail loss-to-receivables measure rose from 48 to 52 basis points. [R3-S01]

**Research interpretation:** at least three different dimensions must survive: activity, absolute contribution, and contribution relative to sales. A capacity reduction may be financially rational and still provide no evidence that customer adoption is accelerating. Do not label a loss-making segment's smaller absolute loss an improving operating margin. Do not infer a transaction price by dividing consolidated revenue by a broader unit population.

**Next discriminating evidence:** matched product deliveries, dealer inventory, recurring contribution after the specified adjustment, product warranty costs and the capital required for the remaining portfolio. No current electric-vehicle theme verdict follows from this one company observation.

### 3.2 Ford Credit: a sale and its financing are connected, not identical

Ford Credit's 2025 annual filing describes financing programs supported by payments from Ford. Earned interest supplements on receivables were $3.0 billion, compared with $2.8 billion of cash received. Operating-lease support is accounted for separately. Some receivables legally sold through securitization remain consolidated; some cash is restricted. The filing defines credit-loss and equity-return denominators and describes how expected vehicle residuals affect depreciation. [R3-S02]

**Research interpretation:** trace the subsidy across the group rather than treating a low customer rate and lender financing income as two free sources of value. The timing of cash received, financing revenue and product support expense needs reconciliation. Loan interest, commission, lease rental and disposal proceeds are separate return streams.

The proposed dossier should show who funds the offer and who retains default and residual risk. Evidence of stronger financing volume is incomplete until the loan cohort's economics, funding cost and required capital are understood. A credit business can improve its current earnings while a particular credit-risk observation deteriorates; these observations need not be averaged into a single state.

### 3.3 AutoNation: revenue exposure and profit exposure are different

AutoNation's June-quarter release reports adjusted net income of $187.8 million versus $209.2 million and diluted average shares of 33.8 million versus 38.3 million; adjusted EPS rose from $5.46 to $5.56. Parts and service represented 18.2% of revenue but 49.3% of gross profit. Same-store new and used retail units fell. The half-year statement reports $48.9 million of operating cash outflow, while the separately defined adjusted free-cash-flow measure was positive $439.2 million. [R3-S03]

Its 10-Q separately describes financing activities. For the quarter, floorplan assistance was $33.9 million against $42.0 million of floorplan interest. Trade and nontrade inventory-financing changes occupy different cash-flow classifications. Inventory financing also covers service loaners; commissions for arranging customer finance are not the same as income from retained loans. [R3-S04]

**Research interpretation:** a dealership group is not a pure bet on new-unit sales. Equally, a service-heavy gross-profit mix is not proof that the whole company is insulated from a vehicle downturn. Expenses, capital requirements and financing risk still have to reach attributable earnings.

Per-share arithmetic can be useful without being demand evidence. From the rounded release inputs, adjusted income declined approximately 10.23% while shares declined approximately 11.75%; the resulting ratio implies an approximately 1.72% EPS increase, close to the 1.83% change in reported rounded EPS. Rounding explains why the reconstructed ratio need not be exact. This is a calculation, not a claim that the capital allocation was attractive at its purchase price.

### 3.4 AutoZone: installed-fleet opportunity and network growth

AutoZone's September 22 release covers a 16-week quarter ended August 29. Total same-store growth was 2.7% reported and 1.5% at constant currency. Commercial sales rose 8.6%, while average weekly sales per program rose 2.7%. The release separately reports program counts; an end-period count is not the quarter's average count. [R3-S05]

In its May-quarter filing, management identifies miles driven and the number of vehicles at least seven years old as the statistics it believes most closely relate to long-run market growth. That is a company hypothesis, not an estimated causal model accepted by this research. [R3-S06]

**Research interpretation:** separate category demand, network rollout, mature-location productivity, service levels and returns on expansion capital. Faster delivery could improve retention and professional-customer share, but the hypothesis must survive inventory investment, local density, credit collection and incremental operating costs. New programs are not interchangeable with mature ones.

A parts supplier should be researched through the serviceable installed fleet, not simply new-car sales or a generic recession label. The relevant asset population includes age, technology, usage and geography. An older fleet might generate more repairs, but reduced driving, scrappage or unaffordable repair bills can offset that mechanism.

### 3.5 Whirlpool: channel, portfolio and capital structure can move separately

Whirlpool's June-quarter release distinguishes reported sales from organic sales. North American major-appliance margin was 2.7% versus 5.9% a year earlier, although it improved sequentially. Management attributes small-appliance sales pressure to lower retailer inventories despite strong sell-out. That sell-out statement is qualitative; a quantified independent consumer-demand series was not acquired. The release kept its operational outlook while changing earnings guidance for financing costs. [R3-S07]

The 10-Q reports the exit from its remaining 25% Beko interest while licensing and supply agreements continued. Whirlpool India remained a 40% equity-method interest at quarter end. Quarterly earnings attributable to all Whirlpool shareholders were $88 million; $13 million of preferred dividends left $75 million for common shareholders. A mandatory convertible preferred issue also creates a future share-count scenario. [R3-S08]

**Research interpretation:** a brand or geography tag cannot replace an interest-by-interest map. Equity ownership, royalties, product supply and retained liabilities can have different termination dates. Deconsolidation is not evidence of an equal change in underlying market demand. Common-shareholder economics need their own numerator and share scenarios even after industrial operations stabilize.

### 3.6 Ethan Allen: orders, deliveries and internal sales

Ethan Allen's July 29 release reports quarterly consolidated sales of $146.8 million, retail sales of $132.0 million and wholesale sales of $79.7 million. Written orders fell 10.8% in retail and 11.9% in wholesale. The segment sales cannot simply be added to produce consolidated sales. [R3-S09]

Its June-year-end filing describes intersegment wholesale activity and a wholesale backlog of $44.3 million, down 9.3%. Customer deposits fell from $75.068 million to $62.730 million as deliveries exceeded incoming orders. Different contract and design-center channels had different order changes. [R3-S10]

**Research interpretation:** better lead times can result from improved throughput, a shrinking order book, or both. A delivery-driven revenue quarter may consume demand booked earlier. A deposit drawdown can be consistent with fulfilling obligations rather than deterioration in collections, while still signaling less advance funding for future output. The design must retain all three interpretations until evidence separates them.

### 3.7 Williams-Sonoma: a deliberate contrast, not an inferred market-share transfer

Williams-Sonoma's August 26 release reports comparable-brand growth of 6.2% and quarterly revenue of $1.960 billion versus $1.837 billion. Non-GAAP operating margin was 17.3%, down 60 basis points; non-GAAP operating income was approximately $338 million. Stronger revenue and a lower margin therefore coexist. Management's market-share assertion remains its assertion. [R3-S11]

The annual filing's comparable-brand definition includes e-commerce, eligible stores and business-to-business revenue but excludes international franchises. Its cost classifications and eligible-store rules also matter for comparisons. [R3-S12]

**Research interpretation:** two furnishing companies with different results are not a controlled experiment. Their fiscal windows, channels, price points, customer populations and product scope need not match. R3 does not infer that one took the other's customers. The positive demand observation should remain visible even where margin compressed; rejecting all favorable evidence would be as misleading as ignoring the margin.

## 4. A proposed replacement-demand model with competing choices

The starting research model is an installed population, not a single growth extrapolation. Let N(a,u,g,k,t) describe assets by age a, usage u, geography g, technology k and observation time t. For each cohort, estimate or observe mutually exclusive next-period choices: maintain, repair, replace with new, replace with used, postpone, or retire without replacement. These probabilities are not supplied or calibrated here.

A conceptual new-replacement estimate is the sum across cohorts of N multiplied by the probability of replacement with a new asset. New households, additional-asset ownership and new commercial capacity belong in separate terms. A sale of an existing used asset changes ownership; it is not production of another new asset. Repeated repairs can occur within a period and require an event-intensity model rather than the same mutually exclusive transition used for the asset's terminal state.

This separation matters for consumer durables because an expanding repair opportunity and a weak new-product market can coexist. It also avoids a common false inference: a large cohort reaching a typical replacement age is a candidate demand window, not a guaranteed wave of purchases. The cohort may face different quality, financing, repair cost or ownership behavior from past cohorts.

The proposed explanatory variables include repair cost relative to replacement cost, reliability, downtime tolerance, compatibility, energy or operating expense, product improvement, household liquidity, financing access, resale value and expected remaining asset life. They should be collected only where lawful, feasible and economically material. Unknown variables remain missing; an LLM should not fill a cohort table with plausible-looking numbers.

### Three different appliance jobs

An emergency replacement, a discretionary upgrade and an appliance package associated with a new dwelling need different demand models. An urgent replacement can be time-sensitive while the buyer trades down in specification. A discretionary upgrade can be postponed despite stable income. A new-dwelling package depends on construction and occupancy timing, not only current retail sentiment.

For furnishings, the proposed lenses are household formation, moves, redecorating, replacement, design-led premium purchases and commercial fit-outs. These overlap with housing but should not be reduced to one national mortgage-rate coefficient. A household can move without buying new furniture, buy furniture without moving, or buy used furnishings. The consumer choice and the manufacturer revenue event are different stages.

### Falsifiable research questions

Does repair expenditure rise before new replacement demand improves, or does sustained repair indicate continued deferral? Does an identifiable product-cohort bulge explain orders after controlling for price and channel inventory? Do financing changes alter conversion for the customers who are actually constrained? Are weaker shipments accompanied by strong end-use consumption or by weaker activity at every stage?

These questions lead to discriminating datasets, not universal answers. The proposed first studies should compare successful and failed replacement-cycle calls. A model trained only on subsequent recoveries would learn the analyst's selection rather than the opportunity mechanism.

## 5. Financing changes the timing, cost and risk bearer of demand

A household affordability panel should retain purchase price, down payment, trade-in proceeds, existing debt payoff, financed fees, rate, amortization term and any balloon payment. Monthly payment alone is insufficient. For a fixed-rate amortizing example, payment equals L*r/[1-(1+r)^(-n)], where L is principal, r is the periodic rate and n is the number of payments.

**Hypothetical example, not a current lending quote or recommendation:** financing $30,000 at 8% annually with monthly payments costs approximately $608.29 per month over 60 months and $467.59 over 84 months. Total interest is approximately $6,497.51 and $9,277.26 respectively, assuming no fees or early repayment. The lower payment reflects longer exposure, not a lower purchase price. The calculation illustrates a measurement distinction; it does not prescribe an appropriate household loan.

Negative trade equity adds another link. Suppose a vehicle sells for $20,000 but its debt payoff is $23,000. The $3,000 shortfall must be funded, forgiven under an actual agreement, or added to permitted new borrowing. A cheaper next vehicle does not by itself prove that the customer's cash constraint improved. Conversely, a high trade-in value can facilitate a purchase even when new-product prices remain elevated.

For the company research, distinguish customer-rate support, merchant discounts, captive-finance funding subsidies, residual guarantees and third-party promotional funding. A discount charged to the manufacturer may aid conversion but consume unit contribution. A lender can receive support upfront and earn it over time. Whether the arrangement creates group value depends on incremental conversion, contribution, credit performance and capital costs, not its presentation as several revenues.

### Conditional transmission from falling used-asset prices

The proposed graph explanation should not attach one positive or negative sign to an entire auto theme. A lower resale price may improve affordability for a buyer without a trade-in; reduce equity for a buyer who must dispose of an indebted asset; lower a dealer's replacement inventory cost while impairing existing stock; worsen a lease book's residual outcome; or change the decision to repair instead of replace.

Each proposed effect needs its actual exposure, contractual allocation, timing and offset. An originator that sold all risk under a verified transaction differs from a lender retaining risk; an accounting sale cannot be assumed from the word securitization. Market correlation can nominate a relationship for study, but cannot establish who bears the economic loss.

### Credit evidence must match the question

The New York Fed's August 11 release reports an annualized transition into serious auto-loan delinquency of 3.00% for Q2, versus 2.93% a year earlier. This is a balance-transition measure, not the proportion of all borrowers currently delinquent or a lender's loss ratio. [R3-S13]

The proposed comparison layer should preserve arrears threshold, annualization, population, loan vintage, seasoning, outstanding-balance versus origination weighting, and charge-off/recovery treatment. An improving average book can conceal worsening new vintages, and faster book growth can change a loss ratio's denominator before losses mature. Those are research risks to test, not claims about a particular issuer's undisclosed borrowers.

## 6. Inventory and backlog are economic state, not labels

For a matched physical population, the stock identity is:

`ending inventory = beginning inventory + receipts - customer deliveries + net returns/transfers - physical disposals`

For a dollar inventory balance, cost changes, write-downs, exchange effects and accounting policies require a separate valuation bridge. A change in dollar inventory cannot identify unit accumulation without those terms. Manufacturer wholesales and a retailer's sales may use different channels, affiliates, shipment points or geographies; their difference is not automatically an inventory estimate.

Consider a hypothetical manufacturer shipping 100 units while the matched channel sells 120 units and has no other flows. Channel inventory falls by 20 units. The manufacturer's lower shipment growth could understate current consumer activity. Reverse the flows, and a sales increase at the factory could accompany channel accumulation. These examples are identities with assumed matched scope, not an assertion that a current company is loading its channel.

For ordered goods, use a separate flow:

`ending backlog = beginning backlog + accepted orders - recognized conversions - cancellations + scoped amendments`

The amounts must share a basis. A gross order figure cannot replace net bookings; an order value before freight, tax or discount changes may not reconcile to recognized sales. Backlog should also retain scheduled delivery, age, cancellation rights, deposit requirements and capacity needs where the source allows them.

### Distinguishing four apparently similar recoveries

**Demand-led recovery:** comparable end-demand improves, the channel remains balanced, and retained contribution improves without a disproportionate increase in support. The next proof is persistence and cash conversion at the same economic scope.

**Inventory normalization:** shipments recover toward consumption after destocking. This can create a real near-term earnings improvement without demonstrating faster end demand. The next proof is whether sell-through sustains the replenishment rate after inventory returns to target.

**Capacity or cost repair:** lower fixed costs, improved throughput or withdrawal of uneconomic output reduces losses. This can create value even with flat sales. The next proof is recurring savings after restructuring costs and whether retained operations remain competitive.

**Financial-claim improvement:** refinancing, a different share count or a changed ownership structure alters the equity outcome. The next proof is the cost and durability of that capital structure, not necessarily stronger customer demand.

These are proposed explanatory dimensions, not a new lifecycle engine. More than one mechanism can operate at once. The view should show contributions and uncertainties rather than force a company into an exclusive permanent bucket.

## 7. Identify value capture and moats at the right layer

A manufacturer's potential advantages include product quality, platform reuse, qualified supply, efficient production, distribution and recurring services. Each is a research question, not a moat inferred from scale. A lower-cost plant can still create poor returns if it produces the wrong inventory. A premium product can retain pricing while losing enough units to reduce contribution. A supply bottleneck matters only if it is actually binding and the relevant contract permits the company to retain the economics.

A dealer's potential advantages include local availability, service relationships, customer acquisition, reconditioning, financing access and operational density. The research should connect these to measurable outputs: conversion at comparable price, service retention, repair capacity, days held, loss on disposal and net contribution per transaction. A dense network might lower delivery cost, but acquired scale is not equivalent to organic customer retention.

An aftermarket network's candidate advantages include availability of the correct part, delivery reliability, professional relationships and inventory pooling. The useful metric is not simply the number of warehouses. It is whether the network can improve fulfilled demand, cost per successful delivery and returns on incremental inventory while maintaining collections. Separate customer credit supplied as part of distribution from the merchandise margin itself.

An integrated furniture business may coordinate design, manufacturing and fulfillment, but intercompany sales must be eliminated. Internal transfer prices allocate profit between units without necessarily changing consolidated value. Brand desirability, customization and service are possible differentiated jobs; evidence should connect them to repeat purchasing, realized price, cancellations and fully loaded delivery economics.

For an appliance brand, separate directly owned manufacturing, sourced goods, distribution, royalties and equity-method stakes. Portfolio restructuring can alter the margin denominator and capital burden. A higher reported percentage margin after exit is not automatically better economics for the remaining common shares. The research needs retained contribution, capital released, recurring cash obligations and any surviving contractual relationship.

## 8. Valuation research: match the valuation object to the economic claim

R3 proposes a sum-of-parts discipline where manufacturing and financing coexist. One useful representation is:

`common equity value = industrial enterprise value - industrial net debt and other senior claims + attributable finance equity value + other attributable investments - unallocated obligations`

This is a conceptual reconciliation, not a present valuation. The finance equity value already reflects its funding and loan assets. Subtracting all finance debt again from group value without adding the associated asset value would mix enterprise and equity bases. Conversely, labeling all finance debt harmless ignores credit losses, funding liquidity, collateral and required equity. Both shortcuts are rejected.

Use normalized operating cash or earnings for the industrial business only after identifying cycle position, recurring product support, maintenance investment, warranties and inventory needs. Use an appropriate equity-capital framework for the finance business, with return on equity, sustainable distributions and credit/residual stress. Do not export a single manufacturing EV/EBITDA multiple to both activities. Multiples should remain empirical questions against comparable definitions and contemporaneous expectations.

### A hypothetical value bridge

Assume an industrial enterprise value of $20 billion, industrial net debt of $5 billion and an already debt-inclusive finance equity value of $4 billion, with no other claims. Common equity is $19 billion. If industrial enterprise value increases by $2 billion while credit deterioration reduces finance equity by $1 billion, group equity increases by $1 billion to $20 billion. A favorable operating story has been partly offset rather than fully transmitted.

This example deliberately has no company ticker, market price or probability. It demonstrates why an apparently strong macro impulse can have offsetting effects inside one issuer. A real application would need current book values, restricted cash, pension or lease obligations, minority interests, tax consequences and the actual scope of each valuation term.

### Three kinds of re-rating hypothesis to study

**Normalization:** the market revises the sustainable contribution of an existing business as demand, price discipline or capacity utilization becomes clearer. Disconfirming evidence includes support costs that rise with every additional unit or inventory that fails to convert.

**Quality or duration:** investors may recognize a more durable service, recurring customer or contractual earnings stream. The evidence must show retention, cash economics and capital efficiency; a growing service share caused solely by collapsing product sales is not sufficient.

**Risk reduction:** funding access, leverage, execution uncertainty or credit dispersion improves. This can support equity value without accelerating revenue. Refinancing that extends maturity but substantially increases cash cost is a different scenario from cheaper sustainable financing.

The research must preserve negative alternatives: peak earnings can make a cyclical P/E appear low; a rapidly growing receivable book can postpone loss recognition; an asset-light presentation can conceal contract obligations; and future dilution can limit common-share upside. No current issuer is labeled cheap or expensive here.

## 9. From evidence to an early research opportunity

The proposed research scanner should look for conjunctions rather than isolated metrics. For example, a matched channel restocking after inventories normalize, stable consumer absorption, reduced incentives and improving recurring contribution is a stronger demand-led candidate than a shipment rebound alone. It remains a candidate until tested against alternative explanations and actual expectations.

An aftermarket candidate might combine professional-customer productivity, fulfilled-demand improvement and returns on new network capital. An appliance candidate might combine verified replacement demand, normalized retailer inventory and product-level contribution. A furniture candidate might combine improving accepted orders, fewer cancellations and delivery growth that does not merely consume the old book. These are proposed study configurations, not ranking rules.

The user-facing explanation should show the mechanism, exposed business, observed improvement, strongest contrary observation and next condition. It should say when a thesis depends on an unobserved quantity. It should not pretend that source availability itself proves alpha, or that a complete evidence chain guarantees the market has not priced the change.

The market layer remains separately governed: actual security returns, relative strength, breadth, liquidity, volatility and options evidence belong to their existing owners. Fundamental context can explain or challenge a price move without originating a trade. Research salience, forecast probability, technical eligibility, ranking and sizing require distinct acceptance.

A future study should test the incremental value of this mechanism-aware context against simpler baselines, including sector membership, price momentum and raw earnings change. A complex explanation that fails to improve prospective decisions should not retain authority merely because it is persuasive. The moat depends on useful maintained intelligence, not on the number of fields in a packet.

## 10. Data feasibility and honest coverage

Company filings and releases can supply segment observations, scoped metrics, accounting relationships, management outlooks and dated disclosures. They do not automatically supply product-level consumer sell-through, loan-level credit vintages, detailed unit costs or comparable historical forecasts. The current research has not acquired licensed dealer, claims, credit-bureau microdata or private customer records.

FHWA describes a monthly traffic-estimation process drawing on roughly 5,000 continuous counters and revising estimates as information changes. The page offers period files, but no July data file or vintage history was acquired in this tranche. [R3-S14] The Federal Reserve G.19 page read in this session was the September 8 release for July, with revised and preliminary labels. Its current URL is moving; one dated-archive read failed, and no immutable historical archive was established. [R3-S15]

The proposed source matrix should distinguish public observed releases, machine-accessible documented endpoints, licensed services already held by Mastermind, useful but unavailable datasets, and proxies requiring validation. Do not buy new providers or build collectors merely because a source is listed. Verify existing owner coverage and rights before an implementation plan commits to a feed.

Every proposed observation needs economic period, source publication when known, observation time, source locator, definition version, correction lineage, scope and uncertainty. A filing's acceptance time is not necessarily the earliest public disclosure of every fact within it. Current exact intraday first-public times remain unknown for the newly collected filing facts.

Comparison sets need historical company membership and failed firms, not only surviving current issuers. Loan cohorts need comparable seasoning; network cohorts need opening and closure histories; inventory comparisons need valuation-policy consistency; financial periods need actual dates and duration. These are requirements for the later studies, not capabilities claimed to have been built here.

## 11. Proposed product integration and acceptance questions

The existing earnings-inflection dossier from R2 should gain a **recovery-mechanism view**, not be replaced. The intended journey is:

`Theme Tracker / sector detail → subtheme → business exposure → demand and inventory stages → contribution and capital → expectations → next research condition`

A compact primary panel should answer what changed and why it matters. A second level should expose physical activity, channel balance and financing. A financial bridge should identify the common-equity claim without silently mixing different earnings or debt bases. The evidence drawer should retain the exact scopes and unresolved contradictions. Existing authenticated transport and publication rights remain required; no new public full-fidelity payload is authorized.

The selected prospective examples deliberately contain positive, negative and mixed evidence. The model must preserve a better revenue observation without ignoring worse margin; explain smaller losses without inventing demand growth; and maintain surviving contracts after an ownership exit. It must avoid turning every uncertainty into a generic warning that prevents synthesis.

Six representative acceptance questions are:

1. Can the user distinguish contraction-led loss reduction from a recurring demand recovery?
2. Can revenue weight and profit weight differ without the view pretending either is measured cash exposure?
3. Can a financing business show improved current earnings alongside a deteriorating credit observation?
4. Can the ownership view terminate an equity interest while retaining separately evidenced contracts?
5. Can the backlog view explain that faster delivery and weaker incoming demand can coexist?
6. Can a positive company observation be retained without projecting it onto an unmatched peer or the whole sector?

The accompanying structured research contains twenty prospective counterexamples. They remain NOT_EXECUTED and are not an approved production contract. Event handling, period arithmetic and identity checks should eventually be executed by deterministic existing-owner code. Language models may draft interpretations and nominate contradictions; they should not silently change the data or create trading authority.

## 12. Study design before any predictive promotion

Predeclare the unit of analysis as a company-business-mechanism observation, with a dated information cutoff. Use a limited number of hypotheses, explicit horizons and a comparison set that includes failed recoveries. Keep all observations for the same issuer and related companies from leaking between dependent folds. Training on a later corrected financial history while claiming as-known performance is not permitted.

Separate two evaluation jobs. The first asks whether the system interpreted the evidence correctly: matched definitions, appropriate ownership, valid arithmetic and calibrated abstention where evidence is missing. The second asks whether the interpretation added useful forecasting or decision information. Passing the first does not prove the second.

Candidate explanatory outcomes include subsequent end-demand, recurring contribution, inventory conversion and credit losses, not just share returns. Market outcomes should use predeclared horizons and suitable sector/factor controls. Report sample size, uncertainty, costs, failed hypotheses and sensitivity to rebalance conventions. Do not optimize a handful of impressive examples into a universal signal.

An ablation should compare raw sales/earnings changes, ordinary sector context, market-only context and the proposed mechanism-aware explanation. Another should remove sensitive assumptions such as undisclosed sell-through or a guessed customer cohort. If usefulness disappears when speculative inputs are removed, the correct result is an evidence gap, not an invented data point.

No coefficients, probabilities, prediction intervals or expected returns were estimated in R3. No simulated example is labeled a backtest. The current output is research that makes later evidence collection and implementation more discriminating.
