## External research synthesis
Institutional thematic/index systems demonstrate useful separation between granular business classification/exposure and investable expression. Revenue/business-activity and supply-chain evidence are more meaningful than label matching; themes can cross conventional sectors; baskets need separate investability/weighting methodology. Evidence retrieval platforms demonstrate the importance of traceability but do not solve ontology/PIT/state authority.

Academic/quant literature gives a credible rationale for testing economically coherent group information and delayed diffusion: industry momentum, customer-supplier cross-predictability, dynamic product-market similarity and investor attention. It does not prove arbitrary modern theme baskets generate alpha. The highest-value hypotheses are transitions and disagreements rather than “themes that went up keep going up.”

Primary literature/methodology families to retain in the final evidence bibliography: Moskowitz & Grinblatt (industry momentum); Hong, Torous & Valkanov (industry lead/lag); Cohen & Frazzini and Menzly & Ozbas (economic links/cross-predictability); Hoberg & Phillips (dynamic product-market networks); Da, Engelberg & Gao (attention); official FactSet RBICS/Revere, S&P Kensho New Economies, MSCI/FTSE/Morningstar thematic methodology documentation.
