## Primary-source evidence map

These are directly inspected primary publication, author or official-methodology sources. No published effect size is imported as a GMI expected return. Where only an abstract or an earlier author manuscript was inspected, that limit is stated.

### Academic rationale

**R1 — Moskowitz and Grinblatt (1999), Do Industries Explain Momentum?** [Published paper](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/0022-1082.00146); [author-associated description](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Do-Industries-Explain-Momentum). The inspected material supports industry-level momentum and its contribution to individual-stock momentum, emphasizing intermediate horizons. It does not establish a 1–3-session or intraday signal, modern source-local thematic baskets, or child acceleration plus breadth. Use as rationale for a plain group-momentum baseline and horizon-specific tests. Publication abstract and author-associated summary inspected, not a full independent re-estimation.

**R2 — Hong, Torous and Valkanov (2007), Do Industries Lead Stock Markets?** [Published version](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X06001383); [author manuscript, December 2005](https://www.columbia.edu/~hh2679/industry-12-05-05.pdf). The author manuscript tests monthly industry-to-market predictability using lagged industry returns, macro predictors and lagged market returns. Crucially, industry signs need not all be positive: economic transmission can differ by industry. This supports testing specified lagged information channels, not a universal child-leads-parent law. It examines conventional industry portfolios and an older historical sample, not GMI subtheme labels. The journal full-text fetch was blocked; author manuscript and journal search metadata were inspected.

**R3 — Cohen and Frazzini (2008), Economic Links and Predictable Returns.** [Author-hosted published paper](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~afrazzin/pdf/Economic%20Links%20and%20Predictable%20Returns%20-%20Cohen%20and%20Frazzini.pdf). The paper studies documented material customer-supplier links and whether lagged customer returns predict supplier returns after controls. It provides an economic-link hypothesis and shows why own returns/industry effects must be controlled. It does not validate arbitrary thematic co-membership, any particular modern forecast horizon, the completeness of today's supplier data, or universal positive spillovers. Its original return estimates are historical study findings, not Mastermind performance forecasts.

**R4 — Menzly and Ozbas (2010), Market Segmentation and Cross-predictability of Returns.** [Publisher](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01578.x). The inspected abstract supports cross-predictability for economically related supplier/customer industries and variation with analyst coverage/institutional ownership. This gives a falsifiable attention/information-friction interaction. It does not imply every discovered graph edge has a stable causal or directional meaning. Full empirical replication was not performed.

**R5 — Hoberg and Phillips (2016), Text-Based Network Industries and Endogenous Product Differentiation.** [Publisher](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/688176); [authors' TNIC data documentation](https://hobergphillips.tuck.dartmouth.edu/tnic_basedata.html). Product-description text supports annually evolving, firm-centered competitive similarity rather than one fixed industrial bucket. This supports preserving multiple, time-varying relationships and separating similarity from economic transmission. It does not turn embedding proximity into a supplier link, prove real-time theme discovery, or certify predictive alpha. The publication abstract and official data documentation were inspected; a source's annual data label still needs its actual historical availability clock before PIT use.

**R6 — Da, Engelberg and Gao (2011), In Search of Attention.** [Author-hosted published paper](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/Google.pdf). In its 2004–2008 Russell 3000 sample, Google search volume measured a form of retail attention associated with near-term price pressure and subsequent reversal. The paper explicitly distinguishes attention proxies and attention from sentiment. Thus attention is a separate, horizon-dependent observation; it is not bullish thesis confirmation, economic growth, positioning or a generic crowding score. Ticker ambiguity, source coverage and the exact search-vintage construction matter for any new implementation.

## Measurement contract that should precede predictive evaluation

1. **Objects and sampling.** Identify the evaluated object as canonical theme, source-local concept, issuer, security, curated basket or consumer decision. Economic theme membership is not an investable portfolio. Deduplicate multiple share classes at the issuer level for membership breadth; define the representative tradable security separately. Primary market/currency/session rules must be explicit. Leave cross-market lead tests in a separate protocol if market closes are asynchronous.

2. **Cohort and return rule.** For a frozen-cohort outcome at decision time t, preserve the eligible membership and starting weights at t. Keep subsequent members out of that outcome. Include corporate actions, delistings and exits under a predeclared rule instead of dropping bad performers. A dynamically rebalanced thematic index is a separate outcome requiring its own published-in-advance membership and rebalance schedule. Use total-return and currency conventions consistently. “Current membership applied to history” stays a descriptive counterfactual, even if the price data are old.

3. **Named price measurements.** A daily group return is the sum of start-of-period weights times member total returns. For an H-session strength observation, compound the daily strategy returns under the stated rebalance policy. Relative strength must name its benchmark and whether it is arithmetic excess return, return ratio, or a risk-model residual. Never use those labels interchangeably. Acceleration requires a declared formula; a reasonable research candidate is the difference between recent and earlier adjacent-window average residual returns, scaled using only trailing volatility. Rank change is different and depends on changes to the comparison universe. Multiple formulas are separate research trials.

4. **Breadth and concentration.** Breadth is a named condition's weighted or unweighted fraction among observable eligible members, reported alongside observed/eligible/expected counts and coverage. Denied or missing observations are not decliners. HHI is sum of squared normalized nonnegative weights; effective weight count is its reciprocal. Report positive-return and negative-return contribution concentration separately when needed; near-zero net returns make net-contribution percentages unstable. A cohort of 20 correlated names does not supply 20 independent observations.

5. **Exposure and economic evidence.** Distinguish disclosed revenue share, estimated revenue share, profit sensitivity, capex intensity, customer dependence, qualitative role, and forecast exposure. Preserve source document/accession, fiscal measurement period, first-known date, extraction method/reviewer, units, numerator/denominator, and an evidence interval/range if a point estimate is unjustified. Overlapping theme shares need not sum to 100%; sums across different measurement kinds are prohibited. Negative exposure means a declared scenario can hurt cash flow or value, not simply negative price correlation. Unknown magnitude remains null.

6. **Expectations and recognition.** “Economic improvement” needs a domain-specific metric and baseline; “expectation improvement” needs a PIT forecast/consensus revision or a separately identified expectations proxy. Economic change without expectation change can be fully anticipated. A recognition proxy can measure price response following a time-stamped economic surprise, but a stronger “underpriced” claim requires a named expectations/valuation model and its own evaluation. None follows automatically from a flat chart.

7. **Evidence quality before alpha.** Validate extraction, identity, mapping, timestamp, source support and abstention on blinded stratified examples independently of returns. Verify true negatives, unmapped local concepts, misleading ticker/name collisions, overlapping roles, sparse firms and contradictory evidence. Return prediction cannot certify that an economic claim is factually correct.

The settings below are proposed research designs, not validated thresholds or authorization to alter policy. ### Common preregistration and promotion rules

Each manifest declares experiment ID/version, source contracts and hashes, claim/estimand, full eligible universe, object and cohort construction, decision cutoff, benchmark, primary horizon/outcome/loss, baseline/challenger specifications, feature and model versions, feature-availability and abstention rule, nonoverlap/cooldown policy, correction handling, all attempted variants, family of confirmatory tests, minimum practical improvement, dependence-aware interval method, analysis date/information threshold and stop/futility rule. Freeze fitted parameters or the legal schedule for re-fitting from past-only data.

Use blocked walk-forward training with all transformations and threshold choices fitted inside training periods. Purge training records whose outcome intervals extend into held-out decisions; introduce separation where feature construction or temporal overlap requires it. Preserve the entire cross-section inside time-block resampling and examine economic-family/member overlap rather than randomly shuffling theme rows. Require enough independent time/family information to distinguish the declared benefit margin; insufficient power is INCONCLUSIVE. Do not look weekly and stop at the first favorable p-value without a predeclared sequential design.

For a small sealed primary-test family, a familywise method such as Holm at a proposed 5% family level is a defensible starting choice; an exploration screen may use a declared false-discovery method. These are design choices for the Evaluation owner, not laws derived from the finance papers. Promotion requires both uncertainty-adjusted incremental utility beyond the practical margin and acceptable harm/coverage/latency limits. A scalar score without probabilistic meaning is not tested with probability calibration; Brier/log loss and reliability plots apply only after a probabilistic event forecast exists.

### E1 — Does child information predict the rest of its parent?

**Primary estimand:** reduction in out-of-sample forecast loss for the next 20 completed sessions' parent-minus-child frozen-cohort excess total return when acceleration and breadth are added. A 5-session result is a predeclared secondary horizon. Use the same decision cutoff, benchmark, cohort availability and return conventions across comparisons.

**Cohort definition:** parent-minus-child is the parent constituent complement: eligible PIT parent members at the cutoff, excluding all child constituents and normalizing remaining starting weights under the frozen rule. It is not parent index return minus child index return. Require an admitted parent relationship and observable complement; otherwise abstain or mark not applicable. Match random children on cohort count, starting weight concentration and trailing volatility. Missing prices remain subject to the frozen missingness rule.

**Baselines:** parent-minus-child's own lagged returns plus market regime/volatility; then add the child's ordinary lagged relative return and cohort size/concentration. **Challenger:** add the frozen acceleration and participation-change measurements. Compare the interaction term against an additive model as a separate predeclared trial. Sparse parent-minus-child cohorts abstain.

**Falsifiers:** no improvement beyond the strong price-only baseline; benefit vanishes after excluding child members, matching cohort volatility/size, or deduplicating overlapping campaigns; matched random child partitions perform similarly; evidence only appears in the previously discussed CPU/Memory episode. Whole-parent returns remain descriptive secondary outputs. This tests forecasting association, not causal transmission.

### E2 — Does economic evidence improve company expression selection?

**Primary estimand:** incremental forecast/selection utility for companies with documented theme exposure conditional on the same PIT theme state. Start with one domain-defined economic shock family, a 20-session company return outcome and a separately predeclared next-disclosure operating outcome. Do not combine unrelated sectors' operating metrics numerically.

**Baselines:** theme membership only; then lagged company/sector returns, size, liquidity, concentration, known revisions and the incumbent company's evidence. **Challenger:** typed direct/proxy exposure magnitude or role interacted with an independently time-stamped economic surprise. Freeze the relation and magnitude before the surprise. Keep expected beneficiaries, adverse exposures and nonexposed matched firms separate.

**Falsifiers:** exposure is assigned after favorable returns; incremental value disappears against revisions already in the baseline; association is present equally in unrelated links; sign contradicts the declared cash-flow mechanism; only heavily documented large companies benefit while sparse coverage is silently dropped. A useful descriptive exposure map can survive even if the predictive interaction fails.

### E3 — Does GMI improve Prophet's existing process?

Freeze the pre-GMI candidate ledger, incumbent score/context, intended horizon and allowed candidate budget. Compare the incumbent policy with a shadow policy using one GMI feature family at a time on exactly those opportunities, including no-coverage cases. Report rank/forecast improvement plus candidate-level opportunity costs, selection overlap, adverse excursions and coverage strata. If the product's useful horizon is 1–3 sessions, that is the primary test; success at 20 sessions cannot substitute.

**Falsifiers:** GMI merely reproduces existing sector/momentum evidence, gains disappear after realistic entry time/costs, improvement comes only from changing the eligible population, or harm exceeds the predeclared limit. Keep automatic rank/gate authority off; retrospective policy replay and shadow forecasts alone do not demonstrate causal improvement in users' realized decisions.

### E4 — Does Terminal context reduce alert burden or improve timeliness?

Freeze the incumbent event definition, alert budget, deduplication/cooldown and allowed timestamps. Choose one tactical horizon owned by Terminal and compare event recall/detection lag at the same false-alert budget with and without GMI context. Record source event time, GMI production time, Terminal receipt time and first feasible action time. Count events never alerted and alerts with no ensuing event; do not score only successes.

Calibrate thresholds and cooldowns only on permitted development/training data, then freeze for confirmation. Report achieved held-out false-alert burden alongside recall/lag. Any predeclared operating-curve comparison is an evaluation statistic, not authority to deploy a threshold selected using confirmatory outcomes.

**Falsifiers:** nominal lead exists before the source could have been received, signal is stale at first feasible action, fewer false alerts are bought by unacceptable missed events, or the comparison allows the challenger many more alerts. A daily thematic label can improve context without earning intraday alert escalation authority.

### E5 — Does discovery improve semantic coverage without polluting the graph?

Create a blinded, pre-outcome adjudication set of proposed concepts/relations and difficult negatives, stratified by domain and local/canonical status. Compare LLM/embedding proposals to the incumbent curation workflow for acceptance precision, missing-concept recall where a reference set exists, duplicated/incorrect mappings, abstention quality, time to adjudication and reviewer burden. Reviewers see source evidence, not future returns. Separately track how many accepted proposals actually gain lawful observations and consumers.

**Falsifiers:** higher nominal coverage consists of duplicates or speculative labels; reviewer time grows disproportionately; supposed economic links are only lexical similarity; latent future information enters historical adjudication. Do not require semantic discovery to predict returns in order to be useful, and do not let return performance legitimize an incorrect ontology.

### Corrections to W2's interpretation

**W2-C1 — High, confirmed mathematical error: common shrinkage does not inflate Spearman rank stability.** Prereg 64–65, report 423–427 and code 195–203 say shrinking beta would inflate H3 stability. The exact implementation is b_shrunk = 0.66*b + 0.34*mean(b), with the same positive slope and constant within each cross-section. Consequently every ordering and tie is preserved: for any i,j, sign(b_shrunk_i − b_shrunk_j) = sign(b_i − b_j). H2 and H3 use Spearman ranks (code 248–255), so their values are unchanged on the same observations in exact arithmetic. Dispersion is reduced, but that is a different statistic. Exceptions would require rounding, clipping, noncommon shrinkage, numerical pathologies or changed missingness; none is in this function.

A deterministic demonstration executed the exact function extracted from the source AST without importing/running the probe: x = [-0.2, 0.3, 0.3, 1.4, 2.0], y = [-0.1, 0.5, 0.1, 1.1, 1.8]. Receipt: [W2 rank-invariance check](audit_v3/W2_RANK_INVARIANCE_CHECK.json), with the [audit-only source probe](audit_v3/w2_rank_invariance_probe.py).
