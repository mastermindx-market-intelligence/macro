## Supplied facts

The BLS release labelled March 8, 2024 reports February nonfarm payroll employment up 275,000 and unemployment at 3.9%, up 0.2 percentage point.
Payroll employment comes from the establishment survey; unemployment comes from the household survey. Household employment was 161.152 million in January and 160.968 million in February; the civilian labor force was 167.276 million and 167.426 million respectively.
December payroll growth was revised from 333,000 to 290,000 and January from 353,000 to 229,000; the combined revision was -167,000. Revisions incorporate additional reports and recalculated seasonal factors.
The release describes different survey coverage and sampling precision. It gives roughly 130,000 and 600,000 as monthly-change significance thresholds for establishment and household employment, respectively.

## Root adjudication (judge only)

The two surveys measure different populations and concepts, so payroll growth and a higher unemployment rate are not a same-contract contradiction. Household employment fell 184,000 while the labor force rose 150,000; their difference implies 334,000 more unemployed, subject to the release's rounding. The household employment change is below its stated approximate significance threshold; the payroll change exceeds its separate stated threshold. Those thresholds do not alone establish a recession, causality, a directional trade or confidence in every subgroup. The unemployment-rate increase is 20 basis points, not 0.2 basis points. The -167,000 revision describes prior months and must not be presented as February payroll growth; 275,000 minus 167,000 equals 108,000 arithmetically but is not the released February change.
