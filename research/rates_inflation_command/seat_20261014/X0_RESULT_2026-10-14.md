first-print surprise co-move with Treasury yields, consistently, in both the 2010s and

**This is descriptive co-movement only.** It is not a prediction, not a sizing input and
not a causal explanation of why rates moved. H3's vocabulary is closed:

## 2. Method in one paragraph

- **Surprise.** The first print measured against a **no-change benchmark**. No consensus series is licensed here, so this is *not* a consensus surprise: part of what it measures was expected.
- **Response.** The release-day change in the daily close, from the prior trading day to the release day (h0, the only tested window), in bp. The next-day window h1 is descriptive only.
- **Coefficient.** beta is bp per one standard deviation of surprise, estimated separately for the 2010s (2010–2019) and 2021+.
- **Correction.** Holm is applied within each era across the eight tests. A test is identified only if it clears under both normal and t p-values **in both eras, with the same sign**.
- **Exclusions.** Days that collide with another tier-1 release are dropped and named (`collided_with`, 468 occurrences in the receipt).
- **Episodes scored.** CPI 177, NFP 184.

**What it says, plainly.** The protocol's standard asks for a stable reaction. On that
standard, we cannot say from this data that a CPI or payrolls surprise moves Treasury
yields by a stable amount.

- The CPI reaction looks like a 2021+ phenomenon: in the 2010s it is flat, or opposite in sign.
- The payrolls → 2-year reaction clears in the 2010s but not in 2021+.
- The 2021+ point estimates are larger everywhere, which fits a more data-dependent policy regime. That reading is a hypothesis this run does not identify, and it is not a finding.

Nothing here is promoted to any consumer, score or sign.

## 4. Disclosures carried from the independent review

- **Dual-p disclosure (review SF8).** A test clears only if *both* the normal and the t p-values clear after Holm. CPI→DGS2 2021+ clears under the normal p but not under the t p, which is why it is marked borderline and not cleared. Both p-values are printed for every row.
- **Dependence among responses.** T10YIE is close to DGS10 − DFII10 by construction. The four responses therefore are not four independent tests, and the eight-test family overstates independent evidence. This makes the Holm correction conservative, not lenient. With every test not identified, it changes no label.
- **§7 source-outage carve-out (review §5, seat accepted).** The PPI and GDP date receipts came through the owner-remedy path, which §7 allows on a source outage. They were committed and rights-stamped *before* the run, and their bytes are hash-checked in the receipt. The run is therefore not demoted.
- **Non-blocking notes.**
  1. `sign_flip` can fire when a beta is missing. It should instead emit `beta_missing`. That did not occur in this run: all 16 betas are present.
  2. When the release date equals `asof`, h1 reads the next close. This is descriptive only, and does not apply here.
  3. The CPI core m/m surprise is a `published_proxy_1dp`, rounded to one decimal.
  4. The ledger reason format differs between the M0 and X0 runners.
  5. The budget is declared before `_read_receipt`. The order was harmless in this run.
