# Optical connectivity and fabrication: source-grounded pilot cases

Research supplement to `MASTER_PLAN.md`, October 2, 2026 (America/Los_Angeles).

These are candidate curation/evaluation cases, not canonical Theme Graph records, an investable universe, a current leadership ranking, a historical signal or a forecast. The companies were selected because they motivated the user's question; this is therefore not an unbiased backtest sample. No source was proven to have been ingested by Mastermind on its historical publication date. Dates below are publication dates from the retrieved primary pages; missing intraday availability remains unknown.

## 1. The refinement that matters

A company-level tree is too coarse, but the opposite shortcut is also wrong: Lam should not simply be declared unrelated to lithography. Its disclosed work includes etch/deposition and High-NA EUV process development. The distinction is between process steps and tools, not a binary 'lithography company' flag. A scanner supplier, a resist/process partner and a photonics supplier can all mention similar terms while having different demand and profit sensitivities. [C4-C5]

A common AI backdrop is not proof that one group's gains came from money leaving another group. These cases support testing several economic mechanisms independently; they do not establish the contemporaneous relative-return ordering.

## 2. Case register

### C1 - Lumentum: separate an established narrative from later evidence of commercialization

Primary source date: November 4, 2025. Lumentum's first-quarter fiscal 2026 release already discussed optical circuit switching and co-packaged optics as prospective growth drivers. Fiscal-year naming must not turn this into a November 2026 event.

Primary source date: August 11, 2026. Its fourth-quarter release discusses OCS and cloud modules, 1.6T adoption, CPO laser demand, an initial external-laser-source module order and NPO engagements. These are distinct disclosed product/commercialization claims; they do not all represent the same stage. [C1a-C1b]

Candidate research: distinguish additional demand in established products from a newly commercializing product. Test whether the change in orders or deployment evidence adds information beyond the already-public narrative and prior returns.

Do not infer: that all optical demand is new in October, that an engagement equals recognized revenue, that the initial order implies a disclosed product-level revenue share, or that the stock is currently an attractive entry.

What changes the read: shipment/revenue evidence, conversion of engagements, customer qualification delays, pricing pressure, capacity/yield constraints, or a material architecture change. Product-level economics and consensus comparisons remain unmeasured in this case packet.

### C2 - Coherent: announcement is not launch, and launch is not realized revenue

Primary source date: August 25, 2026. Coherent announced that it would unveil PhotonLink on September 21 and described support for CPO and NPO.

Primary source date: September 21, 2026. The launch describes integrated photonics and assembly/test for CPO, NPO and emerging chip-to-chip links. It reports more than ten engagements each for CPO and NPO, more than five for chip-to-chip, and management's expectation for a revenue ramp beginning in Q4 calendar 2026. These are company statements, not independent confirmation of future revenue. [C2a-C2b]

Candidate research: maintain one related event family with stage-specific updates. Isolate what was newly learned at launch versus what had already been announced. Compare the information delta with changes in estimates and prices, using actual availability timestamps.

Do not infer: that August and September are two independent confirmations, that a September 22 syndicated article is the first source, that forecast revenue has already occurred, or that a scheduled webcast time equals the release's actual publication time.

What changes the read: realized revenue/shipments, customer-specific deployment evidence, margin and capital needs, qualification conversion, competitive substitution or rollout delays. Product-page breadth does not establish economic materiality for each component.

### C3 - Coherent: product-path decomposition is finer than a sector label

The current PhotonLink product page organizes the optical path around generation, shaping, guiding and detection. It describes components and integrated assemblies rather than a single generic 'laser' business. [C3]

Candidate research: represent the disclosed products/functions and their relevant architectures as separate evidence-backed exposure claims under the existing owner. Retain both component and integrated-assembly participation without double-counting the issuer in a basket.

Do not infer: that a full-stack product portfolio gives equal revenue exposure to every stage, that every product is capacity-constrained, or that four functional labels create four independent investment opportunities. The dynamic product page's historical content vintage was not recovered here.

### C4 - Lam Research: process enablement and long-horizon R&D

Primary source date: March 10, 2026. IBM and Lam announced a five-year collaboration for sub-1nm logic development, including new materials, etch/deposition and High-NA EUV process work. The announcement names Lam's dry-resist, etch, deposition and packaging technologies. [C4]

Candidate research: represent complementary process exposure and expected technology-adoption horizons. Distinguish a long-horizon development agreement from an order, shipment or next-quarter earnings catalyst.

Do not infer: that Lam sells the same scanner systems as ASML, that the R&D agreement proves commercial revenue today, or that a six-month-old announcement is a fresh explanation of October price strength without new corroborating evidence.

What changes the read: production qualification, adoption, share/content per wafer, customer capital plans, timing changes and commercial receipts. This packet does not estimate the revenue contribution of the collaboration.

### C5 - ASML: equipment demand can relate to both logic and memory

Primary source date: July 15, 2026. ASML's second-quarter results describe demand from advanced logic and memory investment, customer capacity expansion and plans to increase low-NA EUV and DUV immersion capacity in following years. These are management demand and capacity statements; future capacity plans are not current shipments. [C5]

Candidate research: distinguish equipment/process intensity and future installed capacity from near-term chip-price or memory-stock performance. A price leadership difference between memory and equipment need not imply that their operating economics have opposite signs.

Do not infer: a current shortage from a future expansion plan alone, or the size/timing of a supplier read-through without separate evidence. Linkages need signed economics, not blanket propagation of 'AI demand'.

### C6 - Micron: price rotation and business deterioration are different hypotheses

Primary source date: September 30, 2026. Micron reported fiscal-year/fourth-quarter results and described AI-driven demand and memory/storage activity. The corresponding SEC 8-K identifies that result announcement and reporting period. [C6a-C6b]

Candidate research: compare changes in memory fundamentals and expectations with relative price recognition rather than label memory fundamentally weak because another group is outperforming. The report's publication date, fiscal period end and stock-response window are different clocks.

Do not infer: that record historical results establish future stock leadership, that management's outlook equals consensus, or that this packet has verified the current memory-versus-optics price ranking. No reported financial figure is used here to size a position.

## 3. Required event-model tests derived from the cases

1. Fiscal year 2026 can contain a November 2025 publication: normalize the actual reporting and source dates.
2. Scheduled announcement, actual launch, initial order, engagement, shipment, revenue and management guidance remain distinct.
3. One original source appearing in many articles does not increase independent evidence count.
4. A later event update cannot retroactively improve the features of the earlier event.
5. CPO, NPO, optical circuit switching and fabrication processes retain different economic meanings even when a company participates in several.
6. A single issuer across multiple functions cannot create artificial breadth or independent portfolio exposure.
7. Long-horizon R&D and future capacity targets must not inherit near-term revenue/earnings labels.
8. A public page available now does not establish its historical content or actual prior Mastermind ingestion time.
9. Positive operating news in two groups does not establish a zero-sum capital migration between them.
10. Do not nominate the case companies into a graded historical universe just because they were chosen for today's question.
