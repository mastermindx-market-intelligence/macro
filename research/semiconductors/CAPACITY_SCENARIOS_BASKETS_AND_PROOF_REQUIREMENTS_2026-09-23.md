## 3. Recover the issuer's denominator before interpreting the trend

### UMC reconstruction

From C06's Q2 table, the native cells are 80 thousand 6-inch wafers; 1,102 thousand 8-inch wafers; and 795 thousand 12-inch wafers. The source's squared-diameter conversion gives:

`80 × (6/12)^2 + 1102 × (8/12)^2 + 795 = 1304.7778 thousand 12-inch-equivalent wafers`.

That agrees with the rounded reported 1,305 thousand. Prematurely substituting the printed rounded 0.44 factor changes the result. Summing unconverted wafer counts would be wrong. Area equivalence does not equalize die area, defect density, process complexity, edge loss, product mix or yield.

The 1,129 thousand shipment figure divided by capacity gives 86.5134%, but reported utilization is 85% because its numerator is wafer **output**, not shipments. Preserve both definitions. The SEC C27 exhibit confirms the reported utilization and selected release facts but does not contain the full C06 factory table; no full mirror byte validation is claimed.

## 4. Tested arithmetic: five explanations a useful system should challenge

These are deliberately simplified **synthetic counterexamples**, not measured company yields, cost estimates, forecasts or trade rules. The offline diagnostics use exact rational arithmetic. No application or production model was built.

### A. Larger wafers need not initially lower variable cost per good unit

Assume the same die design, wafer count, process-cost boundary and loading. Ignore edge/layout differences. A 150mm-to-200mm transition increases geometric area by `(200/150)^2 = 1.7778`.

With old yield 90%, new yield 70%, and processing cost per wafer 60% higher, acceptable output rises by `1.7778 × 0.70/0.90 = 1.3827`. Variable cost per good unit changes by `1.60/1.3827 = 1.1571`, or **+15.71%**. If the new yield later reaches 90%, the same simplified cost ratio becomes 0.90, or **−10%**.

The industrial conclusion is not that a particular transition fails. It is that diameter alone cannot answer the cost question. Actual die mix, recipe, yield, utilization, start-up costs and capital depreciation must be researched separately. Unknown yield stays unknown; known zero output makes unit cost undefined, not a fabricated average.

### B. More volume can coexist with less contribution

An old illustrative business sells 100 units at price 10 with variable cost 6: contribution is 400. A new case sells 130 units at price 8 with variable cost 5.4: contribution is 338, **15.5% lower** despite 30% more units and a lower unit cost. About 153.846 units would be needed to restore the old contribution under those new price/cost assumptions.

These are contribution calculations before fixed costs, financing and taxes, not reported gross margins. Supply relief can improve customer access and simultaneously pressure supplier prices. Its economic effect needs both channels.

### C. A relieved upstream constraint may leave finished output unchanged

For an explicitly complete, compatible fixed route with upper bounds of 150 logic, 110 memory, 90 packaging and 100 test finished units per period, the necessary-input bound is 90. Increasing only logic to 220 leaves it at 90. Increasing packaging to 140 then moves the bound to 100, where test binds.

This is a toy bound, not a factory scheduling or equilibrium prediction. Product mix, shared tools, routing, yields and allocation still matter. Unlike units, missing stages and incomplete routes refuse the calculation rather than become zero or infinite capacity.

### D. Period-end and relocated capacity must not inflate actual supply

Synthetic monthly outputs of 40, 70 and 100 total 210, not 300 obtained by multiplying the final month's run rate. A relocation with 40 newly qualified units and 60 retired units has an initial net change of −20, even if the destination's eventual nameplate is 100. That eventual target would imply a different future net change, not an already achieved one.

Joint-venture capacity is counted once physically even when displayed from two parent views. Conflicting observations for the same facility require reconciliation, not summation.

### E. Later knowledge is not historical knowledge

A July filing describing a Q2 operational event supports a subsequent research view but not an earlier June source-availability claim without another source. Unknown publication time cannot be replaced with midnight merely to make a point-in-time replay convenient.
