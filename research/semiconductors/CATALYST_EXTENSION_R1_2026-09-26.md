### 2.1 Micron 8H HBM3E: an easy way to manufacture false lead time

The 26 February 2024 announcement already reported volume production. March prepared remarks reported first revenue in fiscal Q2; June remarks reported fiscal-Q3 revenue exceeding $100m and margin accretion. These are distinct milestones. Sources: MU-LAUNCH, MU-Q2, MU-Q3.

**D2:** an observation first admitted after the target is disclosed cannot earn credit for predicting that target. Keep two research populations: pre-commercial observations forecasting a precisely defined future confirmation, and already-commercial ramps forecasting subsequent economics. An 8H revenue observation is not a first-volume prediction and is not a 12H qualification outcome.

The phrase “sold out” also needs a scope: period, product, allocation versus binding quantities, price coverage, cancellation terms and capacity denominator. It does not by itself establish end-customer demand, realized pricing, economically good yield or the amount of shareholder value still missing from the price.

June remarks separately describe DRAM pricing strength and lower bit shipments. That is contrary evidence to a volume-only explanation of company revenue growth. The roughly three-to-one same-bit/same-node HBM-versus-DDR5 wafer trade relationship is an issuer estimate, not a 33% device yield or a universal capacity conversion. Source: MU-Q3.

### 2.2 A financial result can beat the midpoint without beating the range

The same-period, same-basis comparison is deterministic: fiscal-Q4 Micron revenue of $7,750m is $150m above the $7,600m guidance midpoint but below the $7,800m upper bound. Non-GAAP gross margin of 36.5% exceeds the prior 35.5% upper bound by 100 basis points. Sources: MU-Q3-RESULTS and MU-Q4-RESULTS.

**D3:** preserve metric identity, fiscal period, accounting basis, perimeter and currency. Publish midpoint surprise and interval exceedance separately. The result does not isolate HBM's causal contribution, prove a stock-price surprise, or establish an attractive entry valuation. No consensus vintage or contemporaneous stock reference has been admitted in R1.

### 2.3 Micron 12H and NVIDIA Blackwell: do not convert conditional wording into success or failure

Micron's September remarks describe 12H units that enable customer qualification and a later expected ramp. This must not inherit the 8H commercial outcome. Source: MU-Q4. NVIDIA's August CFO commentary describes a mask change to improve Blackwell yield and a fiscal-Q4 ramp outlook. It does not supply a numeric yield or, alone, prove a delay against an earlier comparable promise. Source: NV-BLACKWELL.

**D4:** maintain configuration-specific evidence and the vintage of every timing promise. “Delay” requires an identified endpoint and an earlier forecast window; otherwise it is ramp risk or unresolved timing, not a fabricated failed-event label. Physical production, public confirmation and customer approval are distinct observation processes. The Blackwell terminal outcome remains unfilled in this installment.

### 2.4 NVIDIA H20: event clock, publication clock and economic loss are different

The recovered 8-K reports government notices on 9 and 14 April 2025; its SEC index shows acceptance on 15 April at 17:22:59. The recovered index page does not itself establish the timezone conversion. Sources: NV-H20-NOTICE and NV-H20-INDEX. A private notification date is not a public-information trading timestamp.

The initial charge estimate was up to $5.5bn; the later reported charge was $4.5bn. The company separately described $2.5bn of unshipped sales and subsequently a $180m reserve release. Sources: NV-H20-NOTICE, NV-H20-Q1, NV-H20-Q2.

**D5:** inventory write-downs, unavoidable cash commitments, salvage, foregone revenue and profit contribution need separate lines and explicit overlaps. Adding the charge to foregone revenue as independent equity destruction is invalid. Revisions update the relevant branch; they do not erase the original estimate. Policy licensing gets its own outcome model, not a semiconductor-qualification prior. This is a realized adverse event case, not a measured stock-return failure or a claim that the whole company failed.

### 2.5 onsemi: stronger free cash flow does not establish stronger demand

Using the issuer's annual cash figures, the $774.0m FCF improvement decomposes into a $71.1m decline in operating cash plus an $845.1m benefit from lower capital expenditure. Source: ON-FY2024. The reference calculation reconciles exactly.

**D6:** earnings, cash generation, reinvestment and shareholder return are separate outcomes. A short-term cash improvement may reflect slower spending, not superior design-win conversion. This annual company result is not proof that a named SiC agreement failed. Weighted-average EPS shares likewise cannot silently become the future diluted denominator for a valuation.

## 3. Event families and measurable observations

The following is the research coverage architecture, not a claim of complete source enrollment or calibrated coverage. Existing GMI owns membership and identity.

| Event family | Potential leading observations | Proper endpoint / main falsifier |
|---|---|---|
| Design-in / customer qualification | Product-specific samples, qualification language, customer confirmation, required tests and dated scope | Defined customer/product approval or commercial confirmation; missing confirmation is not automatic failure |

| Foundry customer adoption | Qualified process/customer scope, committed versus planned orders, utilization definition | External billable economics, not internal transfers or total wafer area |

| Export/license policy | Primary rule/decision scope, effective dates, issuer exposure, inventory and shipment rights | Permission, shipment and economic recovery are separate targets; reversals and partial permissions remain explicit |

## 4. Research model direction and its identification limits

**D7: separate the target populations before estimating a prior.** The initial engineering proof slice is commercial-ramp/economics comparison, not an assertion that latent customer qualification is forecastable from public text. Micron is a source-rich research fixture; it is not yet proven enrolled in the implementation's limited witness scope. TSMC and onsemi remain the incumbent witnesses. Their evidence cannot be pooled as interchangeable event-success labels.

For a future qualified forecast, represent evidence about qualification, orders, usable output, pricing/mix and economics separately. The industrial process is not forced into a universal monotonic ladder: requalification, product changes, alternate sites, withdrawals and reversals are allowed. Customer budgets, manufacturing dependencies and policy states are common causes. Supplier and customer claims that describe the same system build must not be summed as independent evidence or upside.

The first empirical comparison should use a simple, disclosure-scoped competing-outcome baseline and a partially pooled event-family model. Outcomes must include on-time confirmation, late confirmation, adverse/cancelled, competing/superseded and unresolved. A public-confirmation model is not a model of the latent physical event date. Record both when evidence allows, and use interval uncertainty otherwise.

No prior, numerical probability, confidence band or universal sample threshold is supplied in R1. Those need a source-complete denominator and a predeclared test. Sparse qualification histories remain qualitative evidence; the product can still show conditional economics and what evidence is missing without manufacturing a 70% score.

A suitable economic decomposition is:

`compatible delivered good units = min(qualified demand, allocated good-output capacity, allocated compatible packaging/test capacity)`.

Each input must use the same product/configuration and period. Raw wafers are not HBM stacks. An already yield-adjusted good-output count must not be multiplied by yield again. Revenue then needs net realized price, cannibalization, cancellations and contractual rights. Margin needs cash variable cost, fixed absorption, depreciation, ramp charges and operating expense. Cash needs taxes, working capital and investment. A reported charge is mapped once to the affected cash/asset/earnings path, not added again downstream.

## 5. Executed diluted-equity reference, explicitly fictional

**D8:** financial arithmetic is deterministic once assumptions are specified; it is not a source of calibrated probabilities. `catalyst_reference_r1.py` is a pure, isolated research calculator. It imports no Mastermind production module, writes no store and exposes no production authority.

The Fictional ChipCo worksheet uses one annual horizon, USD, a fictional $25 initial price, no dividends and round-trip costs equal to 1% of initial capital. Legacy enterprise value is $1,600m, opening unrestricted cash $100m, debt $300m and initial shares 100m. Branch enterprise values are authored horizon assumptions, not a fitted DCF. Legacy cash flow, net interest, buybacks, dividends and other claims are zero in this isolated example; real capital/tax/convertible schedules remain owner work. Pre-horizon cash is not also capitalized into horizon enterprise value.

| Branch | Revenue ($m) | Unlevered FCF ($m) | Ending cash ($m) | Diluted shares (m) | Conditional value | Net total return |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Profitable ramp | 1,000 | -16 | 84 | 100 | $36.84 | +46.36% |
| Same volume, price compression | 800 | -176 | 66.50 | 110 | $15.15 | -40.40% |
| Delayed ramp / financing | 500 | -150 | 45 | 110 | $16.77 | -33.91% |
| Failed launch / residual company | 0 | -90 | 10 | 100 | $13.10 | -48.60% |

The first two branches both ship two million compatible units. In the second, $150m gross equity issuance at $15 with a 5% fee adds $142.5m cash and ten million shares. Borrowed cash adds matching debt rather than free value. An unresolved negative cash balance withholds equity value. The rest of the company is valued once, including in the failed-launch branch.

The bridge is `(horizon EV + unrestricted cash - debt - other claims) / horizon diluted shares`; negative residual common value is floored at zero only under the explicit limited-liability residual scenario convention. A dated spot price, future share count, currency/ADR mapping, taxes, retained rights and claims must be owned inputs in the real implementation. There is no current real-stock valuation in this installment.

**D9: price-implied expectations are an identification problem.** Under the worksheet's fixed branch values, zero net-return convention and 1% cost, the $25 reference implies a $25.25 terminal-value target. Different branch mixtures fit it. The profitable-ramp weight can range from approximately 42.24% to 51.18%. The reference enumerates the vertices of the one-moment, nonnegative, sum-to-one scenario-weight problem. It does not estimate physical probabilities or an independently observed market belief. Out-of-range prices return infeasible rather than clipped 0%/100% values. Risk premiums, additional moments and uncertainty in the branch values would change the inference.

No expected return is emitted because no probabilities are qualified. A $20 price-only projection preserves the same branch values and changes returns only. This implements the conceptual boundary of the shared decision-review contract without claiming a production policy integration.
