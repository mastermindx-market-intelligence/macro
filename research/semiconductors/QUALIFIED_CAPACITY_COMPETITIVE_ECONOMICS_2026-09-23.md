## 1. What this research resolves

The earlier installments explained semiconductor functions, manufacturing dependencies and demand/economic drivers. This module resolves a more discriminating question: **Which apparently available supply is actually relevant to a specific demand, and who captures the economics when that changes?**

The proposed intelligence must distinguish physical expansion, technical capability, customer-qualified capacity, actual output, shipment, supplier revenue and shareholder economics. Neither a larger wafer diameter nor an additional factory is a sufficient answer. Unused aggregate capacity may coexist with a shortage of a particular qualified product; that is an analytical possibility to test, not a claimed universal current shortage.

A useful readout should explain the constraint, its scope, the next observable release condition, the likely economic transmission mechanism and the strongest alternative interpretation. A map containing more factories but lacking those distinctions would not meet the user job.

## 3. UMC: a resolved denominator and geometry example

UMC's recovered July 29 issuer release provides the previously missing capacity detail. It defines estimated capacity using calculated maximum output rather than designed capacity, with actual outcomes affected by equipment timing and process migration. Its conversion footnote uses wafer-area ratios. [C06]

A bounded reconstruction from its displayed Q2 factory cells is:

- 6-inch wafers: 80 thousand.
- 8-inch wafers: 215 + 125 + 118 + 131 + 146 + 117 + 250 = 1,102 thousand.
- 12-inch wafers: 409 + 172 + 95 + 119 = 795 thousand.
- Area-equivalent total: 80 × (6/12)^2 + 1,102 × (8/12)^2 + 795 = approximately 1,304.78 thousand, consistent with the rounded reported 1,305 thousand.

The printed 0.44 conversion is rounded; prematurely using it instead of the stated squared-diameter formula loses precision. Raw mixed-diameter counts cannot be summed. Area-equivalent wafers remain an accounting comparison of wafer area, not a good-die, bit, application-performance or yield equivalence.

Similarly, 1,129 / 1,305 is approximately 86.51%, but the issuer's reported 85% uses **wafer output** in the numerator. We must preserve both the definition and the report. It is not a contradictory data point merely because the shipment-based quotient differs.

## 4. Commercial roles: complementary contracts need not conflict

A semiconductor company may use different manufacturing partners for different device generations or voltage families. The safe representation is a relation scoped by product, process, application, agreement stage and applicable date. One partner name does not overwrite all other relationships.

The two Navitas examples show why a global 'manufactured by' field is insufficient. The Oxford–ROHM case identifies a specific process supplier rather than an unnamed industry beneficiary. The onsemi–Innoscience MOU must preserve its nonbinding state. These are industrial relationship facts, not evidence of exclusivity, interchangeable allocation or revenue purity. [C08,C09,C12,C13,C14]

The supplier-quality evidence supports this caution. Infineon's published notification process includes transfers to previously unqualified fabrication, assembly or test sites and requests affected parts, qualification results and relevant approval material. onsemi's AEC/PPAP portal resolves status by device. Renesas says its AEC-Q101-qualified products are designated in their datasheets, not that all company products qualify. [C17,C18,C21]

Research proposal: preserve facility quality-system evidence, device stress-test qualification, customer part approval and actual delivery as separate evidence. Do not convert a general company or fab certificate into approval for an arbitrary part/application. No new interpretation of the standards or blanket qualification duration is invented here.

## 5. Competitive economics: five different outcomes under one technology label

### Optical demand can drive the equipment company tagged as a power-semiconductor beneficiary

AIXTRON's July 30 release attributes about three quarters of equipment order intake to optoelectronics while describing power electronics and LEDs as awaiting recovery. Its strong first-half cash generation is substantially connected to customer advances. [C11] Therefore the proposed analytical basket must explain the **actual source of order acceleration**, not assign all issuer growth to SiC/GaN or treat advances as recurring profit.

### Design and application momentum can coexist with unprofitable production

Wolfspeed's August 19 release describes AI-related growth but reports negative gross margins. It also explicitly warns that fresh-start accounting from September 29, 2025 breaks comparability between predecessor and successor accounts. [C05] Technology relevance is therefore insufficient for a profitability conclusion. Historical per-share comparisons require the appropriate corporate-action and accounting bridge.

### Comparable-looking business series can change perimeter

X-FAB's Q2 table says GaN enters the wide-bandgap grouping from Q1 2026 and photonics enters its other grouping from that date. It also separates an IFRS 15 over-time revenue adjustment. [C15] A year-on-year category growth number is not automatically a pure same-perimeter SiC growth rate. Store series definitions and reconciliation, not just values.

### A real Robotics application is not automatically material Robotics revenue

Innoscience reports 464,600 humanoid-application chips against 547 million total chips for H1 2026. Its filing describes the application as early commercialisation. [C16] These are chip counts, not robot counts or revenue weights. A count ratio is an explicitly limited descriptive measure, never a forecast, market share or financial-exposure proxy.

### Capacity competition changes both supply reliability and price economics

Our hypothesis is that qualified additional supply can reduce delivery risk while intensifying price competition. Whether margins improve depends on realized yield, product mix, selling price, cost per good unit, fixed-cost absorption and capital employed. None of the factory announcements supplies all those inputs. This is a proposed scenario framework with explicit missing data, not a current stock-price conclusion.

## 6. Proposed qualified-capacity representation

This is a research requirement for existing native owners, not a new production schema or state machine. Each observation should retain:

1. Source-scoped facility and line identity; physical site separate from parent/legal ownership.
2. Process/material, wafer diameter, product/generation/configuration and application scope.
3. Announced, installed, process-qualified, product/customer-qualified, producing, shipped or retired status, with exact source language.
4. Observation type: cleanroom area, equipment count, gross wafer capacity, wafer output, shipments, good units, revenue or allocation.
5. Unit, period, average-versus-period-end basis, gross/net basis, native conversion and rounding convention.
6. Historical fact, issuer target, conditional plan, catalog claim, derived calculation or analyst hypothesis.
7. Relevant dates separately: publication, source observation, business applicability and future target window.
8. Known limitations, customer-binding status, compatible alternatives, shared upstream dependencies and requalification triggers.
9. Revision/supersession evidence without destroying earlier point-in-time knowledge.

The factory stages are not assumed universally linear: qualification can occur in parallel, a product can move to a second line, a process can be requalified, and an approval can cease to apply. Absence of public proof is unknown, not proof that no production exists.

## 7. Geography and aggregation requirements

The map should offer separate layers for physical fabrication, epitaxy/substrates, packaging/test, ownership, customer destination and shared upstream dependencies. It should not use incorporation or headquarters as a manufacturing proxy.

Count physical capacity once. For a joint venture, keep ownership economics separate from wafer output. For a relocated process, distinguish the old capacity retired from the new line added. For a co-located memory campus, distinguish NAND wafers and HBM packaging. For a multinational supplier, distinguish geographic diversification from validated substitute supply: two sites can still depend on the same qualified material, process IP or test partner.

No cross-country risk ranking, trade-policy conclusion or geopolitical forecast is produced. Regional coverage is incomplete: attempts to recover primary SMIC/Hua Hong current detail were unsuccessful. Secondary figures remain leads, not accepted observations.

## 8. Initial research questions for the subsequent scenario module

- When added wafer area is discounted by yield and ramp availability, how much usable output is actually implied?
- Can a plausible cost reduction offset a simultaneous selling-price decline and higher fixed costs?
- Which constraint is binding after a wafer bottleneck is relieved: package, test, approval, or customer deployment?
- Can the same growth headline be explained by price, volume, mix, acquisition or a change in the reporting perimeter?
- How should research memberships preserve competing effects without turning confidence into portfolio weights?
- What evidence would distinguish local qualification scarcity from broad industry shortage?

The next same-turn action is to turn these into bounded calculations, falsifiable monitoring cards and useful shared-template requirements. Persistence of this evidence module is not a reason to stop the research turn.
