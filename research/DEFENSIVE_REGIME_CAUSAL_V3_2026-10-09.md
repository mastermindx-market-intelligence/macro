## 1. Central finding: match a trading style to a causal claim

The proposed sequence is macro/issuer development → operating cash-flow and funding implications → changed expectations/required return → repricing → observable price acceptance → executable entry. Operational defensiveness does not establish price or valuation safety. A ticker's historical personality can change when its business, financing, expectations or investor environment changes.

Maintain two main entry paths. An intact-business pullback needs evidence that the business thesis survived the price reset. A deterioration-to-stabilization reversal can become interesting before operating levels are healthy, but requires evidence of a change in trajectory and a defensible downside case. Requiring all indicators or fundamentals to be green would exclude many reversals; accepting every oversold reading would admit business deterioration. A material issuer-event state is separately classified, not silently absorbed into either style.

Prioritize the difference between expected cash-flow deterioration and the reset already embedded in price. No consensus-vintage or reverse-DCF dataset was completed here, so this remains a proposed predictor rather than a measured mispricing score. A hypothetical 8% increase in expected EPS combined with a 15% lower multiple implies -8.2% price change; improving earnings alone is insufficient.

## 2. Regime/style map

| Observable environment | Mechanism to investigate | Appropriate research style | Shortcut rejected |
|---|---|---|---|
| Real yields down, credit stable, issuer outlook intact | Benign required-return relief | Intact-business pullback / valuation recovery | Every rate decline is bullish |
| Yields up with broad demand and stable credit | Growth can offset discount-rate pressure | Continuation / shallow reset | Every rate increase is bearish |
| Real yields or term premia rise without earnings improvement | Valuation compression | Wait for stabilization and forward-value room | Buy every oversold reading |
| Inflation and household squeeze | Pricing, volume, substitution and effective costs diverge | Issuer-specific relative-value or margin recovery | Inflation helps all staples |
| Credit/volatility stress | Risk-premium and funding pressure can dominate | Capital preservation or bounded stabilization research | Relative defense means positive absolute return |
| Orderly defensive rotation | Industry demand for exposure without broad liquidation | Supported pullback / continuation | Sector/index ratio alone proves safety |
| Narrow index leadership / mixed transition | Index concentration and breadth disagree | Selective low-confidence diagnosis | Equal-weight lag proves an imminent index top |
| Issuer event / structural break | Deal, legal, product or financing change dominates | Separate event-risk assessment | Old oscillator statistics survive every business change |

Nominal-versus-real/breakeven decomposition and expected-short-rate-versus-term-premium decomposition are different lenses. Do not double-count components or treat breakevens as pure inflation expectations. Policy and asset prices respond jointly to news; ordinary correlations do not identify causal policy shocks. [1–4]

## 5. Additional ideas to test, not optimized claims

1. Price-versus-point-in-time-expectations reset: compare price damage with the plausible change in recurring cash flow and required return. Falsify against matched exposure and known events; missing forecasts stay unknown.
2. Reaction to fresh information: measure whether another adverse release causes less incremental relative downside, or good news repeatedly fails. Event/window definitions must precede future returns; do not infer institutional activity without data.
3. Change in deterioration: reversal and continuation styles require different operating predicates. Easier comparisons or shipment timing must not masquerade as stabilization.
4. Hedge-adjusted input-cost transmission: map effective costs to the relevant earnings horizon rather than use a contemporaneous spot correlation. Include pricing/volume reinvestment offsets.
5. Separate stock/industry, industry/index and absolute-price behavior. They answer different questions and are not independent votes. Test missed winners and delay costs, not precision alone.
6. News-anchored recovery: select the anchor deterministically after a known material event, never from a future pivot. Label daily-bar volume proxies accurately.
7. Corporate-era tagging: use independent business events to define structural changes. Do not invent regimes after seeing losses.

Conditional inverse channels can be stated economically, but **no V3 correlation sign or coefficient has been accepted**. Rates can hurt valuation while helping operating income; FX exposure is net of costs/hedges; oil can affect both expenses and renewable revenue; defensive and technology equities can fall together in a common discount-rate shock.

## 6. Allocation and evidence requirements

Do not equate high hit rate with favorable expected value. Hypothetically, a 70% chance to gain1 and30% chance to lose3 gives -0.2 units before costs. A screen can raise precision while rejecting the largest winners or increasing residual loss severity.

Assess return over cash, market exposure, tail/gap risk, liquidity, uncertainty and shared risk clusters. WM/RSG, ADP/CTAS, global consumer/FX names, retailers and cocoa-sensitive producers can share economic risks despite different tickers. This is a qualitative risk map, not a covariance matrix or recommended weight vector. Actual holdings, risk budget and calibrated forward evidence remain required for live allocation.

The workbook checklist has eight mandatory evidence requirements: available data, defined style/countercase, issuer economics, current events/structure, macro interpretation, valuation/payoff, technical/execution definition and portfolio risk. It does not require every underlying factor to be bullish. A critical failure produces Reject; missing evidence produces Incomplete; a completed record produces Research candidate only. Five formula cases, including fail/unknown/missing-source handling and restoration, passed.
