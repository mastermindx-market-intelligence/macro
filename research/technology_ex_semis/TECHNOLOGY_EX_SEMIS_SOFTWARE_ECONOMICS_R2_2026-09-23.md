## 1. What this research unit adds

R1 established the whole-sector map. R2 investigates D01–D07 through 29 named company/business examples, 34 primary-source records, seven native economic models, a metric-comparison grammar, three reproducible management-guidance bridges and concrete proposed product behavior. The examples are deliberately chosen to expose different economics and disclosure failures. They are not an exhaustive software census, a securities identity registry, a new basket, a ranked stock list or a validated return model.

The central finding is that the next useful Technology capability is not another theme label. It is the ability to answer: **did an apparent improvement change paid demand, forward expectations or retained economics, or did its measurement, timing or business perimeter change?** A correct answer can remain useful even when an exact exposure percentage, customer usage series or trade conclusion is unavailable.

The source register in section 11 identifies the original public documents. Dates in the dossiers are reporting periods unless explicitly called publication dates. Management forecasts, claims about product adoption and our analytical hypotheses retain separate status. A current reading of a document is not historical first-observation proof or native source retention.

## 2. The seven economic models

### D01 — Enterprise application and workflow economics

Research product/workflow and buyer budget separately: sales/customer service; finance/ERP; HR/payroll; collaboration; IT service/workflow; marketing; procurement. A workflow can use multiple products, and an issuer can sell under several pricing arrangements. The proposed accounting bridge is installed paid customers × applicable paid units × realized price, plus separately measured usage or transactions, less contraction/churn and with recognition timing explicit.

The leading observations to investigate are paid deployment, renewal cohort, expansion at unchanged perimeter, accepted go-live, realized price, implementation completion and customer budget allocation. Generic user count, software availability or a feature launch does not supply those observations. Outcome-based pricing may expand the addressable budget beyond a seat, but it can also replace seat revenue or shift delivery risk onto the vendor. The research must test both effects.

**Salesforce — application/workflow plus AI charging alternatives.** The August 26 Q2 release revises the scope of Agentforce ARR, while the public pricing page offers credit-, conversation- and user-based arrangements [S01–S02]. Research consequence: retain product-definition vintages and price-unit/entitlement versions. A billable action, conversation, user seat and useful completed business task are different denominators. Investigate renewal after paid deployment, incremental recurring economics after legacy substitution, and realized discounts. Do not infer paid yield from a catalog price or apply the new ARR definition retrospectively without a bridge.

**ServiceNow — enterprise workflow monetization.** The July 22 release distinguishes AI annual contract value from subscription revenue and remaining obligations [S03]. Research consequence: separate contracted adoption from recognition and useful production operation. The relevant causal hypothesis is workflow expansion within a customer, supported by implementation and renewal, rather than AI ACV mechanically becoming next-quarter sales. Test whether integration and implementation capacity delay conversion, and whether new products expand the budget or substitute for existing commitments.

**SAP — enterprise-system migration.** Its Q2 release reports current cloud backlog and Cloud ERP Suite revenue separately, including constant-currency growth [S04]. Research consequence: track committed migration, accepted go-live, legacy license/support transition, partner implementation and cloud cost. Cloud growth can coexist with declining legacy activity. The necessary value question is the consolidated recurring and cash outcome after transition, not the cloud line alone. Industry-specific rollout and customer change-management constraints belong in the mechanism.

**Workday — HR/finance and attached AI.** Q2 distinguishes twelve-month backlog from total backlog; the release also identifies a $1.52-per-share tax benefit and describes AI participation in new ACV [S05]. Research consequence: new-business mix, total installed-base revenue and accounting earnings are separate. Test paid expansion against customer employment, finance transformation and cross-sell, without treating agents used as a disclosed AI revenue share. A tax-related EPS increase does not establish stronger operating demand.

**HubSpot — smaller-business customer platform.** The Q2 disclosure reports customers, average subscription revenue per customer and calculated billings as different metrics [S06]. Research consequence: segment paid-customer additions, product/tier mix, price realization and churn. Average customer revenue is not a pure price index: cohort mix can change it. Investigate whether higher functionality widens paid adoption or whether customers consolidate seats, down-tier or leave; retain the issuer's exact annualization definition before comparison.

### D02 — Vertical workflows and transaction-enabled software

Industry-specific software needs a second lens: the customer's operating cycle. For an insurer, life-sciences organization or merchant, the trigger for spending and the cost of migration differ. Separate customer-business activity, platform conversion, implementation, paid usage and recurring revenue. A vendor can serve a sector without acquiring that sector's research ownership. Medical software is not drug efficacy; merchant software is not automatically a payment-company classification.

**Guidewire — insurer core-system conversion and contractual ramp.** Fiscal-2026 disclosure distinguishes constant-currency current ARR of $1,242 million from fully ramped ARR of $1,578 million; rebased amounts use a different currency basis [S07]. Research consequence: the $336 million difference is a defined contractual-ramp measure, not additional current revenue, new annual bookings or a guaranteed next-twelve-month uplift. Investigate implementation schedules, customer acceptance, contractual step-ups and renewal. Revenue recognition and services cost can lead or lag that ramp.

**Veeva — industry-specific commercial and development workflows.** The Q2 release distinguishes customers already live on Vault CRM from later commitments and anticipated future go-lives for other offerings [S08]. Research consequence: retain product, business function and actual deployment status. A signed customer and an operational implementation cannot share a single adoption count. Research commercial migration separately from clinical, regulatory, safety or quality workloads; test time to live deployment, retained subscription economics and implementation capacity. Events described after quarter-end need their own dates.

**Shopify — commerce software with transaction-linked economics.** Its Q2 release reports merchant activity, revenue, gross profit and cash measures separately [S09]. Research consequence: merchant gross merchandise value is not vendor revenue. Do not estimate a single take rate by dividing mixed subscription and merchant-services revenue by total merchandise value. Investigate the eligible transaction denominator, payment/service attachment, merchant cohorts, gross-versus-net accounting and retained gross profit. Customer sales expansion and a higher monetization rate are different mechanisms.

### D03 — Data infrastructure and consumption economics

A useful first-order research identity is:

`Revenue = useful workloads × chargeable resource units per workload × realized price per resource unit`

This is a proposed economic decomposition, not an issuer accounting rule. Contract floors, credits, tiering, on-demand use, foreign exchange and recognition timing can require additional terms. The important consequence is that more useful workloads need not imply more billed consumption. Compression, caching, query efficiency, model efficiency and architecture changes may reduce paid resource intensity; whether demand elasticity offsets that reduction is an empirical question.

**Snowflake — consumption and defined retention cohorts.** The July-quarter filing reports 126% net revenue retention for both April and July 2026 and defines its two-year comparison cohort [S10]. Research consequence: do not call an unchanged sequential retention figure an acceleration. Separate compute/storage/data-transfer consumption, contractual obligations, customer organization scope and infrastructure cost. Query or AI adoption is financially useful only after connecting it to paid resource consumption and retained economics. Price optimization can improve customer ROI while suppressing vendor revenue per workload.

**MongoDB — operational data with channel-specific measurement.** Its Q2 definitions annualize consumption over 90 days for certain direct-sales Atlas customers and 30 days for self-service customers when calculating customer ARR [S11]. Research consequence: the same issuer's threshold cohorts can depend on different measurement windows. Atlas-related revenue is not identical to AI revenue. Investigate production workloads, customer/channel mix, resource intensity and migration from other deployment forms. Source-scoped customer definitions must survive comparisons with other vendors.

**Elastic — overlapping product and sales-channel views.** The Q1 fiscal-2027 table separates Annual Cloud, Monthly Cloud, other subscription and services, and also shows a sales-led subscription view [S13]. Research consequence: these are not all disjoint markets. Annual and monthly purchasing can behave differently, while search, observability and security are overlapping product purposes. Compare each native revenue partition once, then connect product purposes as non-additive exposures. Vector/retrieval functionality alone does not disclose its contribution to total sales.

### D04 — Developer, observability and automation economics

The economic unit may be a human seat, host, workload, log volume, telemetry event, pipeline execution, automation runtime, licensed capacity or successful task. More generated code is not automatically more paid developers, higher-quality releases or more profitable software. The model must allow automation to reduce one paid unit while expanding another.

**Datadog — observability consumption and account expansion.** Its Q2 release defines ARR from monthly recurring revenue multiplied by twelve, including committed amounts and additional usage; it explicitly distinguishes this metric from GAAP revenue or a revenue forecast [S14]. Research consequence: do not compare the $100,000 customer threshold mechanically with MongoDB's window-specific measure. Investigate workload/telemetry growth, data selection/retention policies, product adoption and bill efficiency. Cost optimization is a possible counterforce to growing infrastructure complexity.

**GitLab — software delivery and security platform.** Its Q2 release separately reports revenue, dollar retention, customer thresholds, RPO and current RPO [S16]. Research consequence: model developer collaboration, release workflow, security and AI assistance as product/workload exposures, not a single assumed seat trajectory. Product-specific pricing and contract limits still require deeper source work. Test whether automation-related use creates paid expansion, displaces human seats or is included in existing plans. Reported activity is not a measured marginal revenue stream.

**Atlassian — work management, knowledge and service workflows.** Its August 6 release gives fiscal-2027 growth targets of approximately 25.5% for Cloud and a 17% decline for Data Center, alongside approximately 13% total growth [S17]. Research consequence: whole-company deceleration must be decomposed by business transition. Those numbers are management forecasts, not realized results or a complete causal explanation. Track paid migration, expansion, renewal and the economics of the receiving product; an agent integration's monthly users are not paid customer additions.

**UiPath — automation subscriptions, licenses and execution.** Q2 reports ARR under the name Annualized Renewal Run-rate, while recognized revenue contains both licenses and subscription services [S18]. Research consequence: do not map its acronym into a universal annual-recurring-revenue field without its definition. Investigate installed automation, paid orchestration, workflow replacement, license/service mix and renewals. Agentic positioning does not prove incremental revenue; the guidance bridge below tests how much of a reported annual increase remains after the completed quarter.

### D05 — Security buying units, platform consolidation and business perimeter

Security product roles include endpoint/workload, network, identity, data, application/API, security operations and managed response. A supplier may charge by user, endpoint, workload, data volume, appliance, capacity or service commitment. The relevant protected unit and budget owner must be explicit. More machine identities or generated logs do not automatically create more revenue under every contract.

The principal research alternatives are: a growing protection budget; vendor share consolidation within an unchanged budget; a free/bundled feature increasing retention; and a workload change raising the vendor's cost. Treat these as competing explanations. Technical risk research here is business/evidence analysis, not exploit or offensive workflow development.

**CrowdStrike — installed-platform breadth versus incremental monetization.** The Q2 release reports net-new ARR growth separately from total ARR growth and describes nested adoption thresholds for six-or-more, seven-or-more and eight-or-more modules [S20]. Research consequence: never label the faster net-new growth as total ARR growth or add nested cohorts. Investigate whether platform commitments become paid product consumption, renewal and gross-profit expansion; platform presence alone does not reveal incremental module economics.

**Zscaler — cloud-delivered security and acquired scope.** Fiscal-2026 disclosure reports ARR of $3,771 million and $3,630 million excluding Red Canary, with different growth rates [S21]. Research consequence: the acquisition bridge belongs beside the growth number. Investigate seat versus non-seat demand, product attachment, customer consolidation, contract duration and implementation. Acquired revenue is economic exposure from the applicable ownership date, not organic validation of every subtheme.

**Okta — workforce, customer and machine identity.** Q2 outlook identifies approximately one percentage point of revenue-growth effect from moving professional services to partners [S22]. Research consequence: a business-perimeter/channel change can alter revenue without representing weaker identity demand. Research identity type, active-versus-registered population, paid entitlement and governance attachment. Human seat counts, consumer identities and software-agent identities require different denominators and cannot be added as paying users.

**Palo Alto Networks — several security platforms and acquisition lineage.** The fiscal-2026 release reports Next-Generation Security ARR; its earlier earnings announcement confirms CyberArk had closed earlier in 2026 [S23–S24]. Research consequence: do not preserve CyberArk as an assumed independent current issuer basket constituent without the canonical identity owner. Product capabilities can survive an acquisition while issuer scope changes. Organic growth, acquired contribution and product integration require separate evidence; the exact closing date is not established by this unit.

**Fortinet — appliances plus recurring services.** Q2 disclosure shows product revenue growing at a different rate from company revenue [S25]. Research consequence: an appliance replacement or deployment cycle is not the same as SaaS subscription expansion. Investigate shipment versus channel inventory, installed services attachment, support renewal, product mix and architecture substitution. Refer semiconductor dependencies to their incumbent owner; do not rebuild chip research or infer shortages from a product-growth headline.

### D06 — Cloud platforms, infrastructure and capital at risk

The proposed chain is contracted demand → financed/available capacity → qualified service capacity → paid utilization → recognized revenue → gross profit → cash return on deployed capital. Different contracts put equipment, working capital and utilization risk on different parties. Parent-company cash flow, segment operating profit and product revenue are not interchangeable.

**Microsoft — cloud, productivity and development tools.** Its July call distinguishes over 30 million paid Microsoft 365 Copilot seats from 50 million GitHub Copilot users; it also reports Microsoft Cloud gross margin at 65% [S26]. Research consequence: do not pool user populations or turn a lower-bound count into a precise growth rate. Model paid-seat and consumption mechanisms separately, with cost and product mix. Higher gross-profit dollars can coexist with a lower margin; neither observation alone completes the investment thesis.

**Oracle — two cloud economic models inside one issuer.** The September 10 release reports IaaS and SaaS growth separately and identifies equity financing during the quarter [S27]. Research consequence: infrastructure growth is not an applications growth rate. Distinguish commitments, capacity rollout, customer funding arrangements where disclosed, depreciation, financing and per-share economics. Large RPO does not eliminate delivery, duration, concentration or funding risk. Semiconductor supply remains an input dependency, not transferred scope.

**Amazon/AWS — segment operations versus parent cash.** The Q2 release separately discloses AWS operating results and consolidated free cash flow [S28]. Research consequence: never relabel parent free cash flow as AWS free cash flow or parent investment gains as AWS operating income. A quarter annualized is a run rate, not next-year guidance. Research cloud workload migration, realized usage, capacity utilization and reinvestment; the retail business remains outside this domain's principal scope.

**Alphabet/Google Cloud — backlog definition changes.** The June-quarter filing states that first-quarter 2026 backlog reporting began including contracts with original expected terms of one year or less [S29]. Research consequence: backlog growth requires a definition bridge, not only a growth calculation. Google Cloud's revenue is a scoped business observation, not a reassignment of Alphabet's formal sector. Customer utilization and ability to deliver affect backlog conversion; recognize the native contract perimeter before comparison with vendors that omit short contracts.

**Cloudflare — network services, security and developer workloads.** Q2 reports gross-profit dollars increasing while gross margin is below the prior-year rate [S30]. Research consequence: model network cost and workload mix alongside revenue. Traffic, requests, AI-agent activity and paid usage are separate. Research whether traffic growth is monetized, bundled, mitigated or costly, and whether new platform use expands committed spend. No particular traffic type is assigned a revenue share without evidence.

### D07 — Engineering, design, scientific and creative software

The economic buyer can be an engineer, project team, industrial organization, creative professional or regulated workflow owner. A design project's cadence differs from a production line's unit output. Paid seats, active projects, simulation jobs, compute use, token consumption, recurring contracts and lifetime platform adoption require separate observations.

The competing mechanisms are: more design/simulation demand from product complexity; fewer human seats from automation; greater value from validated outputs; substitution by another tool; and migration among perpetual, recurring and consumption charging. Chip-specific EDA/IP remains excluded pending the existing Semiconductor interface; this section concerns the non-semiconductor product/workload scope.

**Adobe — creator/productivity adoption versus paid monetization.** The September release reports a broad monthly-active-user population and rapid AI-first ARR growth [S31]. Research consequence: monthly users are not paid subscriptions, and growth without an absolute starting amount cannot establish material total-company exposure. Test conversion into paid use, retained subscription or usage economics, and generation costs. AI can expand creation activity while changing seat or credit demand; the sign is not assumed.

**Autodesk — design, make and customer-industry exposure.** Q2 distinguishes product-type and product-family revenue views and says reduced multiyear discounts affect RPO; its outlook includes MaintainX [S32]. Research consequence: do not sum the two reporting views or interpret all guidance growth as organic. Investigate architecture/construction, manufacturing and media workloads separately, with project budgets, paid renewal, contract duration and price realization. Software adoption does not measure downstream construction or manufacturing output directly.

**PTC — engineering/product lifecycle and divested perimeter.** Its quarterly-results page states current figures exclude divested Kepware and ThingWorx and supplies different currency views of ARR [S33]. Research consequence: old product catalogs cannot establish current PTC revenue ownership. Investigate CAD, product lifecycle, application lifecycle and service lifecycle scope using retained ownership dates. Compare like-for-like currency and business boundaries before inferring growth or theme participation.

**Dassault Systemes — engineering platforms and annual run rate.** Its Q2 disclosure reports recurring software, cloud and 3DEXPERIENCE views and uses Annual Run Rate as a metric [S34]. Research consequence: expand the acronym as the issuer defines it, rather than assuming a universal annual-recurring-revenue convention. Platform and delivery-form views can overlap. Investigate industrial customer budgets, simulation complexity, project timing and paid recurring conversion, without folding chip-design-specific tools into this program.

## 3. A metric dictionary that preserves meaning

The following are semantic requirements to map into incumbent financial, company and evidence owners. They are not a proposed new database or metric-registry authority. Each metric needs issuer/business scope, definition version, numerator, denominator where applicable, unit, accounting basis, period, source publication/observation clocks, and explicit absence/correction behavior.

| Metric family | Required distinction | Refuse or qualify when |
|---|---|---|
| ARR and similar acronyms | Full issuer expansion, annualization method, included products, run-rate or contract basis | An acronym is the only evidence of comparability |
| Consumption-derived ARR | 30/90-day or other observation window; seasonality and channel | Windows or cohorts differ without a bridge |
| Net-new ARR | Addition to an ending run-rate stock, with acquisition/FX rules | Used as total ARR growth or recognized sales |
| ACV | Contract annualization, new versus installed scope, duration and optionality | Presented as annual GAAP revenue |
| TCV/bookings | Contract term, committed versus optional, cancellation and start date | Long duration is interpreted as immediate demand |
| RPO | Short-contract exclusions, enforceability, recognition schedule, consideration | Different disclosure exemptions are silently merged |
| Current RPO/backlog | Explicit conversion horizon; stock as of date | Current is assumed to mean a universally identical horizon |
| Fully ramped ARR | Future committed unit/price steps and ramp schedule | Counted as current revenue or a next-year promise |
| Deferred revenue | Billed/collected status and recognition obligation | Used as a complete demand or cash proxy |
| Calculated billings | Revenue/deferred changes plus stated adjustments | Assumed equal to cash receipts |
| NRR/NER/DBNR | Revenue versus contract base, cohort membership, churn, FX, M&A, observation horizon | Ratios with different cohorts are averaged |
| Gross retention | Customer versus dollars; exclusion of upsell; window | Confused with net expansion |
| Customer count | Organizations versus accounts; affiliates, reseller end-users and free tiers | A common label hides different unit boundaries |
| Large-customer cohorts | Threshold currency, annualization and nested membership | Counts are added despite overlap |
| Paid seats | Paid entitlement versus active use and included seats | Availability or registrations substitute for payment |
| Monthly/weekly users | Product population, activity rule, paid status and duplicates | Used as paid customer or retention denominator |
| Product/module adoption | Threshold/cohort nesting; included versus incremental paid products | Adoption implies separate revenue for each module |
| Work units/actions | Task/action/outcome, retries, credits and bundled allowance | Billing units are described as successful business outcomes |
| Usage | Useful workload versus billable resources and optimization | Raw traffic or storage is called revenue |
| Price | List versus realized; discounts, commitment, geographic tax/currency | Catalog price is used as contracted realization |
| Revenue partition | Disjoint categories versus overlapping reporting lenses | Cross-view totals are added |
| Organic growth | Issuer definition, acquisition perimeter and FX | Total growth is renamed organic |
| Constant currency | Fixed-plan versus rebased rates and comparison year | Two constant-currency measures use different bases |
| Gross profit and margin | Dollars, rate and incremental cost separated | A rate decline alone is a demand verdict |
| Operating profit | GAAP versus adjusted reconciliation and segment allocation | Parent/segment or adjusted/reported figures are mixed |
| Free cash flow | Native definition, leases, capitalized software and working capital | Identically named cash measures are assumed identical |
| EPS/share count | Tax/investment effects, dilution, buyback versus authorization | Earnings improvement is attributed wholly to operations |
| Capex/capacity | Cash/noncash/lease basis; installed/qualified/available/utilized | Accounting reclassification is called physical spending change |
| Guidance revision | Selected vintage, horizon, actual-period roll-forward, perimeter | Beat-and-raise becomes a forward-demand signal without reconciliation |
| Theme exposure | Disclosed amount, derived share, modeled range, qualitative or unknown | Missing disclosure becomes zero or an arbitrary weight |

Some comparability failures can be resolved by a deterministic bridge. Others should remain side-by-side with a named scope difference. Do not manufacture a normalized number merely to fill a heatmap. Company-custom metrics can remain valuable without being suitable for cross-company ranking.

## 4. Paid adoption is a multi-dimensional evidence problem

Do not create one progression that forces every customer through a presumed sequence. Availability, trial, contracted entitlement, installation, active use, paid consumption, accepted business output and renewal are different observations. A customer may prepay before implementation, use a free tier indefinitely, adopt a bundled feature, or pay for a seat that is rarely used.

For a useful adoption record, specify product/version, named or anonymous customer scope, buyer/user, payment status, deployment state, observed activity, business output, period and source limitations. Known dimensions should display even when others are unknown. Examples: paid but activity undisclosed; live but contract economics undisclosed; large active audience but paid conversion unknown.

Proposed investor questions:

- Has a product moved from an attributed claim to a paid production use case?
- Is adoption expansion incremental, or part of an existing commitment or product migration?
- Does activity produce customer value sufficiently reliable to support renewal?
- Which cost or delivery obligation increases when the activity rises?
- Does the change survive the next renewal, price reset or contract-ramp checkpoint?

These are research questions, not an automatically promoted score. Strong evidence of product use can coexist with unknown supplier materiality or unattractive valuation.

## 5. Expectations research: remove the completed-quarter contribution

A full-year guidance increase is not wholly an increase in the outlook for future quarters. For selected comparable management-guidance vintages, define:

`B = newly reported quarter revenue - previous guidance midpoint for that quarter`

`G = new full-year guidance midpoint - previous full-year guidance midpoint`

`R = G - B - any revision to earlier reported quarters`

Here R is the change in the implied remaining-year revenue midpoint. It is not analyst consensus surprise, an organic-demand estimate or a return forecast. Check the same issuer, fiscal year, metric, accounting basis, currency and perimeter. A changed acquisition or FX outlook can remain inside R until separately quantified.

The three examples below use total reported revenue, USD millions, the specifically dated Q1 and Q2 company releases, and exact actual figures from the tables. Ranges retain their original precision. Midpoints are descriptive arithmetic, not probability estimates. We do not assert that these were the only communications between the selected dates.

| Company; selected publications | Prior Q2 guide | Q2 actual | Prior FY guide | New FY guide | Q2 above prior midpoint B | FY midpoint increase G | Implied remaining-year revision R |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Datadog; May 7 -> August 6, 2026 [S15,S14] | 1,070–1,080 | 1,121.454 | 4,300–4,340 | 4,450–4,470 | 46.454 | 140.000 | 93.546 |
| MongoDB; May 28 -> September 1, 2026 [S12,S11] | 729–734 | 771.773 | 2,920–2,960 | 2,990–3,030 | 40.273 | 70.000 | 29.727 |
| UiPath; May 28 -> September 3, 2026 [S19,S18] | 395–400 | 410.256 | 1,776–1,781 | 1,789–1,794 | 12.756 | 13.000 | 0.244 |

The previously reported Q1 figures reconcile to the new first-half tables: Datadog 1,006.426 + 1,121.454 = 2,127.880; MongoDB 687.616 + 771.773 = 1,459.389; UiPath 418.382 + 410.256 = 828.638. Consequently there is no earlier-quarter revenue restatement adjustment in these particular arithmetic examples.

An independent form subtracts actual revenue from each year's outlook: old implied remaining year = old FY midpoint minus Q1 actual minus old Q2 midpoint; new implied remaining year = new FY midpoint minus first-half actual. The differences reproduce R exactly using decimal arithmetic.

The result is a better question, not a preferred-stock ranking. UiPath's selected annual increase is almost entirely accounted for by the completed quarter, while the other examples retain larger remaining-year midpoint increases. Different business scales, valuation, expected conservatism, contract mix, acquisitions, currency and market expectations prevent an investment ordering from this table alone. Three selected cases establish a discriminating research transformation, not predictive validity.

Do not extend this subtraction mechanically to EPS, profit margins, ending ARR or ratios: these are not necessarily additive across quarters. For ARR, compare ending stocks and native net additions under their own scope; for margins, reconstruct numerator and denominator; for EPS, account for period-specific shares and tax/adjustment basis. Correlated guidance ranges must not be treated as independent statistical intervals.

## 6. Competing rerating hypotheses and falsifiers

The proposed research object should contain a mechanism, time horizon, required observations, alternative explanation, falsifier and confidence limitations. An evidence-supported hypothesis is not a model-originated trade. The following are analytical hypotheses, not claims that they currently hold across the sector.

| Mechanism | What should be observable if useful | Main competing explanation or falsifier |
|---|---|---|
| Paid AI expansion | Incremental paid units/usage and renewal at a defined contract perimeter | Free/bundled usage, relabeling, acquired sales or displacement of existing paid seats |
| Data workload growth | Useful workload and monetization expand after efficiency and price effects | Workload growth is offset by lower billable intensity or customer cost optimization |
| Security consolidation | Larger retained wallet and renewal economics at an unchanged or bridged perimeter | Free module adoption, acquisition or longer contract duration explains the headline |
| Enterprise migration | Accepted go-lives convert contracted demand into recurring cash economics | Delayed implementation, canceled migration or cannibalization overwhelms receiving-platform growth |
| Engineering complexity | More paid simulation/design work or durable price/value capture | Automation reduces paid seats; project delays or lower-cost tools capture the activity |
| Cloud capacity monetization | Paid utilization, delivery and cash return match deployment assumptions | Idle or customer-concentrated capacity, unfavorable funding, lower realized prices or delivery delays |
| Developer-platform expansion | Additional paid workflow/security/runtime demand exceeds displaced human-seat economics | Activity expands without incremental fees or with higher support/compute cost |
| Remaining-year revision | The reconciled forward outlook improves beyond the completed-period contribution | Perimeter/FX change or unusually conservative prior guidance accounts for the increase |
| Margin improvement | Operating or cash contribution improves with retained service quality and demand | Accounting changes, one-off gains or underinvestment produce the appearance |
| Valuation rerating | A durable change in growth, cash generation, risk or capital needs is not already priced | Multiple expansion already anticipates the optimistic case or discount-rate repricing offsets improvement |

Leading data should be ranked by proximity to the mechanism, not by how quickly it updates. A delayed signed contract may be more informative for one question than daily social mentions; real paid usage may be more informative for another. Source frequency is not independent predictive power. Earnings release, transcript and reposts from the same event should not be counted as three independent confirmations.

## 7. Valuation and regime: use assumptions rather than a universal multiplier

A comparable operating outlook is necessary but not sufficient for a rerating thesis. For software, assess sustainable gross profit, customer retention, sales efficiency, reinvestment, taxes, working capital, dilution and capital intensity. Compare EV/revenue only after explaining why revenue quality and retained economics differ. For cloud, a revenue multiple that ignores financing and replacement capital is especially incomplete.

Use three linked scenario layers: customer demand/usage and price; vendor profit/cash generation; and valuation/share-count/financing. Do not hide all three in a single score. A reverse valuation asks what combination of future growth, margins, duration and dilution a given market value would require. This unit did not load current prices or produce company target prices; a production implementation must consume the incumbent price and financial owners at aligned clocks.

Regime sensitivity is conditional. Falling discount rates could support long-duration valuations while falling customer spending weakens future cash. Higher rates could penalize a funded capacity expansion more than a cash-generative installed-base business. Tight budgets could hurt discretionary project work while supporting some cost-saving products, but proof requires paid adoption, not a marketing claim. FX affects translated results and can alter customer purchasing power; constant-currency comparisons need explicit definitions.

The empirical research should separate discount-rate exposure, customer demand exposure, funding/refinancing requirements and input-cost exposure. Investigate these interactions by archetype and horizon rather than assigning a fixed Technology beta or a rule that lower yields always improve every technology company. Estimated sensitivities and prediction intervals remain absent until a proper historical panel and evaluation support them.

## 8. Product design consequences

R2 recommends a first useful research capability: **Software Economic Change and Expectations**, inside the already-approved Technology/theme/company journey. This is a proposed domain module, not a new dashboard, scoring engine, source registry or approved implementation.

A reader should be able to start with a theme claim and reach a concise answer: what changed; what paid unit or financial measure supports it; how much is already realized; what remains implied for later periods; where the economic benefit could arise; what could invalidate it; and which evidence/price/entry owners have additional context.

The shared shell should present a source-scoped product/workload map, a metric definition drawer, a reporting-perimeter bridge, an expectations bridge and an economic interpretation with alternatives. Each is a rendering of accepted owner data or a documented deterministic transformation. The browser must not invent missing denominators, reconstruct independent ThemeState, scrape sources or quietly normalize incomparable values.

For example, a future UiPath research view can show the selected revenue guidance vintages and the calculated remaining-year midpoint change while separately displaying native ARR, renewal and license mix. It should not convert the near-flat residual into a bearish label. A MongoDB/Datadog comparison can show different ARR windows beside the figures rather than pretending the threshold cohorts are interchangeable. A Google Cloud backlog panel must disclose the short-contract definition change.

Layered software maps should organize capability and budget ownership rather than impose physical containment: buyer workflow -> application/orchestration -> data/runtime -> infrastructure, with separate hosting, integration, substitution and evidence relations. A platform may span layers. Only sourced customer/partner relationships are factual edges; plausible compatibility remains a hypothesis. Do not invent quantitative Sankey widths when disclosed monetary partitions do not exist.
