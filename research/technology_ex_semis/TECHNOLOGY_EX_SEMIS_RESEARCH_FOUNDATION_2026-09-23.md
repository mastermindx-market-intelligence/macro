## 1. Outcome and principal conclusion

The investor's job is to follow a change in technology spending or adoption through a particular workload, product and business; identify who captures revenue, gross profit and cash; compare that change with expectations and valuation; and reach the existing governed company, opportunity and entry workflows. The machine's job is to retain the causal distinctions, economic denominators, information vintages and counterevidence without forcing each reader to reconstruct the company from headlines.

The principal design conclusion is that Technology cannot be modeled as one taxonomy tree or one generic score. Structural industry classification, product function, workload, business model, theme exposure, financial materiality and investable security are different dimensions. AI is an overlapping demand/technology attribute, not a replacement for those dimensions. A positive adoption observation is not proof of supplier profit growth, valuation upside or an actionable entry.

This chapter establishes the research coverage and economic measurement grammar. It is not the final implementation plan. R1 contains 15 proposed coverage families with 144 research prompts, 16 economic archetypes, 15 primary-source records covering 14 company examples plus a classification reference, and 24 future acceptance cases. These counts describe research organization, not validated global coverage or live product capability.

## 3. Scope and semiconductor interfaces

Include non-semiconductor Technology products, software, services, systems, communications equipment, components, manufacturing/distribution and relevant engineering/measurement businesses. Use a provider-aware structural classification crosswalk rather than asserting that every technically sophisticated business belongs to the same formal sector.

Exclude dedicated semiconductor designers, foundries, IDM manufacturing, memory/logic cycles, fabrication materials/processes and semiconductor manufacturing equipment. Chip-specific test, EDA and IP require an explicit interface ruling; do not duplicate them here by relabeling them engineering technology. Systems/board-level engineering software and non-semiconductor measurement remain candidate scope. Sensor die are not the same object as a finished sensing module. Optical semiconductor devices are not interchangeable with assembled optical modules or complete networking systems.

A mixed company is not entirely excluded or entirely included by ticker. Research the relevant business/product and retain the excluded dependency as a referenced input. Likewise, an out-of-sector cloud operator, power supplier, cooling vendor, data-center property owner or customer can be an economic dependency without changing its formal classification or transferring the other sector's research mandate. Payments, entertainment, medical instrumentation and industrial automation need the same boundary discipline.

Current vendor taxonomy, source-local concepts, house research facets and investable baskets must have separately named authorities. GICS is a useful reference because it is explicitly a four-tier principal-business classification, not a multi-exposure product graph [S01]. This document does not supply a licensed company-by-company GICS assignment or replace Mastermind's incumbent provider classifications.

## 4. Ontology: seven connected dimensions, not one forced tree

### 4.1 Structural classification

Retain provider, version, classification date, industry hierarchy and original label. A later reclassification must not rewrite the classification visible in an earlier as-known replay. Different providers can disagree without either label being silently overwritten.

### 4.2 Business and product scope

Represent issuer, consolidated segment, business unit, source-named product/service, version or configuration, and applicable ownership dates. Until the existing identity owner supports global product identities, retain source-scoped descriptions as the Robotics design proposes. A product family name is not automatically a legal entity or canonical theme.

### 4.3 Workload and buyer

Ask what work the product performs, who uses it, who approves the budget and who pays. A developer user, CIO buyer, procurement team and business-unit economic beneficiary can be different parties. Model enterprise, small business, consumer, hyperscaler, telecom operator and regulated/public buyer contexts separately where disclosed.

### 4.4 Economic archetype

Describe the charging unit, recognition basis, contract duration, cost unit, capital intensity, working-capital cycle and renewal/replacement mechanism. A single issuer can contain several archetypes. Product function does not uniquely determine the revenue model.

### 4.5 Research themes and mechanisms

Use overlapping facets such as AI deployment, cloud migration, security consolidation, data modernization, enterprise cost optimization, product refresh, bandwidth transition or engineering automation. A facet is a research filter until accepted by the existing ontology owner. A new facet must not automatically acquire a price basket or ThemeState.

### 4.6 Economic relationships

Keep separate relations for physical containment, supply, distribution, cloud hosting, implementation, interoperability, ownership, customer use, substitution and an attributed transmission hypothesis. An integration logo does not prove a procurement relationship; a commercial relationship does not prove material revenue; material revenue does not prove causal stock sensitivity.

### 4.7 Security and measurement

Use existing validated issuer/security/listing bindings for stock analytics. Separate direct revenue share, gross-profit share, contract exposure, installed-base exposure, operational dependency, narrative association, empirical price sensitivity and portfolio/basket weight. They have different denominators and cannot be represented by one ambiguous exposure percentage.

## 6. Sixteen economic archetypes and their rerating hypotheses

The following are analytical decompositions to be populated and tested, not empirically validated return rules. Revenue equations are simplifying economic bridges, not replacements for issuer accounting policies. Price, mix, FX, acquisition scope and recognition timing can require explicit residual terms. The same product/business can legitimately use multiple rows.

| Archetype | Economic bridge and leading observations to investigate | What could support rerating; what could invalidate it |
|---|---|---|
| A01 — Seat/organization subscription | Paid customers × paid seats or organizations × realized price × recognized service period. Inspect cohorts, paid expansion, churn, discounting and renewal dates. | More durable expansion and cash generation at a given valuation. Invalidator: free/bundled seats, shrinking paid population, price concessions or acquisition-driven growth. |
| A02 — Usage-based software | Sum of billable compute, storage, data, requests or other units × realized unit prices. Separate raw workload, efficiency and billable usage. | Workloads and monetization grow with attractive incremental gross profit. Invalidator: efficiency or competition lowers chargeable units/prices faster than adoption expands. |
| A03 — Transaction-enabled software | Contracted base fees plus eligible transaction count/value × realized fee, with principal/agent accounting explicit. | Higher merchant/customer activity, attachment and net take rate without disproportionate credit/support cost. Invalidator: pass-through volume, mix-driven take-rate decline or risk transferred onto the provider. |
| A04 — Term/perpetual license plus maintenance | License recognition + eligible maintenance/support + subscription migration. Track duration, renewal economics, pricing and recognition policy. | Recurring quality and installed-base monetization improve. Invalidator: a reporting or channel transition inflates apparent growth, or longer contracts inflate bookings without stronger annual demand. |
| A05 — Outcome/AI hybrid software | Base commitment + chargeable tasks/outcomes/credits, less included allowance and concessions. Track paid production usage, verified output quality, unit cost and renewal. | The vendor captures useful customer value after inference, support and reliability costs. | A06 — Capital-intensive cloud capacity | Available capacity × paid utilization × realized yield, with explicit depreciation, leases, power, networking and maintenance/reinvestment. | Capacity converts into durable profitable usage and cash returns. | A07 — Consumer/device installed-base business | Units × realized selling price/mix, plus separately scoped installed-base services and accessories. Invalidator: inventory pull-forward, mix reversal, refunds or one-off benefits misread as permanent pricing power. |
| A08 — Enterprise system vendor | Accepted shipped systems × realized system price, plus integration/support/financing. Reconcile component content and customer acceptance. | Design/integration value and cash conversion improve along with demand. Invalidator: material-cost inflation raises revenue but not value added, delayed acceptance or financing absorbs cash. |
| A09 — Networking architecture supplier | Deployed systems/ports × generation/configuration mix × realized price, plus software/service attachment. Follow named customer programs and qualification. | Increased content or share at an architecture transition and persistent service economics. Invalidator: concentrated customer timing, architecture substitution, inventory digestion or falling price per bandwidth overwhelms demand. |
| A10 — Qualified electronic component supplier | End-system units × component content × supplier share × realized price. Separate commercial qualification, inclusion and actual procurement. | Higher content, qualification barriers and sustainable margin capture. Invalidator: a catalog capability becomes an invented design win, second sourcing erodes share, or acquisitions explain the apparent demand acceleration. |
| A11 — Build-to-print manufacturing | Accepted production × manufacturing value added, with material ownership and pass-through explicit. | A13 — Distribution/channel | Recognized resale revenue or net commission, gross profit and working-capital return. Cybersecurity is not a seventeenth charging model: it can use subscription, consumption, appliance, managed service and outcome-like contracts. Engineering software similarly spans licenses, subscriptions and compute consumption. Treating industry labels as financial archetypes would lose this distinction.

## 7. Rerating: separate operating improvement, expectations and valuation

For a consistently defined positive earnings basis, the identity is:

`Price = Revenue × Net margin ÷ Diluted share denominator × P/E`

Consequently the corresponding log change decomposes into revenue growth, margin change, share-denominator change and multiple change. This is an accounting/valuation identity, not a forecast. The earnings period, adjustment basis and share denominator must match. Changing from trailing to forward earnings or switching GAAP/non-GAAP mid-series breaks comparability. Negative or zero earnings make the log/P-E expression unusable.

For other businesses, use an enterprise-value bridge to revenue, gross profit or cash flow with a documented debt/cash/other-claims bridge to equity. Do not attach an arbitrary revenue multiple and call it an intrinsic value. Normalized cash economics, reinvestment, financing and dilution still matter.

The research should estimate a vector rather than one Technology score:

- operating trajectory: volume, usage, price/mix, share, retention and margin;
- expectation change: management guidance, available dated consensus, revision breadth and implied expectations;
- value capture: gross profit, cash conversion, reinvestment and per-share economics;
- valuation state: scenario sensitivity, peer definition and discount-rate exposure;
- market incorporation: existing price, relative strength, participation and entry owners;
- uncertainty: disclosure quality, coverage, contradictory evidence and alternative explanations.

These are design dimensions, not new production owners or permissions. The native output may remain descriptive or unavailable. Do not assign numerical weights before definitions, historical samples and evaluation are adequate.

A company can be a good business, an improving business, improving faster than expectations, or already priced for exceptional improvement. These are separate questions. A sector-wide rise in multiples is not proof that a particular demand mechanism improved. A strong quarter following lowered guidance is not the same observation as a strong quarter against an unchanged demanding baseline.

For software, compare cash flow and dilution consistently. One analysis can retain reported cash flow and model actual/expected share dilution; another can model an explicit cash replacement cost for equity compensation. Do not mechanically subtract the same economic burden twice. For capital-intensive cloud, include lease/payment and replacement assumptions rather than treating all reported cash capex definitions as interchangeable. For hardware/manufacturing/distribution, gross profit and working-capital productivity are necessary checks on top-line scale.

## 8. Primary-source findings that change the design

The source register in section 14 supplies exact documents. The following observations are dated evidence, not current stock recommendations. Interpretive consequences are this research's proposed design responses, not issuer claims of predictive alpha.

### F01 — Consumption is not contracted revenue

Snowflake's July 2026 quarterly filing distinguishes recognized platform consumption from contracted remaining obligations and describes third-party cloud/inference costs [S02]. Proposed consequence: separate workload, billable units, price, contract duration and cost per unit. RPO must not be a universal substitute for future revenue, and software adoption must not automatically imply expanding gross margin.

### F02 — AI labels and KPI definitions can change

Salesforce revised its product-revenue presentation in fiscal 2027; the Agentforce Apps category includes established application families and Slack. Its Q2 release also expands the Agentforce ARR definition [S03]. Proposed consequence: retain metric-definition vintages and reclassification bridges. Do not classify the entire renamed revenue line as newly created AI revenue or compare differently scoped ARR series without disclosure.

### F03 — Capex accounting can change without a spending-demand change

Microsoft's July 2026 call explains that a useful-life change shifts some future data-center leases from finance to operating classification while underlying calendar-year investment expectations are unchanged apart from that effect [S04]. Proposed consequence: record economic commitments and reported cash/noncash/lease classifications separately. A capex headline alone cannot be interpreted as a demand cut.

### F04 — Customer concentration changes the mechanism

Arista's 2025 annual filing reports two customers representing 26% and 16% of revenue [S05]. Proposed consequence: customer-program concentration belongs next to theme exposure. These historical shares do not establish the current quarter's concentration or identify unnamed customers. Broad networking demand cannot replace evidence about the relevant buyer and architecture.

### F05 — Revenue, cash flow and adjusted cash flow are different measures

Dell's quarter ended July 31, 2026 reports revenue growth of 58% while operating cash flow fell 13%; its cash-flow reconciliation separately identifies financing-receivable and lease adjustments [S06]. Proposed consequence: preserve the full bridge and its definitions. This juxtaposition alone does not prove deteriorating demand, accounting misconduct or poor economic quality.

### F06 — AI services bookings are not AI services revenue

Accenture's fiscal 2025 report separates generative/agentic AI revenue of $2.7 billion from bookings of $5.9 billion and excludes data, classical AI and AI used in delivering its services from those amounts [S07]. Proposed consequence: preserve contract-to-revenue timing and the exact definition of AI exposure. Historical full-year values are not the current run rate.

### F07 — Contract duration can weaken RPO growth while price realization improves

Autodesk's Q2 fiscal 2027 disclosure attributes pressure on unbilled deferred revenue/RPO to reducing multiyear discounts, while describing a benefit to price realization over time [S08]. Proposed consequence: investigate duration and pricing separately before interpreting slower RPO as weaker underlying demand. Management's explanation remains attributed and needs later outcome verification.

### F08 — Distribution billings are not software revenue

Arrow explicitly says ECS gross billings are amounts invoiced before net-sales accounting and other adjustments, and are not a substitute for revenue [S09]. Proposed consequence: prevent gross billings, recognized sales and retained gross profit from sharing a single cross-company growth field. The company's semiconductor distribution scope remains excluded from this commission's semiconductor research.

### F09 — Acquisition scope and organic demand differ

Amphenol's Q2 2026 release reports 55% dollar sales growth versus 30% organic growth [S10]. Proposed consequence: maintain the issuer's organic-growth definition and acquisition/FX bridge. Neither total-company growth nor a product catalog establishes the revenue share of a specific interconnect theme.

### F10 — Different reporting views are not necessarily additive

Celestica reports CCS and ATS segment figures alongside Hardware Platform Solutions revenue [S11]. Proposed consequence: retain reporting-view identity and verify containment/disjointness before summing. A platform label also does not establish a particular amount of proprietary IP margin. The detailed program-level boundary remains a later research task.

### F11 — Measurement companies cross several demand domains

Keysight's Q3 2026 release describes communications and electronic-industrial segments, with the latter spanning semiconductor, general electronics, automotive and energy markets [S12]. Proposed consequence: do not allocate the entire company to excluded semiconductor equipment or to an undifferentiated scientific-technology basket. Product and customer-market scope are both needed.

### F12 — Device economics need an installed-base and quality bridge

Apple's June 2026 quarter discusses device/service growth and an expanding installed base, and separately identifies a favorable gross-margin impact from tariff refunds [S13]. Proposed consequence: keep device demand, recurring attachment and nonrecurring accounting/economic effects separate. A margin change alone is not proof of a better replacement cycle.

### F13 — Nested platform adoption metrics must not be added

CrowdStrike's Q2 fiscal 2027 release reports module-adoption shares for customers using six-or-more, seven-or-more and eight-or-more modules [S14]. Proposed consequence: these threshold cohorts are nested, not mutually exclusive customer groups. Cross-sell evidence also needs paid economics and denominator consistency rather than a sum of overlapping percentages.

### F14 — ACV, annualized rates and recognized revenue need separate labels

ServiceNow's Q2 2026 release reports AI annual contract value alongside subscription revenue and remaining performance obligations [S15]. Proposed consequence: retain each metric's charging, time and recognition basis. Crossing an ACV threshold cannot be presented as the same amount of recognized annual or quarterly AI revenue.

## 9. Exposure mathematics without invented precision

Use five explicit evidence states for numerical materiality: directly disclosed; arithmetically derived from compatible disclosures; estimated range with a named model; qualitative only; unavailable. These are proposed research labels, not new enrolled production states.

A revenue share requires a compatible numerator and denominator: same consolidated entity/business scope, fiscal period, currency, accounting basis and acquisition perimeter. A disclosed segment share can be calculated where valid; it must not be renamed AI share or security exposure if the segment contains other products. Unknown remains unknown, not zero, a default 100% or a guessed midpoint.

When only a product's catalog capability is verified, show that capability with no quantitative materiality. An announced customer arrangement is not a shipped design win. A qualified design win is not yet recognized revenue. A minority investment is not consolidated supplier revenue. An acquired business becomes part of an issuer's applicable economic scope only from the evidenced transaction/accounting boundary, not the announcement headline.

Overlapping theme shares need not sum to 100%. They answer different questions. A company can earn the same revenue from a cloud-delivered cybersecurity product used for AI workloads; that dollar belongs to overlapping research views but is still one dollar of company revenue. Only an explicitly disjoint allocation partition may be summed. Label the partition and residual coverage.

Basket weight remains a separate governed investment construction. A high revenue share does not by itself justify a high basket weight, forecast probability or trade size. Price beta is a fitted historical association, not a substitute for revenue share or a causal dependency.

## 10. Spending flows, bottlenecks and value capture

Do not add buyer capex, equipment-vendor sales, contract-manufacturer sales and component sales and call the result the theme's total market. Those can be observations of different transfers within one chain. Every monetary edge needs payer/payee scope, gross/net basis, period and inclusion boundary. Where no non-overlapping economic partition exists, present an unweighted relationship map rather than invented Sankey widths.

The graph should support several views, each with a different question:

1. Physical architecture: what is contained in a system and what is purchased at each assembly boundary?
2. Commercial network: who sells, distributes, hosts, implements or supports which product, under what evidenced relationship?
3. Budget allocation: which buyer budget funds which workload or service?
4. Value capture: which participant retains gross profit, recurring service economics or differentiated IP, and who supplies capital?
5. Substitution: what customer behavior, architecture or technology could remove a vendor's role?
6. Hypothesis transmission: which conditional demand propagation is being proposed, and what observation would refute it?

These are views composed over existing owners, not six new stores or graph engines.

For a bottleneck claim, require an explicit constrained object, location/application, time interval, observable demand, qualified available capacity, substitution options and economic consequence. Criticality alone does not establish scarcity. Backlog can reflect long contract duration, delivery schedules or duplicate ordering. Book-to-bill is a monetary flow ratio, not capacity utilization or lead time. Published technical capacity is not the same as qualified output available to a customer.

Software's constraint can be data readiness, integration, procurement, reliability, compliance, implementation capacity, developer distribution or measurable customer ROI. A constraint is financially relevant only after connecting it to paid deployment and value capture. When it eases, some suppliers may gain volume while losing scarcity pricing; that outcome must be investigated rather than presumed positive for everyone.

For AI, explicitly allow opposite economic effects in connected businesses: lower inference cost may expand useful consumption; automation may reduce software seats or consulting hours; a platform may bundle a former point solution; more traffic may raise security/observability expense; efficiency may defer hardware demand. These are competing hypotheses, not current sector forecasts. The final model needs signs conditional on contract type, customer response and time horizon.

## 11. Measurement, evidence and data feasibility

Every proposed operating observation should carry source identity/locator, source family, upstream publication date and precision, observation/retention time, business-effective interval, fiscal period, source scope, metric definition/version, unit, currency, stock/flow/rate basis, denominator, reported/derived/estimated status, comparison basis, corrections, rights, review state and explicit limitations.

Those are semantic requirements to map into existing native owners. They are not permission to add a parallel observation warehouse. The final implementation plan must inspect the accepted owner schemas and preserve fields through persistence, producer, transport and browser. A schema with a field that storage drops is not a working contract.

Source publication, our observation date, system retention and a business event's effective date must never collapse into a single as-of. Today reading a 2025 annual report does not make its operating measures current. A corrected KPI definition or recast segment series needs both originally-known and latest-presentation views. Historical tests must not use a later reconstruction as though it existed at the earlier decision date.
