## 1. The outcome: make expectations explicit instead of attaching a multiple to a theme

The investor should be able to distinguish an attractive business, improving operations, better-than-expected operations, and a valuation that already requires substantial improvement. The proposed capability answers: **what must happen, how much capital must be committed, who receives the resulting cash, how long can favorable economics persist, and what observable development would change the answer?**

The same revenue acceleration can have different implications for a consumption platform, contract manufacturer, managed-service provider and serial acquirer. A descriptive theme relationship does not establish a profit pool. A disclosed profit pool does not establish distributable cash. A large scenario valuation does not establish a likely outcome or an actionable price discrepancy.

The starting finance distinction is conventional: cash available to all capital providers and cash available to common equity are different objects, with corresponding discount rates and ownership bridges [V01]. This chapter's specific scenario design, interpretations and proposed product behavior are original work. The numerical examples are deliberately fictional so they do not smuggle in unsupported live prices, consensus, financing assumptions or investment recommendations.

## 2. Five distinct expectation objects

| Object | Required meaning | What it must not become |
|---|---|---|
| Reported operating result | Source, period, definition, currency, perimeter, correction and public-availability time | A forecast or an observation available before publication |
| Management guidance or long-horizon target | Attributed speaker/document, measure, horizon, range, assumptions and revision history | Analyst consensus, realized output or an accepted house forecast |
| Third-party consensus | Actual entitled provider, sample/composition, statistic, fiscal basis, vintage and availability | A number inferred from a press release, today's overwritten history or an invented market whisper |
| Conditional price-implied requirement | Valid price/enterprise-value bridge plus explicit model and all fixed assumptions | A uniquely identified market belief, objective probability or proof a company will achieve it |
| House research scenario or forecast | Assumptions, mechanism, owner, falsifier, uncertainty and the applicable evaluation/authority | An automatic Prophet rank, entry, sizing or trading permission |

The R2 management-guidance roll-forward remains useful: for compatible additive revenue measures, isolate how much a full-year increase incorporates already-completed performance versus the implied remaining year. That is still a management-guidance comparison, not consensus. Do not rebrand it here.

A future comparison must freeze its information cutoff. A quarter-end accounting period is not its release timestamp, and later recast metrics were not known at the earlier price. A scenario may deliberately use latest corrected data, but then it must not be presented as a historical as-known signal. Prices and securities must come through their native owners, with listing, currency, corporate actions and time preserved.

## 3. Valuation routes native to the business model

The following are proposed research routes, not an approved model registry. A company can require more than one route. Multiples may be useful descriptive cross-checks, but only after matching the numerator, denominator, ownership, periods and business perimeter.

| Economic archetype | Operating bridge to investigate | Cash and valuation requirements | What could falsify an apparent improvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seat/organization software | Paid population, realized price, retention, expansion and new products | Reconcile recurring revenue with margins, support, selling/development expenditure, dilution and cash conversion; compare duration of durable renewal economics | Bundling, customer consolidation, automation reducing seats, unfavorable renewals or costly replacement of expiring business |
| Consumption/data/observability software | Useful workloads × billable intensity × realized price | Resource efficiency, infrastructure cost, contractual floors, gross-profit dollars and reinvestment; do not treat RPO as mechanically recognized sales | Paid intensity or prices decline faster than workloads expand; low-cost alternatives take the economic role |
| Transaction-enabled/vertical software | Paid workflows, implementation, customer activity, eligible transactions and realized fee | Separate subscription from processing/pass-through and risk-bearing activities; retain payment/credit liabilities and seasonality where relevant | Volume grows with lower retained fees, weak customer economics, implementation delay or additional credit/support cost |
| Services and managed outcomes | Contracts, mobilization, delivery, pricing unit, utilization and renewal | Contract-type contribution, labor/nonhuman costs, collection, capacity and working capital; distinguish hourly deflation from fixed-fee productivity | Savings passed to customers, idle freed capacity, delivery overruns, repricing or contracts that do not become work |
| Devices and systems | Unit demand, mix, accepted systems, installed-base service and purchase boundary | Retained gross profit, warranties, financing, inventory and replacement economics; normalize cycles before selecting an earnings period | Bought-in component inflation drives sales, channel stocking substitutes for sell-through, acceptance or collection slips |
| Network/component suppliers | Architecture, qualified content, customer programs, share and price | Normalize engineering/qualification investment and production capital; separate scarce-component rent from durable design value | Integration removes a role, faster capacity needs fewer units, customer concentration or substitution weakens economics |
| Manufacturing and distribution | Value added, design responsibility, service mix and execution | Gross versus net revenue, purchased materials, working-capital productivity, utilization and normalized cash; book-to-bill is not cash return | Pass-through scale, lower retained margin, capital-intensive ramps or excess inventory |
| Cloud and capital-intensive infrastructure | Paid utilization of usable capacity, price/yield and customer concentration | Economic leases, cash/noncash deployment, replacement needs, depreciation lives, funding and return on incremental capacity | Weak utilization, stranded assets, customer or funding stress; reported capex classification mistaken for an economic change |
| Serial-acquisition software | Organic development plus acquired businesses and their retention | Explicit acquisition funding, integration, contingent consideration, minority ownership and returns on each relevant deployment of capital | Reported growth is capital-consuming, acquired cohorts underperform or the model gives acquired earnings a zero purchase cost |
| Mixed or reorganizing groups | Compatible consolidated business boundaries and central costs | Sum-of-parts only after intersegment eliminations, shared assets/costs, noncontrolling interests and financing bridge | Nested product revenue is added to parent segments or management-adjusted income erases economic claims |

Loss-making businesses are not valueless merely because a positive-earnings model is unsuitable. They need a separately specified transition-to-profitability and financing path, with customer economics, cash burn, dilution, survival/failure scenarios and terminal constraints. The small positive-NOPAT laboratory below intentionally refuses negative starting earnings; that is its implementation boundary, not a prohibition on valuing loss-making companies.

## 4. Growth, reinvestment and returns: a reproducible laboratory

### 4.1 Explicit timing and definitions

Let N_t be net operating profit after tax, before financing. Let h be interim earnings growth, r the return on incremental investment, w the weighted-average cost of capital, g the long-run growth rate and r_T the terminal return on incremental investment.

The original scenario has an already-funded operating business generating N_1=100. It includes ten explicit annual periods. Growth from year1 through year10 therefore has **nine annual transitions**. At each year-end from t=1 through9, investment I_t=N_t*h/r produces the next period's incremental earnings. In year10, the investment switches to I_10=N_10*g/r_T to fund the first terminal transition. Free cash flow is F_t=N_t-I_t.

First terminal-year earnings are N_11=N_10*(1+g). Terminal cash flow is F_11=N_11*(1-g/r_T). The continuing operating value is:

`EV = sum(t=1..10, F_t/(1+w)^t) + [F_11/(w-g)]/(1+w)^10`

This simplified relation between growth, incremental return and required reinvestment is consistent with the conventional steady-state reinvestment framework [V02]. The finite-period timing here is an explicit research assumption. It is not a historical accounting ROIC estimate, a real project valuation or a substitute for a company's detailed operating model.

The starting installed business is assumed already funded; no additional time-zero acquisition or construction price is deducted. Negative interim cash flow is permitted and represents a funding requirement. The model does not prove financing is available on these terms. It holds margins, funding risk and competitive relationships outside the selected variables, so correlated real-world effects require separate scenarios rather than mechanical extrapolation.

### 4.2 C08: the same earnings growth, three different operating values

Fix interim growth at12%, discount rate10%, terminal growth2.5% and terminal incremental return10%.

| Interim return on incremental investment | First-year net reinvestment | First-year cash flow | Illustrative operating EV |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 8% | 150 | -50 | 735.916 |
| 10% | 120 | -20 | 1,000.000 |
| 25% | 48 | 52 | 1,633.802 |

All values use fictional currency units. A funded no-growth business paying100 each year at a10% discount rate has a reference value of1,000. Under the selected assumptions, growth invested below that required return reduces value relative to the reference; equal-return growth is neutral; higher-return growth adds value. Earnings growth is identical across the rows. The difference comes from what must be reinvested to produce it.

This is an argument for researching capital productivity, not a claim that a real Technology company's incremental return is any of these numbers. Marginal return, historical average ROIC and an issuer's management-defined adjusted return are different measurements. A negative denominator, acquisition goodwill, development-cost treatment or changing consolidation can make a simple accounting ratio misleading.

### 4.3 C09: sensitivity is a surface, not a preferred point estimate

Keep interim growth12%, terminal growth2.5% and terminal return10%. Vary the two assumptions below:

| Discount rate | Interim incremental return10% | Return15% | Return25% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 8% | 1,698.062 | 2,085.292 | 2,395.076 |
| 10% | 1,000.000 | 1,352.112 | 1,633.802 |
| 12% | 628.759 | 950.188 | 1,207.331 |

This is not an estimated current cost of capital or fair-value range for an issuer. The terminal return remains10% even as the discount rate changes, which deliberately changes terminal excess returns. A future real application must expose that assumption instead of silently forcing or preserving it.

The calculated terminal present-value fraction can exceed100% where explicit-period present value is negative. It is an attribution ratio, not a probability; clipping it to100% would hide funding dependence. Show explicit and terminal contributions separately, alongside capital requirements and scenario limits.

## 5. Reverse expectations: what has to be true under stated assumptions?

### C10: one fictional enterprise value, several compatible growth requirements

Set a fictional target operating EV of1,800, first-year NOPAT100, ten explicit periods, interim incremental return25%, terminal growth2.5% and terminal return10%. Solve for constant interim NOPAT growth while varying only the discount rate:

| Assumed discount rate | Required year1-to-year10 NOPAT growth |
|---|---:|
| 8% | 6.65393% per year |
| 10% | 14.04387% per year |
| 12% | 21.47581% per year |

Each root was reconstructed through the forward model and returns EV1,800 within numerical tolerance. The solver used a stated finite bracket and refuses an infeasible target instead of silently clamping to an endpoint. Growth here is **after-tax operating earnings growth**, not automatically revenue growth, EPS growth or cloud consumption.

A single observed price could not uniquely identify all these assumptions. The correct future product expression is a family of conditional requirements, with the fixed variables shown. A dashboard saying 'the market expects14%' without the cost of capital, margins, reinvestment, terminal conditions, capital claims and price timestamp would overstate what was identified.

The useful research comparison is whether a source-backed business trajectory can plausibly satisfy a selected requirement, what evidence would change that assessment, and which competing parameter combinations remain feasible. The mathematical inverse itself supplies no probability, recommendation or timing signal. Management ambition is not independent evidence that the implied condition will be achieved.

## 6. Terminal growth is not free

### C11: a separate steady-state comparison

Hold the first terminal-year NOPAT fixed at100 across scenarios, with discount rate10%. Then:

`Continuing value = 100 * (1 - g/r_T) / (0.10 - g)`

| Terminal return on incremental investment | Growth0% | Growth2% | Growth4% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 8% | 1,000.000 | 937.500 | 833.333 |
| 10% | 1,000.000 | 1,000.000 | 1,000.000 |
| 15% | 1,000.000 | 1,083.333 | 1,222.222 |

Holding the **first terminal-year** earnings fixed is essential to this invariance statement. Holding a different year's earnings fixed while changing growth changes the numerator. Return equal to cost—not return equal to growth—is the zero-excess-return condition. The algebra above and executed cases govern the research calculation; an isolated wording slip in a teaching page must not override the equation [V02].

The analytical implication is that a long-term growth story must answer how much investment it consumes and what return that investment earns. A new total-addressable-market estimate is not itself a source for a perpetual growth assumption. The transition into maturity must be economically coherent: competition, replacement investment, customer pricing and margin normalization cannot all be held artificially favorable simply to produce a desired value.

## 7. Cash claims, accounting adjustments and per-share economics

### 7.1 Real discriminator: an adjusted loss item does not remove the underlying claim

Lenovo's June-quarter statement separately identifies a warrant fair-value effect and potential ordinary shares. Its basic and diluted loss-per-share denominator is the same because potential shares are excluded when their effect is antidilutive; that does not mean the financial instruments have disappeared [R4A-S06].

Research consequence: reconstruct the current economic capital claims before calculating a common-share value. Do not treat a lower reported diluted denominator in a loss period as proof of buybacks, or assume that an adjusted earnings presentation removes the value of outstanding warrants. This is a source-specific discriminator, not a new accounting-policy ruling.

For share-based compensation, a forecast can use a coherent cash-equivalent cost convention or model the actual prospective claim/dilution economics, depending on its purpose. The representation must reconcile with the operating forecast and capital bridge. Charging the same future grant as both a full economic cost and the full equivalent dilution can double-count; ignoring both can overstate shareholder value. Existing awards and future grants require distinguishable treatment. The methodological reference on options establishes that existing and prospective equity awards matter; its2005 accounting discussion is not treated as current accounting law [V04].

The same consistency applies to leases and research/development adjustments. If an economic lease is treated as financing, the operating cash forecast and claims bridge must reflect that choice together. Do not subtract a liability while also charging the same principal again as an unadjusted operating expense. Capitalizing development for analytical comparability requires coherent earnings, amortization, capital and reinvestment changes; it does not manufacture cash or automatically establish a superior business.

### 7.2 C13: buybacks change both the cash asset and the share count

Consider a fictional business with operating equity value900, excess cash100 and100 shares: total equity value1,000, or10 per share. Spend all100 of excess cash repurchasing shares, with no tax, financing or transaction costs and no change to the operating value.

| Repurchase price | Shares remaining | Equity value after cash expenditure | Value per remaining share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 87.5000 | 900 | 10.2857 |
| 10 | 90.0000 | 900 | 10.0000 |
| 12 | 91.6667 | 900 | 9.8182 |

The original10 value is a scenario assumption, not an estimate of any market security. The example demonstrates why mechanically lowering the denominator while leaving the spent cash in the numerator creates fictitious value.

CGI's July release provides a real reason to keep operating and per-share comparisons separate: adjusted net earnings growth of1.7% differs from adjusted diluted EPS growth of9.0% [R4A-S12]. Those rounded rates do not supply an exact share count or a judgment about the effectiveness of its capital allocation. The final model needs the actual denominator, timing, share transactions and cash cost.

### 7.3 C14: acquisition-generated growth requires acquisition funding

Constellation's Q2 release distinguishes reported from organic growth and defines FCFA2S before acquisition, investment and debt-repayment uses. It also discloses acquisition consideration that includes acquired cash [R4A-S11]. This is useful financial information, but it is not permission to forecast recurring acquired growth without investment.

In a separate fictional scenario, cash generation before acquisition spending is100, current acquisition payment80, and current remaining cash20. The acquired business adds10 in the next period, producing110 before any additional acquisitions. The incremental10 was not free. A full valuation must then model retention, organic growth, subsequent purchases, integration, financing, contingent consideration and who owns the acquired cash flow.

Do not mechanically subtract Constellation's reported acquisition headline from FCFA2S to calculate an actual residual: acquired cash, noncontrolling interests, consolidation and timing need a compatible bridge. Nor should all acquisition spending be labeled maintenance by default. The requirement is consistency between the growth forecast and the investments it assumes.

### 7.4 C15: consolidated business value is not entirely common-share value

In a fictional consolidated group, operating EV1,000 includes a debt-free subsidiary worth200, of which outsiders own25%. With100 excess cash and200 debt, common equity equals1,000+100-200-50=**850**. Valuing the parent activities at800 and its75% subsidiary interest at150 before adding cash and debt reaches the same result.

Do not subtract the50 noncontrolling claim again if the forecast already contains only the parent's economic share. Minority interests, preferred instruments, convertibles, pensions, contingent consideration and other material claims require their own supported treatment; this simplified example does not claim they are all debt or that book values equal economic values.

### 7.5 Targets and adjusted cash measures are not distributable cash by definition

Fujitsu's September investor materials distinguish long-horizon operating/cash ambitions and cash measures before selected investments or excluding specified one-time effects [R4A-S09/S10]. The proposed model records the target, its definition, the underlying investment plan and subsequent realized results separately. A target is neither consensus nor an observed annual run rate. The definition of a cash metric must survive the interface so a user can see what still needs to be funded.

## 8. Currency and regime sensitivity without one 'Technology duration' score

### C12: match cash-flow and discount-rate basis

Take hypothetical real year-end cash flows100,105,110, a7% real discount rate and3% inflation. A consistent nominal rate is `(1.07*1.03)-1 = 10.21%`. Inflate each year's cash by1.03 to its year power and discount nominally, or retain real cash and discount at7%; both present values are **274.9618**. Mixing nominal cash and the real rate would not represent the same model [V03].

Separately, suppose a local-currency share price rises20% and investor-currency value per unit of local currency falls15%. The price return in the investor's currency is `1.20*0.85-1 = 2%`, before dividends, taxes, fees and hedging. This is not an actual exchange-rate observation or forecast.

R4A's Infosys figures make the reporting distinction tangible: the same period's local-currency and USD presentation produce different reported growth. Constant-currency operating context is not itself cash converted into the investor's currency. Listing venue does not select the appropriate valuation currency or nominal discount rate.

### Proposed regime-by-mechanism matrix

This matrix defines research questions and conditional sensitivities, not current macro readings, estimated coefficients, directional stock calls or a policy recommendation.

| Channel | Software/data/security | Services | Systems/components/cloud infrastructure | Required discriminator |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Required return changes | Revalue the timing of future cash while separately testing customer budgets and pricing | Separate valuation discount from project starts, renewals and collection | Separate asset value from financing and the return required on new capacity | Observed rate/credit input and its vintage; unchanged fundamentals cannot be assumed unless it is an explicit sensitivity |
| Enterprise demand change | Seats, workloads, optimization, churn and paid expansion | Discretionary projects versus committed operations, utilization and mobilization | Customer programs, complete-system orders, inventory and accepted deployment | Buyer-specific evidence; one macro label is not a paid-demand observation |
| Inflation and input costs | Cloud/inference expense, wages and realized subscription/usage pricing | Wage/subcontractor changes versus bill rates and fixed-fee repricing | Components, energy, construction, logistics and contract pass-through | Gross-profit and cash bridge; revenue inflation is not volume growth |
| Currency move | Reporting translation versus transaction pricing and vendor costs | Customer billing currencies versus delivery labor and hedges | Sales, purchasing, manufacturing and financing currencies | Compatible geography/currency exposures; no direction inferred from headquarters |
| Credit or funding stress | Customer survival/collection and provider financing needs where material | Receivables, project delays and labor obligations | Capacity financing, vendor/customer credit, leases and working capital | Maturities, funding obligations and sensitivity without duplicate financing charges |
| Adoption or efficiency change | Paid intensity, substitution, retention and delivery cost | Fewer hours versus output fees and freed capacity utilization | Required physical capacity, content, useful utilization and architecture shifts | Technical efficiency, useful demand and paid economics separately measured |

The same shock can act through discount rates and operating cash in opposite directions. A controlled sensitivity changes one input to make a mechanism understandable; a joint scenario must then model plausible co-movement and make those assumptions explicit. Do not treat the mathematical partial derivative as an empirical causal effect or an automatic trading response.

## 9. Moat and rerating evidence should explain persistence

A proposed durable-advantage assessment must identify a mechanism, its economic effect and its expiry or falsifier. Potential mechanisms include difficult-to-replace workflows, qualified product content, customer integrations, distribution, efficient operation at scale and a supported ability to reinvest profitably. The research should distinguish their existence from evidence that they produce excess returns for the specific issuer.

The empirical questions are concrete: do comparable customers renew at attractive economics, does a platform retain paid value rather than merely usage, does qualification survive substitution, do new programs earn an adequate return after capital and support, and do acquisitions retain the cash generation assumed when bought? Market share, developer activity, catalog breadth and customer logos can be evidence about parts of this chain; none independently supplies its entire financial answer.

Rerating hypotheses should therefore specify which expectation changes: longer growth duration, higher margin, better capital efficiency, lower cash-flow risk, stronger per-share conversion or less financing dilution. A short-term valuation expansion can also reflect a discount-rate or market-risk change rather than company-specific moat improvement. Subsequent evidence should test the stated explanation rather than retrospectively replace it with whichever story matched the price.

Relative valuation needs comparables on business mix, growth horizon, cash conversion, capital intensity, ownership, currency and accounting basis. Industry labels alone are insufficient. A descriptive peer premium is not proof of overvaluation, while a low multiple may reflect a risk the operating model has not captured. The current commission has not established a licensed, historical point-in-time consensus/valuation panel and must not pretend otherwise.

## 10. Product proposal: exposure-to-expectations, not a price-target machine

Within the existing Technology/theme/company journey, the proposed read-only experience should connect five views:

1. **Economic perimeter:** the exact business/product, reported materiality or supported bounds, geography basis and source vintage from R4A.
2. **Operating requirements:** the paid unit, revenue/margin bridge, investment and financing that a scenario assumes.
3. **Expectation comparison:** reported results, dated management guidance, available entitled consensus and conditional implied requirements, each visibly typed.
4. **Sensitivity and falsifiers:** alternative parameter combinations, funding needs, terminal contribution, unallocated claims and the next decisive observation.
5. **Existing workflow:** company analysis, theme context and existing governed entry/outcome owners, without inventing new trading authority.

An explanation should read as a conditional statement: 'At the selected price/claim bridge and these assumptions, the required operating path is X; these disclosures support parts of it and these important inputs are unknown.' It should not imply that X is the market's single belief or that the model has measured a probability unless a separate accepted process actually has.

The producer must own arithmetic, definitions, validation and versioning. The browser renders accepted outputs and explanations rather than recomputing valuation with hidden defaults. Any later user-adjustable scenario should preserve the original source snapshot, explicit changed assumptions, computation version, feasibility limits and scenario status. It must not overwrite source facts, management guidance or accepted forecasts. This is an interface requirement to reconcile with existing owners, not authorization to build a new scenario store, quote service or forecast registry.

Missing inputs need differentiated behavior. A missing price prevents a price-implied analysis but need not hide a useful operating scenario. Missing future consensus allows a management comparison without a fabricated consensus row. Unresolved capital claims can block a per-share value while leaving a compatible operating-EV scenario visible. Mixed units or source-future leakage must fail, not degrade into an apparently precise result.

Paid/private source access, retention, correction, identity and publication requirements remain with incumbent owners. No live full-fidelity research payload may enter public static Git or browser caches merely because this original public research proposal is versioned there. Fable must receive the exact accepted interfaces and tests before implementation, not guess how to merge the analysis into GMI, F04, Financial/Earnings, K1 or Prophet.
