## 1. What must be proven, separately

The desired end-state is a useful intelligence system, not a source browser with many citations or a backtest with a high-looking number. Five questions need different acceptance evidence:

| Question | Evidence required | Evidence that is insufficient |
|---|---|---|
| Is the observation correct? | Source/version/identity/unit/period/definition match and reproducible calculation | A plausible model summary, search snippet or file hash without semantic review |
| Is the economic explanation useful? | Correct mechanism, business scope, counterevidence and a reader completing the task | More detailed prose, a bigger graph or an attractive dashboard |
| Could the information be used at the stated historical cut? | A declared research mode and its required source, universe, identity and model vintages | An old publication date attached to a newly downloaded/recomputed value |
| Does the feature add predictive information? | Predeclared target, baseline, sampling, uncertainty, negative findings and a genuine holdout/prospective test | Correct arithmetic, an anecdotal stock move or a model's confidence language |
| Does it improve investment decisions? | Accepted native candidate/entry/outcome path, incremental risk/cost-aware evidence and lawful promotion | A predictive association treated as a trade-size instruction |

No new state machine is proposed for these questions. They are the evidence obligations attached to the existing source, product and Evaluation OS/QLedger responsibilities. A display capability may become useful before it can lawfully influence a rank. The parent delivery is not complete merely because one research stage passes.

## 2. Three research modes, plus a model-era warning

### Current evidence view

Use the currently accepted source versions and definitions, preserving date and correction history. This answers what is supported now. It cannot label a later correction as known to the system before it was captured.

### Public-information reconstruction

Select actual historical source versions that were demonstrably available by the cut, with compatible historical identities, definitions and an explicitly reconstructed event universe. A researcher can perform a new calculation on those old inputs, but the result is a reconstruction, not something the original system necessarily produced.

Using a present-day language model to analyze an old source also requires disclosure of model-era risk: its parameters, prompt choices or retrieval selection may reflect later information. An old source cutoff alone does not prove a hindsight-free predictive experiment. Such work is suitable for hypothesis formation and mechanism review. Confirmatory claims need a defensible frozen-model/history protocol or prospective evidence.

### Original-system replay

In addition to original source versions and the appropriate event population, require historical owner observation/recording/computation, actual method/configuration and applicable identity states at the cut. A source public at 12:00 but captured at 12:40 cannot support a claim that the system used it at 12:30. Later calculations do not rewrite that fact.

R5A's native review found that K1 generic composition and Prophet's current episode projection have distinct limits. In particular, the B04 census records current-generation event discovery rather than complete historical event-set reconstruction. A valid retained event revision is not enough to prove the historical denominator. Missing event history must remain unavailable, not become a claim that no event existed [N1].

The research laboratory's two modes are local illustrative names. They do not replace native mode enums or certify the platform's actual historical state.

A fiscal-year result shared across several quarterly outlook events creates dependent outcomes. ## 4. Cohort construction and historical denominator

The initial domain inventory contains fifteen research families. A validation population should retain that breadth but separate business archetypes and reporting comparability. There must be an explicit frozen issuer/security universe, eligibility rule, reporting period, source coverage and observation-era start. Do not treat the hand-selected source examples as a census or use current constituents to infer historical constituents.

Record counts at each stage through the existing evidence/evaluation owners: in-scope population; sources expected; sources available; identities resolved; definitions comparable; eligible features; abstentions by reason; mature outcome labels. A denominator is not the number of successful cards rendered. Missingness may correlate with region, company size, business model or performance and must be visible.

Coverage questions precede modeling questions. For example, a method tested only on US software firms with detailed quarterly ARR cannot claim transfer to Chinese hardware suppliers, Canadian acquisitive software groups or Indian services firms. Stratification is not permission to infer an unobserved region's answer. Small-sample strata may remain descriptive.

For retrospective studies, retain corporate actions, exits, renamed securities, acquisition boundaries and historical classifications where supported. If that population cannot be reconstructed, label the experiment as a selected-sample study. Do not silently drop companies because their current symbol no longer resolves.

Use an issuer/fiscal-event level where the economic claim lives. Share-class listings are not independent businesses. Source-local themes overlap: one event can be relevant to several research facets but remains the same underlying event and information source.

## 5. Targets and horizons

The primary economic targets are native reported periods, not arbitrary day counts: one and two subsequent comparable fiscal reports, or completion of the explicitly referenced fiscal year. Distinguish the economic period from the date the outcome becomes knowable. A report about a quarter ending in June can arrive after a July decision; its arrival determines eligibility for that decision.

Secondary market observations can use 1, 5, 20 and 60 completed trading sessions only where the existing market and corporate-action owners can supply a defensible basis. These horizons are proposed study choices, not trade-holding instructions. Use the appropriate market calendar and original tradability constraints. An after-hours disclosure cannot be evaluated using a pre-disclosure closing price as though execution occurred there without an explicitly hypothetical event-study label.

Financial forecast accuracy, price association and executable return are different targets. Benchmark-adjusted returns are descriptive, not proof that a particular revenue fact caused the price move. If the model already uses price momentum, evaluation must ask whether economic context adds anything beyond that momentum, not merely confirm it.

Immature outcomes remain pending/censored. Missing future reports, delisting data or a valid return basis cannot become zero return, a failure, or a selectively excluded inconvenient observation. Publish the number and cause of missing labels. A study may need to stop at an economic target when the return target is unavailable.

## 6. Source dependence and multiple testing

Three kinds of dependence need separate handling:

**Document lineage:** ten articles copying one issuer release are one originating information source. Different extracts from one release can measure different facts but are not ten independent confirmations of the release.

**Economic mechanism:** genuine observations at a buyer, system vendor and supplier can respond to the same spending program. Their separate provenance does not establish statistically independent shocks. Keep the mechanism linkage and assess clustered uncertainty without discarding actual distinct cash flows or holdings.

**Outcome reuse:** several theme views, horizons or model versions can reuse the same issuer event and later outcome. Count the experiment search, not just the winning variant.

The research literature specifically warns that reporting only selected successful backtests and ignoring trial counts can inflate performance; the Deflated Sharpe Ratio is one proposed treatment for selection and return-distribution effects [E1]. This chapter does not implement that statistic, assert an effective independent trial count or use one corrected number as a promotion gate.

For a simple illustration, under twenty independent correctly sized null tests each at 5%, the probability of at least one nominal false positive is `1 - 0.95^20`, approximately 64.15%. At one hundred independent null tests it is about 99.41%. These figures are arithmetic under explicit independence, not estimates of this project. A Bonferroni family-wise 5% threshold for twenty predeclared tests is 0.0025 per test; choosing a correction after seeing the results is not a substitute for protocol design.

Report every tested material variant, hypothesis revision and discarded run through the existing evaluation provenance. Prespecify the primary endpoint and any hierarchical testing or multiplicity adjustment. Correlated trials do not justify inventing an effective trial count that makes a result look significant. Exploratory findings can guide the next study but need fresh evidence.

## 7. Train/test separation and the LLM-specific risk

A random split of individual rows is inadequate when the same issuer/fiscal year, source document, customer program or overlapping label window occurs in both sets. Design temporal and group separation around the actual feature and outcome dependencies. Where label windows overlap a test period, remove incompatible training observations and define any necessary separation from the feature/label timing—not a decorative fixed embargo applied without rationale.

Freeze feature definitions, units, normalization, candidate cohort, missing-value treatment and primary benchmark before accessing holdout outcomes. Fit scalers, embeddings, clustering, theme assignments and calibration only using the permitted development/training information. A taxonomy curated using later winners cannot serve as a point-in-time predictor without an explicit reconstruction label.

Current LLM extraction from old documents is acceptable as labeled reconstruction research, but it does not erase the possibility that the model already knows later company outcomes. Where a meaningful historical model-era guarantee cannot be made, prioritize prospective frozen extraction and blinded fact review. Do not claim to have solved training-data contamination merely by hiding the ticker or omitting the future document from the prompt.

Compare with useful simple baselines: latest management outlook; source-native trailing trajectory; existing sector/regime/price context; and a clearly defined unadjusted measurement. Ablation should isolate the contribution of economic definitions, exposure weighting or source timing. A stronger complex model that costs much more must demonstrate sufficient incremental user or prediction value; complexity is not itself the moat.

## 8. Probabilities, uncertainty and decision value

Source confidence is not a forecast probability. A reliable filing can support a fact without establishing a 90% chance of a stock outcome. A model's adjective or token likelihood must not be turned into a calibrated financial probability.

When a native forecast owner eventually registers a probabilistic prediction, evaluate the stated outcome and horizon with an appropriate proper scoring rule. Proper scoring rules reward honest probability distributions in expectation; this does not make a tiny sample calibrated or prove investment utility [E2]. For binary outcomes the illustrative Brier score is mean squared error between predicted probability and the realized zero/one outcome.

Our four fictional predictions `[0.7, 0.7, 0.3, 0.3]` against outcomes `[1, 0, 1, 0]` have Brier loss 0.29. A constant 0.5 has loss 0.25 on that same tiny illustration. Stronger-looking confidence can therefore be worse by the selected loss. These four points do not estimate calibration of any real model.

For actual studies, report uncertainty and coverage using dependence-aware methods accepted by the evaluation owner. Calibration by region/archetype/horizon needs enough independent information; a minimum row count alone is not an adequacy rule. Sample size should relate to the predeclared effect, variance, dependence and intended decision cost. Do not declare success after a conveniently long number of days or repeated informal peeks.

A statistically detectable association may still have little practical value after delay, costs, turnover, concentration and user effort. Conversely, a correct exposure bound or a warning that two metrics are incomparable can be valuable for research even before any return prediction is established. Keep the product and predictive evidence separate.

### Three other analytical examples

1. **Censoring:** three mature outcomes `[1,0,1]` and one future unknown give a mature success fraction of 2/3. Filling the unknown with failure changes it to 2/4. The study must report the pending observation, not claim either ratio is a complete-population forecast result.
2. **Duplicate renderings:** four fictional events return `[10%,-2%,-2%,-2%]`, an event-balanced mean of 1%. Copying the first event into ten renderings yields a naive thirteen-row average of approximately 7.23%. These are duplicate reports of one event, not ten distinct tradable holdings. A genuinely weighted portfolio answers a different question and must not be deduplicated by this toy rule.
3. **Statistical interpretation:** the multiple-testing and binary-scoring examples in sections 6 and 8 are reproduced exactly with Decimal arithmetic. They are not real backtests, confidence intervals or estimated model probabilities.
