## 2. Four separate questions, plus evidence quality

For each business/cohort, the analyst asks:

1. **Adoption and capability:** is the service available, being used and retained by the relevant population?
2. **Retained economics:** do monetization, costs and investment turn that use into durable contribution and cash?
3. **Shareholder exposure:** how much survives financing, partner/minority claims, dilution and other issuer businesses?
4. **Expectations and market behavior:** is the change different from a specifically dated outlook or otherwise qualified expectation, and how is the instrument behaving?

Evidence completeness is a separate dimension across all four. A negative but well-established operating outcome is not bad data. Strong price performance does not independently prove operating success. Missing cohort economics does not erase valid adoption evidence. A current business can be mature while one product is emerging; a mature industry can contain a company recovery or an investment cycle. Neither company maturity nor a listing venue determines the lifecycle of every business row.

The proposed presentation says, for example: “Payer retention improved; cumulative contribution is not yet established.” It does not compress this into “early growth, confidence 82.” Confidence values, quantitative thresholds and probabilities remain unavailable until appropriately calibrated. The research map's current economic base, incremental sensitivity and conditional future exposure remain separate.

### Evidence needed for a changed interpretation

A candidate transition requires a matched subject/population and measurement definition; observed support; applicable contrary evidence; economic and publication windows; a reproducible reviewed rule where arithmetic is used; and a stated limitation. A changed denominator, acquisition perimeter, reclassification or source correction is first a comparability event, not a business inflection. It can change the interpretation only through the qualified comparable bridge.

A decisive hard event can immediately invalidate a claim. Conversely, repeated publication of one source is not persistence evidence. Rules such as “two quarters confirm acceleration” are not universal defaults. The relevant window is tied to renewals, cohort retention, a rights season, settlement, commissioning or repayment. Statistical persistence and false-alarm thresholds are parameters for a later preregistered evaluation, not numbers invented in this document.

A stage description may reverse, split by cohort or remain unknown. Expiry of a data-review date means review is due; it does not establish economic decline. A project passing its target date does not establish commercial success, and absence of a report does not prove failure. A source-rights withdrawal suppresses material and dependent conclusions; it is not a signal that the underlying business deteriorated.

## 4. Three clocks, not one monotone theme timeline

Every proposed transition has an **economic clock** (when the activity or target occurs), an **evidence clock** (when a relevant result is observable and retained), and an **investment clock** (when a governed instrument outcome can be measured). These are related but not interchangeable.

A subscription renewal, a sports season, a satellite commissioning milestone and a quoted-stock horizon are different rulers. The next source is often a quarterly report, but the outcome may concern a cohort older than the quarter or a contract spanning several years. A half-year total is not quarter-only evidence; a cash settlement is not necessarily contemporaneous with revenue recognition. No study may turn 180 calendar days into 126 trading sessions without an accepted and explicit question change.

**Fixed illustration EX06:** a hypothetical target period ends March 31, 2027, while the relevant report is expected April 30. On April 1, failure is not established merely because the outcome is not yet public. The original economic target remains fixed; the evidence-observation window records the unresolved outcome. An explicit cancellation would be different evidence and should not be hidden as mere censoring.

**EX07:** a March announcement moving a milestone from March to June is a new plan observation. It can explain changed project value or expectations, but cannot erase the original March forecast obligation. Compare original and revised questions separately under existing claim/version owners. A cancellation, postponed service and temporarily missing disclosure remain distinct.

## 5. Regime is a transmission explanation, not a universal sector verdict

The same market shock can act through demand, costs, financing, discounting and currency. A company can benefit on one channel and lose on another. The proposed view states the shock, exposed economic population, timing, causal hypothesis and observation that could distinguish alternatives. It consumes the existing macro/regime owner rather than generating another competing regime label.

The Federal Reserve distinguishes nominal and TIPS yield curves and model-based expectations/term-premium components; its model outputs are research products subject to revision [W02]. Therefore observed nominal-yield movement alone does not identify a unique policy, growth or inflation shock. An issuer's discount rate is not automatically a Treasury yield; risk, currency, duration and financing assumptions remain explicit. No current yield, policy forecast, macro regime or stock recommendation was estimated in this pass.

### Joint-shock scenarios avoid a misleading rates shortcut

In **EX01**, entirely invented annual rates change from 4% risk-free plus 2% credit spread to 3% plus 4%. Total borrowing cost rises from 6% to 7% even as government yields fall. On 1,000 units of debt with only 25% assumed to reprice, the annual interest increase is 2.5 units, not 10. This omits taxes, fees, hedges and further maturities deliberately; it is a reasoning check, not a company forecast.

In **EX02**, a level-perpetuity illustration changes cash from 100 to 85 while the discount rate falls from 10% to 8%. Value changes from 1,000 to 1,062.5. In **EX03**, cash falls to 75 under the same rate change; value becomes 937.5. These examples neither recommend a perpetuity model for transition-stage businesses nor claim a current fair value. They demonstrate that a lower discount rate does not determine the net outcome without a cash-flow hypothesis.

A demand rebound may support advertising budgets while higher real yields affect valuation. A recessionary easing can improve the government-rate component while harming advertising demand, customer credit or refinancing spreads. A nominal-revenue increase with cost inflation can weaken retained margins. These are competing conditional paths; their relative sizes cannot be inferred from a sector name.

### Regime must respect geography and release vintage

The study unit records audience/customer geography, costs/assets, funding currency, reported currency and listing market separately. A global internet platform and a domestic operator need different demand proxies. An FX translation change is not necessarily changed local purchasing behavior, and a reported constant-currency bridge is not an equity beta.

FRED documentation says the default real-time period ordinarily represents information available today; historical real-time windows can retrieve earlier vintages [W01]. Its vintage-date endpoint lists releases that changed observations, not every unchanged release [W04]. The existing data owner must retain both the original series vintage and the publication/availability evidence required by the actual question. Documentation discovery is not current API access, a retention receipt or complete historical coverage.

For evaluation, a regime classification formed from revised macro data or full-sample thresholds can leak future knowledge into an apparently historical test. Freeze source vintages, transformation, training window and classification rule before the target. If only a current retrospective series is available, label the work retrospective and do not put it in the prospective track record. Unknown regime remains a visible missing dimension, not a neutral zero.

## 7. A bounded prospective study design for later owner acceptance

This is a design checklist, not a preregistration already filed or a forecast already made. Before collecting a score-bearing cohort, the owner must freeze the following in its existing contract or refuse the unsupported question:

- Exact economic subject and population, including geography, acquisition perimeter, units, metric basis and any comparable-definition bridge.
- Prediction origin and its actual source/recorded cutoffs; target interval or event; separate expected publication/observation window; treatment of date-only evidence.
- Claim type: point estimate, probability, interval, direction or event condition. A conditional scenario without a probability cannot acquire a probability after the outcome.
- Method/version and baseline(s), permitted features and transformations, exclusions and missing-input rules fixed before evaluation.
- Outcome source and original-release versus revised-value scoring policy, including correction-linked re-evaluation that preserves the previous grade.
- Eligible population, event/company clustering, maturities, early cancellations, acquisitions/delistings, and unresolved observations.
- Loss/scoring rule appropriate to the target, minimum evidence and uncertainty reporting, and the review/promotion owner. No result here changes those owner's thresholds.

### Four useful candidate tests

**Owned-platform retained economics:** predict a defined next reported profit or cash measure using only admissible earlier observations, and compare with a same-season baseline. Do not use contemporaneous target revenue as a predictor; its contribution belongs only in post-outcome error attribution. Claim-specific inflation and advertising-budget proxies require correctly dated availability. This may be feasible for consolidated outcomes but does not identify AI-only economics [P15,P16].

**Subscriber/content economics:** predict a defined retention, contribution or cash outcome for a fixed subscriber population. If only global issuer totals are disclosed, do not fabricate cohort returns. The initial descriptive module can operate while the cohort-prediction lane remains unqualified [P03].

**Games and deployment events:** freeze a milestone/availability condition and a later paid-use/economic condition separately. Forecasting a launch is different from forecasting durable contribution. Retain withdrawals, canceled projects and refunds; do not build the denominator only from surviving titles [P04,W03].

**Networks and investment:** compare like build vintages or contract service populations. A forecast may concern activation or payment; a separate scenario concerns lifetime investment return. Treat targets reset after the forecast as new information, not an edit to the original outcome definition [P05,P06].

These are not mandated extra A1 implementation tasks. They define the later E1 qualification path and identify when public data is insufficient. The Phase16 four-company retrospective pilot remains a diagnostic, not a training contest whose best result is promoted [P16].

### Scoring and uncertainty without false certainty

Continuous financial targets require errors in the original unit, plus a prespecified scale where meaningful. Near-zero or negative bases make naive percentage-error statistics unstable or misleading. A directional classifier needs balanced outcomes and an abstention/coverage report; high accuracy from predicting the majority class does not establish useful information. Probability forecasts can be evaluated with a proper probability score only when the probabilities were actually recorded before the outcome. Intervals need both coverage and width; extremely wide intervals are not automatically useful.

Use time-ordered training/validation/test windows, fit preprocessing and regime thresholds only on permitted training data, and handle overlapping target horizons when splitting. Company, date and shared-event/source dependence must remain visible: many sentences, web copies or correlated targets do not create independent samples. Report test families, attempted specifications and negative results. A model chosen after seeing the four Phase16 outcomes has no untouched evaluation set. Do not present p-values, confidence intervals or model superiority that this package has not estimated.

## 8. Coverage, censoring and historical failure

**EX04** has five hypothetical registered questions. Four have reached their economic maturity, with two known successes, one known failure and one unresolved outcome. One is not yet mature.

The resolved success fraction is 2/3 **conditional on the resolved subset**. Resolution coverage of mature eligible cases is 3/4. With no assumption about the unresolved case, the mature-eligible success fraction is bounded between 2/4 and 3/4. These are arithmetic bounds, not a statistical confidence interval. The unmatured case remains accruing rather than counted as a failure or silently dropped from cohort accounting. Report coverage alongside performance; selective disappearance of difficult observations must be detectable.

Withdrawal or explicit cancellation may be an observed failure for the specifically defined target, not a censoring excuse. A merger can produce a composite outcome or terminate comparability; use the prespecified rule and identity history, not today's survivors. A failed source fetch is missing observation, not evidence that the economic event did not occur. A late disclosure changes the evidence clock, not the originally specified target date.

**EX05** has one issuer source reproduced by seven outlets and illustrated by two derived charts. It remains one source family, not ten independent confirmations. Repeated reporting can help availability or error checking but must not multiply economic confidence. Same event across parent/subsidiary, internal trades or multiple distribution roles likewise needs a defined counting boundary [P07,P17].
