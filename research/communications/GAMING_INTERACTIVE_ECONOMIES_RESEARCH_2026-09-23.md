# Communications Phase 4 — Gaming and Interactive Economies

**Research date:** 2026-09-23. **Operation:** `gmi-communications-research-20260923-sol-001`.
**Carrier:** Macro Draft/HOLD PR #7794, `claude/communications-sector-research-20260923`.
**Status:** Original research and proposed product requirements. Not an accepted final specification, live taxonomy, securities recommendation, validated forecast or Fable dispatch.

## 1. What this phase resolves

The investor needs to distinguish a popular launch, a durable player-spending franchise, a productive creator ecosystem and an attractive equity expectation gap. The machine must connect a title or business mechanism to the correct financial channel without confusing activity, purchases, accounting recognition, retained economics and market expectations.

The proposed organizing question is: **Can this business replenish profitable player relationships more effectively than it consumes development capital, distribution fees and operating investment?** Our working hypothesis is that durable advantage is better represented by the renewal of economically valuable cohorts than by a static label such as gaming, metaverse or AI beneficiary. This hypothesis is not a validated stock-selection factor.

The phase adds six issuer/business anchors: Roblox, Take-Two, Nintendo, Sony, Tencent and NetEase; a historical withdrawal/refund case; and official measurement documentation. It extends, rather than repeats, the prior advertising and content-rights work. Shared sector architecture, GMI semantics/evidence, ThemeState, F04 composition, existing identity/rights/clock owners and evaluation/selection authority remain intact. No new graph, collector, metric store or ranking policy is created.

## 2. Seven economic roles to distinguish

These are research categories to reconcile with existing vocabulary, not seven new canonical themes or baskets.

| Proposed role | Economic question | Candidate mechanism to investigate |
|---|---|---|
| Premium franchise publisher | Does development investment earn durable contribution across launches and the catalog? | Net realized price, sell-through, discounting, refunds, installed franchise, sequel conversion and development recovery |
| Live-service operator | Can retained and reactivated payers replenish spending faster than cohorts decay? | Content cadence, payer retention, spend per payer, acquisition cost and live-operations burden |
| Mobile acquisition operator | Does incremental acquisition create contribution after distribution and advertising? | Cohort payback, attribution uncertainty, paid versus organic acquisition, advertising monetization and channel incentives |
| User-created-content platform | Does better creator supply improve retained player contribution enough to justify payouts and infrastructure? | Creator incentives, discovery quality, conversion, distribution costs and content diversity |
| Console/platform ecosystem | What is the lifetime contribution of an activated customer, not merely a shipped device? | Hardware contribution, first-party software, third-party distribution, subscriptions and network activity |
| Developer, licensor or service supplier | Which cash flows are owned, shared, licensed or contracted? | Development milestones, royalties, minimum guarantees, publishing rights and revenue-sharing terms |
| Cross-media intellectual property | Does reuse create incremental earnings rather than simply more awareness? | Documented licensing, territory, duration, ownership and the division of economics across media |

One company can occupy several roles. A hardware or developer-tools adjacency need not be a Communication Services constituent. The existing classification owner, not a research label, decides structural sector membership. Issuer, subsidiary, business, title, platform, franchise and tradable security remain different objects.

## 3. The analytical unit: a business-scoped cohort

A proposed cohort has a title or platform scope, country, acquisition or activation window, monetization channel, release/version identity and observation horizon. It should distinguish new, retained, reactivated and migrated players. Person-level tracking is unnecessary for this research; public aggregate evidence or properly authorized aggregate panels are sufficient inputs.

For a matched population and period, the following is a decomposition, not a forecasting claim:

`player spending = active players × payer fraction × spending per payer`

The factors must refer to the same universe. Monthly unique payers are not a quarterly sum of distinct people; concurrent users are not monthly actives; an account is not necessarily one human; annual active players cannot be substituted into a monthly yield formula.

The next step is the economic bridge:

`spending → taxes/refunds/distribution → contractual receipts → recognized revenue → creator/rights costs → operating investment → cash → diluted equity claims`

The ordering is conceptual. Actual accounting follows the issuer's reported treatment. Revenue recognition may defer both income and associated processing costs while other costs are incurred immediately. A prepaid balance can support current cash while carrying future service obligations. Consequently, no universal subtraction of current costs from bookings can be assumed to yield a comparable contribution margin.

### A more useful durability model

I propose separately modeling: acquisition quality; payer survival; repeat purchase and reactivation; release/content replenishment; distribution bargaining power; reinvestment intensity; and concentration in individual titles. A fall in current spending can coexist with stronger future retention, but that is a hypothesis requiring subsequent evidence. Equally, a release spike can be profitable without being durable.

The strongest opposing explanation should be recorded beside every thesis. A new title may expand the franchise or cannibalize an old one. Higher creator payouts may improve supply or simply transfer economics without incremental demand. A direct channel may reduce fees while requiring user discounts, support and fraud expense. More AI-generated content may lower production cost while increasing discovery congestion and moderation requirements.

These alternatives determine what to measure next. They are not reasons to average everything into a generic confidence score.

## 4. Six current issuer anchors

All current financial comparisons below concern the quarter ended June 30, 2026 unless expressly stated otherwise. Fiscal-year names differ among issuers. Each cited table, policy or management explanation keeps its own publication date; none is a same-day stock recommendation.

### 4.1 Roblox: retention, monetization and creator incentives

Average monthly unique payers were 27.0 million versus 23.4 million, while monthly bookings per payer were $19.25 versus $20.48. Its disclosed Q3 outlook allowed revenue growth of 4–10% alongside a bookings decline of 14–18% [R1]. These observations require distinct spending and recognition views.

Management attributed weaker hourly monetization partly to a shift toward lower-monetizing games and recommendation changes favoring retention; its claim that longer retention should eventually offset lower yield remains a managerial hypothesis, not independently established causality [R3].

The filing describes a 42% prospective DevEx increase from June 8 for eligible in-game spending by age-checked US adults, not a platform-wide rate change. Processing costs largely follow deferred revenue timing, while creator-related expenses have a different recognition pattern. Advertising and licensing were insignificant to reported revenue [R2].

**Proposed model.** Evaluate discovery changes using matched cohort retention and cumulative contribution, not immediate spend alone. Preserve geographic and eligibility boundaries for incentive changes. Compare the incremental creator supply and payer contribution with additional payouts and operating cost. Do not assign all platform growth to AI, age checks, one recommendation change or an adult-content strategy.

**What would change the read.** Evidence that more-retentive cohorts eventually contribute more after incremental costs would strengthen the reinvestment hypothesis. Persistently lower cumulative contribution, concentration in a few subsidized experiences, or a widening infrastructure burden would weaken it. Management experiment claims are useful context; without accessible methods/results, they are not a replication.

**Measurement boundary.** The disclosed 27-month paying-user estimate governs accounting recognition; it is not a subscription contract, guaranteed customer lifetime or a measured forward investment horizon. The earlier foundation's bookings reconciliation is reused, not claimed as a new discovery.

### 4.2 Take-Two: the existing portfolio versus the launch option

Reported revenue increased from $1,503.8 million to $1,533.9 million while bookings fell from $1,423.1 million to $1,385.9 million. Mobile bookings were $739.5 million. Recurrent consumer spending accounted for approximately 84% of bookings and includes discretionary purchases and advertising, not only contracted subscriptions. The August 7 outlook retained $8.0–8.2 billion full-year bookings and referenced a November 19 GTA VI launch expectation [T1]. This is that dated outlook, not certification of the latest release calendar.

The filing reports $2,431.0 million of current plus noncurrent development/licensing assets, and $43.4 million of impairment for a canceled unannounced third-party project. The $173.0 million development/licensing cash-flow movement is already inside operating cash flow [T2].

**Proposed model.** Separate existing franchises, mobile acquisition economics, new releases and royalties. A large future launch is an option on timing, realized demand and subsequent engagement; it should not overwrite the current business. Capitalized development is a carrying amount to recover, not an estimate of a title's fair value or undisclosed budget.

**What would change the read.** More durable spending from existing cohorts, disciplined acquisition payback and a launch converting interest into net realized contribution would strengthen the thesis. Delay, weak conversion, deeper discounting, refunds or poor repeat spending would weaken particular components. A delay can move cash between fiscal years without destroying the same amount of lifetime value; the timing and demand effects need separate scenarios.

**Financial boundary.** Expensed research, capitalized development, amortization, impairment and cash investment cannot be summed indiscriminately. Portfolio-level development recovery matters even where successful-title adjusted earnings exclude failed projects.

### 4.3 Nintendo: installed cohorts and software mix, not shipment growth alone

Nintendo reported sales of ¥517.813 billion, down 9.5%, while operating profit rose 150.5% to ¥142.596 billion [N1]. Its explanatory material reports a 61.5% digital share of software revenue; first-party software is presented gross and other publishers' software on a net-commission basis. Bundle software units are included in unit counts while bundle revenue is classified as hardware. Annual playing-user measurements can be revised when offline activity is later received [N2].

**Proposed model.** A console cohort's value is hardware contribution plus future retained software, distribution and subscription economics, less support, development and capital requirements. Current shipment changes are not a complete demand forecast. Compare launch stocking with mature-cohort replacement carefully; channel sell-in and consumer sell-through require different observations.

**What would change the read.** A broader activated base with sustained software purchases and profitable first-party releases supports lifetime contribution. Weak consumer adoption, channel accumulation, discounts or insufficient release cadence weakens it. Do not infer channel inventory from unmatched hardware and software totals.

**Measurement boundary.** An apparent software average price can be distorted by bundle classification and third-party commission reporting. Current annual-user figures revised with later data cannot silently replace what was knowable in an earlier study.

### 4.4 Sony: ecosystem economics versus reported profit catalysts

Sony's Game & Network Services revenue was ¥937.1 billion versus ¥936.5 billion, while operating income rose from ¥148.0 billion to ¥202.0 billion. Its presentation identifies currency and US tariff refunds among the operating-profit drivers, offset in part by investment and restructuring [S1]. This does not establish equally strong underlying recurring operating leverage.

The supplement reports ¥208.6 billion network-services revenue versus ¥172.6 billion and an 82% digital-download ratio measured in full-game units. Digital software and add-on revenue includes gross retail transactions for first- and third-party content; network services includes advertising as well as subscriptions [S2].

**Proposed model.** Separate device economics, first-party content, distribution transactions, add-ons and network services before estimating equity sensitivity. Reported earnings changes require a source-bound driver bridge; do not turn nonrecurring benefits or translation into player-spending acceleration.

**What would change the read.** Greater recurring contribution from active cohorts and productive first-party releases supports the thesis. Higher retention expense, subscription downgrades, poor release economics or hardware cost pressure weakens it. Hardware supply and component cost are relevant where they constrain this model, but physical BOM analysis must not dominate all gaming research.

**Peer boundary.** Sony's 82% unit ratio and Nintendo's 61.5% revenue ratio cannot determine which company has superior digital monetization. Their gross/net presentation also prevents a naive revenue-scale comparison of third-party distribution economics.

### 4.5 Tencent: domestic, international and social activities

Tencent reported domestic-games revenue of RMB47.3 billion and international-games revenue of RMB18.6 billion. International growth was negative 0.8% as reported but positive 4% at constant currency. VAS revenue of RMB98.4 billion also contains social-network activities. Its own evergreen-game definition combines platform-specific active-user thresholds with annual gross-receipts criteria [TX1].

**Proposed model.** Preserve domestic/international demand, title mix, distribution and currency separately. A title can be important to an operator through development, publishing, licensing or investment; the relationship does not determine its profit share without additional evidence. Do not treat all VAS or consolidated AI spending as gaming exposure.

**What would change the read.** Repeat contribution across a renewing portfolio, not just one launch, supports durability. Weak contribution despite more releases or growth concentrated in acquired/licensed economics could weaken it. A company-defined evergreen label is research evidence about management's classification, not a universal lifecycle standard.

### 4.6 NetEase: owned-game economics and portfolio replenishment

NetEase's games and related services revenue was RMB25.023 billion against RMB30.107 billion consolidated revenue. The segment generated RMB19.049 billion gross profit. Management linked year-on-year games growth to self-developed franchises and lower consolidated revenue-sharing/product costs; those explanations do not quantify every title's contribution [NT1].

**Proposed model.** Distinguish self-developed and licensed economics, but do not assume every owned game has better returns after development risk. The relevant comparison includes failed projects, content refresh, marketing, distribution and operating investment. Consolidated music, education and other businesses remain outside a pure gaming-revenue proxy.

**What would change the read.** Portfolio renewal that preserves contribution after development and acquisition costs is stronger than growth reliant on one franchise. A better reported gross margin without adequate next-generation title replenishment may be temporary. These are proposed analytical tests, not conclusions about present valuation.

## 5. How gaming businesses could rerate

The proposed rerating mechanisms are conditional economic explanations. No coefficient, probability or stock ranking is approved.

| Mechanism | Why expectations could change | Discriminating evidence and opposing explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Franchise renewal | Investors revise the duration and reliability of future contribution | Repeat spending from comparable cohorts; rule out launch-only novelty and discounts |
| Creator-market deepening | Better supply broadens profitable demand | Incremental contribution after payouts; rule out subsidy-driven activity |
| Distribution migration | More spending reaches a lower-cost channel | Net retained economics after incentives/support; rule out simple cannibalization |
| Platform-cohort maturation | Installed customers buy more profitable content/services | Matched active cohorts and contribution; rule out shipment stocking and gross/net mix |
| Launch de-risking | Uncertain timing/demand becomes better evidenced | Actual release, net sales and retention; interest and trailers are not realized cash |
| Development-capital discipline | More invested cash produces usable, profitable releases | Whole-portfolio cash recovery, including cancellations; adjusted profit may omit failures |
| Geographic expansion | New markets add valuable players or distribution | Country-specific monetization and cost; rule out low-yield audience growth and FX translation |
| Catalog/IP reuse | Existing rights support incremental contribution | Documented contracts and incremental demand; awareness alone does not establish earnings |

### Equity valuation discipline

Use a sum of operating scenarios for materially different businesses rather than a universal gaming multiple. Mature cash-generating franchises, early creator-platform reinvestment and unreleased titles can have different uncertainty and capital requirements. Keep the issuer's financing, tax, minority interests and diluted claims visible.

Any comparison with expectations must distinguish original guidance, a subsequently revised guide, dated analyst consensus and a house scenario. Rolling a forecast to a new fiscal period is not the same as revising the original period. A forward release date needs a source date and scope; company enthusiasm is not a probability.

A useful hypothetical timing calculation illustrates the distinction: receiving 100 units one year later instead of now is worth 90.91 today at a chosen 10% discount rate, before any change in demand or cost. Those values are invented assumptions, not a discount-rate recommendation. A delay model must also test whether quality improves, costs continue, competitors launch or the installed platform base changes.

## 6. A creator-platform reinvestment model

The hardest hypothesis is not that more content or more creators is good. It is that the additional supply improves profitable demand enough to cover the investment needed to obtain it.

I propose a two-sided model with a separately observed creator side and player side. Creator-side variables include accepted/active supply, repeat publishing, content quality, earnings concentration, net migration and incentive eligibility. Player-side variables include discovery-to-play conversion, early abandonment, retention, payer conversion and cumulative spending. The connecting evidence must establish the relevant cohort and time window.

Lower payout retention today can be rational if supply elasticity is sufficiently strong, but the required elasticity is not known from an announcement. In a synthetic example, a platform retaining 40 units per 100 of spending requires spending to rise to 114.29 to retain the same 40 after its retained share falls to 35%. This ignores other costs and is a break-even illustration, not a Roblox estimate.

For AI creation, measure a proposed chain of qualified content production, actual availability, discovery, player satisfaction and retained economics. Asset counts or a developer survey do not establish incremental player value. Faster development could lower costs, increase output, change the competitive bar or increase discovery congestion. It may also shift bargaining power between tools, developers, publishers and distribution. Those mechanisms should remain distinct from a generic AI tag.

## 7. Leading indicators and feasible data

| Candidate evidence | Useful question | Required restriction |
|---|---|---|
| Official releases/filings | Did demand, recognition, costs or guidance change? | Fiscal period, original version, scope, currency and measure basis |
| Title release and update history | Is expected content actually becoming available? | Announcement, test, regional launch and broad release are different |
| Public concurrent-player observations | Is activity persistent at comparable times? | Platform-specific concurrent use, not unique customers or revenue |
| Sales rankings | Is relative commercial activity changing? | Window, territory, bundle grouping, discounts and ordinal nature |
| Wishlist information | Is pre-release interest forming? | Aggregate access and context; no universal conversion factor |
| Reviews/engagement | Is experience quality changing? | Selection effects, ownership basis, moderation and release age |
| Price/promotion history | Are net monetization and acquisition incentives changing? | Territory, tax, package, currency and actual realization |
| Licensed cohort panels | Is retention or acquisition payback improving? | Coverage, permissions, definition changes and historical availability |
| Cash/development disclosures | Is portfolio renewal productive? | Recognition/capitalization/impairment and cash already inside CFO |

Steam's official documentation defines Top Sellers using multiple revenue streams, describes a trailing 24-hour real-time ranking with greater weight on recent activity, and distinguishes presentation contexts [ST1]. This is not an absolute unit-sales series. Its current-player API excludes offline players and measures players currently active in a specified Steam app [ST2]. Wishlist breakdowns are publisher-reporting data; Valve explicitly warns against a universal sales-prediction formula [ST3]. No private publisher access or new ingestion is claimed here.

### Research should begin with information we can qualify

Start with original issuer releases, measured public title history and source-defined public observations. Richer panels can be evaluated under existing data procurement and rights owners. Undisclosed contracts, lifetime payer value, refund cohorts and private experimentation remain unknown unless appropriately sourced.

Do not build a second historical store because these questions are interesting. Existing evidence and observation owners must preserve the necessary vintage and corrections. Current data availability is not proof that a historical decision maker could observe the same feature.

## 8. Historical failure and evaluation design

Sony's September 3, 2024 Concord notice announced removal from sale, service withdrawal on September 6 and refunds for purchasers [C1]. It does not disclose a development budget or a verified number of units sold. Those quantities must not be invented.

This is an important negative case: commercial launch is not durable adoption. An evaluation universe assembled only from games still operating will exclude precisely the failures that a useful research system must learn from. Take-Two's disclosed project cancellation provides a separate example of development capital that did not reach commercial release [T2].

### Proposed three-stage validation

**Description:** Can the dossier correctly explain what was observed, at the time, with comparable metrics? Tests cover recognition differences, accounting basis, source scope and issuer contribution. This is necessary but not evidence of forecasting edge.

**Operating forecast:** Does a pre-specified signal improve a later operating forecast over simple lifecycle, seasonality and franchise baselines? Use fixed observation checkpoints such as day 7, 28 and 90 after an actually observed launch. Separate premium, free-to-play, mobile and platform cohorts. Use out-of-sample or walk-forward evaluation, include failures and control title overlap across train/test periods.

**Investment-use qualification:** Does the information improve an existing governed decision beyond dated expectations, price/factor context and costs? This remains a separate evaluation and authorization task. No trading policy is created by passing an operating test.

Abstention must be measured. Unavailable console data or uncertain title ownership is not zero activity. Delays, withdrawals, refunds, fiscal changes and later corrections remain in the cohort. Avoid using today's user-definition history, later age classifications, revised annual activity or post-launch popularity to select historical opportunities.

There is no historical stock-return backtest or accepted predictive model in this phase. The Concord example is an original-source failure case, not a reconstructed trading result.

## 9. Proposed product integration

The existing Theme Tracker and company/detail workflow should expose **growth quality and reinvestment**, with a plain-language explanation of which dimension changed. This is a content and contract contribution to the shared template, not a new standalone dashboard.

A useful card might say: “Payer growth is stronger, but spend per payer is lower. Management is prioritizing retention; later cohort contribution must establish the payoff.” Another could say: “Profit improved sharply, but the report identifies currency and refunds; recurring player economics need their own evidence.” These are source-bound explanations, not generic buy/sell calls.

The detail journey should show the business role, material current changes, title/cohort context, financial bridge, dated expectations, contrary/missing evidence and the existing company/watchlist route. A graph should explain a documented development, publishing, distribution or licensing relationship, not imply causal profit sharing from a similar title name.

### Mapping into existing owners

GMI retains semantic identity and accepted assertions; native evidence owners retain sources and corrections; the existing issuer/security owner resolves the tradable instrument; F04 composes the product; current market/selection owners supply their own dimensions. Where a required field does not yet exist, this research defines the need, not an unauthorized schema migration.

The advertising-first candidate vertical is not displaced by this phase. Gaming offers additional fixtures for the same template: incompatible denominators, active-use versus paying-use, managerial reinvestment hypotheses, title failure and calendar changes. Fable should receive a coherent reusable contract rather than isolated sector-specific widgets.

## 10. Coverage, limitations and continuation

This phase supplies six issuer anchors, seven proposed economic roles, a cohort/reinvestment model, fifteen primary references, an explicit failure case, leading-indicator feasibility and product/evaluation requirements. It is not a global gaming census. Company-level financial observations do not resolve every franchise's revenue weight, all securities, private contracts or any present stock recommendation.

The accompanying `GAMING_METRICS_AND_ACCEPTANCE_2026-09-23.md` contains proposed definitions, checked research arithmetic and future acceptance cases. Written cases are not executed application tests. Research-only local exports do not become a new production data authority.

The next substantive economic family is terrestrial connectivity: wireless, fiber, cable and fixed wireless. Research should connect customer cohorts, pricing/promotions, deployment, utilization, capital intensity and financing across US, Canadian and other material operators. Satellite follows as a distinct deployment/optional-growth model, including physical constraints where economically important. Remaining geography, full exposure mapping, historical qualification and the final approved specification/implementation plan remain owed before Fable's build handoff.
