## 3. Positive counterexample: a corporate title can contain real guidance

TTD's 26 January announcement is primarily titled as an interim-CFO appointment. Its body nevertheless explicitly reaffirms the 6 November outlook for **Q4 2025**, with revenue of at least USD840m and adjusted EBITDA of approximately USD375m [S15].

This is a useful positive guidance event, but it is not a Q2 2026 event. A title-only financial-news filter would miss it; a company-plus-calendar-year selector could misassign it. Both defects are avoided by retaining the actual statement, speaker/issuer attribution, target period, measure, bounds and source date. This study does not evaluate who currently holds that corporate role.

The converse occurs in Magnite's June partnership release: a customer uses the word guidance to describe business assistance, not a financial outlook [S11]. Keep the commercial relationship as relevant context without converting the keyword into an issuer forecast. Keyword presence and a numerical-looking paragraph nearby are insufficient.

## 4. Positive counterexample: a later event changes expense, not every outlook

Meta's 26 August issuer announcement says it expects an approximately USD10bn Q3 legal expense that was outside the expense range discussed on its Q2 call. It says the other July guidance ranges remain unchanged [S16]. The associated call explicitly reaffirms Q3 revenue [S17]. This event is **after the Q2 result**, so it cannot revise what management had said before that result.

The useful interpretation is a scoped change: **revenue outlook reaffirmed; expected expense increased by a separately described item; the timing and nature of cash consequences require their own evidence**. Do not replace a whole guidance bundle with a single changed/unchanged flag. Do not infer a Q4 revenue forecast. Do not manufacture exact new annual-expense endpoints by adding an approximate item and label the result a newly issued management range.

This gives Communications a financially meaningful transmission model. An unexpected expense can lower reported earnings expectations without establishing weaker advertising demand. A revenue reaffirmation does not, by itself, validate margins, free cash flow or valuation. The research should show which financial line changed, its timing, the source's qualifiers, and the unresolved bridge to cash and per-share economics. No tax rate, dilution assumption or price reaction is supplied by these statements.

### The nested-speaker trap

The call's question about an affirmed Q3-and-Q4 range is read aloud **inside the CFO's transcript turn**, attributed to an analyst. The CFO then says the Q3 revenue outlook is unchanged and no outlook beyond that quarter has been provided [S17, printed p5]. Merely labeling everything within a CFO speaker block as management guidance would admit the analyst's false premise.

A future extraction therefore needs the supported statement's attribution and discourse role, not just the outer speaker heading. Keep the quoted question distinct from the response that rejects or corrects its premise. This is a source-context requirement for the existing guidance owner, not an instruction to build another transcript parser or event system.

The event-index entry presents 25 August at 00:00, while the issuer release and transcript heading state 26 August [S13,S16,S17]. For these case records, retain the source-stated 26 August date and the separate index discrepancy. Do not invent a midnight timestamp, explain the difference as a timezone conversion without evidence, or silently alter an historical selection. Exact original knowability is unproved.

### Accrual and cash are not interchangeable

The call describes the approximately USD10bn accrual as discounted guaranteed payments **net of prior accruals** [S17]. The announcement separately discusses nominal payments and conditional components [S16]. The 10bn is not simply an immediate cash payment, a measure of the entire undiscounted obligation, or an independently invertible present-value equation. Prior accruals and timing matter. This study draws no conclusion about legal enforceability or subsequent court approval.

## 5. Included assumptions prevent double-counting a headwind

In the 29 April follow-up, management says the revenue effect of Europe's updated less-personalized-ad flows will be larger in Q2 and that the expected impact is already factored into its Q2 outlook [S12, printed p3]. No numerical amount for that effect is established by the selected passage.

A model starting from that management outlook must not subtract the full same headwind again merely because it discovers the explanation later. The economically relevant adjustment is the difference between the scenario being modeled and the assumption already embedded in the starting estimate. If the embedded assumption is unquantified, a precise numeric adjustment is unsupported.

A synthetic illustration makes the distinction explicit. Let an unaffected starting value be 110 and an embedded headwind be 5, producing a guide-like baseline of 105. Subtracting the same 5 again yields an unjustified 100. If a genuinely new scenario increases the headwind from 5 to 8, the comparable scenario changes 105 by **−3**, reaching 102. These are invented values, not Meta estimates, a validated model or a trading recommendation.

A statement that management included a factor is not proof analysts adopted it, that the market priced it, that the estimate is unbiased, or that another firm's outlook includes the same factor. The system needs separate management, consensus and price-incorporation evidence. This distinction preserves the original project goal: discover genuine expectation gaps rather than manufacture them by counting a disclosed risk twice.

The later Meta example can be presented as “Revenue outlook reaffirmed; an additional expected expense affects the earnings interpretation.” It must not turn a Q2 history page into a current Q3 forecast, or imply that an original-guide delivery reading has changed retroactively. Source history and system replay stay distinct.

Three related outputs should remain separate:
- **Original-guide delivery:** the specified original statement versus the matching result.
- **Latest reviewed statement:** the newest qualifying statement within an explicitly named reviewed collection, with its coverage limitations.
- **Latest issuer statement before the result:** a stronger claim requiring an accepted completeness boundary and the relevant temporal/identity proof.

The second must not be shortened to the third. A corrected source is another dimension: retain what was available then and what is now understood, rather than using a current corrected body as silent historical evidence.
