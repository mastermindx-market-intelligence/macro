## 1. Decision and contribution

The first comparison should explain what happened using qualified facts. Its next intellectual layer should answer **what operating improvement would have to occur for an investment or valuation assumption to make economic sense**. Do not jump from a thematic association or a historical growth rate to a stock-price target.

This study adds a conditional chain:

**Business driver → incremental retained revenue → incremental operating contribution → required investment and cash timing → taxes and financing → claims and share count → conditional value → dated expectation comparison.**

The chain is an analytical model, not a new source store, runtime state machine or trading authority. A missing link does not erase a useful earlier observation. It does prevent the missing link from being silently filled with zero, a peer average or an LLM guess.

Three approaches were considered. Extrapolating the latest reported incremental margin is superficially simple but confounds demand, fixed-cost changes, mix and reporting presentation; reject it as the default. Conditional, source-scoped driver models expose assumptions and break-even conditions; use these for research now. Statistically fitted sensitivities may later improve prediction, but only after original-vintage inputs, identifiable questions, stability checks and out-of-sample evaluation exist. The eighteen examples here do not establish that third capability.

No live price, current market capitalization, current multiple, analyst consensus, issuer-specific discount rate, probability or target price was obtained. The four A1 companies remain Meta, Alphabet, The Trade Desk and Magnite; no automatic winner or new basket member is produced.

## 3. Reported base, historical slope and marginal sensitivity are different

A reported revenue base states the size of an activity under a disclosed definition. Its percentage change describes two comparable observations. Neither establishes the profit effect of the next unit of demand.

Let operating profit be `P = m × R − F`, where `m` is a retained variable-contribution rate and `F` is the collection of fixed costs included in this simplified model. Across two periods, changes in `m` and `F` affect `ΔP / ΔR`. Consequently that historical ratio need not equal the marginal derivative with fixed costs and mix held constant.

**SX01 — identification failure.** Two invented models both reproduce revenue 100 → 110 and operating profit 30 → 32. Model A is `P = .2R + 10`. Model B is `P = .5R − F`, with fixed cost 20 then 23. At revenue 120, A produces 34; B produces 37 if fixed cost remains 23. The two observed points do not select one model. A precise fitted slope cannot resolve an omitted mechanism.

For real research, determine whether the proposed shock is new volume, price, mix, cost, accounting presentation, an acquisition, or a combination. State which variables move together. A unit-price increase with unchanged demand is a scenario assumption, not a fact about customer elasticity. A current operating margin is not a forward incremental margin, and an accounting item classified as fixed may become step-variable at a different scale.

Record the affected business, unit and period before calculating. An extra dollar of Meta advertising, Alphabet Search revenue, TTD retained fees and Magnite contribution ex-TAC is not the same accounting object. They cannot be summed into a sector profit pool or ranked by a shared unqualified margin.

## 4. Four role-specific sensitivity models

### 4.1 Meta / owned audiences: monetization gains versus retention, mix and cost

For one compatible audience/format population, write revenue as `R = V × Y`, where `V` is monetizable volume and `Y` is realized net yield. The exact change is:

`ΔR = Y0 ΔV + V0 ΔY + ΔV ΔY`.

The interaction is real arithmetic, not a third independent driver. If volume has already been modeled as users × activity × ad opportunities, do not add those factors again as independent percentage lifts. Cross-format migration must be counted as replacement where applicable, not entirely as newly created demand.

An illustrative operating bridge is:

`P = V × (Y − c) − F − D`,

where `c` is variable serving/content cost per compatible unit, `F` is remaining operating cost and `D` is depreciation included in this profit definition. Costs already included in `c` cannot also be deducted in `F`. This is not a statement of Meta's disclosed unit cost or an advertising-only allocation of consolidated expenses.

**SX02.** With volume 100 → 110, yield 1 → 1.02, cost/unit .30 → .32, fixed costs 20 → 23 and depreciation 10 → 15, revenue rises from 100 to 112.2 while operating profit falls from 40 to 39. Favorable monetization does not settle the profit question.

The useful rerating hypothesis is that a monetization or retention improvement persists long enough, and at sufficient retained contribution, to exceed the cost of sustaining it. Its contrary evidence includes cannibalization of higher-value activity, rising service costs, customer/advertiser reaction, or new infrastructure commitments that overwhelm the near-term gain.

**What remains unestimated:** marginal cost by format/market, adoption and retention elasticity, share of an improvement caused by AI, utilization of new infrastructure, economic replacement life, and the operating/capital allocation to each initiative. The research should not invent those coefficients by spreading company capex in proportion to thematic revenue.

### 4.2 Alphabet / discovery: substitution, distribution and incremental intent

Separate legacy and new discovery surfaces before asking whether a new interface creates value. For migrated interactions, the relevant comparison is **new retained contribution versus the contribution displaced**. For genuinely additional interactions, compare their contribution with their additional servicing and acquisition requirements.

**SX03.** An invented incumbent serves 100 legacy interactions with contribution .8 each. Thirty migrate to a new format contributing .6 each. Contribution falls from 80 to 74. Ten genuinely additional new-format interactions restore 80 before any extra fixed investment. Counting every migrated interaction as new growth would hide the six-unit loss. This is not an estimate of Alphabet's AI-search economics.

**SX04.** Two categories have unchanged acquisition-cost rates of 10% and 50%. Moving revenue mix from 80/20 to 90/10 lowers the aggregate cost rate from 18% to 14%. This can improve the blended economics while establishing nothing about renegotiated unit pricing. Separate the favorable mix effect from a favorable price effect; do not count both for one observation.

The model therefore needs commercial-intent composition, monetization event, paid distribution exposure, serving/inference expense, publisher/content terms and substitution. A query is not automatically an ad impression, conversion, paid action, or incremental user task. Google's Services segment is broader than Search; group margins cannot simply become Search's marginal margin. Shared AI expense and future depreciation must enter the whole-issuer bridge somewhere, once.

**What remains unestimated:** incremental versus substitutive discovery, commercial conversion, marginal acquisition payments, new-format contribution and the precise allocation of shared development/infrastructure. A rising aggregate revenue-per-query measure cannot independently resolve these quantities.

### 4.3 The Trade Desk / buying services: retained fees versus workload and funding

A conceptual retained-fee model is `R = S × t + service fees`, with `S` the correctly defined expenditure base and `t` the economically retained fee rate. The formula is not permission to infer an undisclosed quarterly gross-spend denominator from revenue. Specify whether service fees are already included in `tS` before adding them.

**SX05.** Re-presenting a supplier component of 15 from a revenue deduction to an expense moves reported revenue 100 → 115 and costs 40 → 55 while leaving profit at 60. A statistical revenue-sensitivity model trained without the comparable presentation bridge can learn an accounting change instead of demand economics. This does not assert that all of TTD's observed revenue change arose from re-presentation.

The cost base may depend on queried opportunities as well as purchased impressions or customer spending. **SX06** assumes spend 1000 → 1100 at a retained fee of 20%, while request volume rises from 10,000 to 15,000 at .005 per request and remaining costs stay at 100. Retained fees grow 200 → 220 but modeled profit falls 50 → 45. These assumed costs are not TTD disclosures.

A second bridge concerns collection and settlement. Growth can require financing of buyer payments before amounts are recovered. Evaluate the contracts and balances rather than assuming an intermediary has negligible working capital because its revenue is net.

**What remains unestimated:** a comparable gross-spend and retained-fee series, unit infrastructure workload economics, cohort spending, recurring cash operating costs, receivable/settlement distributions and buyer credit exposure. Ending recognition of an old compensation charge can improve a comparison without proving labor productivity or removing outstanding security claims.

### 4.4 Magnite / selling services: contribution, channel mix and seller settlement

Use the issuer's contribution ex-TAC definition where that is the relevant comparison, while retaining the bridge to GAAP revenue and profit. Do not subtract the same TAC again from a contribution value that already excludes it. A CTV share of contribution is not the share of net profit attributable to CTV.

**SX07** holds total retained receipts at 100 and moves channel mix from 40/60 to 50/50. Under assumed contribution rates .6 and .3, operating contribution increases from 42 to 45. A mix shift matters only through its economics; those margins must not be inserted as Magnite estimates. Unallocated costs, new product costs and compensation still need the issuer bridge.

**SX08** assumes annual gross billings of 3,650, a 10% fee, uniform daily activity and ten additional days to collect, with seller settlement unchanged. Incremental funding is 100. Using net fees instead of gross collections would estimate only 10. In a separate assumed no-recovery credit case, a 1% loss on gross billings consumes 10% of the fee pool. Real contracts may allocate loss differently; do not infer liability from a platform relationship alone.

**What remains unestimated:** channel contribution margins, price/volume and retained-fee splits, contract-specific seller economics, transaction timing and recoveries. Netting broad seller balances against cash is not a substitute for a maturity, control, currency and availability analysis.

## 5. Reinvestment and capacity change the marginal answer

The immediate marginal contribution below capacity can differ from the economics of sustained growth. **SX09** assumes .5 contribution per unit and an additional capacity cost of eight once activity exceeds 105. Growing 100 → 110 reduces profit by three; growing 110 → 120 adds five without another capacity block. Applying either slope universally would be wrong.

For research, separate current operating expense, paid capital expenditure, acquired-but-not-yet-paid assets, construction, assets in service, committed leases, partner funding, guarantees and future replacement. They have different timing and claim meanings. Do not add an undiscounted lease schedule to capitalized liabilities and then deduct the same full payment schedule again. A maximum guarantee exposure is not automatically its expected present-value loss.

**SX10** isolates a book-depreciation assumption. EBITDA remains 200, depreciation changes from 100 to 75, cash tax stays 20, capex 120 and additional working capital ten. EBIT rises from 100 to 125; cash after the stated uses remains 50. This is a controlled illustration, not an allegation that a company's useful-life judgment is wrong. If tax deductions or actual replacement economics also change, model those changes separately; the bookkeeping change alone supplies neither.

A short-run cash improvement achieved by deferring essential investment should not automatically raise sustainable free cash flow. Conversely, a cash trough during an investment cycle need not imply a permanently impaired franchise. The discriminating evidence is the subsequent capacity utilization, retained contribution, timing and repeat investment needed to preserve service.

### A measurable investment hurdle

**SX18** assumes 1,000 of investment now, five equal year-end net incremental cash payments, a 10% discount rate and zero residual value. The required annual cash payment is:

`C* = 1000 / Σ(t=1..5) (1.10)^(-t) = 263.797481`.

If all five payments arrive one year later while initial investment stays today, the hurdle becomes 290.177229. If an independently assumed 40% conversion of extra revenue to net incremental cash covers every required recurring use in this example, required annual revenue is 659.493702. That 40% is a thought-experiment input, not an empirical coefficient or a company forecast.

This question is actionable research: which adoption, pricing, cost or timing evidence would make the hurdle achievable? It avoids declaring a current investment a success merely because its assets or audience expanded. A residual value may be appropriate in another model, but it must be explicitly justified rather than added to make a weak case pass.

## 6. Translate earnings into the shareholder's claim

For matched earnings and shares, the identity is:

`EPS1 / EPS0 = (NI1 / NI0) / (N1 / N0)`.

**SX11:** net income +8% and shares +10% produce EPS growth of approximately -1.8182%. Weighted-average diluted shares used for accounting, period-end basic shares, conversion terms and valuation-date claims serve different questions. Do not pick whichever denominator produces the most favorable result. An antidilutive instrument for one reported loss period may still be a real contingent economic claim.

**SX12:** an assumed equity value of 1,000, including excess cash, supports 100 shares. Repurchasing 100 of shares at the assumed fair value of ten leaves value of 900 and 90 shares: value per share remains ten. Mechanically holding earnings of 100 constant raises EPS from one to 1.1111. The illustration deliberately excludes taxes, fees and foregone cash income; it shows why EPS accretion alone is not value creation. Buying at eight or twelve changes the remaining hypothetical value per share to about 10.2857 or 9.8182, respectively, under the same fixed value premise.

### Compensation and dilution: a consistency requirement

Separate future compensation needed to run the business from existing outstanding claims. A model must recognize the future economic cost once, whether through a reviewed cash-equivalent expense treatment or explicit expected future security claims. It also needs separate treatment of existing claims not already captured. Historical GAAP expense is not automatically the fair economic value of future grants.

**SX13** is only an algebraic consistency test: perpetual pre-compensation cash of 100, an assumed annual compensation claim of 20 and discount .10 give 800 after the claim. Subtracting its 200 present value from a pre-claim value of 1,000 also gives 800. Doing both for the same future claim gives an erroneous 600. A *distinct* existing claim worth 50 would then leave 750. No option-pricing model, grant probability or conversion to real issuer shares is established by this example.

Keep buybacks, net-share-settlement tax payments, awards, exercise proceeds and repurchase authorization separate. Authorization is not executed net share reduction. Do not assume all cash on an intermediary's balance sheet is distributable before settling its operating obligations.

## 7. Valuation growth must pay for its investment

For a restricted stable-state illustration, let `NOPAT1` be next stable-year after-tax operating profit, `g` its sustainable growth, `r` the return on *incremental* invested capital and `k` a consistent nominal cost of capital. With constant definitions and no other reinvestment requirement, the reinvestment fraction is `g/r` and:

`V = NOPAT1 × (1 − g/r) / (k − g)`.

This follows the standard growth/reinvestment link [S06]. It is not an estimate for a company transitioning through a high-growth or heavy-construction phase. The experiment restricts itself to positive profit and `0 ≤ g < min(k, r)`; outside that domain it refuses instead of returning a persuasive number. It does not claim no broader financial model can represent a company needing external funding.

**SX14: first stable-year NOPAT held at 100, k = 10%.** All values below are invented model outputs, not currency-denominated security prices.

| Incremental return | Value at 2% growth | Value at 4% growth | Interpretation within this model |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 20% | 1,125.00 | 1,333.33 | Growth creates value after required reinvestment. |
| 10% | 1,000.00 | 1,000.00 | Growth earns its funding cost; the effect nets to zero. |
| 6% | 833.33 | 555.56 | Faster growth requires more value-destroying investment. |

This is not a perpetual forecast for advertising. It proves why raising a growth assumption while leaving capex, development and working capital unchanged can overstate value. Incremental return is not necessarily reported return on book capital; acquisitions, expensed development, intangible assets, leases and timing can change the comparison.

### Reverse valuation gives a conditional hurdle, not the market's unique belief

Define `M = V/NOPAT1` in that same stable-state model. Solving for incremental return yields:

`r = g / [1 − M(k − g)]`.

**SX15:** with M = 12 and g = 3%, k = 10% implies r = 18.75%; k = 9% implies about 10.7143%. The same multiple therefore does not identify one return assumption. One price is insufficient to solve simultaneously for growth, margins, investment, risk and durability.

At k = 10% and g = 3%, the model's multiple approaches 14.2857 as r tends to infinity. A multiple of 15 has no positive finite r solution *under those fixed assumptions*. That is a model-consistency signal, not proof that an actual stock is overpriced. A finite high-growth phase, other assets, a different profit definition or different assumptions may change the result. Do not manufacture a negative required return or silently change a parameter to force a fit.

For actual reverse research, first establish valuation date, issuer/security claims, operating versus nonoperating assets, selected forecast horizon, currencies and the source of each assumption. Then show a family of conditional combinations. Do not label the fitted combination 'consensus' without a qualified consensus source, or treat it as actual investor positioning.

## 8. Why better earnings can coexist with a lower price

The matched-horizon identity `P = EPS × P/E` gives:

`P1/P0 = (EPS1/EPS0) × (multiple1/multiple0)`.

**SX16:** EPS increases 20% while the multiple falls from 20 to 16. The hypothetical price falls 4%; adding +20% and -20% would incorrectly predict no change. This is not a prediction for one of the four companies. Actual attribution also needs dividends, corporate actions, appropriate security rights and comparable estimate horizons.

A business improvement can coexist with disappointing delivery versus an even higher expectation, higher required investment, a shorter period of competitive advantage, a changed discount rate, or a different distribution of future outcomes. The model should keep those hypotheses separate until supporting evidence discriminates among them. The fact that price fell does not prove which hypothesis caused it.

Use a consistent currency and real/nominal basis for cash and discount rates. Do not explain every Communications stock with the same interest-rate direction: operating sensitivity, cash interest, borrowing costs and valuation duration can offset one another. A sum-of-parts framework must include corporate costs and overlapping shared investment once, not assign every dollar to several attractive themes.

## 9. Scenario ranges are not confidence intervals

A low/high coefficient envelope is a set of assumptions, not a statistically calibrated confidence interval. Preserve relationships between inputs rather than sampling everything independently.

**SX17** uses two invented, equally weighted states: volume 80/yield 1.2 and volume 120/yield .8. Each produces revenue 96. Multiplying mean volume 100 by mean yield one gives 100 instead. Joint-state revenue must be calculated before averaging. The weights in this illustration do not estimate real probabilities.

For an actual model, state which dependencies are assumed, measured, contractually constrained or unknown. Volume and price, growth and reinvestment, grants and share prices, and cash balances and settlement obligations need coherent joint treatment. A rectangular sensitivity grid can reveal dependencies without pretending every corner is a plausible forecast.

A source definition or target period being wrong remains an independent failure even when every formula balances. This replay cannot detect incorrect real-world annotations, estimate behavioral elasticities, validate source rights or reconstruct information available to investors at a historical cutoff.

## 10. Evidence required before any predictive promotion

Research records should distinguish a reported base, a historical accounting attribution, a conditional scenario and an empirically evaluated forecast. These are explanatory labels within existing owners, not new admission states.

A useful next validation panel would retain, for each fixed business question: original input vintage; precise operating shock; response horizon; units and population; acquisition/reporting changes; controls for relevant common drivers; forecast issued before the outcome; realized financial line; cash/claim bridge; and a benchmark. Missing target data is not a zero outcome. Survivors and favorable cases alone are insufficient.

Candidate questions are specific:
- Does a compatible monetization change add information about later retained contribution after already-known cost and investment guidance?
- Does comparable intermediary customer spending or workload improve later contribution/cash forecasts beyond a revenue-only baseline?
- Does a channel-mix change predict economic improvement once its channel contribution and shared costs are qualified?
- Does a publicly available investment milestone add information about cash timing, rather than merely restate a previously issued plan?

Before fitting coefficients, test whether the input was actually observable at the proposed decision time. Avoid annualization that conceals seasonality, overlapping forecast windows counted as independent samples, and model selection on the same episodes used to report success. Compare a revenue-only or last-observation baseline, and retain abstentions and failed hypotheses. Any portfolio/trade use additionally requires the existing evaluation and investment owners; this study grants none.
