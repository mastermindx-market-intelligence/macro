## 1. The investor question and the new intellectual contribution

The investor needs to distinguish a technically impressive satellite system, a commercially useful service, a financeable operating business and an attractive equity expectation gap. Our proposed organizing question is: **Can usable capacity, delivered to the right place at the right time, generate enough retained customer value to pay for deployment, operation, replacement and the claims ahead of common equity?**

This phase completes the initial economic-family pass begun in the Communications foundation. It does not complete the entire commission. It connects AST SpaceMobile, Globalstar, Iridium, Viasat, Telesat, SES and Eutelsat with manufacturing and distribution examples including MDA Space, Amazon, KDDI, SoftBank and the proposed US mobile-operator joint venture. These are business-role examples, not a resolved security universe or a claim that every company is structurally classified in Communication Services. Private networks and cross-sector counterparties may be economically important without being direct basket constituents.

The distinctive contribution is a **capacity-to-cash model with conditional commercial and financing milestones**. It avoids three tempting but unproven shortcuts: counting launches as usable network capacity, counting partner customers as paying satellite subscribers, and valuing growth cash before the cost of sustaining the constellation. Provenance and accounting checks enable that explanation; they are not substitutes for it.

The shared sector template, GMI graph and evidence ownership, ThemeState, existing relationship/propagation owners, F04 composition and evaluation/selection authorities remain intact. No new technical simulator, map service, rights registry, lifecycle, valuation engine or publisher is being built here.

## 2. Six distinct economic roles

| Research role | Economic job | Retained-value question |
|---|---|---|
| Direct-to-device network | Reach compatible handsets or low-power endpoints without a separate consumer dish | Which service classes work, how often, and what does the wholesale or retail contract pay? |
| Fixed broadband network | Sell broadband using dedicated customer equipment | Does contribution recover terminal support, acquisition, capacity and replacement cost? |
| Mobility and managed connectivity | Deliver service to aircraft, ships or other moving customers | Does installation, integration and reliability retain enough economics after purchased capacity? |
| Narrowband, safety and industrial connectivity | Deliver valuable messages, telemetry, voice or continuity with limited traffic | Is the service indispensable and contractually remunerated, even when byte volume is small? |
| Spacecraft, terminal and ground supplier | Supply the infrastructure that enables a network | Does a funded order become accepted delivery and cash at a sustainable margin? |
| Retail/distribution or device partner | Bundle access, own the customer and support the experience | Does incremental revenue or retention exceed wholesale and support obligations? |

These roles can coexist in one issuer. An operator may own one orbit and buy capacity in another. A supplier can benefit from construction before the operator earns material service receipts. A platform may offer an end-user feature without a separately itemized retail fee while paying another company under a commercial agreement. The exposure graph must represent these different claims, rather than create mutually exclusive winner/loser labels.

A service definition needs at least the endpoint type, application class, geography, direction of traffic, availability requirement, observation period and commercial status. Satellite backhaul into a terrestrial cell is not direct communication with a handset. Messaging, selected-app data, native voice and general broadband are not interchangeable product states.

## 3. Capacity belongs to a location, time and service class

A headline throughput figure describes a specified test or design. Our proposed model instead asks what traffic can actually be served during the customer's relevant demand window. Define usable capacity for a location and interval subject to the access link, scheduled spectrum and power, feeder/backhaul path, routing and gateway constraints, terminal compatibility, and the required service quality. The binding constraint can change as deployment proceeds.

For a simple serial path with capacities expressed on the same basis, the minimum link capacity bounds end-to-end throughput. For a constellation with alternate paths and shared resources, merely taking one global minimum is insufficient: allocation, geography, simultaneity and routing matter. Nor should we multiply several guessed availability percentages and call the result an engineering forecast. The research initially records the binding constraint, supporting observation and unresolved dimensions; any quantitative network model requires separate evidence and validation.

This distinction is supported by a 2026 end-to-end NTN research paper using ns-3 and 3GPP calibration scenarios. Its authors explicitly limit their evaluation to static, single-user, downlink-oriented UDP scenarios and discuss future multi-user, TCP and moving-geometry work. Their results depend on assumed antenna, power and bandwidth configurations. They are not a field benchmark or a ranking of commercial constellations [NT1].

For the investor, the more useful quantity is **monetizable service capacity in the intended market**, not the total theoretical traffic a fleet could carry if geography and demand did not matter. A beam over an unserved region may create valuable resilience without filling every minute. The economic value must follow the actual service contract, not an automatic revenue-per-terabyte multiple.

### 3.1 Why coverage and capacity can disagree

A system can increase the number of places that receive occasional connectivity without proportionately increasing simultaneous high-quality service. Conversely, added capacity in an already-covered busy area may materially improve customer value without changing a geographic coverage percentage. A lower orbit, a larger antenna, a faster processor and a different spectrum band address different constraints; none alone proves superior retained economics.

The proposed views therefore keep four dimensions distinct: geographic reach; fraction of relevant time available; service-class performance under stated load; and paid utilization. Unknown dimensions remain useful questions rather than invented zeros.

### 3.2 What a credible bottleneck claim needs

A claim that a component constrains supply should specify the component/configuration, affected generation, qualification requirement, required quantity, available qualified output, lead time or delivery evidence, and the downstream milestone it constrains. Announced factory capacity is not achieved, qualified output. A catalog product is not proof of a supply award. A shipping container, launch manifest, separated satellite, deployed antenna and accepted network asset describe different observations.

For satellite systems, physical bottlenecks may genuinely warrant BOM-style research. Candidate constraints include antenna/payload manufacture, power and thermal systems, radiation-qualified electronics, integration/testing, launch cadence and orbit delivery, gateway commissioning and terminal installation. This is a research checklist; no undocumented shortage or named supplier relationship is asserted merely because it is technically plausible.

## 4. Dated evidence anchors and the interpretations they support

The facts in this section are deliberately compact. The surrounding economic questions are our proposed analysis, not company guidance. Figures retain native currencies, periods and source-defined bases. The companion calculates selected arithmetic from these observations without claiming independent source audit.

### 4.1 AST SpaceMobile — revenue is not yet service adoption

AST's August 10 filing reports Q2 product revenue of $24.4m and service revenue of $7.1m, principally gateway/software and government development/testing-related activities. It states that SpaceMobile Service had not generated revenue. Management's $21–23m average direct-material-and-launch estimate for Block 2 satellites is scoped, assumption-dependent and not a fully loaded network cost [AS1].

**Implication:** gateway deliveries can be useful commercial progress without proving paid consumer-network adoption. The next evidence belongs to the actual service: eligible endpoints, market availability, contract billing and collected economics. A partner's whole mobile base is not the paying denominator. A cost estimate excluding other infrastructure, operations, spectrum-related commitments and replacement cannot be multiplied by satellite count and presented as complete funding need.

The same filing records BB7 being delivered into an unsustainable lower orbit and de-orbited. It reports $21.6m insurance collected, $10.9m receivable and a $125.9m net loss after $32.5m recoveries; a replacement launch is expected under the contract [AS1].

**Implication:** insured asset recovery and restored service timing are different. A receivable is not cash collected, and a replacement-launch entitlement is not an in-service replacement. The historical observation belongs in the failure population, not excluded because the broader technology remains promising.

### 4.2 Globalstar — customer funding versus recurring cash generation

Globalstar reports H1 operating cash flow of $159.7m and capital expenditure of $208.3m. Operating cash includes $104.8m infrastructure prepayment and $15.0m accelerated service fees. Its company-defined adjusted FCF is not simply operating cash less capital expenditure. Some network costs are reimbursed and recognized in revenue [GS1].

**Implication:** commercial prepayments may substantially reduce financing risk, while also committing future capacity or service. The useful question is how much contribution remains after satisfying the obligation. Deducting the two disclosed early receipts is only a sensitivity on timing, not a certified normalized cash measure. We must not label all customer funding low quality or ignore its obligation: both would distort the economics.

Amazon's April 14 announcement describes an agreement to acquire Globalstar, a separate Apple agreement, and a next-generation Amazon D2D deployment beginning in 2028. It distinguishes existing device satellite features from the future system [AM1]. Globalstar's August release still describes closing conditions and an expected 2027 closing [GS1].

**Implication:** merger consideration, future service capacity and current operating value are separate research objects. This chapter does not qualify present transaction completion, merger-arbitrage economics or current instrument eligibility.

### 4.3 Telesat and MDA — one program, different denominators

Telesat's August 4 announcement expands the fully funded Lightspeed constellation from 156 to 225 satellites. MDA's same-day announcement expands its prior manufacturing scope from 198 to 225, an increment of 27. The C$474m supplier contract increase also includes a spectrum-payload configuration change and other long-lead items [TS2, MD1].

**Implication:** 69 additional funded satellites and 27 additional ordered satellites are compatible because their starting populations differ. Dividing the full contract change by 27 produces a mathematical quotient, not a disclosed satellite price. A product should resolve this scope split explicitly rather than hide it as a contradiction or select the larger number.

Telesat's contract headline is C$2.3bn, with two approximately C$0.2bn options giving C$2.7bn including options. Its August results report C$1.1bn June LEO backlog and C$5.6bn pro forma, alongside C$2.0bn milestone payments expected from Q3 2026 through Q4 2028. Commercial service remains a management target for around the end of Q1 2028 [TS1, TS2].

**Unresolved component bridge:** the selected public materials do not reconcile 1.1 + 2.7 to 5.6. The 1.8 difference is not evidence of an additional unnamed award, error or a second cash receipt. Nor may milestone funding simply be added to the service contract without qualifying overlap. A primary contract/filing reconciliation is needed before constructing a clean backlog waterfall. This source question does not invalidate the separately supported count reconciliation.

Telesat and Orange's September 17 notice reports commissioning a European Lightspeed gateway [TS3]. That is a ground-system milestone, not evidence that the planned global constellation is commercially available.

### 4.4 Viasat — an orbit competitor may also supply the service

Viasat's Q1 FY2027, ended June 30, reports communications revenue of $825m versus $827m and adjusted EBITDA of $311m versus $322m. Aviation service revenue grew 11%, while fixed services/other declined 27%. Its NexusWave offering combines Ka-band, LEO, LTE and L-band connectivity [VS1].

**Implication:** compare customer jobs, distribution and retained service economics—not just orbit labels. A managed provider may monetize installation, routing, support and contractual reliability while buying some capacity from another network. The opposing hypothesis is that lower-cost direct offers compress those retained economics. Both require evidence on renewals, paid installations, purchased-capacity costs and margins; a named integration does not disclose its unit profitability.

Viasat's cash discussion reports $291m operating cash and $72m FCF excluding specified sale-related cash taxes, versus $219m capex. These adjusted headline amounts cannot be silently used as unadjusted GAAP cash [VS1]. We retain the prose-defined basis; the cash-page chart could not be visually rendered during this pass and is not used for additional chart-only arithmetic.

### 4.5 Iridium — valuable connectivity is not always high-volume broadband

Iridium reports Q2 total revenue of $225.237m and service revenue of $161.328m. Service categories differ: IoT revenue rose from $44.741m to $47.071m, broadband fell from $12.724m to $11.674m, and hosted/other rose from $14.545m to $16.571m. OEBITDA fell from $121.313m to $119.107m. Its government service contract and engineering revenues have distinct bases [IR1].

**Implication:** the economic role of availability, device integration and a customer workflow can matter more than bytes carried. This is a hypothesis about value, not proof that any low-bandwidth franchise is protected from competition. Research should test renewals, substitution, contribution and future replacement needs separately. Total connections cannot serve as a universal ARPU denominator across these businesses.

The source also discusses subsequent corporate transactions. Its reporting period is retained here; this chapter does not establish current merger completion or use a static issuer record as security-identity truth.

### 4.6 SES and Eutelsat — constellation growth is not issuer growth

SES reports H1 revenue growth of 72.4% at constant currency on its reported perimeter, but a 5.0% constant-currency decline on the like-for-like perimeter including Intelsat in the comparison. Eutelsat reports FY2025–26 LEO revenue of €297m, up 69.5% like-for-like, while total revenue was €1,235.9m and reported growth was negative 0.6%; its EBITDA margin and legacy activities remain separate [SE1, EU1].

**Implication:** a fast-growing business can offset a contracting one without producing the same growth for the issuer. Acquisition perimeter, currency, segment mix, integration costs and financing each require their own bridge. The model must not apply a pure growth-business multiple to all consolidated cash, or interpret acquisition-driven reported expansion as organic satellite demand.

A raw growth calculation from displayed euro amounts is not the source's constant-currency result. A segment share needs the named denominator, especially where a headline's rounded share and operating-vertical totals differ.

## 5. Retail proof, device scope and the proposed US joint venture

KDDI's September 15 announcement describes existing light-data/messaging service and an August agreement targeting native voice and broadband by the end of 2027. Its earlier satellite backhaul and current handset service are distinct [KD1]. SoftBank's April 10 launch notice specifies eligible services, devices and plans, with no additional charge for specified plan groups [SB1]. These are dated service disclosures, not universal present-day availability or tariff certifications.

The commercial interpretation must distinguish the customer who buys a device, the subscriber who has an entitlement, the person who uses satellite service, and the entity contractually paying the network. Free-to-user can be consistent with wholesale payment or a partner-funded retention strategy. But the amount and contribution remain unknown until disclosed or legitimately modeled. Do not multiply a carrier's entire base by a made-up satellite ARPU.

The May 14 AT&T/T-Mobile/Verizon announcement is an agreement in principle, subject to finalization. It states that existing satellite relationships continue and that carriers can pursue independent efforts [JV1]. The bounded later search did not establish completion. The correct research status is **completion not verified**, not a functioning unified platform and not proof the proposal was abandoned.

Amazon's future system [AM1] and KDDI's next-generation target [KD1] illustrate why an announcement can be commercially important today without being operational today. The expectation effect may precede revenue, but identifying a dated catalyst is not proving a stock-return opportunity.

## 6. Twelve candidate subtheme mechanisms and opposing explanations

The following are research hypotheses to map into existing owners. They are neither admitted canonical themes nor ranked investments.

| ID | Mechanism and economic beneficiary to investigate | Evidence of progress | What would weaken the interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| SM01 | D2D messaging/continuity adoption for network and retail partner | Scoped availability, billed entitlements, use and retention | Broad entitlement but weak retained contribution or costly obligations |
| SM02 | D2D voice/broadband capability transition | Repeatable end-to-end performance, compatible devices, paid availability | Peak demonstrations without continuous service under relevant load |
| SM03 | Fleet commissioning converts construction into revenue | Accepted in-service capacity and customer activation on the same geography/time basis | Launch counts rise but acceptance, gateways or device integration lag |
| SM04 | Government anchor reduces financing risk | Binding funded scope, milestones achieved, collected cash and remaining costs | Options or funding ceilings treated as guaranteed service margins |
| SM05 | Multi-orbit managed-service value retention | Installation/renewal contribution after purchased capacity | Direct substitution or wholesale costs compress retained margin |
| SM06 | Aviation or maritime installed-base maturation | Activated aircraft/vessels, utilization, contract yield and installation recovery | Wins remain uninstalled or customer traffic does not monetize |
| SM07 | Industrial/safety workflow durability | Renewals, service quality and customer economics | Comparable alternative satisfies the workflow at lower total cost |
| SM08 | Spacecraft/payload supplier execution | Funded order, qualified output, acceptance and cash collection | Backlog expands with working-capital consumption or rework |
| SM09 | Gateway/terminal integration unlocks bottleneck | Commissioned site, connected route and paid service enablement | Installed hardware without compatible operational network |
| SM10 | GEO/LEO transition preserves whole-issuer cash | New contribution exceeds legacy erosion and duplicate operating costs | Segment growth masks weakening consolidated cash and funding need |
| SM11 | Replacement productivity sustains economic returns | Cost per comparable accepted capacity-year improves | Low near-term capex conceals a large unfunded replacement wave |
| SM12 | Distribution/interoperability changes bargaining power | Binding commercial terms, retained fees and customer adoption | Partnership publicity without material economics or exclusivity |

The moat is the explanation and its update discipline. Every mechanism needs business scope, a cash channel, an expected lag, a dated expectation reference and contrary evidence. A rising stock price or a repeated company presentation cannot supply all five.

## 7. Deployment economics need a replenishment model

A growing fleet's first major investment cycle and its sustained operating economics are different. The proposed model separates initial deployment, expansion, maintenance/ground support and replacement. Accounting depreciation may be informative but is not automatically the replacement cash requirement. Technological improvement may reduce replacement cost per useful unit, while larger new generations may increase the cost per spacecraft. Comparable service capacity and useful life are the proper research denominators.

For an illustrative steady fleet of 120 spacecraft, a six-year economic life and eight monetary units per replacement imply a long-run simple replacement allowance of 160 units per year. This ignores staggering, failures, launch prices, improvements and discounting; it is not an operator forecast. Its purpose is to show why zero near-term replacement spending does not establish sustainable free cash flow.

A mature network and a growing one require different interpretations. Some negative cash reflects value-creating expansion, but calling every outflow growth investment would erase the cost of sustaining the service. Conversely, expensing all infrastructure in one year's model can conceal a productive long-life asset. The research must preserve both the project economics and the owner's reported cash.

### 7.1 Stage-conditioned value, without invented probabilities

Model plausible operational scenarios as conditional paths: deployment/acceptance, customer activation, retained price, utilization, replacement and financing. Do not multiply unsupported independent success probabilities; engineering, supply, timing and financing outcomes can be related. Keep probability weights unavailable until evidence supports them.

An announced addressable market is not a cash forecast. Separate market size, realistically serviceable demand, capacity-constrained sales, contractual receipts and per-share distributable value. Additions to supply can increase volumes while reducing the price per unit. A lower unit cost may help the customer more than the operator if competition passes the saving through.

### 7.2 Delay and dilution can overwhelm project progress

Consider a hypothetical project equity value of 1,000 units and 100 shares. A delayed scenario produces only 950 units of final equity value but requires another 200 units of funding. Issuing that equity at five units creates 40 new shares, reducing the resulting value to about 6.79 per share. Raising at ten units creates 20 new shares and produces about 7.92 per share. These are invented financing scenarios, not a valuation or probability for any company.

The important research conclusion is that project progress and per-share progress can diverge. The appropriate bridge identifies debt, restricted cash, options/convertibles, partner interests, prepaid-service obligations and prospective funding. Restricted funds and uncommitted borrowing capacity are not interchangeable with freely deployable cash. No current company is assumed to have a financing gap merely because this scenario exists.

### 7.3 Match valuation to the actual stage

For an established service business, investigate normalized customer contribution and replacement-adjusted cash with explicit financing and customer concentration. For a deployment-stage network, investigate cost-to-complete and conditional cash scenarios. For a supplier, investigate accepted backlog, margin, warranty and working capital. For a retailer, investigate incremental customer value net of access costs. For an announced merger, separate the dated transaction contract from an independent-business scenario and defer current instrument status to the identity/corporate-action owner.

No universal EV/revenue multiple, satellite-count multiple or LEO premium is approved. An adjustment that removes a noncash mark must not erase the underlying economic claim or dilution represented by that instrument.

## 8. Historical and forward validation

BB7 provides a current documented failure case [AS1]. It illustrates why launch, target orbit, deployment, acceptance and useful service should be stored as distinct observations. It does not estimate the failure rate of a constellation or launch provider. The later dataset must include failed deployments, degraded payloads, canceled projects, recapitalizations and acquired issuers, not only networks operating successfully today.

Maintain three evaluation jobs: descriptive accuracy; forecast of an operating milestone or economic outcome; and usefulness for a governed investment decision. The evidence burden increases between them. Technical model output is not a trading signal, and public narratives are not complete engineering telemetry.

For a forward milestone study, freeze the exact source date, promised scope and then-current target window. Score whether the same promised capability was delivered, partially delivered, delayed, changed or not observable. A changed satellite generation or narrower country rollout must not count as unqualified delivery of an earlier global promise. Treat target revisions as revisions, not retroactively correct forecasts.

For an operating study, use consistent segment/perimeter/currency and compare forecasted billable service to realized receipts. For an investment study, additionally qualify security identity, capital actions, original expectations, executable price timing, total returns and market/sector controls. Success in explaining a press release is not out-of-sample alpha. No empirical return or forecast study was run here.

## 9. Leading evidence and practical data feasibility

The feasible public starting set is issuer financial disclosure; original contract and amendment announcements; dated service and supported-device pages; defined deployment/commissioning events; documented technical studies; and source-qualified customer/installed-base measures. Higher-frequency physical observations or network tests are useful only when lawful, relevant and comparable. A public source does not automatically confer redistribution rights.

The data plan should prioritize **decision-changing measurements**: accepted capacity in a targeted region, recurring billed-service onset, paid customer utilization, backlog-to-cash conversion and remaining funded obligations. It should not begin by collecting every satellite position or reproducing an entire vendor catalog. Those datasets need a named intelligence job and a lawful existing owner.

For each indicator, record release lag, revision policy, coverage, denominator, whether it is a design/test/live observation, and the entity authorized to use it. Satellite position or a launch notice can support a deployment claim but cannot establish payment, full health or consumer service quality. Retail plan inclusion can support an entitlement claim but not the supplier's realized revenue. A government framework ceiling is not an awarded, funded, recognized-revenue line.

The technical paper's simulation boundaries [NT1], KDDI's separation of current and future products [KD1], and MDA/Telesat's different count bases [MD1, TS2] give concrete fixtures for these safeguards. They are not independent predictive experiments.

## 10. Product implication: useful evidence-to-economics, not a space dashboard

The proposed investor journey is the existing theme view into a scoped service/business dossier, then the material change, binding constraint, economic exposure, expected timing, financing/valuation context and existing company/watchlist workflow. The glance view should answer what improved, why the business may benefit, and which remaining condition matters most.

Three candidate components deserve integration into the shared template: a service-capability matrix; a deployment-to-billed-service timeline; and a retained-cash/remaining-commitments bridge. The matrix distinguishes messaging, selected data, voice and broadband by market and evidence stage. The timeline exposes corrections and partial delivery. The cash view identifies funding that carries a future obligation. These are read projections over existing owners, not new canonical systems.

For the Telesat example, the UI should explain the compatible funded/order counts, show the original contract scope, and leave the unreconciled backlog composition visible without constructing a false total. That is a useful resolution-plus-uncertainty result. For AST, product and development revenue can remain visible while consumer-service receipts are explicitly unproven in the selected filing. For a mature operator, a fast-growing segment should not hide legacy erosion or the replacement burden.

Scope and time differences are not automatically genuine contradictions. Conversely, citations cannot make inconsistent arithmetic disappear. Existing conflict categories and evidence gates should handle both; this sector program must not invent its own confidence engine. Research priority, economic forecast and trade eligibility remain separate.
