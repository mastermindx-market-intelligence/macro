# Communications Phase 3 — Streaming, Content and Music Rights

**Research date:** 2026-09-23. **Operation:** `gmi-communications-research-20260923-sol-001`.
**Carrier:** Macro Draft/HOLD PR #7794, `claude/communications-sector-research-20260923`.
**Status:** Original research and proposed requirements. Not an accepted final specification, production dataset, securities recommendation, causal return study or Fable dispatch.

## 1. Outcome and material conclusion

The investor should be able to distinguish a popular service, a commercially successful content investment, and an attractive equity expectation gap. Those are different objects. This phase extends the existing Communications foundation and advertising work into subscriptions, content investment, sports, recorded music, publishing, licensing and fan monetization. It preserves the existing sector template, GMI semantic/evidence owners, ThemeState, F04 composition and evaluation/selection authorities. It creates no new rights registry, graph, publisher or trading score.

Our proposed organizing question is: **Does the business retain more customer value than it must spend to acquire, serve and retain that customer, replenish its content, and finance its obligations?** Content popularity is supporting evidence, not the answer. A rights holder's receipts, a distributor's revenue and a customer's spending may describe overlapping parts of the same transaction.

Three intellectual improvements follow from the research. First, model the paid relationship and its survival rather than just a subscriber count. Second, explain accounting profit, content cash investment and contractual obligations separately. Third, distinguish ownership, administration, distribution and licensed access before attributing economic exposure to a company. The final stock judgment additionally needs dated expectations and the whole-issuer capital structure; no such judgment is made here.

## 2. Four economic roles, not one media model

| Proposed analytical role | Economic mechanism to investigate | Primary dossier anchors |
|---|---|---|
| Subscription distributor/aggregator | Net realized price, retained paid months, advertising contribution and bargaining with content suppliers | Netflix, Spotify; Disney and Comcast streaming businesses [N1,N2,S1,D1,C1] |
| Mixed content owner/distributor | Content exploitation across own services, third-party licensing, theatrical and other businesses; group-level offsets | Disney, Comcast [D1,D2,C1] |
| Recorded-music/publishing rights business | Contractual participation in licensed consumption, catalog mix, artist terms, advances and reinvestment | UMG, Warner Music [U1,U2,W1] |
| Regional music/audio/IP ecosystem | Membership, distribution, audio, fan spending and acquired-business perimeter | Tencent Music [T1,T2] |

These are research roles, not sector reclassifications or newly admitted baskets. One issuer can occupy several roles. A streaming business's economics need not describe the entire issuer. Names and listing labels are navigation; the existing identity owner must resolve legal entity, subsidiary, security, depositary ratio and point-in-time membership before live use.

### 2.1 Subscription economics: price is only one term

Proposed revenue model: sum retained paid relationship-months multiplied by net realized revenue per relationship, plus separately defined advertising and licensing. Explicitly distinguish a paying account, a household, a profile, a bundled entitlement and an active user. Gross adds and ending subscribers cannot substitute for average paid months.

For a cohort acquired in month c, a useful research formulation is:

`cohort value = sum_t [survival(c,t) × contribution_per_active_relationship(c,t) / (1+d)^t] − acquisition_cost(c)`.

Contribution must state whether content and service costs are allocated or marginal. Shared content investment must not be charged once per title attribution and again in consolidated cash flow. A constant-hazard `ARPU/churn` shortcut is only an approximation under restrictive stationary assumptions, not the default valuation engine.

A price increase can raise current revenue while impairing lifetime value through later cancellations. Distinguish customer segments, channel commissions, promotions, grandfathering, tax and geography. The price displayed to a new customer is not the effective realized price of every existing relationship. Rejoining users can generate revenue again without making an uninterrupted survival cohort recover retroactively.

### 2.2 Bundling is a joint-economics problem

A bundle can reduce churn, reduce selling cost or increase utilization; it can also discount customers who would otherwise have purchased both services. The research must separate genuinely incremental customers, reactivated customers, existing single-service upgrades and already-dual subscribers receiving a discount. A reported subscriber gain for each service need not mean two new households.

Evaluate incremental bundle contribution versus the appropriate counterfactual, not the sum of standalone list prices. Allocate revenue and cost only under a disclosed or labeled modeled rule; internal allocations are not new external money. The correct measurement horizon includes promotion expiry and renewal, not just launch sign-ups.

## 3. Netflix — distinguish demand, content efficiency and cash investment

### Source observations

Netflix's July 16 Q2 letter reports revenue of $12.560bn, operating margin of 33.4%, and H1 viewing of 97bn hours. Management differentiates acquisition and retention roles of content rather than valuing every hour equally. It plans to replace its twice-yearly engagement report with an annual release beginning Q1 2027, while retaining weekly Top 10 reporting. The roughly $3bn 2026 advertising target is an outlook, not reported annual advertising revenue [N1].

The Q2 10-Q cash-flow table, in thousands of dollars, reports content additions of 4,927,523, a content-liability change of -181,794 and content amortization of 4,311,309. The implied cash-content bridge is therefore 5,109,317, approximately 1.185 times amortization. Operating cash flow less PP&E purchases is 1,743,812 − 218,644 = 1,525,168. H1 also contains a $2.8bn termination fee and separately disclosed unusual tax payments; annualizing a quarter or treating all H1 cash as subscription economics is unjustified [N2].

### Proposed economic interpretation

The investment question is whether new and existing content increases retained contribution enough to cover the cash investment and the opportunity cost of other uses. A service may use an expensive event to acquire members, a broad library to retain them, and recommendation improvements to connect them. There is no reason to assign an identical value to each viewing hour.

Build a content investment bridge with separate clocks: contractual commitment, production/license payment, availability, audience observation, amortization, customer billing and cash collection. A change in any one can lead or lag the others. High cash investment relative to amortization might finance future growth or reveal a weaker cash profile; it is not automatically evidence of deteriorating content quality. Conversely, reduced spending can temporarily lift cash without making the franchise more durable.

Public title statistics cannot identify customer-level incremental lifetime value. At the public-data tier, the product should describe engagement, release cadence and company-reported financial outcomes, then explicitly withhold a title-level return-on-investment claim. A scenario may test what retention improvement would be necessary, but must not present that assumed improvement as measured.

The engagement-report cadence change creates a practical requirement: freshness must follow the source's declared release schedule and period coverage. A lack of a six-month report after a switch to annual publication is not evidence of zero viewing, a failed collector or a stale company financial filing. Different dimensions can legitimately have different clocks.

## 4. Disney and Comcast — a streaming milestone is not whole-company acceptance

Disney's fiscal quarter ended June 27, 2026. Entertainment SVOD operating income was $712m versus $329m, with a 12.9% margin. Its definition excludes Hulu Live TV and Fubo vMVPD results. Total segment operating-income improvement of $980m decomposes into Entertainment +$658m, Sports -$179m and Experiences +$501m; the SVOD improvement accounts for $383m within Entertainment. The fiscal 53rd week is separately identified in annual guidance [D1].

Disney's filing reports Sports revenue of $4.500bn versus $4.308bn, while programming/production costs rose to $3.050bn from $2.762bn and operating income declined to $858m from $1.037bn. It attributes costs to contractual increases, new rights and NBA-renewal recognition timing that shifted costs from the first half into the third quarter [D2].

Comcast reports Peacock's first positive quarterly adjusted EBITDA, $189m versus a $101m loss, with approximately $1.9bn revenue and 48m paid subscribers. The quarter includes World Cup effects. Group reporting also distinguishes completed Versant/Sky-Germany perimeter changes from an announced intention to separate NBCUniversal and Sky. The announcement does not itself establish a completed future separation [C1].

### Proposed decision model

Keep the service, segment and issuer distinct. A streaming profit inflection may matter greatly, but its contribution to the equity depends on other operating businesses, corporate costs, tax, financing, minority interests and the capital invested. A sum-of-parts analysis must eliminate intercompany receipts and charges; licensing between two subsidiaries is not new group revenue.

For sports, evaluate a rights season or contract cohort rather than extrapolating one quarter. Define fixed commitments, contingent rights payments, production costs, advertising inventory, contractual distribution fees, subscription acquisition and subsequent retention. Strong event-related acquisition does not establish that members remain after the season. A change in cost-recognition timing should not be confused with an equally sized change in cash commitment.

Test two competing hypotheses. One is that distinctive rights support a higher-value retained bundle and additional advertising. The other is that bidding and distribution costs absorb those gains, or that benefits accrue elsewhere in the group. The necessary evidence includes effective rates, audience definitions, advertising realization, retention after the event, and a season-level cost bridge. A sporting-event audience record alone cannot decide between them.

A positive quarter is meaningful evidence, not proof of sustainable annual profitability. The UI should show it as the measured milestone and attach the next qualification: full-season contribution, repeatability and cash. Nor should a negative quarter caused partly by cost timing automatically label the entire rights strategy unsuccessful.

## 5. Spotify — subscription value, cost mix and reverse causality

Spotify reports 300m Premium subscribers and €4.777bn Q2 revenue, with operating income of €655m versus €406m. Social charges included in operating expenses were €1m versus €115m a year earlier. The current deck restates an operating-income guide of €630m; €9m of the €25m excess came from social charges below forecast. Removing those charges symmetrically gives a mechanical operating-income sensitivity of €656m versus €521m, not a fully normalized or constant-currency result. Its FCF reconciliation is €816m operating cash flow − €21m company-defined capex + €2m restricted-cash change = €797m [S1].

### Proposed economic interpretation

The research must distinguish willingness to pay for music access, the economics of a broader audio bundle, advertising monetization, and platform operating expenditure. Higher realized price can support margins if retention and supplier economics cooperate; it can also create later cancellations or higher contract costs. Product expansion should be evaluated as incremental retained contribution, not merely as more features included with a subscription.

Social charges are a particularly important causal guard. Where expense depends on share-based award value, stock-price movement can change reported profit. A model can otherwise mistake an effect of the stock price for a fundamental cause of the same price move. Do not automatically interpret lower charges as restructuring savings. Keep the reported result, symmetric sensitivity, management's attribution and any predictive hypothesis separate.

The current report's actual-versus-guidance table is not an independently recovered pre-release forecast archive. It supports a clearly labeled comparison to guidance as restated by the issuer. A historical strategy still requires original availability timestamps and any intervening revisions.

Subscription accounts, active users and paying users require their own definitions. Do not sum displayed subscriber and advertising-user series into a global count of unique people without an overlap contract. An app can be a discovery funnel, a distribution channel, a rights counterparty and a commerce surface; each relationship needs a separate economic basis.

## 6. Music rights — the valuable constraint may be a contract, not a component

Spotify's royalty guide distinguishes recording and composition/publishing royalties. It describes streamshare-based allocation rather than a universal fixed per-stream price, with territory and monthly context, followed by payments under the rightsholder's agreements with artists and songwriters [S2]. This is the platform's explanation, not a substitute for any company's actual contract.

The proposed analytical model is a scoped royalty pool multiplied by eligible share and contractual participation, less the relevant creator payments, distribution/administration costs and investment. All factors need matching territory, service, period and rights type. A higher total stream count need not imply a proportionally larger pool. One recording can involve several composition and recording claims; the same cash must not be counted twice.

### UMG: acquisition mix and intangible investment

UMG reports Q2 constant-currency revenue growth of 13.3%, or 6.4% excluding Downtown. Recorded-music subscription growth was 16.6%, or 6.7% excluding Downtown. Adjusted EBITDA margin was 20.5% versus 22.7%, with mix and acquisition effects described separately [U1].

The H1 report shows operating profit of €901m, net royalty-advance payments of €292m and other working-capital outflow of €452m in the operating-cash reconciliation. Its newly defined FCF is €24m; the prior-year comparable is restated to €163m. The new definition precedes strategic catalog/equity/M&A investment. Net catalog book value is €3.017bn and royalty advances €3.376bn: accounting values, not estimates of market value [U2].

**Proposed interpretation:** rights businesses can be capital intensive through advances, catalog acquisition and contract renewals even with modest PP&E. The investigator should classify investment by economic purpose and contractual terms, not equate a non-PP&E asset with costless growth. Some spending may acquire new opportunities and some may help sustain the existing business; their split is unknown until evidence supports it.

An advance can affect cash before it becomes royalty expense or is recouped. Do not subtract the same advance again from a cash-flow measure that already includes it. A reduction in advances can reflect stronger recoupment, timing or reduced investment. It does not, by itself, prove improved bargaining power.

The same logic applies to acquisition-adjusted growth. An acquired revenue stream can be economically valuable while diluting margin; neither headline acceleration nor margin dilution alone establishes the acquisition's return. The research needs the price paid, financing, retained economics, integration costs and realistic counterfactual.

### Warner Music: contractual terms can differentiate peers

Warner Music's quarter ended June 30 reported adjusted OIBDA of $433m versus $373m and CFO of $142m versus $46m. CFO less capex reconciles to FCF of $114m versus $7m. Management cites contractual per-subscriber minimum increases and other operating factors. The release separately adjusts for a prior copyright settlement and BMG distribution termination; some debt is secured by specified rights and limited to the relevant entities [W1].

**Proposed interpretation:** a growing industry does not give every rightsholder the same outcome. Scope, contract renewal, catalog mix, artist economics, cost base and debt claims can differentiate per-share cash generation. However, unlike growth rates do not prove a bilateral market-share transfer. Compare matched territories and pools, not one company's reported dollars with another's acquisition-adjusted euros.

Rights financing also creates a distinct analytical question: which entity receives the cash, and which claims have priority over it? This is a diligence requirement, not a legal opinion or an assumption that consolidated cash is entirely available to common shareholders.

## 7. China/Hong Kong anchor: Tencent Music is not a Spotify clone

Tencent Music reports Q2 revenue of RMB8.93bn, up RMB491m, including RMB407m from Ximalaya after its May 18 acquisition. Membership-services revenue was RMB4.79bn; social entertainment and others declined 16.4%. The issuer renamed online music services to music-related services and states that the name change itself did not change historical revenue/accounting. Each ADS represents two Class A ordinary shares [T1].

The prior-year release used a music-subscription revenue figure of RMB4.38bn, whereas the current membership-services comparison is RMB4.43bn [T1,T2]. The wider membership wording must not be joined to the older narrower label as a guaranteed identical metric merely because both concern paid content.

**Proposed interpretation:** the regional model must allow a company to combine music, long-form audio, premium membership benefits, social entertainment, merchandise and live experiences. The mere presence of a common subscription model does not make every driver comparable with a Western distributor. Distribution within a larger ecosystem, acquired audio businesses and fan monetization require separate assertions and perimeter dates.

A mechanical diagnostic subtracts the disclosed acquired contribution from the total reported revenue increase: RMB491m − RMB407m = RMB84m. This is not a complete certified organic-growth calculation: it does not independently qualify every currency, perimeter or reclassification effect. It is useful evidence that the consolidated growth rate alone is insufficient for the incumbent-business thesis.

This adds a real China/HK research anchor, not a complete regional census or an accepted security crosswalk. Canada, Japan, Korea and other markets still need their own primary-source research.

## 8. Licensed AI content: separate permission, product and financial milestones

UMG's September 10 announcement describes a multi-year ElevenLabs agreement and a licensed AI creation platform in development, involving participating artists. It does not disclose a complete revenue allocation, establish that the product has launched, or quantify a Q2 financial contribution [A1].

The proposed milestone sequence is: disclosed agreement → verified participating scope → product available → paid adoption → recognized revenue → retained cash contribution. Not every agreement follows every step; missing terms stay unknown. Source evidence about an announcement cannot prove rights outside its stated scope or grant Mastermind ingestion/display permission.

Investigate two-sided economics. AI-enabled participation may create a new licensing pool or fan experience; abundant generated supply may also compete for attention, raise authentication costs or change discovery. The relevant issue is the net contractual and behavioral outcome for the business, not a universal AI-beneficiary tag.

Recording rights, composition rights, voice/likeness permissions, distribution permissions and data-processing permissions must not be collapsed into one binary licensed flag. These are diligence dimensions to map into existing owner contracts; this research does not adjudicate disputed rights or create a new licensing authority.

## 9. A point-in-time historical falsifier, not a stock-return backtest

Netflix's April 19, 2022 letter reports net paid additions of -0.2m versus a prior guide of +2.5m; excluding the -0.7m Russia service-suspension impact, additions were +0.5m. Revenue still grew 9.8%, and the next-quarter net-add forecast was -2m. Management described acquisition weakness and sharing/competition issues while maintaining the broad streaming thesis [H1].

This case distinguishes four propositions: the industry's long-term adoption thesis, the company's current acquisition trajectory, near-term delivery versus its own forecast, and an eventual stock-price response. The first proposition can remain plausible while the second and third weaken. The document was available at the event; an eventual recovery or later new monetization model cannot be inserted into the decision-time feature set.

Use the case to test whether the proposed system would have surfaced the gap and its scope without overstating it. Do not convert the adjustment excluding a market exit into delivery of the original guide. Do not claim that the research would have traded the event successfully: no original consensus panel, executable price-event study, transaction-cost model or counterfactual return test was completed here.

The later phase must add failed, acquired and delisted businesses where applicable, not just a successful survivor. It must also preserve changed metrics and subscriber-disclosure regimes, rather than stitching hindsight into a clean historical series.

## 10. Leading data: the denominator and access contract matter

Antenna describes a modeled, opt-in transaction panel for the 50 United States and DC, with excluded broad bundles and other coverage gaps. Its churn timing differs for iTunes loss-of-access versus explicit cancellation action on other distributors. Its uninterrupted-survival measure excludes re-entry, while resubscribe is a separate measure [M1]. Public methodology is available; licensed data access and Mastermind entitlement are not established.

Its 2024 market study reports 174.3m gross additions and 147.8m cancellations, yielding 26.5m net additions. This is a dated subscription-activity measure, not 174.3m newly reached unique households [M2]. IFPI's March 2026 release concerns 2025 recorded-music trade revenue: $31.7bn and 6.4% growth. It is not all audio/entertainment consumer expenditure or the sum of label and platform revenue [M3].

### Proposed evidence tiers

**Immediately researchable:** public issuer releases/filings, source-defined content charts, company-announced price/plan changes, release and sports schedules, publicly described rights agreements and accessible industry methodology. Availability does not certify production ingestion rights or historical completeness.

**Conditional on existing access and coverage:** cohort retention, distributor-level cancellation/renewal data, audience panels and richer music consumption series. Check existing Mastermind entitlements before considering a provider. No new major paid provider is assumed or requested.

**Generally unavailable from public observations alone:** title-level incremental LTV, private contract waterfalls, complete paid-household overlap, exact marginal serving cost and issuer-level consensus for every operating KPI. The product must remain useful with these fields unavailable, rather than impute false precision.

Each leading-indicator proposal must specify what it measures, its source population, units, revision policy, publication lag and hypothesized financial lag. A public popularity ranking is ordinal, not proportional revenue. Historical research must store when a number became knowable, not when its title was released.

## 11. Rerating mechanisms and strongest opposing hypotheses

| Proposed mechanism | Evidence chain to investigate | What would change the interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Sustainable repricing | Effective price rises; renewing cohorts and contribution hold | Delayed churn, downgrade, channel discount or supplier escalation absorbs gains |
| Content-capital efficiency | Retained contribution improves per comparable cash investment over a full slate | Cash deferral, thin future slate, accounting estimate change or narrow hit concentration |
| Profitable sports bundle | Full-season incremental subscriptions/ads exceed attributable costs and retention improves | Costs migrate between quarters; churn after event; internal transfer mistaken for group gain |
| Rights-pool expansion | Larger monetized pool and retained share after creator/distributor terms | Lower-yield mix, lost share, higher advances or contract minimums cap benefit |
| Distribution bargaining improvement | Better disclosed terms or matched cohort economics | Presentation change, changed rights scope or costs relocated elsewhere |
| Regional premium/fan monetization | Distinct paid tiers and ancillary contribution improve within a comparable perimeter | Acquired revenue, merchandise costs, weaker social business or launch-only demand |
| AI licensing contribution | Scoped available product progresses to paid revenue and retained contribution | Development-only announcement, undisclosed rights, no adoption or adverse substitution |

These are research hypotheses, not probability estimates or entry signals. For each company, bridge to the whole issuer and fixed-horizon per-share cash estimates before investigating an expectations gap. For valuation, separate franchise persistence from the discount rate, debt claims and future investment needs. A change in nominal/real yields can affect valuation and funding without improving customer economics; estimate sensitivities only after historical qualification.

The desired opportunity is not necessarily the business with the fastest usage growth. It may be a business where cash investment moderates without weakening retention, where contract economics improve before estimates reflect them, or where a difficult transition has become less costly than expected. The opposite situations must be equally visible. Their predictive usefulness remains unproven.

## 12. Product and learning requirements for the existing template

The second-depth view should answer: **Is the streaming/content transition producing durable retained economics, and what evidence would change that read?** Preserve the advertising-first candidate vertical; this phase supplies a reusable extension, not a competing first build or a new commission.

Recommended view sequence: identity and scope → material operating change → price/retention or rights mechanism → profit/cash/commitment bridge → dated expectations → missing or contrary evidence → existing company/watchlist workflow. The default glance should be a useful explanation, with detailed formulas and source receipts one level deeper.

A content-to-economics view should link release, territory and exploitation window to the company assertion and actual observed outcomes. A season/cohort timeline should expose lag rather than pretend all metrics are current. A bridge should explain why a segment improved while group cash or another segment weakened. Neither a graph nor a waterfall is sufficient without an investor question it answers.

Shared graph identity, evidence time/correction, rights/display permissions, ThemeState lifecycle and F04 rendering remain with existing owners. New requirements must be mapped into those owners before implementation. Research associations are not accepted graph facts; incomplete identity or licensing remains explicit. No universal score, autonomous research watcher, separate outcome database or new publisher is proposed.

Learning should separately evaluate descriptive correctness, operating forecast accuracy and later investment-use value. Include abstentions and missing data. A cohort model should not learn post-event retention from a feature supposedly available on announcement day. Contemporaneous stock-linked expense effects must be separated from exogenous operating changes. The phase's arithmetic and boundary checks validate examples only; they do not establish product behavior or predictive edge.

## 13. Research coverage and exact next phase

This chapter provides seven current issuer/business anchors, one historical event case, subscription/rights models, a scoped China/HK extension, measurement feasibility and opposing hypotheses. The companion defines proposed metrics and acceptance behaviors plus reproducible worked examples.

Unfinished: full sector/global company census; exact financial exposure weights; pricing/retention panels; original consensus-vintage qualification; comprehensive historical cases and return tests; gaming, terrestrial networks, satellite and complete regional dossiers; current source-contract mapping; accepted written design and implementation plan; Fable delivery; product tests, CI, release and browser proof.

Next substantive research: **gaming and interactive economies**, distinguishing premium releases, recurring/live-service spending, mobile acquisition economics, user-created economies and platform distribution. Begin with Roblox, Take-Two, Nintendo and Sony primary disclosures, adding Tencent where relevant. Reuse these content, recognition, cohort and rights principles without repeating this phase's discovery. The eventual Fable package must include the economic answers and acceptance conditions, not require a second principal to rediscover them.
