Deep Research Prompt: Mastermind Options
Intelligence and Signaling System
Role and mission
You are conducting a deep institutional-grade research program on options-market information,
options flow, options structure, dealer positioning, volatility surfaces, open interest, Greeks,
expiration dynamics, and their predictive relationship with future price behavior.


The objective is not merely to understand options or build another “unusual options activity” scanner. The
objective is to determine, with empirical rigor, which information embedded in the options market is
genuinely useful for forecasting direction, volatility, range, support/resistance behavior, regime
changes, tail risk, candidate deterioration, and short-horizon market moves, and then turn that
knowledge into a concrete architecture for Mastermind Terminal, Prophet, Mastermind candidate
evaluation, risk monitoring, and future products.


Treat options as a potentially independent information layer alongside price action, volume, market
structure, macro, fundamentals, catalysts, sentiment, and other Mastermind inputs. Do not, however,
assume that options are inherently more predictive because they are “forward looking.” The central
scientific question is:


       What incremental information does the options market contain that is not already
       present in spot price action, equity volume, volatility, trend, momentum, catalyst
       information, or broader market state—and under exactly what conditions, instruments,
       horizons, and regimes does that incremental information remain statistically and
       economically useful?


Academic evidence gives good reason to investigate this seriously. Pan and Poteshman found that buyer-
initiated option opening volume contained information about future stock prices, with their put/call signal
showing meaningful next-day and next-week return differences. Easley, O’Hara, and Srinivas earlier
documented the informational relationship between option volume and stock prices. Cremers and
Weinbaum found predictive information in call-versus-put implied-volatility deviations, while Johnson and
So studied predictive information in option-to-stock trading activity. These results strongly suggest that
how option activity is classified matters much more than simply counting calls and puts. 1


This research must end with a buildable Mastermind Options Intelligence architecture, a prioritized
research/backtest program, explicit formulas and schemas for the first generation of signals, and a clear
answer about what Mastermind should build first.


Use information available through October 3, 2026, and explicitly date all research findings, product
comparisons, API capabilities, and market-structure assumptions.

The research must distinguish information signals from mechanical market-impact signals. For
example, an informed option buyer anticipating a future event is fundamentally different from dealers
dynamically hedging large existing gamma exposure. Both may predict price behavior, but for different
reasons and different horizons.


Determine what “options flow” actually means

Do not accept the retail definition of options flow as “large call trade = bullish” or “large put trade = bearish.”


Research how professional desks classify and interpret flow. Determine what can and cannot be inferred
from public market data.


For every option print, conceptually distinguish:


call/put × buyer/seller initiated × opening/closing × outright/spread/complex × directional/volatility/
hedge/arbitrage × moneyness × DTE × size × premium × delta exposure × gamma exposure × vega
exposure × execution aggressiveness × market regime.


Explicitly study why:


      • Buying calls can be bullish, hedging, volatility buying, spread construction, stock-replacement
        activity or arbitrage.
      • Selling calls can be bearish, covered-call supply, spread construction or volatility selling.
      • Buying puts can be directional bearishness, portfolio protection, volatility buying or part of a spread.
      • Selling puts can be bullish, volatility harvesting or a leg of another structure.
      • Contract count can be far less informative than premium, delta-adjusted notional, vega notional or
        gamma notional.
      • A huge trade may be less meaningful than persistent coordinated smaller activity.
      • Deep-ITM flow may behave like synthetic stock rather than a volatility or directional bet.
      • A large print may be a closing position rather than new information.
      • A “sweep” is not automatically informed.
      • Multi-leg orders can generate individual prints that look directionally significant when interpreted in
        isolation.

The research should determine how accurately Mastermind can infer these categories from the data
actually available.
