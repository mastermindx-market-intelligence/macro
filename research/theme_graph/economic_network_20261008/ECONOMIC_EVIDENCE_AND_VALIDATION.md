## 1. Decision the evidence supports

Economic relationship graphs are defensible as **structured exposure maps, source-grounded explanations, shock-retrieval aids, and testable forecasting inputs**. The strongest evidence supports propagation of identified real shocks through actual production relationships. It does not establish that graph topology, centrality, multiple hops, or a neural message-passing model automatically produces independent forecast improvement, much less current tradable alpha.

Three objects must remain distinguishable:

| Object | What its edge means | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Documented economic relationship | A dated assertion of sales, purchases, ownership, financing, contractual capacity, or another real relationship, with identified counterparties and scope | Current flow size, exclusivity, capacity availability, shock sign, equity response, or future return |
| Thematic/product/narrative similarity | Shared product vocabulary, topic, technology, embedding proximity, or potentially compatible upstream/downstream products | A contract, actual trade, revenue dependence, or causal propagation |
| Statistical price dependence | Conditional covariance, residual correlation, lead-lag dependence, or forecast dependence estimated from an explicitly defined return sample | A real relationship, structural direction, shock origin, or economic causation |

These distinctions are empirically consequential. The author documentation for VTNIC explicitly labels pairwise scores as *potential* vertical relatedness rather than actual flows (S10). TNIC intentionally removes vertical relationships and describes competing product similarity (S09). DCC estimates conditional dependence (S12). Combining all three into an undifferentiated positive adjacency would erase the distinctions the primary sources themselves require.

**Adjudication recommendation (inference):** Accept the relationship graph research program and its exposure/explanation consumer. Hold neural graph forecasting and alpha claims behind independent chronological validation. Reject semantic promotion of similarity or observed covariance into actual supply relationships. Reject universal edge signs and automatic multi-hop monetary attribution. Preserve incumbent deterministic computations, grounded synthesis, descriptive exposures, and validated signals as separate products.

## 3. Mechanism adjudication

### A. Customer news → supplier cash flows → supplier prices

**Supported bounded proposition:** material customer links can carry information about suppliers' future fundamentals, and historical published work documents delayed price adjustment. **Heterogeneity:** customer salience, supplier/customer relative size, coverage, cross-industry exposure, customer revenue share, contract terms and investor attention can alter speed and magnitude (S01–S02). **Hold:** “past customer return predicts today's supplier return” is an empirical hypothesis requiring contemporary validation. A stock return mixes cash-flow news, discount-rate news, factor exposures and measurement effects; it is not a pure customer-demand shock.

**Falsifier proposal:** conditional on the same macro/industry/size/own-momentum information, true pre-existing links should improve linked suppliers' revenue/earnings forecasts relative to matched unlinked firms and rewired edges. Return predictability should survive later chronological samples, proper public availability and costs; if only common-factor controls or large-to-small lag explain it, retire the alpha interpretation while retaining the real-exposure map.

### B. Supplier outage → customer capacity/output losses

**Supported bounded proposition:** an identified disruption at an actual supplier can impair customers, particularly when the relevant input is hard to replace (S03–S05). **Do not generalize:** presence of a relationship is not sufficient for a binding bottleneck. A tiny-dollar unique part can constrain production; a large commodity purchase can be replaceable. Revenue concentration and technological criticality therefore answer different questions.

**Falsifier proposal:** prediction of customer output/revenue losses must improve when critical product, inventory coverage, alternative sourcing, affected facility and duration are included. If the system makes identical predictions across replaceable and specific inputs, it fails the mechanism rather than merely needing a larger model.

### C. Customer demand shock → upstream purchase/order changes

**Supported bounded proposition:** demand and supply shocks propagate with different directional emphasis in sector-level designs (S07). A customer's higher sales may require more inputs, reduce inventories, draw imports, or raise utilization before causing supplier expansion. Relative price and contractual effects can offset quantity changes.

**Falsifier proposal:** model upstream outcomes using supplier sales shares to the shocked customer, and separately model downstream cost/capacity outcomes using customer input shares. Reverse-direction and randomized-direction ablations should be worse when a direction-specific hypothesis is true. A homogeneous symmetric adjacency cannot pass this test by construction.

### D. Rival similarity → competitive pressure or common demand

**Supported bounded proposition:** product text can establish a useful *candidate rival neighborhood* and a distinct *potential vertical relationship* (S09–S10). Similar producers can benefit together from market expansion, harm one another through substitution, or share upstream cost exposure. Similarity alone gives no effect sign.

**Falsifier proposal:** relation classifications must recover held-out, contemporaneously documented named competitors/product overlaps and distinguish competing from complementary products. Forecast tests should separate common-demand news from firm-specific substitution/market-share news. If generic “AI” embedding proximity performs like industry labels and unconditioned topic counts, reject a claimed new economic exposure even if the embedding is useful for retrieval.

### E. Centrality/concentration → importance, fragility or premium

**Supported bounded proposition:** asymmetry, higher-order role and substitution can make particular nodes economically important (S06, S11). Centrality's relationship with expected returns is model- and network-dependent (S13). Concentration can express strong dependence; many connections can express diversified reliance.

**Hold:** degree, PageRank, eigenvector centrality, betweenness and input-output influence are not interchangeable. Network “importance,” shock vulnerability, forecastability and priced compensation must be separate targets. No inspected paper establishes generic centrality as an independent portable alpha ranking.

**Falsifier proposal:** compare topology-based scores to sales/GDP weights, firm size, industry, observed customer concentration and estimated factor exposures. Rewire while preserving degrees, weights, industries and node size to determine whether actual paths matter. If a centrality forecast reduces to size or a common factor, retain it only as a descriptive feature.

### F. Correlation network → exposure graph

**Supported bounded proposition:** dependence estimates can be useful for covariance/risk and benchmarking (S12). **Reject:** correlation does not verify a supply, ownership or financing relationship. Factor-adjusted residual correlation is still an estimate subject to omitted common shocks, volatility shifts and estimation noise. Granger-type dependence is conditional predictive ordering, not intervention causality.

**Falsifier proposal:** freeze the estimation window; compare rolling correlations, shrinkage covariance, DCC and common-factor residual models. Check held-out covariance loss separately from expected-return loss. Future-return data must never enter the graph used to predict them. Correlation improvements must not be relabeled as revenue-shock explanations.

## 4. Temporal and accounting invariants for any later experiment

These are proposed acceptance requirements inferred from the source weaknesses, not an authorized implementation plan.

1. **Public availability, valid period, fiscal period and observation timestamp are distinct.** An edge known after a 10-K filing cannot enter a prediction on fiscal year-end. TNIC explicitly is not lagged. A fixed six-month lag can be a conservative research convention, but filing acceptance timestamps are more exact and late filers may violate the convention.
2. **Revision vintage matters.** Keep data/source version used at each prediction origin, including corrected accounting, entity matches, IO revisions and text parsing. A retrospective graph must not import relationships discovered later without a separate hindsight flag.
3. **Universe entry/exit must be replayable.** Include delisted firms and historical identifiers where outcomes require them. Preserve failed/ambiguous matches, anonymous counterparties, private firms, governments and foreign firms as missing/other categories; do not erase them or interpret lack of an edge as no dependence. S01/S02's public-public matching is not a census of commercial relationships.
4. **No identity inference from concentration alone.** A filing can report “Customer A” and material revenue while leaving the name unidentified. Search discovered a relevant FASB text and SEC issuer-correspondence quote indicating that segment disclosure need not disclose customer identity. Direct FASB page returned empty; SEC correspondence retrieval failed. Current eCFR Item 101 was opened (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-229/subpart-229.100/section-229.101) and requires material descriptions but does not by itself prove all 10%-customers are named. Do not cite S01's summary of SFAS 131 as controlling disclosure law. Before a legal/completeness assertion, inspect exact ASC 280-10-50-42 and applicable historical/current S-K provision. This is a flagged source-law issue, not legal advice.
5. **Edge weights require units and denominators.** Supplier revenue share to customer, customer spend share from supplier, unit production capacity, backlog, credit exposure, ownership share and dollar input flows are different quantities. Even a confirmed direction does not permit mirroring its weight.
6. **Stock, flow, capacity and commitment remain separate.** Installed asset value and power capacity are stocks/capacity; annual capex, chip deliveries and annual electricity use are flows; lease obligations/backlog are commitments. They require explicit conversion assumptions and time periods before multiplication.
7. **A monetary path cannot be counted as independent dollars at every hop.** A server purchase can contain a GPU, which contains foundry and packaging inputs. These are gross transactions at stages, not additive final demand or value added. Combine final demand with a coherent direct-requirements model or explicit stage accounting; do not add both a total-requirements edge and all its constituent paths.
8. **A firm's physical, financial and security identities differ.** Facility disruptions, subsidiary orders, consolidated financial statements and a traded share class require dated mappings. Intracompany flows eliminated in consolidated financial statements are not automatically external demand. Financial contagion through credit, liquidity or discount rates needs its own edge and mechanism; physical production propagation cannot substitute for it.
9. **Cycles need economic interpretation.** Ownership cross-holdings, reciprocal trade and reseller routes can legitimately create loops. Naive repeated message passing can count the same shock multiple times. Repeated traversal is not fresh evidence or a new independent exposure.
10. **Confidence in existence is not confidence in effect.** Separately assess entity identity, relation type, direction, currentness, quantity, substitution, shock classification and forecast calibration. A highly credible contract can still have an uncertain economic response.

## 5. AI-capex multi-hop case: what should be tested

Consider a proposed chain “hyperscaler spending → accelerator/server vendor → foundry/packaging/memory → equipment/materials,” alongside “datacenter construction → electrical/cooling equipment → grid/power providers.” These are **hypotheses about allocation and propagation**, not assertions that every named participant has a confirmed edge to every other participant.

Microsoft FY2025 demonstrates why one capex headline is insufficient (S16). A valid demand origin must specify the relevant cash/accrual/lease measure, period, announced change versus realized purchase, replacement versus expansion, AI attribution and recipient. The next step must establish which supplier/product received the order and how much of that supplier's own input demand is incremental. The financial response must distinguish customer capex burden, vendor revenues/margins, working capital, deferred recognition, funding and valuation effects.

**Failure alternatives worth retaining:** purchases moved forward rather than net increased; orders redistributed among competing architectures; backlog merely converted into deliveries; imported components rather than domestic supplier revenue; capacity already booked under take-or-pay; product mix or lower prices offsets unit demand; uninstalled equipment awaits power/cooling; investment already priced; end-user monetization misses expectations despite supplier order growth. These alternatives prevent graph traversal from becoming an inevitability narrative.

**Falsifiable case protocol proposal:** choose an exact dated capex surprise and a frozen set of contemporaneously documented counterparties. First predict orders/bookings/deliveries/revenue and test stated lags. Compare direct named relationships, sector IO imputation and narrative-only firms. Then test whether farther documented hops add explanatory or forecasting value beyond the first hop and common industry demand. Only after this real-outcome test should an independently specified residual-return forecast be evaluated. Do not condition the entire case on selecting firms that later appreciated.

## 6. Benchmarks, acceptance and strongest nulls

The commission should require *incremental* utility for a named consumer, not a graph that merely reproduces plausible stories. No experiments were run in this lane.

| Target | Mandatory benchmark family | What a convincing result would have to show |
|---|---|---|
| Relationship extraction | Human-reviewed contemporaneous source records; simple pattern/name matching; source-specific entity resolution | Precision/recall by edge type, named vs anonymous entity, geography and age; evidence snippets actually entail each asserted edge |
| Exposure explanation | Official sector IO; simple confirmed one-hop revenue/dependency view | More correct, timely or decision-useful explanation, with clear unknown quantities and mechanism-specific direction |
| Revenue/earnings forecast | Own history; sector/macro factors; analyst-consensus where licensed; simple linear direct-link regression | Chronological loss reduction/calibration improvement across held-out events, firms and regimes; no filing/relationship leakage |
| Disruption forecast | Size/sales weights; direct critical-input exposure; geography; inventories/alternative suppliers | Improves on these baselines; degrades when true direction or actual paths are removed; performance is not confined to one earthquake |
| Covariance/risk forecast | Shrinkage covariance, factor model, rolling residual dependence, DCC | Improves out-of-sample covariance loss and calibrated risk, separately from expected returns |
| Expected-return forecast | Factor/industry/size models; own momentum/reversal; customer momentum; flexible nongraph model using identical inputs | Independent chronological gains after costs and universe corrections; wins are not just additional data, feature selection or common factors |
| Multi-hop inference | One-hop graph; total-requirements IO with proper accounting; matched node/degree/weight-preserving rewiring | Farther paths add robust measurable utility without duplicated dollars or event evidence |
| Neural architecture | Same-feature linear/elastic net/tree/temporal baselines, simple graph aggregation; layer-specific ablations | Added graph/model complexity causes reproducible improvement under equal source availability and tuning budget |

**Strongest currently supported null alternatives:** ordinary common factors account for dependence; actual input relationships affect real outcomes but are priced promptly; publicly visible return effects diminish after discovery; size/attention/lead-lag accounts for apparent diffusion; sector IO or sales weights captures the effect without a neural graph; substitution and buffers erase presumed bottlenecks; apparent network paths merely duplicate the same final-demand shock; retrospective entity/relationship reconstruction supplies hindsight rather than predictive information.

### 7.2 Separate targets and admissible labels

| Target | Evaluation truth and unit | Primary comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Document extraction | Whether source text entails entity, relation, direction, scope and dates; one adjudicated assertion | Same-document rules/entity matching and human review |
| Relation prediction | A previously unknown relationship subsequently independently established for a specified valid period; one entity-pair/type/time claim | Same candidate universe and information, without graph structure |
| Fundamental transmission | Future orders, deliveries, output, revenue growth or margins at a declared horizon; physical units, dollars or percentage points | Own history, macro/sector, direct exposure and same-feature nongraph models |
| Conditional covariance | Forecast covariance of decimal returns at a declared horizon; squared decimal-return units | Shrinkage, factor and DCC models |
| Conditional returns | Future total or declared residual return; decimal return or basis points, before/after explicit costs | Same-information factors, momentum, customer momentum and nongraph models |

Predicting a later *disclosure* may predict disclosure behavior rather than relation formation. Score those separately. No extraction F1, revenue coefficient or covariance gain substitutes for a return result. Causal transmission additionally needs an identification design; predictive accuracy alone does not identify intervention effects.

An unreported pair is **unknown**, not a negative. Legitimate extraction negatives include text that does not entail the proposed type/direction or resolves to a different entity. Bounded existence negatives require independent evidence of absence/cessation covering the same entities, product, relation type and time. A terminated contract does not disprove every other commercial relationship. Preserve ambiguous, anonymous and source-uncovered cases as unresolved; do not reward a model for declaring them absent. Evaluate extraction recall within exhaustively adjudicated source documents and label it accordingly. For relationship discovery, report recovered verified positives and review burden; population recall is unidentified without ascertainment assumptions. Positive-unlabeled estimation needs explicit, sensitivity-tested selection assumptions, especially because large disclosed customers are selectively observed (S01–S02).

Use two independent reviewers blinded to model identity on a stratified gold sample, with recorded disagreement and adjudication. Distinguish evidence sufficiency from later economic truth. Sample both accepted and rejected candidates; otherwise precision may be measurable while missed edges remain invisible. Keep source/type/geography/age coverage denominators, including records that could not be resolved. Report adjudicated precision \(TP/(TP+FP)\) on resolvable cases separately from verified-support yield \(TP/(TP+FP+U)\), where \(U\) is unresolved/unknown. The latter is not identified true precision; under correct adjudication, all-sampled precision lies between \(TP/N\) and \((TP+U)/N\), with \(N=TP+FP+U\). Explain selection limitations. Unresolved cases cannot supply successful automatic-admission evidence; retain them in coverage and abstention accounting.

### 7.4 Mechanism and null tests

For fundamental outcomes, freeze pre-shock edges and specify exposure, expected direction, lag and substitution moderator before outcomes. Event studies or difference-in-differences need pre-trends, common-location/sector controls, anticipation checks and explicit spillover handling; connected controls may themselves be treated. Instrumental-variable designs need their own exclusion argument. Customer stock returns are not an exogenous demand instrument by default. Hold causal language where identification fails even if forecasting succeeds.

Run Commission4's layer matrix and disclosed-versus-inferred split. Add matched rewiring preserving degree, weight distribution, node size, industry and coverage, plus direction reversal, placebo dates, delayed availability, hub removal and same-theme unlinked firms. A rewiring permutation is a structural diagnostic; it is a formal randomization test only if its exchangeability assumptions hold. Match control information rather than giving placebo networks inferior coverage. Compare one hop, two hops and any exceptional farther paths with identical origins and accounting basis. Remove total-requirements shortcuts when testing constituent direct paths. Record cyclic-path exclusions and duplicated shock dollars.

A useful null is that graph features are redundant with sector/size/factors; another is that real transmission is promptly priced. Failure to reject either does not invalidate source-grounded exposure explanation. Conversely, a model may predict well while reversed directions work equally well: that supports statistical utility, while withholding the claimed directional mechanism.

### 7.5 Scoring, calibration and power

Let \(L_{Bj}\) and \(L_{Gj}\) be baseline and graph losses for the same independent evaluation block \(j\). Define \(D_j=L_{Bj}-L_{Gj}\); positive mean difference favors the graph. Revenue-growth MAE is in percentage points; dollar MAE needs a declared size normalization. Binary Brier loss \((p-y)^2\) is in squared probability units; log loss is in nats. Report both aggregate and coverage-stratified scores. Reliability plots, calibration slope/intercept and interval coverage need uncertainty bands; calibration-bin choices are frozen. Report interval width alongside coverage so arbitrarily broad intervals cannot count as a useful improvement.

For covariance, one candidate loss is \(\log\det\hat H+u^\top\hat H^{-1}u\), where \(u=r-\hat\mu\) uses the same frozen mean forecast for every covariance candidate, with positive-definite forecasts and fixed decimal-return units. This compares predictive covariance quality rather than assuming the true covariance is directly observed. Test declared portfolio variance and tail-coverage consequences separately. An expected-return forecast is a different object, even when both use the same returns.

Threshold-based abstention must report **coverage** \(c=\#\mathrm{accepted}/\#\mathrm{eligible}\) and **selective risk** \(R(c)=\mathrm{mean\ loss\ among\ accepted}\). Count unsupported/unresolved cases and manual-review costs; do not hide them through a changing denominator. Choose thresholds on tuning data and show the risk/coverage frontier on untouched data. Calibrate relationship existence, direction and magnitude separately rather than using a universal confidence scalar.

**Proposed power rule:** predeclare a minimum useful gain \(\delta_{\min}\) in the endpoint's units, family-adjusted significance level \(\alpha\), and target power \(1-\beta\). Under approximately independent normal block differences, a planning approximation is

\[
\mathrm{MDE}\approx (z_{1-\alpha}+z_{1-\beta})s_D/\sqrt K,
\]

where \(K\) counts independent blocks and \(s_D\) is the training/pilot estimate of paired-loss standard deviation; \(z_q\) is a standard-normal quantile. Use a two-sided quantile if the registered test is two-sided. For a gate requiring a lower confidence bound above \(\delta_{\min}\), plan an alternative \(\delta_{\mathrm{design}}>\delta_{\min}\) and require MDE no larger than \(\delta_{\mathrm{design}}-\delta_{\min}\). Estimate serial/event dependence and heterogeneity by blocked simulation before fixing sample size. If \(K\) is too small, classify the result as inconclusive rather than widening the acceptable error after seeing it. Cluster dependence, multiple comparisons and uncertain pilot variance can greatly increase the required sample.

Cost-derived tolerances can prioritize human-reviewed candidates but cannot relax an automatic-admission floor. Retain Commission4 §§H1/L’s proposed one-sided 95% exact-binomial lower bound ≥95% for role+direction precision in every promoted relation/source stratum, plus the economic-cost gate and any stricter adopted source-owner rule. All new gates remain proposed; changing this baseline requires explicit owner/Sol adjudication, never post-result weakening. If zero errors occur in \(n\) independent audited accepted assertions, a one-sided binomial upper bound is \(1-\alpha^{1/n}\), approximately \(-\log\alpha/n\). At \(\alpha=.05\), the familiar approximation is about \(3/n\). This is a planning illustration, not a chosen gate; correlated templates, shared issuers and stratified samples require adjusted inference. For zero role+direction errors, the corresponding precision lower bound is \(.05^{1/n}\); at \(n=59\) it is about .95049. This requires independent adjudicated positive assertions within each promoted stratum; clusters reduce effective information. It is not sufficient sample proof for recall, coverage or every stratum. The masterplan’s 120-issuer pilot is diagnostic, not precision qualification.
