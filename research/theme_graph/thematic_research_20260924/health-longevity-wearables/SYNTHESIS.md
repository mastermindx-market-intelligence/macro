## Result in one view

A recurring sensor, an hourly care operator and a consumer watch ecosystem do not have one universal adoption denominator. **Measured use, payable service and company revenue can describe different populations.** The useful dossier makes their relationship explicit or states why it cannot yet be quantified. It does not produce a reassuring common score when those links are absent.

## C1. Recurring monitoring: the missing bridge is cohort compatibility

**Question.** Does growth in a recurring monitoring business establish growth in sustained, paid patient use?

**Reported-source packet.** Dexcom reports Q2 2026 revenue of $1,308.4 million, including distribution and direct channels; this rechecks the existing R4 revenue observation, rather than adding a patient count. [S01] CMS L33822 uses monthly supply allowances, with up to three allowance units per 90 days; billing units are not measured sensor wear. [S02] The acquired CMS methodology covers Medicare FFS and suppresses cells with ten or fewer beneficiaries. Its aggregate design does not supply a linked current brand-patient cohort. No claims rows were acquired. [S03]

**New actual-use witness.** The July-2026 PLOS study analyzes VA/Dexcom data from 2015-2023: 2,351 Veterans for engagement patterns and 1,763 for the outcome analysis. It excludes very low-use starters and has a predominantly male sample. Within its fluctuating-use group, 73.2% still meet an 80%-of-days-worn threshold. These are observational, selected historical results; company-linked authorship/funding and uploaded-data dependence are disclosed. They are not today's commercial retention rate or causal treatment proof. [S04]

**House interpretation.** A day-level threshold can hide meaningful interruptions within or between days. Therefore, a proposed paid-time model needs a declared measurement convention: entitlement time, actual reading time, dispensed supply or adjudicated covered service. Those quantities should not share a display label merely because each can be expressed in months. A manufacturer can recognize revenue while the downstream record needed to determine actual use is absent.

The VA study, Medicare FFS policy/data methodology and Dexcom consolidated revenue do **not** constitute one matched cohort. Joining their numbers would manufacture a commercial persistence estimate. The advance over R4 is an observed example of measurement/selection risk plus a precise join that remains unavailable, not a second theoretical sensor-duration example.

**Non-US contrast.** Somerset ICB's official page distinguishes patient eligibility, locally approved products and a cost condition for an alternative CGM approach. Its linked comparison is dated April 2026, but the page does not establish a complete current national net-price matrix. This regional access record must not inherit US Medicare allowance units or an FDA label. No UK paid-user count, manufacturer net realization or national coverage census was obtained. [S12]

**Strongest competing explanation.** Apparent revenue momentum may reflect channel, realization, product or replacement dynamics rather than more continuous paid users. Conversely, fewer physical units could accompany longer useful duration without deteriorating customer service. The acquired evidence does not identify how much each mechanism explains.

**Next discriminating observation.** A lawfully available same-product, same-payer, same-period initiated cohort connecting dispensing, reading continuity, replacements, paid service and net commercial realization would distinguish these interpretations. Early abandonment and missing uploads must remain visible. Until then, O1-O3 are individually useful, but the combined revenue-per-paid-wear-unit estimate is **NOT_IDENTIFIED**, not zero.

**Capital/expectations requirement.** The supplier bridge would also need manufacturing investment, channel working capital, customer support and replacement obligations. Those costs are not allocated to the observed patient cohorts here; no retained unit-cash or expectation-surprise estimate is identified.

**Economic/ownership boundary.** Sensor manufacturer, channel supplier, prescribing/care provider and payer are different roles. Payment to one is not the retained margin of another. No clinical outcome, insurer saving or manufacturer-wide cash flow is allocated to a security from the study.

## C2. Senior/home-care operations: more hours is not the same as more clients or staffed slack

**Question.** Can a care operator convert age-related service need into delivered care and repeatable retained economics?

**Reported operating witness.** Addus's Q2 personal-care revenue is $295.995 million versus $269.183 million; billable hours are 11.145 million versus 10.558 million, and revenue per billable hour is $26.55 versus $25.49. Its same-store average billable census is 49,955 versus 50,347. Government programs and managed-care organizations are distinct payer categories. Preserve the issuer's comparable-cohort footnotes rather than treating an acquired operation as an unchanged store. [S05]

**Original arithmetic, not independent evidence.** Multiplying the rounded disclosed hours by realization gives $295.89975 million, $0.09525 million below reported current-period revenue. The prior-period proxy is $269.12342 million, $0.05958 million below its reported figure. The small residuals remain explicit; these are approximate disclosure bridges, not a reconstructed billing ledger. Same-store census changes by approximately -0.779%. Higher realized revenue per hour is not pure price: mix and scope can matter. The arithmetic cannot establish patient benefit, market-share gain or a causal labor-productivity increase.

**Financial boundary.** The 10-Q distinguishes personal-care service costs, segment income and consolidated cash flow. H1 operating cash is $92.376 million; subtracting reported property/equipment spending of $3.050 million and acquisition outlay of $12.182 million produces $77.144 million. That is a named selected cash subtotal, before other investing/financing effects, not normalized free cash to common equity and not a Q2 personal-care figure. Management attributes much of the operating-cash increase to receipt/payroll/payable timing; the filing separately records temporary funding effects and corporate obligations. [S06]

**Age and payment scope.** Illinois's Community Care Program includes an age-60 threshold, assessed long-term-care need and other eligibility conditions. That establishes a specific age/care mechanism, not eligibility for every older resident or proof of beneficial outcomes. [S08] Its January-2026 fee schedule distinguishes $30.80 per in-home service hour from a $32.57 insurance-related rate. These program terms are not the rate on all Addus hours or a measure of retained margin. [S07]

**House interpretation.** Delivered billable hours are stronger evidence of actual service than a location count. They still do not tell us how many qualified staff-hours were available, how many authorized hours went unfilled, or whether service intensity changed appropriately. For a home-care business, the missing capacity denominator is labor/time/service compatibility, not a guessed licensed-bed count.

Use two separate future ratios: fulfilled verified hours divided by eligible authorized requested hours; and delivered hours divided by qualified available staff-hours. Neither denominator is supplied by the reviewed issuer tables. The public case supports realized delivery and a payer/cash bridge; a quantified staffing bottleneck or fulfillment rate remains **NOT_IDENTIFIED**. The claimed clinical/service-quality consequence also remains unproved.

**Strongest competing explanation.** Rates, intensity, acquisitions and cash timing can improve headline financial results without an expanded comparable client population or structurally better staff availability. The opposite interpretation also remains possible: effective scheduling could raise useful delivery, but it requires the missing denominator and quality evidence.

**Next discriminating observation.** Obtain comparable authorized, staffed, scheduled and verified-delivered hours, reasons for unfilled care, payer realization, complete labor/travel/supervision costs and subsequent collections. A rate-minus-hourly-wage shortcut omits material obligations. A later adverse collection or staffing/quality outcome can falsify a favorable operating interpretation even if revenue keeps growing.

**Capital/expectations requirement.** A labor-intensive service model still needs payroll funding before collection, local operating infrastructure and any acquisition/financing commitments. Low property spending alone cannot establish capital-light expansion or cash available to shareholders. No contemporaneous valuation or consensus benchmark was acquired.

**Business boundary.** Addus is an operating-care witness outside the archived senior-living constituent set; it is not being added to a basket. Caregiver payroll, delivered services and collection are not landlord rent or property ownership. This case extends R6's explicit home-care gap without replacing the separate REIT model or claiming all skilled-nursing/residential operators covered.

## C3. Consumer wearables: recurring customer value need not equal paid subscription growth

**Question.** What distinguishes consumer engagement, service attachment and replacement economics from regulated recurring monitoring?

**Reported/attributed evidence.** Garmin's Fitness segment reports $756.823 million revenue for the thirteen weeks ended June 27, 2026, up 25%; a segment is not a watch-only shipment or user cohort. [S09] Its March-27-2025 Connect+ launch offered an optional paid layer while describing existing Connect functionality as free; the stated launch prices were $6.99 monthly or $69.99 annually after a trial. Those are historical offer terms, not a verified September-2026 checkout quote. [S10]

FDA K221774 is a January-12-2023 clearance for a specified Garmin ECG application and compatible-use context. Its indication addresses a single-channel ECG/rhythm-classification function and excludes under-22 use; it is not blanket validation of all wellness features, a payer decision or a paid-service attachment measure. A later database update is not a new clearance. [S11]

**House interpretation.** A consumer ecosystem can monetize through an initial device, replacement/upgrade or optional service. Engagement could support any combination, and a useful free service may contribute to hardware retention. It is therefore misleading to evaluate every wearable as a recurring reimbursed sensor business or to treat the availability of a premium plan as realized subscription revenue.

The proposed consumer view needs four separate cohort questions: are eligible people using the product; which pay for the optional service after trials/refunds; which renew when due; and why/when the installed base replaces hardware? Unique accounts and device shipments are not interchangeable. A person owning two devices must not mechanically halve the measured attachment rate. Warranty replacement and paid elective upgrade must not be conflated.

**Buyer and capital requirement.** In this case the customer voluntarily buys hardware and may elect the service; no medical payer is established for that purchase. The economics would need hardware inventory/engineering/warranty and service acquisition/support costs on compatible cohorts, rather than importing a sensor reimbursement formula.

**What cannot be concluded.** The acquired results do not disclose matched active-account, paid-conversion, renewal or replacement cohorts. They do not identify service acquisition/support costs or customer lifetime contribution. The product announcement is marketing evidence, not independently demonstrated health utility. The ECG record does not fill those commercial gaps. No payment-eligibility rule from C1 is imported into C3.

**Strongest competing explanation.** Strong segment sales could be a product cycle, channel or broader mix effect rather than durable paid attachment. Conversely, modest attachment would not by itself falsify hardware-led ecosystem retention. The discriminating evidence is cohort contribution across the hardware/service lifecycle, not a forced subscription-success score.

**Next observation.** A qualified release of eligible active accounts, net paid accounts, renewal cohorts and paid-versus-warranty replacements, with matched geography/offer versions and complete delivery costs. Current pricing remains unresolved where the accessible product page did not supply usable terms; the historical offer stays visibly dated.

## Cross-case synthesis and limits

The shared product should federate evidence, not force one conversion formula. C1 needs product/payer-matched continuity and payment; C2 needs authorized care, qualified staff-time and collection; C3 needs voluntary engagement, paid account cohorts and replacement economics. Research priority can increase because a consequential link is missing, but that is not a security rank, forecast probability or trade trigger.

No cost of capital, valuation, contemporaneous consensus or validated market-response study was acquired. These mechanisms explain what to investigate, not whether a security is cheap or which treatment a person should choose. A historical published case is development evidence; it cannot be replayed as if it had been known before its actual publication.
