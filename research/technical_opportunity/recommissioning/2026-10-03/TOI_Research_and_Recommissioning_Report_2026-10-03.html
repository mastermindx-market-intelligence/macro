The strongest architecture supported by this research has five characteristics:

How to read the evidence

The report distinguishes four types of statement:

  • Local observation: retrieved current source, current PR metadata, or a specifically dated retained result. Historical tests are not represented as tests executed in this session.
  • External evidence: original papers, author/institutional research pages, official product documentation, and exchange/vendor documentation. Literature findings are priors, not Mastermind validation.
  • Inference: a conclusion drawn from the observed materials, with its scope stated.
  • Proposed architecture: a recommended definition, model, interface, experiment, or build sequence. Numerical choices in the proposed preregistration are design choices, not measured optimums.

The six scientific evidence levels are: published prior → formula/mechanism plausibility → local causal reproduction → retrospective local evidence → genuinely untouched holdout evidence → prospective local evidence. A later level does not arise automatically from the previous one.

B. Revised technical opportunity thesis

B1. What TOI measures

Technical Opportunity Intelligence estimates the state and plausible future evolution of a causally observed price/participation process, conditional on its frozen geometry, information clock, and eligible context. It distinguishes whether a setup may activate, what path is plausible after activation, what would invalidate the setup, what confirmation costs, and how much usable path may remain.

“Causal” has two meanings that must not be conflated. A computation is observation-causal when it uses only information available at its decision time. That does not prove that a proposed mechanism—accumulation, liquidity absorption, underreaction, or institutional sponsorship—causes subsequent returns. The first is a hard engineering requirement. The second is a scientific hypothesis requiring identification and evidence.

TOI should therefore describe a “narrowing realized range with improving peer-relative price behavior,” not assert “institutions are accumulating” from the same OHLCV values. A close beyond a frozen level is observable; a future profitable trend is not.

C. W1 evidence census and mechanism ontology

C1. Evidence spine and its limits

The literature gives useful prior hypotheses but not a blanket endorsement of technical trading. Brock, Lakonishok, and LeBaron studied moving-average and trading-range rules on a long historical DJIA series. Sullivan, Timmermann, and White explicitly addressed the full searched rule universe. Later work on persistence and transaction costs gives a much less permissive interpretation of apparently successful historical rules. These are reasons to use strong simple baselines and complete search accounting—not reasons to declare every price-derived feature worthless. [R01–R04]

Cross-sectional momentum, own-series momentum, proximity to a 52-week high, and volume-conditioned momentum are distinct published objects. Their populations, holding horizons, portfolios, and outcomes differ. A result about multi-month cross-sectional equity momentum or diversified futures trends does not validate a four-hour individual-stock trigger. [R05–R08]

Algorithmic chart-pattern work shows that pattern definitions can be formalized and investigated; it does not make every named pattern predictive or executable after costs. Image-based price representation learning is an additional published approach, but its existence does not resolve TOI’s observation clocks, original-occurrence geometry, or remaining-path estimand. [R09–R10]

The architectural consequence is to retain a small set of scientifically distinct evidence families, with exact formula passports underneath them, rather than count named indicators as hypotheses.

C2. Normalize at three different levels

Formula equivalence: identical functions after a known transformation, with identical data, window, warm-up, seed, missingness, and availability. These should share one scientific hypothesis unless the transformation changes the decision rule.

Statistical dependency: different formulas that share inputs or often co-fire. Bollinger bandwidth, normalized ATR, range contraction, and realized volatility may overlap strongly without being algebraically identical. Empirical dependency is estimated on training data only; it must not erase genuinely different gap/range/close-volatility information prematurely.

Mechanism relationship: distinct measurements can address the same question. Compression is generally a non-directional formation condition; the direction of release is a separate observation. Participation may corroborate a release but must not be double-counted as both a selection condition and an independently predictive discovery after the event was selected using volume.

The catalog should retain the primitive definitions. The W1 passport layer should reference them, identify equivalence and dependency separately, and state the incremental question. No new indicator registry is needed. [I04–I05, I09–I10]

F. Regime, theme, industry, and instrument context

F1. Context is not another universal score

A market-wide regime variable that is identical for all stocks on a date cannot, by itself, distinguish which stock is better that day. It may change the expected behavior of a stock-specific exposure or mechanism. A volatility regime could alter a breakout’s failure distribution; an industry state could affect a stock’s relative-strength interpretation. These are interaction hypotheses, not independent votes.

Use the existing market/regime and breadth planes; GMI for theme state and membership; Data OS for security identity; and Stock Identity only within its qualified historical/PIT scope. The Early Leadership compiler already describes a downstream contract joining stock, market, leave-issuer-out peers, theme/exposure, setup and entry geometry while preserving false authority flags. TOI should supply its owner-qualified setup observation to that contract instead of creating a competing context compiler. [I18–I25]

F2. Proposed conditioning hierarchy

Start with the following hierarchy, admitting each level only when source quality and support permit:

Population level: common baseline for the species, direction and lawful clock.

Instrument level: a few ex ante liquidity and volatility descriptors; listing age; measured market/industry exposure. Avoid per-name free parameters at the first stage.

Context level: a small declared set of market/industry interactions. Begin with no more than two context axes in the first study, consistent with the existing species architecture’s preference for a small cell structure. That architectural bound does not itself establish statistical power. [I17]

Theme level: PIT membership, leave-issuer-out leadership/participation, and economic exposure only when separately sourced. Mere theme membership is not proof of direct economic exposure.

Partial pooling: estimate deviations from broad effects with shrinkage rather than independently optimize each cell. Sparse or unstable cells should shrink toward a broader estimate or abstain; they should not produce confident bespoke probabilities.

A proposed activation model might use a pooled baseline in setup age, distance to trigger, invalidation distance and liquidity, then add one compression descriptor and one prespecified context interaction. The inferential question is whether the added terms improve held-out calibration and loss, not whether a colorful regime table can be produced.

F3. Causal context safeguards

PIT theme and industry membership must be joined at the observation’s known time, not from the current taxonomy. Leave the issuer out of peer aggregates so a stock’s own move does not manufacture peer confirmation. Preserve coverage and missing-member counts. A context derived from the same stock price must be labeled dependent evidence, not independent confirmation.

Earnings proximity requires the date and schedule version known at the decision time; the eventual actual earnings date may leak rescheduling information. Market-cap and liquidity buckets must be based on available values. Regime labels must be filtered from past observations, not retrospectively smoothed over future crises.

Stock Identity’s historical atlas and replay are reusable measurements, but its survivor-only corpus and unresolved later PIT/fit stages prohibit treating them as an already proven per-name expert router. Its correction separating the historical GOLD dealer identity from a miner interpretation is directly relevant: symbol familiarity is not security identity. [I22]

G. Opportunity lifecycle model

G1. Three axes rather than one overloaded status

The W0 names should remain recognizable. The recommended internal model separates:

  • Process phase: FORMING, ARMED, TRIGGERED, CONFIRMED.

  • Validity / termination: valid, INVALIDATED, FAILED/FAKEOUT, expired, or data-unresolved.

This avoids a false linear assumption that every confirmed opportunity must become extended, every extended move was previously confirmed, or a data outage is a failed setup. W0’s user-facing phase vocabulary is preserved; explicit expiry/data-unknown semantics and any enum extension require a versioned owner-approved amendment, not an undocumented implementation change. An activation model needs every eligible formation at risk, including those that disappear, invalidate, expire, halt, delist, or never trigger. Training only on completed occurrences would condition on the outcome the model is intended to predict.

H. Confirmation-cost framework

H1. Compare policies on the same starting cohort

The central comparison is not “all triggers versus the subset that later confirmed.” The latter subset has already survived long enough to satisfy a future condition. Instead, start from the same eligible trigger cohort and evaluate declared policies: enter at the first lawful reference, wait for one completed confirmation, wait for a defined retest, or abstain. Retain every never-confirmed and pre-confirmation-failed episode.

The Durable Bottom material provides direct local motivation: its retained analysis separates an oracle-selected subset entered at the raw trigger from the same selection after a tradable wait. The apparent selection benefit was largely consumed by waiting. That is evidence about that historical construction, not a measured effect size for TOI Compression Release. [I16]

H2. Proposed measurements

Let t0 be the original trigger knowledge time, tc the confirmation knowledge time, p0 and pc the respective first lawful price references, s be +1 for an upside research path and −1 for a downside research path, and A0 a volatility scale frozen before the trigger.

price_cost             = s × (pc − p0)
confirmation_cost_ATR  = s × (pc − p0) / A0
calendar_wait          = tc − t0
traded_time_wait       = eligible market minutes between t0 and tc

Confirmation cost can be negative: a causal retest may offer a better price. It must not be forced into a nonnegative “lateness” score. Report gaps and overnight waiting separately from traded time, and keep transaction costs separate from the price paid for confirmation.

For each policy, publish: triggered population; entered population; no-entry count and reasons; false-breaks avoided; true opportunities missed; event recall; first-entry lead; price/ATR cost; favorable excursion already observed; subsequent MFE and MAE; barrier outcomes; dead-money risk; and uncertainty clustered at an appropriate episode/calendar level.

H3. Efficient frontier

The frontier is a set of non-dominated policies in fakeout risk, lead/recall, price cost, adverse excursion and remaining-path capture. A single scalar can hide an unacceptable trade-off. Predeclare either a primary comparison or explicit constraints, such as a noninferiority bound on event recall while testing a reduction in fakeouts. Do not pick a favorable point from the frontier after seeing all outcomes and present it as preregistered.

Two analyses are useful but answer different questions:

Policy analysis: from the original trigger population, what happens under immediate, wait, or abstain policies? Missing entries remain part of the result.

Landmark analysis: among episodes genuinely alive and observed at a particular later time, what is the conditional future distribution now? This can support the current product view, but it does not prove that waiting caused an improvement.

Where observation loss is informative—for example, delisting, illiquidity or a halt—neither deletion nor unqualified inverse-probability weighting is acceptable. Define the estimand, record the mechanism of missingness, and report sensitivity bounds when the needed assumptions are not defensible.

H4. Falsifiers

A confirmation rule fails as an economic improvement when its apparent quality gain disappears on the common starting population, depends on retrospectively selecting survivors, or is dominated after the cost of waiting and lost recall are included. It may still be a useful descriptive event. “More accurate among confirmed samples” and “better opportunity policy” must remain separate claims.

I. Remaining-opportunity and chase framework

I1. What is observable and what is predicted

The system can observe distance from the original trigger, favorable excursion already experienced, elapsed traded time, current volatility, and known opposing structure. It cannot observe the eventual total path. Therefore a live statement such as “70% of this opportunity has been consumed” is generally unjustified unless it is explicitly a calibrated model estimate with a defined denominator and uncertainty.

The recommended display is a conditional remaining-path band, accompanied by observed extension, not an invented fraction of a future maximum.

I2. Proposed estimands

At a current landmark t, choose a declared horizon H, direction s, and frozen scale A0. For a chosen terminal convention:

remaining_MFE(t,H) = max(0, max over eligible u in (t,t+H] of s × (P[u] − P[t]) / A0)
remaining_MAE(t,H) = max(0, max over eligible u in (t,t+H] of −s × (P[u] − P[t]) / A0)

Whether the window stops at structural invalidation, follows the full horizon regardless, or resolves at a target is part of the estimand. Report a structural-stop view and an unconditional horizon view separately where both matter; do not silently switch whichever looks favorable.

The first model should estimate quantiles or an empirical conditional distribution, not just a mean. Pair remaining favorable excursion with adverse excursion and dead-money probability. Marginal MFE and MAE estimates do not imply a joint probability of “target before stop”; that order-sensitive probability requires its own outcome model.

I3. Comparable paths and selection

Use all eligible historical analogs selected by information available at the landmark: species/version, phase, clock, geometry, age, extension, and a small set of qualified context variables. Include failed, invalidated, expired and flat paths. Do not select historical winners first or retrieve analogs using the entire eventual chart.

Begin with broad, interpretable strata and shrink toward a pooled distribution. Similarity matching and learned embeddings are additional search choices; their distance function, inputs and selection budget must be recorded. Effective sample support must be assessed after episode and calendar dependence, not by counting every overlapping bar.

The ratio of an observed move to the median total move of a selected historical sample is not automatically a calibrated “fraction consumed.” Ratios of quantiles are not quantiles of path fractions. Any consumed-fraction statistic should initially be an ex post diagnostic, with the live interface emphasizing observed extension and a predicted remaining distribution.

I4. Censoring, uncertainty and abstention

Right-censored episodes have not yet resolved; a halted instrument is not necessarily dead money; delisting cannot simply remove a losing path. Same-bar touches of favorable and adverse boundaries are order-ambiguous without an admitted finer-grain source. Preserve ambiguity or use explicit conservative bounds rather than optimistic intrabar ordering.

Prediction intervals must distinguish sampling uncertainty, observation/correction uncertainty and distribution shift. Adaptive conformal inference offers a framework for long-run coverage under changing distributions, but that is not a guarantee of tight, correct conditional coverage for each stock, phase and regime. TOI also has delayed labels and dependent episodes; calibration updates must wait until the relevant outcome is actually observable. [R17]

Abstain when the source is stale, the identity/recipe does not match training, support is sparse, the state is outside the qualified domain, or prediction intervals are too wide to support the claimed use. A wide interval is a scientific result, not a UI defect to hide.

J2. Dynamic probability coherence

For an at-risk formation, the cumulative incidence of activation must account for survival without activation and without competing terminal events. Summing independent activation and failure classifiers can produce incoherent probabilities. Prefer a shared competing-risk formulation or explicitly constrained probability system.

A one-step hazard conditioned on the current observed state cannot be multiplied through future steps using covariates that are not yet known. For a k-step forecast, either fit a landmark model using the current information set and future elapsed time, or explicitly model the future state transitions. The simpler landmark approach is preferable initially.

Likewise, multiplying P(activation) by a path-success probability is meaningful only when the conditional model’s population, activation definition, horizon and conditioning set match. A Daily activation probability and a Weekly success rate from different selected cohorts do not yield a legitimate combined probability.

J3. Model complexity ladder

Start with frequency baselines and simple regularized models. Add a small number of prespecified nonlinear terms or pooled interactions only when they improve held-out calibration and useful discrimination. A filtered change-point model can contribute a context variable if it beats a simpler rolling-volatility/change baseline. Do not use a retrospectively smoothed hidden state as a causal feature. [R13]

Only after those baselines should a sequence/image representation challenger be admitted, with the same data, labels, population, cost model, search budget and untouched evaluation. A complex model must earn its extra computation and explanation burden. The existence of a published successful representation is a prior, not an entitlement to production. [R10]

K6. Models, labels, and price references

Activation models: A0 uses setup age, normalized trigger/invalidation distance and liquidity; A1 adds the primary compression measurement; A2 adds participation and the single declared context interaction. For R0, use multinomial competing-event logit predictors for trigger / structural invalidation / continued risk, with deterministic expiry handled by the declared horizon. Forecasts from a landmark use its available covariates and a fixed future-step indicator, never realized future covariates. Use training-standardized continuous features, log1p(age), signed normalized trigger distance, normalized invalidation distance, log prior dollar volume and trailing volatility; no splines, trees, per-name coefficients or interaction search. The only added interaction in A2 is the one owner-adopted context interaction. Use a fixed ridge penalty of 1 on the mean negative log-likelihood plus one-half the squared coefficient norm, leaving the intercept unpenalized. Missing load-bearing values cause abstention; non-load-bearing missingness must be a declared indicator, not silently a zero. These are proposed baseline settings, not measured optimal settings. A different solver convention must be translated exactly rather than reuse the same numeric penalty with different scaling.

Post-trigger models: On the common C1 trigger cohort, P0 uses geometry, volatility/liquidity, observed extension and the primary compression measurement; P1 adds participation only; P2 adds the qualified Weekly context scalar. This isolates participation and context increments rather than attributing a compression-plus-volume change to volume alone. Use binary logit for the specified path label, with the same fixed ridge scaling as the activation models. Train-only centering/scaling, zero-variance feature handling, optimization tolerance and software version must be frozen in the executable manifest; coefficient fitting is permitted, model/parameter-grid search is not in R0. Calibrate with one declared logistic recalibration procedure on a separate chronological calibration slice, or withhold numeric probabilities if the calibration slope/intercept are not estimable. A distinct model fitting history must not be hidden inside a “descriptor.”

Primary Daily path label, proposed: favorable displacement of 2 frozen ATR before adverse displacement of 1 frozen ATR, within 21 trading sessions after the first lawful next-observation reference. This is a fixed research ruler, not a recommended trade. Structural invalidation is recorded separately. The primary barrier label follows its full declared observation rule regardless of structural invalidation; an explicitly secondary structure-stopped ruler is reported without replacing it.

Intraday comparison: use the same common 21-session terminal horizon for cross-clock path comparison, while separately reporting intraday resolution time. A 21-bar 4H outcome must not be compared to a 21-day outcome as if they were the same claim.

Fakeout label, proposed: completed close back inside the original frozen range within three recipe bars after the trigger, before the favorable primary barrier. Structural invalidation and return-inside-range are separate subtypes. A “retest succeeds” label may not use future price action to decide whether the initial return inside was healthy.

Dead-money label, proposed: at the common horizon, absolute net displacement is at most 0.5 frozen ATR and neither side has reached 1 frozen ATR. This is a joint path label distinct from an unresolved 2/1 barrier label. Halt, delisting, missing observations and insufficient maturity remain separately classified.

Price reference: a completed-close trigger cannot assume a fill at the price before it was observable. K8. Validation, dependence and search control

The 252-observation percentile lookback is recipe-specific: 252 Weekly observations consume approximately five years before further training and evaluation. The recovered post-July-2021 Daily source therefore cannot be assumed to support a standalone Weekly model with that warm-up. That cell remains held unless a deeper panel is admitted. The proposed 13-week completed-Weekly context for Daily is a different, explicitly declared lower-support requirement; it is not a covert substitute for a full Weekly compression study.

The raw conceptual search space can become enormous. An illustrative—not measured—space of 25 mechanisms × 5 parameter choices × 4 clocks × 6 regime states × 5 theme states × 4 instrument classes × 4 phases × 3 confirmation rules × 4 target horizons contains 2,880,000 combinations, before model architecture and feature selection. This calculation explains why renaming correlated indicators or splitting families after seeing results is dangerous.

Use chronological nested walk-forward evaluation. For fixed-setting R0, inner folds assess specification and calibration behavior using only information and matured labels available before their cutoff; they do not authorize inventing a tuning grid. Any later model-selection extension must declare that grid and count it before outcomes. Outer folds estimate performance without refitting to that fold’s outcomes. Warm-up history may precede a test interval; labels crossing a training cutoff may not leak into training. Purge overlapping label windows and define embargoes from the actual label/episode construction, not from an arbitrary percentage.

A calendar year is not “untouched” merely because it was not used by this one script. Audit historical exposure across S16, Prophet, Technical Lab and related research. If the available historical period is already heavily design-touched, call it developmental/retrospective and reserve a genuinely inaccessible holdout or newly accruing prospective period after registration. No claim of untouched 2025 or 2026 evidence is made here.

Resampling must preserve cross-stock common shocks and within-episode dependence. Use calendar blocks of cross-sectional observations, with episode-aware treatment of repeated landmarks, and name/sector holdouts as separate robustness tests. A stationary bootstrap is one established dependent-data method, but it does not make a nonstationary multi-era process stationary. Block choice and sensitivity belong in the registered method. [R22]

The eight primary confirmatory contrasts are four Daily mechanism questions evaluated separately by direction: A1 versus A0 for activation log-loss; P1 versus P0 for conditional-path log-loss; Q1 versus Q0 for the constrained confirmation comparison; and P2 versus P1 for the Weekly-context increment. Q2 is secondary, not a second chance at the primary confirmation claim. For the proposed Q1 claim, require a lower entered-episode fakeout rate together with opportunity-capture noninferiority within five percentage points and an upper confidence bound on mean confirmation price cost no greater than 0.25 frozen ATR. The joint claim fails if any component fails; do not call trading less an improvement. These margins are proposed decision tolerances requiring owner adoption before outcomes, not empirically optimized thresholds. Keep loss, recall, cost and path outcomes visible separately. Apply familywise control to these prespecified claims; do not select a different favorable endpoint for each. Secondary sensor, clock and context diagnostics belong to the same declared search history and receive an appropriate dependent-test adjustment. BH requires its dependence conditions; BY is more conservative under arbitrary dependence but still needs valid input p-values. [R18]

White’s Reality Check and Hansen’s SPA can address selection among declared predictive-loss alternatives under their assumptions. They do not repair lookahead, a missing universe denominator or an invalid label. PBO/CSCV can diagnose backtest-selection fragility; it is not a substitute for causal chronological validation. Deflated Sharpe is relevant only when a claim is actually made about a return series; it is not a correction for activation probabilities or classifier AUC. [R19–R21]

Placebos must preserve eligibility, available information and the main dependence structure. An unrestricted random shuffle of bars creates an implausible null. A near-boundary placebo should match date, liquidity, volatility and distance without selecting on future paths. A volatility/liquidity-matched breakout comparator is essential to distinguish compression information from ordinary low-volatility geometry.

L2. Technical Opportunity Radar

Use separate queues rather than one bullish-confidence leaderboard:

Invalidated / Failed monitoring: track what changed and why. Preserve watched episodes and failed alerts so users can learn from them; do not leave stale bullish rows on screen after invalidation or source loss.

L3. Security-detail experience

The detail view should answer six questions in order: What process is happening? What phase is it in? What must happen next? What invalidates it? What has waiting cost? What remains uncertain?

The main chart shows immutable trigger/invalidation geometry, observation and confirmation markers, the current provisional/final distinction, and a lifecycle strip. A state-transition history explains the actual changed evidence. A horizon matrix retains contradictions such as Daily repair against Weekly damage. An evidence matrix groups dependent sensors into their mechanism family, rather than listing each as another vote.

A path panel shows conditional MFE, MAE, time-to-resolution and dead-money distributions, with clear support and abstention. Historical analogs include failures and flat paths, selected causally from training-eligible observations. The research passport exposes population, source/basis/clock, label, search history, held-out/prospective state and permitted use.

No mock probabilities are needed before models exist. During early implementation, show “not yet calibrated,” observed geometry and explicit next conditions. A truthful useful product can precede predictive promotion.

L4. Terminal integration

Macro owns canonical opportunity semantics. Terminal renders the same episode and may compute local visual primitives for chart interaction, but it must not invent a competing verdict from differently adjusted bars or a different timeframe recipe.

The interface must carry exact recipe identity, security/episode lineage, source timestamps, phase/maturity/validity, frozen geometry, probability definitions, uncertainty and authority flags. A chart switching from RTH to extended hours must either obtain the matching opportunity object or explicitly mark the current object incompatible. It must not reuse an RTH probability over an extended-hours chart without warning.

Current Terminal already has session-aware construction and source-origin tracing. Extend those owners and the existing chart renderer. Do not create a second minute store, WebSocket path, calendar, chart renderer or technical-verdict engine. [I12–I13]

L5. Alerts and attention economics

Alerts should be keyed to a canonical episode and state transition, not emitted whenever several correlated indicators remain true. Record the transition ID, source version and reason. Vendor corrections should produce an explicit correction/retraction where appropriate, not a duplicate fresh setup.

Use idempotency, user watch scope, transition severity and an explicit attention budget. Deduplicate equivalent sensor notifications without hiding genuine cross-horizon contradictions. Track useful early discovery, missed state changes, false alarms, retractions and user follow-through—not just notification volume.

Product evaluation should test whether a user can correctly identify the trigger, invalidation, contradiction and clock; distinguish an uncalibrated descriptor from a probability; and understand why confirmation may be expensive. A successful usability test is not predictive validation, but it is a necessary product-quality gate.
