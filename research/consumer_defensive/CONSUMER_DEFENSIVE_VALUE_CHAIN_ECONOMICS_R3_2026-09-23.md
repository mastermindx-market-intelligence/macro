## 1. Purpose, source boundary and main result

R3 extends the preserved R1/R2 research into retailers, private-label suppliers, distributors, ingredients and agricultural processing. It contains 17 company/business cases and a 25-entry source register. The cases establish different economic transmission paths; they do not recommend securities, enroll graph facts or certify a complete latest-filing census.

The new principal conclusion is that **consumer savings, channel share, supplier revenue, retained profit and shareholder cash can move in different directions**. Consumer value-seeking is therefore a mechanism to investigate, not a positive label to attach to every discount retailer or private-label manufacturer. A contract can transmit lower raw-material prices into lower supplier revenue without reducing conversion profit. A richer sales mix can improve a percentage margin while producing fewer profit dollars. An ingredient supplier can report stronger specialty activity while another division loses profit. ## 2. Structural distinctions for the research model

Seven roles must remain separately explainable: grocery/discount retail; membership retail; specialty-format retail; wholesale/foodservice distribution; private-label or contract production; formulation/functional ingredients; and agricultural/biological processing. A diversified company may occupy several roles. Roles are research facets over native identities, not seven new canonical sectors or tradable baskets.

The following comparisons are invalid unless their scope is reconciled: retail sales and manufacturer sales; same-store sales and total footprint growth; transactions and purchased units; membership revenue and membership profit; distributor case volume and consumer meals; ingredient revenue and physical throughput; segment sales including internal transfers and consolidated external revenue; food-dollar value added and net profit; or company cash-flow alternatives and GAAP operating cash flow.

A value-chain map must distinguish a physical flow from a commercial relationship and from a house economic hypothesis. Repeated purchases along one chain cannot be summed as independent end-market demand. Unknown supplier contracts and undisclosed customer concentration remain unknown. Catalog capability and private-label exposure do not establish a particular retailer-supplier contract.

## 3. Retail and channel cases

### R3-C01 — Kroger: demand, adjusted operating income and EPS diverge

**Source observation.** September 11 Q2 2026 disclosure: identical sales excluding fuel increased 0.2%; adjusted FIFO operating profit was $1,076 versus $1,091; adjusted EPS was $1.09 versus $1.04. The release separately reports repurchases and defines an adjusted e-commerce perimeter excluding exited businesses. [S01]

**Research interpretation.** EPS improvement does not by itself establish better grocery operating economics. Online growth must preserve channel and exit definitions. The proposed valuation question concerns durable profit and cash per share after investment, not merely a positive EPS comparison.

**Next evidence/falsifier.** Track comparable trips, basket composition, merchandise profit, pharmacy scope, fulfillment contribution, labor, shrink and capital requirements through their respective sources. Reject an operating-acceleration thesis when per-share gains rely on financial/accounting effects while comparable customer economics weaken. Do not impute private-label margins from a grocery group label.

### R3-C02 — Dollar General: traffic growth and temporary margin effects

**Source observation.** Q2 release of August 27 reports comparable sales +3.5%, traffic +2.0% and average transaction amount +1.5%. Gross margin rose 127 basis points, with approximately 81 basis points attributed to net tariff-refund benefits after reinvestment. Consumables sales were $9,263.012 of total $11,290.380. [S02]

**Research interpretation.** Consumables represented a calculated 82.04% of sales, not profits. A larger basket can reflect prices or mix rather than physical consumption. A disclosed temporary benefit must remain separate from recurring merchandising improvements.

**Next evidence/falsifier.** Compare within-store traffic, comparable-category quantities, discretionary mix, inventory availability, shrink, markdowns and operating cost per transaction. A value-seeking thesis weakens if consumables gain share while profit dollars or service quality deteriorate. Do not treat the unallocated remainder of margin improvement as automatically structural.

### R3-C03 — Dollar Tree: an adjusted number can still include a nonrecurring benefit

**Source observation.** Q2 2026 comparable sales increased 3.7%, comprising average ticket +3.3% and traffic +0.4%. Diluted EPS of $2.70 included $1.31 from tariff refunds. The annual adjusted EPS range includes an approximately $0.60 refund contribution; the earlier Q1 range explicitly excluded that contribution. Q2 also includes transition-service income associated with the former Family Dollar business. [S03, S21]

**Research interpretation.** A reported or adjusted label is not a recurring-earnings guarantee. Guidance cannot be compared as a clean operating upgrade until adjustment scopes match. Continuing Dollar Tree operations are not the historical combined business.

**Next evidence/falsifier.** Keep transaction count, ticket, price-point architecture, assortment, transition services and refund accounting separate. The growth thesis needs ongoing customer contribution and inventory turns, not annualized exceptional EPS. Do not mechanically infer current political-policy effects beyond the issuer's financial attribution.

### R3-C04 — BJ's: merchandise inflation and membership economics

**Source observation.** Q2 2026 comparable sales were +11.9% including gasoline and +3.1% excluding it. Membership fee income was $135.604 versus $123.333. Net sales of $6,090.975 plus fees reconcile to total revenue of $6,226.579. [S04]

**Research interpretation.** Gasoline-sensitive growth is not the same as comparable merchandise demand. Fee growth can reflect fee levels, membership mix and cohort timing; it does not directly measure member retention or standalone membership profit.

**Next evidence/falsifier.** Require an explicitly defined renewal population and period, paid-member changes, tier mix, shopping frequency, merchandise contribution, rewards expense and new-club capital. A growing membership franchise can still disappoint if acquisition costs, rewards or store investment absorb its economics. No renewal percentage from another period is carried into this quarter.

### R3-C05 — Sprouts: footprint growth is not same-store acceleration

**Source observation.** Q2 2026 sales were $2,325.804 versus $2,220.602, while comparable sales declined 1.0%. Seven stores opened in the quarter. The outlook distinguishes a 52-week basis from an anticipated additional week. [S05]

**Research interpretation.** New-store growth and existing-store demand are independent dimensions. A chain can increase revenue while mature locations weaken. An extra fiscal week is not an improvement in daily productivity.

**Next evidence/falsifier.** Analyze opening cohorts, cannibalization, comparable-location transactions and basket, labor/occupancy, preopening costs and cash invested per store. Require observed maturation rather than assuming every new location reaches mature margins. Do not compare a 53-week outcome with a 52-week forecast as a pure operating beat.

### R3-C06 — Loblaw: food, pharmacy and financial perimeter

**Source observation.** July 30 Q2 2026 results in Canadian dollars show food-retail same-store sales +1.6% and drug-retail same-store sales +4.6%; pharmacy/healthcare services grew faster than front-store sales. Total revenue includes financial-services activity and is not identical to retail revenue. [S06]

**Research interpretation.** A positive consolidated earnings read can contain non-grocery drivers. Sales geography, healthcare activity and transaction-related reporting boundaries must survive the company-to-theme link.

**Next evidence/falsifier.** Compare food transactions, basket and own-brand adoption separately from prescription/service activity. Cash returns from a financial transaction cannot be projected as recurring grocery cash flow. Any completed transaction date or new continuing-operations perimeter requires its own current source, not inference from a results-period description.

### R3-C07 — Ahold Delhaize: regional and currency splits

**Source observation.** Q2 2026 group sales of approximately EUR23.2 billion grew 1.9% at constant currency and 0.3% reported. U.S. comparable sales excluding fuel rose 0.8%, versus 1.7% in Europe. The release reports regional online growth and underlying margins separately. [S07]

**Research interpretation.** Currency translation and geographic mix can obscure local execution. Online sales remain a channel inside group sales, not an additional market to add to consolidated revenue.

**Next evidence/falsifier.** Maintain local-currency, regional, calendar and acquisition scopes. Compare channel contribution after delivery/fulfillment costs, not online revenue growth alone. A regional advantage thesis weakens when constant-scope customer economics deteriorate even though translation or another region supports the headline.

## 4. Distribution and service economics

### R3-C08 — UNFI: contracting revenue with improving cash generation

**Source observation.** The September 8 fiscal-2026 issuer summary reports annual sales down 2.0%, adjusted EBITDA up 27.0% and free cash flow of $323, up $84. Management attributes sales effects to planned network changes, completed project work and the prior disruption comparison. [S08]

**Research interpretation.** Shrinking low-return business can improve economics, but the exit attribution must be supported rather than assumed. Management's underlying-sales adjustment is not an independently reconstructed series.

**Next evidence/falsifier.** Track continuing customer demand, gross profit per compatible case, route utilization, fill rates, inventory turns, bad debts and implementation cost. Test whether cash improvement persists without business shrinkage or temporary working-capital release. This record uses the issuer summary; its linked IR news index did not expose a full detailed release through the read path, so no detailed cash reconciliation is claimed.

### R3-C09 — Sysco: gross-profit dollars rise while percentage margin declines

**Source observation.** For Q4 fiscal2026, group sales were $22,124 versus $21,138, and gross profit $4,134 versus $3,986. The release reports a 17-basis-point margin decline. The later September 9 plan targets $500 of efficiencies through FY2029, including the $100 already included for FY2027. [S09, S20]

**Research interpretation.** Absolute gross profit and margin rate are different outputs. A future savings target is not realized profit, and its included sub-target must not be counted twice. Group gross profit cannot be divided by U.S.-only case volume to create a valid unit margin.

**Next evidence/falsifier.** Study customer mix, local versus national accounts, route density, delivery frequency, procurement economics and implementation investment. A scale thesis weakens if incremental cases produce insufficient contribution after service costs. Test repeatable cash after capex rather than accepting EBITDA alone.

### R3-C10 — US Foods: customer mix, accounting adjustments and delivery cost

**Source observation.** Q2 2026 total case volume grew 1.9%, with organic cases +1.7%; independent-restaurant organic cases grew 5.0%. Gross profit rose 8.0%, including a favorable $19 year-over-year LIFO comparison; adjusted gross profit grew 6.9%. [S10]

**Research interpretation.** Case growth depends on customer mix and acquisition perimeter. Accounting inventory effects are distinct from commercial price realization. More cases do not automatically mean more meals or more profit per delivery.

**Next evidence/falsifier.** Match physical case definition, geography, customer population and cost scope. Examine service-intensive account economics and route capacity. Stronger independent-account activity should be tested against contribution and retention, not assumed to offset every cost. Preserve the extra-week assumption in the subsequent fiscal outlook.

## 5. Private-label and contract-production cases

### R3-C11 — TreeHouse: private-label growth is not universal supplier growth

**Historical source observation.** Q2 2025 sales of $798 rose 1.2%; the disclosed bridge includes volume/mix -6.2%, pricing +4.2% and acquisition effects +4.5%. A recall insurance recovery contributed to reported gross margin. The February 11, 2026 issuer notice confirms the acquisition closed and common shares ceased NYSE trading. [S11, S18]

**Research interpretation.** Retain the business in the economic map but not as an unchanged current listed-stock candidate. Category adoption does not establish this supplier's share, contract quality or profit. Higher pricing can coexist with reduced or deliberately exited volume.

**Next evidence/falsifier.** Investigate documented retailer contracts, capacity awards, service penalties, recalls, insurance and customer concentration. Those private details are not inferred from aggregate store-brand statistics. A favorable thesis requires retained conversion economics and cash generation after quality, service and investment costs.

### R3-C12 — SunOpta: cost pass-through can reduce reported pricing

**Historical source observation.** In Q2 2025, sales grew 12.9%; the release identified approximately 14.4% volume growth and a 1.4% pricing decrease reflecting raw-material savings passed to customers. Adjusted EBITDA increased. Refresco's May 1, 2026 announcement confirms completion of its acquisition; the announcement describes subsequent delisting steps. [S12, S19]

**Research interpretation.** Price deflation need not mean deteriorating competitive position. It can reflect the contractual economics of production. The business remains research-relevant, but current exchange eligibility cannot be assumed from its historical symbol or financial release.

**Next evidence/falsifier.** Separate throughput, conversion compensation, customer-supplied versus purchased inputs, qualification, utilization and capital expenditure. Without the contract, do not assert a fixed-fee or percentage-markup formula. Compare profitability and cash at a compatible perimeter, not the revenue deflation headline alone.

## 6. Ingredients and processors

### R3-C13 — Ingredion: specialty growth and a weaker division coexist

**Source observation.** Q2 2026 total sales bridge: $1,833 + $36 FX + $20 volume - $39 price/mix = $1,850. Texture & Healthful Solutions sales were $627 versus $599; Food & Industrial Ingredients U.S./Canada sales were $488 versus $523, with operating income $58 versus $86. The release discusses production effects at Argo. [S13]

**Research interpretation.** Specialty product demand cannot be promoted into a positive read for all processing assets. The texture segment represents a calculated 33.89% of group revenue, not a pure profit allocation. End-of-quarter production normalization does not erase a full-quarter disruption.

**Next evidence/falsifier.** Disaggregate texture/formulation demand, throughput, net realization, contract pass-through and affected plant production. A local production issue is not evidence of a global shortage. Any acquisition or disposal changes require source-dated perimeter treatment; do not splice incompatible group histories.

### R3-C14 — Kerry: volume, currency and cash-conversion definitions

**Source observation.** July 29 H1 2026 report, visually inspected PDF pages1-2: volume +3.3%, pricing -1.0%, transaction currency -0.1%, net disposals -1.1%, translation currency -4.8%, giving reported revenue -3.7%. EBITDA margin increased 60 basis points. Free cash flow was EUR262; cash conversion was 76% using average working capital versus 85% using the balance-sheet-date movement. [S14]

**Research interpretation.** Stronger physical activity can coexist with lower reported revenue. Cash-conversion percentages with different working-capital definitions are not conflicting answers. Forward 2030 targets remain targets.

**Next evidence/falsifier.** Compare consistent product/channel volume and retained formulation economics, customer projects, input pass-through and cash investment. A quality-growth hypothesis weakens if improvements depend on transitory translation or end-date working-capital management rather than sustained business returns.

### R3-C15 — ADM: food, feed and fuel cannot be assigned the same demand driver

**Source observation.** The August 4 Q2 2026 release reports segment operating-profit improvement and explicitly identifies approximately $100 of net timing/mark-to-market effects in Ag Services & Oilseeds. Carbohydrate Solutions includes activities with different end-use exposure. [S15]

**Research interpretation.** A processing segment's name does not make all earnings a food-demand signal. Inventory timing, trading, feed, food and fuel exposures can contribute differently. A source-attributed policy effect is not a durable margin coefficient.

**Next evidence/falsifier.** Separate relevant spreads, byproduct economics, logistics, hedging, end uses and working capital. Examine realized cash and normalized returns through the cycle. Reject a generic grain-inflation-beneficiary label without the buying, selling and inventory positions needed to establish direction.

### R3-C16 — Bunge: integration, internal processing stages and cash alternatives

**Source observation.** Q2 2026 incorporates the enlarged post-Viterra business. Soybean Processing EBIT of $804 includes $359 of mark-to-market effects, giving adjusted EBIT of $445. First-half GAAP operating cash flow was negative $1,126, while adjusted funds from operations was positive $1,291. [S16]

**Research interpretation.** Acquisition-expanded revenue is not organic growth. Processed, merchandised and refined volumes can describe successive or overlapping activities and must not be summed into independent food demand. Funds from operations is not cash from operations.

**Next evidence/falsifier.** Reconcile corporate scope, working capital, integration costs, realized spreads, timing effects and shareholder claims. The cash thesis fails when operating success requires growing financing without adequate risk-adjusted return. A low multiple on a cyclical profit peak is not automatically inexpensive.

### R3-C17 — Cal-Maine: external versus internal sales, and denominator-driven mix

**Source observation.** July 22 fiscal2026 Q4 release reports total sales $552.581. Specialty external-customer sales were $232.454 and prepared-food external sales $60.403. Specialty total segment sales of $239.731 include $7.277 of internal sales. The release reports specialty-plus-prepared at 53.0% of total sales and a sharp conventional-egg price decline. [S17]

**Research interpretation.** The external-sales ratio is 52.998%, reconciling to 53.0%. Incorrectly using total segment sales produces 54.315%. This is a scope issue resolved from detailed source tables, not an issuer error. A richer mix percentage can also result partly from a collapsing denominator; require each category's absolute economics.

**Next evidence/falsifier.** Research contracted versus market-linked realization only where disclosed, flock/productive capacity, feed, losses, purchased eggs, prepared-food contribution and capex. Do not treat announced capacity as qualified output or future diversification as already achieved. Recast segments must retain as-known versus later-comparable histories.

## 8. What this resolves for the product and valuation research

The proposed dossier should show four separate economic views: consumer behavior; company/channel/contract exposure; retained profit and recurring cash; and expectations/valuation/market evidence. A page may confidently explain a historical business mechanism while refusing a current security recommendation.

A private-label map should distinguish store-brand adoption from the individual supplier's retained economics. A distributor view should emphasize compatible volume, service contribution, route capacity and cash. A processor view needs cycle and inventory context. A retailer view needs comparable customer activity, gross-profit dollars, fees/services, fulfillment and capital. Shared underlying metrics stay with their existing owners, and the browser renders rather than recalculates them.

Valuation work must test what changes the duration and variability of cash flows. A temporary accounting gain should not be capitalized as a perpetuity; a permanent change in contractual economics should not be dismissed because nominal revenue declines. Conversely, neither temporary cost relief nor management's restructuring attribution establishes sustainable value by itself. These are proposed analytical rules, not a completed valuation model.
