## 1. Selection, estimand and limitation

These are deliberately selected counterexamples to the proposition that reported earnings growth and shareholder outcomes are the same thing. They are not a random sample, a portfolio, a ranking exercise or an estimate of strategy success. Observations within one issuer are dependent. No market-adjusted regression, transaction-cost study or prediction was fitted.

The initial quantities are (a) change in an issuer's annual reported diluted EPS; (b) total shareholder return derived from published cumulative-return indices; and, for one example only, (c) year-end share price divided by subsequently reported same-year EPS. These answer different questions. Total return includes reinvested dividends; price return does not. Subsequent annual earnings cannot be treated as information available at the preceding year-end price.

The source records are the issuer's primary SEC reports and one earnings release. Published total-return indices are rounded to whole-dollar cumulative values, so derived percentages below are approximate. The years are the companies' own fiscal windows, not matched cross-company calendar periods. All observations remain historical, not current 2026 stock assessments. [H01-H04]

### What these observations establish

These examples refute a deterministic mapping from positive annual reported EPS growth to a positive return in that same broad fiscal interval. They do not identify a causal explanation, determine an attractive entry point, or demonstrate that negative-return companies were mispriced.

The financial results and prices move through time, while the table compresses each into annual endpoints. A year's reported results can include surprises, one-time effects, changes in share count and information about an earlier period. The price incorporates expectations about later periods as well. The final report was published after its fiscal period ended, so none of these rows is a point-in-time trading rule.

A descriptive statement such as earnings increased while the stock's reinvested return was negative is useful. The stronger statement that the stock should therefore rebound is unsupported without valuation, expectations, timing and validation evidence.

### Benchmark context is not automatically alpha

Over the reviewed June2024-to-June2025 interval, P&G's published consumer-staples benchmark index rose from152 to170; over December2023-to-December2024, Hershey's packaged-foods benchmark moved from120 to113. The latter broad industry deterioration means an absolute negative return is not the same as an equally large company-specific underperformance. [H01,H02]

From those rounded indices, the first comparison's arithmetic excess versus its industry index is approximately -12.50 percentage points, while relative-wealth change is about -11.18%. The second comparison's figures are approximately -0.74 percentage point and -0.78%, respectively. These are two different statistics, not competing implementations of one field. They are not factor-adjusted alpha and omit investor taxes, fees and trading costs.

The benchmark must be chosen for the actual research question, versioned and kept fixed before a predictive evaluation. Do not choose whichever benchmark makes a narrative look strongest. This small retrospective exercise cannot establish the best benchmark or a generalizable effect.

## 3. Hershey: earnings definition materially changes the apparent valuation contraction

The FY2023 filing gives a last-business-day common-share close of USD186.44 on December29,2023. The FY2024 filing gives USD169.35 on December31,2024. The February6,2025 results release reports adjusted diluted EPS of USD9.37 for2024 versus USD9.59 for2023, in contrast to reported EPS growth. The release's adjustment bridge includes derivative mark-to-market effects and other specified adjustments. [H02,H03,H04]

Using those source values, price declined approximately9.17%. The two explicitly defined retrospective earnings bases produce:

| Calculation | Reported GAAP annual EPS basis | Issuer-adjusted annual EPS basis |
|---|---:|---:|
| Annual EPS change | +20.53% | -2.29% |
| 2023 price / eventual2023 EPS | 20.58x | 19.44x |
| 2024 price / eventual2024 EPS | 15.51x | 18.07x |
| Change in that ex-post multiple | -24.64% | -7.03% |

These are **price divided by eventual annual earnings**, not contemporaneously observed forward P/E ratios and not current fair values. The same-year EPS denominator was reported after the corresponding closing-price date. Calling either column the multiple investors actually saw at that date would introduce look-ahead error.

Both columns reproduce the price identity:

`price factor = earnings factor x defined multiple factor`

The difference is analytical meaning. A system using only reported EPS could present a much larger apparent compression, while the issuer-adjusted measure shows weaker earnings and a smaller remaining compression. Neither denominator is automatically the economic truth. Adjustments must be evaluated, and recurring cash, reinvestment and future cost exposure may call for a different valuation anchor.

The issuer release separately supplies the historical adjustment bridge. The2024 arithmetic is `10.92 -2.26 +0.58 +0.22 -0.03 -0.06 =9.37`; the2023 bridge is `9.06 +0.29 +0.01 +0.37 -0.14 =9.59`. These signs and labels must stay source-native. They must not be relabeled as a uniform recurring/nonrecurring classification adopted by Mastermind. [H04]

### The conclusion we should carry into the product

An apparent valuation anomaly may actually be a denominator anomaly. The dossier must show the chosen earnings basis, the availability date and the alternative defensible bases before claiming expansion or compression. A low backward-looking multiple can be uninformative if the earnings denominator is temporarily elevated. A drop in the apparent multiple does not by itself prove a discount-rate shock, investor irrationality or a forthcoming rerating.

Likewise, the approximately -6.57% return from the rounded reinvested index must not replace the approximately -9.17% price change inside a price/EPS identity. Adding dividends again to the issuer's total-return series would double-count them. Buyback effects already reflected in per-share earnings cannot also be added as an independent earnings-growth contribution without a coherent decomposition.

## 5. The data required to advance from retrospective explanation to a testable opportunity model

A future evaluation must first state which outcome it aims to predict. Five plausible outcomes must remain separate:

- an operating inflection, such as volume stabilization or retained margin improvement;
- a same-period earnings revision or guidance change;
- a change in a defined contemporaneous valuation measure;
- absolute or benchmark-relative security return;
- an entry/timing outcome governed by the existing market and trading owners.

Evidence that predicts one does not automatically predict the others. An event study of immediate announcement returns is not the same experiment as a six-month earnings revision study or a short-horizon entry study.

### 5.1 Minimum admissible observation

Reuse native identifiers and records. Each observation needs an issuer and security identity, corporate perimeter, source publication/availability time, effective/fiscal period, metric definition and adjustment version, currency/unit, and any correction relation. The market side needs exchange/calendar, return definition, corporate-action treatment, endpoint timestamps and benchmark identity.

Expectations require the actual prior vintage for the same fiscal period. A present-day estimate labeled historical is not enough. A rolling forecast horizon must be separated from true revisions. Where no licensed consensus exists, compare to a dated management range and label it a management-reference result rather than a consensus beat.

An annual source that becomes available after the proposed entry time is allowed as later outcome evidence, not as an input feature. The as-known history and a retrospectively comparable history serve different questions and should both remain inspectable.

### 5.2 Predeclared evaluation sequence

First assemble a small source-qualified event panel covering the actual proposed mechanisms and counterexamples. Specify inclusion criteria before examining returns. Include acquired/delisted and unsuccessful businesses where relevant. A pure winners list or surviving current basket is not an acceptable historical universe.

Then freeze a transparent baseline: existing native market/financial information, a small number of interpretable economic observations, declared missingness, one primary horizon and benchmark, plus limited predeclared secondary analyses. Unknown contract terms or brand allocations cannot become numerical features through unreviewed model invention.

Next evaluate incremental information with temporal holdouts and issuer/source clustering. Treat overlapping annual/quarterly events as dependent. Report coverage and error rates, not only hit rate on the cleanest subset. Compare the new information against existing earnings, momentum, theme, macro and attention families so duplicated evidence is not counted as a new edge.

Finally run prospective shadow observations under the existing evaluation owner, preserving the original hypothesis, source vintage, expiry and later outcome. Only a separately accepted validation result can change ranking, entry, sizing or other decision authority. The explanatory research view can deliver useful intelligence before such promotion; predictive claims cannot.

### 5.3 Named failure costs

A false positive is not just a wrong percentage. It may confuse inventory refill with demand, an accounting gain with margin repair, a reporting-currency move with category growth, or a lower multiple with an undervalued security. A false negative may discard a valid contract-profit improvement because pass-through reduced revenue. These failure families should guide the test corpus and error analysis.

Do not solve them with an uncalibrated all-purpose score. The first useful output is a set of separate, source-bound observations and a coherent explanation of how they relate. Confidence describes the evidence and model uncertainty, not permission to trade.

## 6. Proposed product behavior from these empirical controls

The shared dossier should render three explicitly selectable views where supported:

**As-known view:** information actually available at the chosen decision time, including the then-current forecast and adjustment definitions.

**Outcome view:** what subsequently happened to the declared business or market target over its frozen horizon.

**Retrospective comparable view:** later-restated or normalized observations used for explanation, clearly excluded from historical input features when unavailable at the time.

These are views over existing owners, not new evidence, valuation or memory stores. The interface should explain why a comparison is unavailable, offer the narrower supported interpretation and preserve the original question. For example, without contemporary prices and estimates, it can display a historical earnings-versus-return counterexample but must not invent a forward valuation surprise.

A valuation bridge should identify price versus total return, reported versus adjusted/normalized earnings, forecast horizon and vintage, and the treatment of currency, debt and shares. A relation between two displayed numbers is not necessarily a causal relation; explanatory copy must make that distinction.
