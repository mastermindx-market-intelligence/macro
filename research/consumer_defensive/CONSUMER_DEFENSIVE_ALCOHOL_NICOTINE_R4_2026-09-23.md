## 1. Principal result and user job

The Consumer Defensive label is not evidence that demand, profit or valuation is defensive. For alcohol and nicotine, the proposed economic explanation must distinguish consumer demand, the inventory held between producer and user, product and territorial commercial rights, net price retained after relevant deductions, production/investment economics, and shareholder cash.

The user should be able to answer: Is the apparent recovery final demand, a change in channel purchasing, a changed business perimeter, or profit repair? Is an emerging format creating incremental cash or replacing profitable legacy consumption? How much of the apparent growth is attributable to the security rather than to a wider licensed system? What evidence would distinguish these interpretations?

Eight issuer dossiers below provide source-backed anchors. The economic mechanisms and product requirements are Sol's research proposals, not issuer claims or statistically validated signals. Issuer publications are attributed disclosures, not independent audits. Monetary observations preserve original currencies, reporting periods and adjustments. None is a current buy/sell recommendation. No tobacco-health benefit is inferred from a company's category terminology.

## 2. Alcohol dossier anchors

### R4-C01 — Constellation: three demand boundaries and a changed business perimeter

**Disclosure.** The June 30 release for the quarter ended May 31, 2026 reports beer shipments +1.8%, depletions -0.3%, net sales $2,283.5m and segment operating income $891.4m. Wine/spirit reported sales fell 47%, while organic sales rose 8% after divestiture adjustments. Consolidated sales of $2,590.4m less excise of $157.7m produce net sales of $2,432.7m. [A01]

**Research implication.** A shipment recovery and a consumer recovery are not interchangeable. Depletions are a separately defined issuer metric, not automatically a universal household-consumption series. The observed growth-rate gap cannot establish the amount of distributor inventory without compatible beginning stocks and absolute flows. Beer represented a derived 93.87% of this quarter's net revenue, not that share of common-share value. The organic wine/spirit comparison also says nothing about the contribution of divested brands after exit.

**Rerating conditions and falsifiers.** Research sustained underlying demand with disciplined inventory, net realization and attractive incremental capacity returns. A recovery claim weakens when shipment growth lacks corresponding channel evidence or new capacity consumes cash without adequate utilization. Territory-specific rights must be verified before connecting the same brand across issuers; no worldwide ownership inference is made here.

### R4-C02 — Diageo: profit repair is not the same as sales recovery

**Disclosure.** FY2026 ended June 30: organic net sales -2.0%, volume -0.4%, price/mix -1.6%; organic operating profit +2.0%. The source reports free cash flow of $3.211bn and identifies geographic weakness and exceptional accounting charges separately. [A02]

**Research implication.** Preserve demand, recurring profit, reported profit and cash as different observations. A cost program may protect earnings while the commercial proposition remains weak. Without a counterfactual, a favorable profit bridge does not prove that marketing reductions or lower investment are harmless.

**Rerating conditions and falsifiers.** Test whether demand stabilizes without sacrificing necessary brand support, whether savings reach cash after restructuring, and whether leverage falls through recurring cash rather than disposal alone. The opposing case is continued adverse mix or a recovery dependent on temporarily suppressed spending. A broad group average must not conceal regions or categories moving in opposite directions.

### R4-C03 — Pernod Ricard: strategic inventory and near-term cash have different horizons

**Disclosure.** FY2026 organic sales declined 3.9%; H1 -5.9% and H2 -1.3%. Organic profit from recurring operations declined 5.2%. The release attributes free cash flow of EUR1.197bn partly to receivables/finished-goods changes and lower investment; its defined cash-conversion ratio includes strategic inventories and cash capex. [A03]

**Research implication.** Slower deterioration is not positive growth. A cash improvement can coexist with weaker earnings. Strategic aging inventory is an investment in a future product cohort, not simply unwanted finished goods. Lower spending may be appropriate, but its future supply and brand consequences must be modeled before annualizing the cash benefit.

**Rerating conditions and falsifiers.** Research the path from inventory normalization to repeat demand, then from demand to retained profit and debt reduction. Refute premature recovery when replenishment outpaces end-market evidence or inventory restraint merely defers a capacity/aging requirement. Regional comparisons need their own fiscal windows and product scopes.

### R4-C04 — Remy Cointreau: concentrated category economics and time-to-cash

**Disclosure.** The July 29 Q1 FY2026-27 release reports EUR223.2m sales, +1.3% organic: Cognac EUR141.9m, Liqueurs & Spirits EUR79.6m, Partner Brands EUR1.7m. Cognac grew 7.7% organically while Liqueurs & Spirits declined 6.6%. The June 4 FY2025-26 release reports EUR935.3m sales and EUR165.4m current operating profit, a 17.7% margin, with organic profit down 11.5%. [A04,A05]

**Research implication.** Cognac is a derived 63.58% of the reviewed quarter's sales, not an estimate for every future period or the issuer's profit purity. A new quarter's category growth does not by itself repair the previous year's margin or investment burden. Restocking, mix and final consumption need separate evidence.

**Rerating conditions and falsifiers.** Test recovery in a defined market/category cohort, economic conversion of aging stock, productive reinvestment and normalized cash after finance costs. Refute a simple volume thesis if price/mix and conversion costs overwhelm it. Do not turn an aging inventory balance into a mark-to-market reserve of future retail revenue.

### R4-C05 — Heineken: licensed volume is not consolidated production or revenue

**Disclosure.** The August 5 H1 release reports total volume growth of 1.6%, consolidated volume +0.4%, licensed volume +23.2%, net revenue +2.7% organically and net revenue per hectolitre +2.3%. Reported operating profit growth and the issuer's BEIA/organic profit measures differ materially. [A06]

**Research implication.** A global brand may grow strongly where the issuer receives license/partner economics rather than the full retail or manufacturing value chain. Total brand volume cannot be divided into consolidated revenue to produce an economically clean unit yield. BEIA means the issuer's own adjustment convention, not a standardized cross-company cash measure.

**Rerating conditions and falsifiers.** Separate own-produced, licensed and third-party activity; ask whether net monetization and capital intensity improve. A low/no-alcohol format may expand occasions, replace an existing product, or both; growth from a small base is insufficient to estimate profit materiality. Group, holding-company and listed-security claims also need their native identity boundaries.

## 3. Nicotine dossier anchors

This section concerns financial and commercial analysis, not recommendations to use tobacco. The words smoke-free, smokeless, modern oral and New Categories retain their issuer-specific business definitions. They do not independently establish safety, equivalent consumption, equivalent margins or equal regulatory rights.

### R4-C06 — Philip Morris International: shipments, in-market observations and organic profit

**Disclosure.** PMI's July 22 Q2 release reports net revenue +7.6% organically, smoke-free shipments +7.5%, and heat-not-burn shipments +7.6% versus adjusted in-market sales +5.1%. Reported EPS declined 7.7% while adjusted EPS rose 15.2%. The release identifies an investment impairment and describes pantry-loading/price timing in certain markets. [A07]

**Research implication.** New-format adoption, initial launch supply and household inventory must remain distinct. A shipment-to-in-market growth gap is descriptive, not proof of either improper loading or persistent adoption. Hardware placement, active usage and repeat consumable demand require separate evidence and units. Do not use the mature group's margin as the assumed margin of a new market launch.

**Rerating conditions and falsifiers.** Research contribution after device support, distribution, promotion and capacity costs, and test whether new-format cash offsets displaced legacy cash. Existing-user retention and incremental users must not be counted twice. A company-reported user estimate is not a measured subscriber ledger. Regulatory events need exact product and jurisdiction scope rather than a company-wide green flag.

### R4-C07 — BAT: New Categories and Smokeless are not synonyms

**Disclosure.** BAT's July 30 H1 release reports GBP12,235m group revenue and GBP1,928m New Categories revenue. New Categories contribution margin is 13.8% at the stated constant-currency basis; Smokeless is 19.8% of group revenue, compared with full-year 2025. Cigarette-share measurement was separated from heated products from January 2026. [A08]

**Research implication.** The derived New Categories/group revenue ratio is 15.76%, not 19.8%. This is a classification/denominator distinction, not a source inconsistency. A half-year share comparison to the previous full year is not matched H1 year-over-year growth. Historical share comparisons also require the old and new definitions.

**Rerating conditions and falsifiers.** Model contribution retained after commercial investment and legacy decline, with a separate cost-of-transition bridge. Faster new-category revenue growth may be immaterial, dilutive or valuable depending on its absolute contribution, replacement and capital requirements. Unknown overlap among users stays unknown.

### R4-C08 — Altria: net-of-excise economics and mixed oral exposure

**Disclosure.** The Q2 2026 release reports group net revenues $6,111m versus $5,356m after excise. Smokeable segment revenues after excise are $4,660m with adjusted operating companies income $3,018m, yielding the disclosed 64.8% adjusted margin. The oral segment includes more than one format and reports a different profitability trajectory. Adjusted EPS growth also reflects fewer shares. [A09]

**Research implication.** The margin denominator matters: dividing segment income by pre-excise revenue changes its meaning. Oral-segment performance must not be assigned wholesale to nicotine pouches. A profitable shrinking legacy activity and a growing replacement activity can coexist; their common-share effects need a combined cash bridge.

**Rerating conditions and falsifiers.** Test retained net pricing versus volume/promotion mix, the duration of legacy cash, and the cost of new-format competition. Do not add buyback benefits again after they are already in per-share earnings. Litigation provisions, actual payments and cash availability stay distinct and dated.

## 4. Authorization is an event type, not a demand observation

FDA's June 30, 2026 ZYN communication distinguishes modified-risk marketing orders from the products' earlier January 2025 marketing authorization. FDA's April 17, 2026 IQOS communication concerns renewal for specified products and exposure-related claims. These are different product-scoped events, not new manufacturing revenue or blanket approval of every brand variant. FDA also explicitly distinguishes tobacco marketing authorization from a finding that a product is safe or FDA-approved. [A10,A11,A12]

The proposed event record should retain regulator, product/variant identifiers, territory, action type, application/order identifier, publication/effective/expiry clocks where stated, permitted claims, conditions and supersession. Pending application, authorization, commercial launch, availability, repeat consumption and retained profit are separate observations. ## 5. Research models that connect demand to value

### 5.1 Two inventories between production and final use

A same-unit inventory identity can describe distributor stock as beginning stock plus producer deliveries minus outflow, adjusted for returns and losses. A second identity can describe retail/consumer inventory, if measured. The two must not be collapsed. Growth percentages for shipments and depletions cannot recover absolute inventory without consistent levels and scope.

Useful hypotheses include: a producer reduces deliveries while final demand stabilizes; shipment growth follows an inventory drawdown without stronger final demand; promotional purchases pull demand forward; a reporting perimeter change creates apparent growth. Each can produce a different price response depending on prior expectations. None is automatically a trade signal.

The proposed reading needs beginning/end inventory where disclosed, quantity basis, calendar comparability, abnormal events, and an explicit uncertainty state. A manager's attributed inventory explanation can be displayed as such without claiming independent measurement.

### 5.2 Aging stock: production cohort, demand cohort and financing clock

For a proposed spirits model, record production vintage, required/target maturation, expected losses, blending/use restrictions, carrying cost, committed purchases and expected release window when sourced. Brand, grade and geography matter. A volume projection should be constrained by available qualified inventory only when those restrictions are evidenced.

Three deliberately distinct scenarios should be evaluated: sustained premium demand with productive inventory release; weaker demand leaving older stocks or requiring discounting; and underinvestment improving current cash but creating a future product constraint. There is no universal good/bad inventory ratio. Balance-sheet cost is not projected retail selling value, and not every old unit can substitute for every product grade.

The first implementation should use unweighted evidence and explicit scenarios where composition is undisclosed. No guessed age curve, future price or depletion rate becomes a fact.

### 5.3 New-format conversion economics

The proposed economic bridge is active retained customers times repeat consumption times issuer net realization, less consumable production, distribution, device/launch support, ongoing product costs and required investment. It is an accounting/research structure, not an estimated company model.

Separate existing users switching formats, users switching brands, entrants and discontinued users where evidence permits. Cans, pouches, devices, sticks and liquid units are different. Use a publisher's standardized conversion only for its declared analytical purpose, never as proof of equivalent nicotine intake, demand, revenue or profit.

A synthetic one-period contribution change illustrates the issue: new-format contribution +12 less displaced legacy contribution 8 less incremental support 5 gives -1 before financing/tax/capital changes. Counting all 12 as incremental profit would miss the commercial transition cost. The opposite outcome is possible with stronger retention or lower support costs; parameters are scenarios, not facts.

### 5.4 Discount rates and duration, not a guaranteed defensive premium

A model should distinguish recurring business growth, transition investment, temporary inventory release, financial leverage and the discount rate applied to a specific shareholder claim. A brand can have a strong franchise while its stock embeds demanding expectations. A cash generator may support distributions even when its physical category declines, but the terminal value depends on the decline path and replacement economics.

Research should not assign one multiple to mature combustibles, early new-format launches, aging-stock spirits and licensed brewing. For each, identify the financial anchor, whether it is before/after debt and tax, required reinvestment, source date and scenario horizon. No current fair value, discount-rate estimate, consensus surprise or stock-price forecast was calculated in R4A.
