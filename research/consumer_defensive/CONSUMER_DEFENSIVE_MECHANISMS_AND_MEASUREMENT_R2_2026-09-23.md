## 1. The intelligence job now made concrete

A Consumer Defensive dossier should answer five linked but distinct questions: What changed in consumption or commercial execution? Which business actually earns the resulting money? How much reaches sustainable earnings and cash for the relevant shareholders? Compared with which previously known expectation? What valuation and market evidence confirms or conflicts with the thesis?

R1 defined those dimensions. The R2 census shows why the system must preserve commercial rights, reporting perimeter, unit definitions and source dates before it can synthesize them. The selected implementation direction remains reuse of the shared dossier and native owners, with a sector-specific explanatory layer. Three approaches were considered:

1. A generic positive-growth score is easy but rejects the economic distinctions demonstrated in R2; do not pursue it.
2. A fully quantified causal model for every brand would require unavailable private data and unsupported precision at birth; do not fabricate it.
3. A progressive, source-bound mechanism view, with explicit unknowns and narrow validated derived measures, supports a useful real workflow now and an empirical learning path later. This is the research recommendation, subject to accepted written design and owner integration.

The end state remains much larger than a comparison table. It should connect consumer occasions, categories, commercial rights, businesses, leading indicators, expectations, valuation and the existing stock workflow. The first slice must demonstrate an accurate explanation across deliberately different economic cases, not merely expose schema fields.

## 2. Commercial exposure is a scoped relation, not a label

### 2.1 Separate at least five questions

**Business ownership:** which entity owns or controls the relevant business? **Brand/IP rights:** what product and geography may it monetize? **Route to market:** who manufactures, ships, distributes and retails it? **Accounting:** where and when are revenue and expenses consolidated? **Security:** which instrument has the relevant residual economic claim?

R2-C15/S18 is a discriminating license example: packaged-coffee rights do not include the restaurant business. R2-C02/S02 supplies a territory-limited acquisition. R2-C05/S06 shows consolidation, segment transfer and common-share attribution. These are source examples, not new graph edges. The existing identity and relationship owners must establish actual bindings before a consumer joins current market prices.

### 2.2 Dates must not collapse

Preserve announcement, legal close, commercial effective date, accounting consolidation, reporting reclassification, source publication, first retained observation and security-action date where applicable. Some will be unknown or inapplicable. They are different clocks, not successive steps of a new lifecycle service.

A later restated segment table supports comparable historical reporting, but not a claim that the restatement was known earlier. The historical query therefore needs an explicit view: as known at the decision cutoff, or latest corrected/comparable view. Both should retain their provenance. The existing point-in-time/correction owner controls implementation.

### 2.3 Financial exposure rules

A ratio requires an aligned numerator and denominator: entity, consolidation scope, period, currency, financial line and accounting basis. Source rounded inputs retain their precision. An undisclosed brand share is not allocated evenly among the disclosed brand names. Segment operating profit divided by consolidated net profit is not a valid earnings-purity estimate. Unallocated corporate costs, financing, tax, preferred claims and noncontrolling interests must remain visible.

The ten ratios in the companion census are descriptive examples. They are not a cross-sectional ranking and cannot become live basket weights. Overlapping theme facets must not be added as if they were disjoint revenue pools.

## 3. Distribution quality: from availability to retained demand

The proposed analytical chain is:

`commercial authorization -> stocked availability -> in-stock execution -> comparable-outlet velocity -> repeat purchase -> contribution and cash`

This is not a new runtime state machine. Each phrase denotes a separate research question and evidence requirement. A product can be authorized but absent from the shelf, stocked but unavailable during peak demand, trialed but not repurchased, or popular but unprofitable after promotional and delivery costs.

### 3.1 Measures cannot substitute for one another

Store count measures breadth; distribution points may also reflect SKU breadth. Weighted distribution depends on its specified denominator. Revenue per distribution point is affected by pruning and channel composition. Same-outlet velocity requires a stable outlet/product cohort. Repeat purchase requires a defined consumer cohort and enough elapsed time for another purchase. Panel coverage and company estimates must be separately described.

R2-C02, C08 and C09 identify concrete reasons for these distinctions. None authorizes access to a proprietary household panel. Public management statements can support attributed observations while the stronger underlying data remain unavailable.

### 3.2 Cohort analysis design

Where entitled data exist, fix the outlet cohort before evaluating velocity. Separate existing locations, newly added locations, removed locations and assortment changes. Compare consistent calendar windows, products, pack units, promotions and in-stock conditions. Report both all-outlet growth and stable-cohort growth; neither is a substitute for the other.

Removing low-productivity outlets can raise average velocity without improving any remaining outlet. Conversely, adding immature outlets can lower average velocity while creating future profitable growth. A cohort model should distinguish these arithmetic composition effects before proposing an economic interpretation. Historical outlet rosters are required to claim point-in-time evaluation.

### 3.3 Promotion economics

The proposed counterfactual question is whether a promotion generates incremental contribution after discounts, trade support, retailer fees, logistics, cannibalization, returns and later purchase displacement. The sales lift alone cannot answer it. Baseline demand is estimated, not observed; report uncertainty and sensitivity to the baseline definition.

Useful comparisons include matched non-promoted outlets or products, predeclared windows before and after the promotion, and independent repeat behavior where available. Major concurrent distribution or assortment changes can invalidate a simple comparison. Do not estimate causal elasticity by dividing aggregate volume growth by aggregate price/mix in one quarter: mix, market growth, competitor actions and channel inventory are not held fixed.

## 4. Profit transmission: preserve what the business keeps

### 4.1 Revenue realization

Observed retail spending, gross invoice revenue and issuer net sales are distinct layers. Trade deductions, rebates and contract terms belong to the issuer's actual presentation. A route-to-market change can alter reported realization without an equivalent change in end-consumer demand. Do not universally reclassify a distribution cost as a deduction from revenue; preserve the accounting source and use explicit analytical adjustments only when justified.

### 4.2 Input costs and production

A proposed cost map links physical input, contractual buying exposure, hedging, inventory, conversion, output realization and cash settlement. Commodity spot prices are context, not recognized cost of sales. An input supplier and a branded consumer-product business may have opposite exposures to the same price movement; R2-C22 supplies a real business-role example.

No universal procurement lag, hedge ratio or cost-share coefficient is assumed. Begin with source-observed cost effects and company-disclosed policy. If a sensitivity is estimated later, identify the dataset, vintage, economic scope and uncertainty. Keep correlated commodities and shared production assets from being counted as independent evidence.

### 4.3 Reinvestment and cash

Gross-profit improvement can fund advertising, distribution or product investment. Current earnings and future franchise value can therefore move differently. Treat the spending as observed and its future return as a hypothesis. Likewise, cutting support can temporarily improve reported margins without proving sustainable value creation.

For factories and distribution-heavy formats, separate maintenance from expansion investment only when supported. Free cash flow based on total capital expenditure remains observable even when that split is unknown. A new facility does not establish qualified output, utilization, profitable demand or capital returns. An asset disposal is not recurring consumer cash. R2-C13, C17 and C19 are discriminating examples for these boundaries.

These are research hypotheses with required observations and falsifiers, not calibrated predictive factors. Multiple mechanisms can coexist without creating a fused score.

| M01 | Category adoption | Repeat demand and penetration on a defined population | Trial-only activity, novelty or sample changes |
| M02 | Durable share capture | Comparable company/category units and retained contribution | Purchased share through uneconomic promotions |
| M03 | Productive distribution | New outlets mature into stable-cohort velocity and cash | Shelf fill without sustained consumption |
| M04 | Pricing durability | Realization retained with tolerable demand/share effects | Discount reversal, pack changes masking unit loss |
| M05 | Premium mix | Supported shifts to profitable products or channels | Currency or acquired perimeter mistaken for premium demand |
| M06 | Value-format advantage | Trips/retention or branded demand improves with viable margins | Low-price volume destroys contribution or increases service costs |
| M07 | Retained cost relief | Recognized cost savings reach operating cash | Hedge/inventory timing, promotion or reinvestment absorbs savings |
| M08 | Processing spread recovery | Output-input economics and utilization improve together | Output price declines exceed input relief |
| M09 | Profitable scale | Incremental gross profit exceeds incremental operating/capital burden | Logistics, spoilage or selling costs scale faster |
| M10 | Product/portfolio pruning | Removed low-quality activity improves durable cash returns | Lost distribution or weakened franchise obscured by a label |
| M11 | Brand reinvestment | Spend precedes independently observed retention/share and returns | Spend rises without measurable demand durability |
| M12 | Business-format transition | New-format contribution offsets legacy decline and investment | Cannibalization, rights limits or poor unit economics |
| M13 | Expectations repair | Actual or revised outlook improves against a prior same-period reference | Forecast roll, reduced disclosure or one-time benefit |
| M14 | Capital allocation improvement | Attributable cash per share improves after funding and price paid | Leverage, dilution or integration costs consume the benefit |

| M16 | Corporate perimeter simplification | Effective scope, stranded costs and shareholder claims are reconciled | Announced transactions treated as completed value creation |

Absence of confirmation is not automatically a negative signal; insufficient coverage is a separate state under existing owner vocabulary.

## 8. Valuation: translate economic improvement into an explicit scenario

### 8.1 Separate identity from inference

For positive, consistently defined earnings, price equals earnings per share times the corresponding multiple by definition. A change in earnings and a change in the multiple can offset. This arithmetic decomposition does not identify why either changed and is not a causal model.

Forward multiples require a fixed earnings horizon for revision analysis. A rolling next-twelve-month denominator changes as time passes even if each underlying fiscal estimate is unchanged. Preserve same-period revisions separately from horizon roll. Negative or near-zero denominators, inconsistent accounting bases, split changes and currency mismatches require refusal or a different valuation anchor.

### 8.2 Proposed anchors by economics

For an established brand, analyze normalized common-share cash and the reinvestment required to sustain it. For a distribution-led challenger, analyze mature-cohort contribution, expansion investment and the duration of profitable demand. For a processing business, analyze cycle-normalized spreads, assets and liquidity rather than apply a peak-margin multiple. For a diversified issuer, connect business cash flows with group costs and shareholder claims; a revenue sum is not an equity-value sum.

A reverse scenario can ask which combinations of growth duration, incremental margin, capital requirements and discount rate reconcile with an observed market value. Those are conditional assumptions, not implied certainty. R2 has not loaded current prices or a licensed consensus surface and therefore produces no current fair-value numbers.

### 8.3 Simplified reinvestment thought experiment

Under an explicitly simplified stable-growth firm model, reinvestment as a share of after-tax operating profit is growth divided by return on invested capital, so cash available before financing is operating profit times one minus that ratio. This is a modeling identity under restrictive steady-state assumptions, not a claim that actual brand advertising can be capitalized or that book ROIC is the correct incremental return.

The lesson for the research is directional: additional growth can require enough investment to leave little additional distributable cash. A high-growth brand may deserve a lower valuation than a slower one if its required capital, acquisition costs or durability differ. The model must not fit unknown exposure percentages or discount rates merely to generate a precise target.

### 8.4 What a rerating claim requires

Define the valuation denominator and observation dates first. Separate actual earnings growth, forecast revisions, horizon roll, corporate actions, multiple change and total return including distributions. Use the existing prices/corporate-actions and valuation owners; no new daily valuation store is proposed. A positive headline followed by a positive stock move is an event association, not proof of a causal mechanism or repeatable edge.

## 9. Historical and prospective evaluation design

The desired learning outcome is whether source-bound economic changes add information beyond existing technical, market, theme and financial evidence. Do not train a model merely to reproduce current composite scores or assign sector membership a positive bonus.

### 9.1 Questions worth predeclaring

| Study | Question | Essential negative/control case |
|---|---|---|
| E01 | Does within-cohort distribution productivity precede profitable growth? | New-door fill with weak repeat demand |
| E02 | Does retained cost relief add information beyond commodity prices? | Relief offset by promotion or weaker output realization |
| E03 | Does demand recovery differ from price-led revenue growth? | Combined volume/mix cannot resolve physical demand |
| E04 | Does guidance-relative repair matter while year-over-year growth remains negative? | Lowered guidance followed by continued deterioration |
| E05 | Does brand reinvestment predict later profitable demand? | Spending without retention or cash improvement |
| E06 | Does perimeter-corrected growth improve interpretation? | Acquisitions, recasts or licensing changes drive the headline |
| E07 | Does a theme's common input have opposite effects across roles? | Contracts, hedges and downstream pricing neutralize the exposure |
| E08 | Does valuation context distinguish good business news from attractive equity outcomes? | Strong operating numbers at already demanding expectations |

These are proposed studies, not eight successful backtests. Predeclare sampling, horizons, comparators, missingness, source availability and stopping criteria before seeing outcomes. Separate event-level conclusions from company/year inference; repeated releases from one issuer are not independent samples.

### 9.2 Minimum research dataset

Each event needs the native issuer/security binding, target period, source publication/availability clock, metric definition and vintage, applicable corporate perimeter, source family, uncertainty and prior comparable observation. Market outcomes require pre-event and post-event prices/corporate actions, benchmark definition, trading-calendar alignment and a stated horizon. Expectations require the actual prior snapshot, not reconstructed current consensus.

Use historical membership and delisted/acquired companies where relevant to avoid survivors-only conclusions. Exclude future-revised exposures from as-known features. A same-day source without a verified release time cannot automatically be traded at that day's open. Handle after-hours events and holidays explicitly through existing calendar owners.

### 9.3 Modeling sequence

Begin with transparent descriptive tables and counterexamples, then predeclared simple baselines. Where data support estimation, use out-of-time validation and company/source-family grouping to prevent leakage. Compare incremental value over existing features and conduct ablation of duplicated evidence families. Sparse brand-level observations may justify partial pooling for a model's estimated relationship, but never fabricated brand revenue shares or fictional source facts.

Assess directional calibration, error costs, coverage, false discovery and stability across categories/regions. Prospective shadow observations must preserve the original prediction/hypothesis, expiry, later outcome and corrections. A good retrospective explanation is not sufficient for promotion. Any eventual ranking, entry, sizing or trade effect remains an explicit existing-owner acceptance decision.

## 11. Product implication: decision-ready explanations without decision authority

The shared Consumer Defensive dossier should let a reader move from a theme to the business actually exposed, inspect a dated financial bridge, understand conflicting interpretations and return to the existing company/market workflow. Required explanation blocks are: change, affected commercial scope, earnings/cash transmission, prior expectation, valuation/market context, alternative explanation and next discriminating evidence.

The interface should make differences usable. A price/volume matrix must mark mixed fields as mixed. A brand tree must show unknown allocations without equally sized slices implying equal economics. A relationship map should distinguish ownership, license, distribution and source-attributed transmission hypotheses. A comparison table should disable invalid joins rather than silently normalize them. No weighted Sankey without compatible quantities.

Four first-vertical demonstration cases are recommended for the written design: Celsius for brand/distribution differences, Simply Good Foods for expectations versus trend, Hill's for segment/private-label scope, and Clorox for timing/perimeter distortion. The prior R1 candidate examples remain useful; this is a proposed discriminating test set, not an accepted final scope or competing template commission.

The machine must produce a concise synthesis, not only a source drawer. But explanatory language must follow the evidentiary state: observed disclosure, management attribution, derived arithmetic or house hypothesis. A contradiction may be a period, scope, freshness or authority split rather than a data error. Reuse the existing typed conflict and authority grammar instead of defining another classification service.
