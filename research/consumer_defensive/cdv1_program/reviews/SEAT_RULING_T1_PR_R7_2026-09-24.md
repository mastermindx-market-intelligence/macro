Rulings R49–R54 bind round 10 on top of R1–R48. A repair lane may not edit any of the six frozen probe files.

- **R49 (the parser owns the grid).** `colspan` and `rowspan` are facts of the release parser, not of a regex over the markup: `engine/fundamental_forensics/disclosure_diff.py` reads both attributes on every `td`/`th` it emits (an attribute other than `colspan`/`rowspan`, a value outside 1..64, or garbage means 1) and reads the table `<caption>` as the table's own text. `_grid` places cells by the HTML occupancy algorithm — a spanning cell fills every position its colspan covers, a rowspan carries the cell into the rows below, rows are padded to the grid width — from those attributes alone; `_COLSPAN` and the source-window re-read are removed (r43a–d). A header stacked over several band rows and spanning several columns carries the origin tuple of its stacked cells, so the single-literal rule applies to the joined form exactly as to a one-row band; the other spanned cells must be empty or a bare `$` — a sign fragment or a second literal is ambiguous and absent (r43e–g). R43's whole-table refusal and R37's band depth stand.
- **R50 (period vocabulary is fail-closed).** The vocabulary reads digits and words alike ("3 / 6 / 9 / 12 months"), "ending" beside "ended", "YTD", "FY26"/"fiscal 2026", `Q4 FY26`, and dates in either order with or without a comma, parsed through a month-name map (an unrecognised month is a foreign date, never a scope match). Beyond the vocabulary the band is fail-closed: a value column whose stacked band text carries a period marker (`ended`, `ending`, `month(s)`, `ytd`, `fiscal`, `quarter`, `fy<digit>`) that resolves to no known form is "unknown", and unknown refuses the table exactly as annual does (r44a). A `<caption>` is a band label (r44c). A paragraph that is nothing but a period label — a bold "Nine Months Ended …" line — governs the tables and statements after it exactly as a pure-period heading does, and "Three-Month Period Ended <date>" is a pure-period form (r44b, r44d); the round-9 commit message's claim that the pure-period regex already recognised it was false and is corrected here.
- **R51 (row sections).** Below the band, a row carrying only a label in its first cell that names a period opens a section governing the rows after it until the next such label; a row under an annual, foreign or unknown section is never addressed, never cross-checked and never replayed (r43h).
- **R52 (the validator replays the cross-check and the receipt position).** A present total-volume observation validates only when `pg_volume_cross_check` — the extractor's own `_volume_cross_check` over the same scan — finds no conflicting same-period statement of Total P&G volume (r46a). For every present cell and every bounded-text paragraph the stored byte span must equal the span `expected_receipt_span` re-mints from the cell's or paragraph's visible text — the extractor's own masked-markup, unique search — so a receipt on an attribute copy, a comment or a second occurrence inside the cell never validates (r46b, gap g4). `replay_table_layout` enforces the same equality.
- **R53 (statements).** "volume" and "volumes" are one subject (r47a); the R47 qualifier, verb and percentage rules stand.
- **R54 (uniqueness before length; forward-looking headings; six frozen suites).** `_reconciliation_paragraph` judges uniqueness over every candidate paragraph and only then holds the one candidate to `PG_BOUNDED_TEXT_MAX` — a second, over-long paragraph is a conflict, not an invisible one (disposition (a) of round 6 stays open). A governing heading naming outlook, guidance, forecast, expectation, projection, target or estimate never admits a table, by keyword or by quarterly form (round-6 disposition (c)).
