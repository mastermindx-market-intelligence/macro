## 2. What the economic identity must represent

A useful exposure is not just issuer plus brand name. Proposed research granularity is:

`issuer / business / brand or product / commercial right / territory / channel / period / accounting perimeter`

This is an analytical description, not a new global identity registry. Keep source-native labels until the existing identity owner validates stronger joins. A license for packaged coffee does not imply ownership of restaurants. An acquired brand in one territory does not create worldwide rights. A distributor's revenue and the brand owner's revenue are different transactions. Revenue shares, profit shares, physical units, market shares and portfolio weights have different denominators.

Do not force brand precision where the source only discloses a segment. A conglomerate can be represented as a well-measured segment plus unknown brand allocation. The uncertainty is more useful than a fabricated purity estimate. Current snapshot calculations below are neither stable annual exposures nor point-in-time historical memberships.

## 3. Beverages: five issuer systems

### R2-C01 — Coca-Cola: monetization of a wider beverage system

**Source-established scope.** The 2025 Form 10-K distinguishes concentrate operations from finished products. Concentrate revenue was $28,462 of $47,941 total revenue; the filing rounds revenue shares to 59% versus 41%, while the corresponding worldwide unit-case shares are 85% versus 15%. Bottling partners and consolidated operations have different economic boundaries. [S01]

**Research interpretation.** Model system consumption, concentrate monetization and finished-product execution separately. A larger case footprint need not mean an equally large issuer revenue or profit share. The useful comparison is within the relevant business model, not a sales-per-case contest against a bottler.

**Indicators and falsifier.** Track comparable system volume, shipment timing, contract realization, package/channel/geographic mix and retained cash. A thesis based on consumption growth weakens when system demand fails to translate into attributable profit after mix and commercial terms. Brand-level financial allocation remains undisclosed in this record. The valuation anchor proposed for research is normalized attributable cash generation, not total retail system sales.

### R2-C02 — Celsius: separate brand demand from portfolio growth

**Source-established scope.** Q2 2026 revenue was $817.9. Alani Nu supplied $364.4 and Rockstar $66.5; CELSIUS-brand revenue declined 11.7%. The release explains greater distributor mix and associated deductions after the Pepsi distribution transition. It separately reports Circana retail observations and sequential distribution productivity. Rockstar rights acquired in 2025 were for the United States and Canada. [S02]

**Research interpretation.** Treat brand expansion, acquired perimeter, route-to-market changes and within-brand demand as separate mechanisms. A portfolio total can conceal a shrinking incumbent brand. Distribution points and revenue per point must be compared over consistent periods; pruning weaker points can mechanically improve the denominator-based measure.

**Indicators and falsifier.** Compare on-shelf availability, same-outlet velocity, repeat demand, net receipts after deductions, contribution costs and channel inventory. The attractive-sounding distribution thesis fails when new placements do not retain profitable consumer demand. Alani's approximately 44.55% quarterly revenue share is a disclosed-period ratio, not an independent stock or pure-play basket weight. No global Rockstar ownership is inferred.

### R2-C03 — Monster: physical equivalents, geography and realization

**Source-established scope.** Q2 2026 energy-drink volume was 304.944 million 192-ounce equivalent cases versus 249.336 million. Average net revenue per case was $8.20 versus $8.29; this metric excludes Alcohol and Other segments. The release separates Monster Energy Drinks, Strategic Brands, Alcohol and Other, and reports approximately 46% of sales to customers outside the United States. [S03]

**Research interpretation.** A volume-led business can still have lower average realization through geographic or product mix. That observation is not automatically a like-for-like price cut. Alcohol must not contaminate the denominator of an energy-only unit calculation.

**Indicators and falsifier.** Track equivalent units, comparable geography/channel realization, distribution expense, promotional and brand investment, and cash conversion. The hypothesis of profitable international scale weakens if fulfillment or selling costs rise faster than retained gross profit. Compare normalized earnings or cash only after defining reinvestment needs; do not treat every extra case as the same-margin case.

### R2-C04 — PepsiCo: a portfolio of occasions and commercial models

**Source-established scope.** Q2 2026 reported sales grew 6.4%, including 2.4% organic growth, 2.2 percentage points from currency and 1.8 points from net acquisitions. The period was twelve weeks ended June 13. PepsiCo separately announced completion of the poppi acquisition on May 19, 2025. The business combines food and beverage operations rather than a single beverage exposure. [S04, S05]

**Research interpretation.** Link an emerging drink format to its actual business contribution, not the parent's entire sales base. Likewise, price investment in a food category can be a deliberate share-repair strategy without proving future recovery. Functional positioning is a commercial category label, not an independently established health benefit.

**Indicators and falsifier.** Observe food and beverage units, within-channel availability, promotion, contribution after marketing, and the price/volume trade-off. Separate corporate acquisitions from organic repeat demand. The research valuation must reconcile business-specific cash flows and group costs rather than award a multiple premium merely for owning an emerging brand.

### R2-C05 — Keurig Dr Pepper: consolidation and attributable earnings

**Source-established scope.** Q2 2026 revenue of $7,309 included JDE Peet's consolidated from April 1. The company describes legacy KDP constant-currency growth of 7.3% excluding JDE. It also identifies a Peet's pod reporting transfer. Reported net income of $210 becomes $60 attributable to common shareholders after noncontrolling interests and preferred dividends. The announced separation was planned for early 2027, not completed in this release. [S06]

**Research interpretation.** A brand relationship needs ownership, reporting and security clocks. Reclassification can change a segment comparison even without changing consumer demand. EBITDA before financing does not establish per-common-share economics.

**Indicators and falsifier.** Reconcile acquired versus legacy sales, product transfers, integration costs, financing, preferred claims, common shares and retained cash. A separation thesis is still a scenario until its legal and financial perimeter is effective. An acquisition-led increase in sales cannot by itself substantiate an organic-growth rerating.

## 4. Snacks and nutrition: five contrasting companies

### R2-C06 — Hershey: confectionery and salty snacks require separate explanations

**Source-established scope.** Q2 2026 North America Confectionery sales were $2,173.6, North America Salty Snacks $387.8, and International $225.9, totaling $2,787.3. Salty-snack reported growth was 22.9% but organic growth 0.6%, with most of the difference attributed to LesserEvil. Its segment profit declined. [S07]

**Research interpretation.** A faster-growing acquired category does not imply that its organic demand or profit contribution accelerated equally. Cocoa exposure, salty-snack manufacturing, and acquired-brand integration need separate bridges. Segment sales shares of approximately 77.98% and 13.91% are not brand-level purity or attributable earnings shares.

**Indicators and falsifier.** Retain commodity-cost recognition, elasticity, promotions, capacity/service execution and cash investment at the relevant business level. Repeated price increases are not a moat demonstration unless demand and economics remain durable. A margin-recovery thesis fails when cost relief is consumed by weaker mix, promotions or reinvestment rather than retained cash.

### R2-C07 — Mondelez: recovery in volume/mix is not uniform earnings recovery

**Source-established scope.** Q2 2026 organic net revenue increased 2.2%, including 0.7 percentage points of volume/mix. Adjusted EPS declined 2.7% at constant currency while reported EPS increased sharply. The release preserves separate reported and adjusted reconciliations. [S08]

**Research interpretation.** Compare category demand and cost recovery on compatible accounting bases. Combined volume/mix is not pure physical quantity. Better volume/mix can coexist with cost pressure, geographic shifts or required investment; a positive organic number alone does not establish a change in earnings duration.

**Indicators and falsifier.** The next record expansion should bind named brands and categories to disclosed financial segments, then track promotion, input recognition and reinvestment. Until that is done, do not assign the group's full economics to chocolate or to biscuits. Use normalized earnings and cash scenarios rather than extrapolating the reported EPS jump.

### R2-C08 — BellRing: protein demand does not guarantee protected margins

**Source-established scope.** Fiscal Q3 2026 sales were $570.4, up 4.2%. Premier Protein sales grew 0.7%; Dymatize grew 26.7%, with pricing/mix contributing 20.7 points. Adjusted gross margin declined to 27.7% from 35.1%, including an excess-bottle inventory charge. The release's consumption estimates combine panel observations with estimates for untracked channels. [S09]

**Research interpretation.** Protein is an application spanning brands, formats and input positions, not a universal financial advantage. A large brand growth rate may contain substantial pricing/mix, while inventory and production choices materially affect profitability.

**Indicators and falsifier.** Track powder versus ready-to-drink mix, repeat purchase, distribution, ingredient and packaging costs, promotion and inventory obsolescence. Preserve panel-derived and management-estimated components separately. A premium-multiple hypothesis requires retained contribution and cash after growth investment, not merely protein-category exposure.

### R2-C09 — Simply Good Foods: expectations and underlying trend can disagree

**Source-established scope.** Fiscal Q3 2026 revenue was $357.0, versus $381.0 a year earlier. Quest sales grew 1.1%, Atkins declined 24.6%, and OWYN grew 3.6%. Adjusted EBITDA was $57.2 versus $73.9. In the preceding April release, management's Q3 outlook was $329-$338 revenue and $46-$50 adjusted EBITDA. [S10, S11]

**Research interpretation.** The result exceeded a previously published management range while remaining below the prior-year result. These are simultaneously valid observations. A guidance-relative improvement is not a consensus beat, and a reduced-expectations beat is not automatically an operating turnaround.

**Indicators and falsifier.** Keep each brand's shipments, retailer takeaway, new-door velocity and promotional cost. The later growth thesis needs genuine repeat demand and adequate gross profit, particularly where a brand's new distribution underperforms. The correct first valuation research question is which deterioration assumptions have changed, not whether an undifferentiated protein basket is strong.

### R2-C10 — Utz: pruning lower-quality revenue changes the interpretation

**Source-established scope.** Q2 2026 sales were $371.8. Organic growth of 1.4% combined favorable net price realization of 3.6% with volume/mix of -2.2%; the release separately explains a bonus-pack comparison effect. Branded Salty Snacks represented approximately 89% of sales. Adjusted EBITDA rose 14.4%. The release describes a pending take-private transaction and discontinued guidance. [S12]

**Research interpretation.** Lower volume/mix can coexist with improved business quality when unprofitable items are removed, but the attribution must be evidenced. The combined volume/mix measure is not pure physical volume. Do not relabel every contraction as a positive portfolio action. Pending corporate actions also change whether a current security belongs in a future study.

**Indicators and falsifier.** Compare branded and non-branded sales, same-product units when independently available, promotional periods, contribution and working capital. Preserve the transaction as pending at the source date; current closing, delisting or tradability was not independently certified in R2. Product and corporate-action owners must resolve that before security use.

## 5. Pet exposure: five different business perimeters

### R2-C11 — Nestle: global product exposure within a changing reporting taxonomy

**Source-established scope.** H1 2026 Petcare sales were CHF8,933 against group sales of CHF43,109. Petcare organic growth of 2.7% comprised RIG of 1.8% and pricing of 0.9%; its underlying trading operating margin was 21.8%. Nestle issued a March 2026 recast of 2025 segment/product information for the new reporting structure. [S13, S14]

**Research interpretation.** Petcare supplies a measurable product-level exposure of approximately 20.72% for this half year. RIG retains Nestle's own definition; it is not automatically physical units comparable with Freshpet's volume. The recast creates two legitimate historical views: as originally known and later comparable presentation.

**Indicators and falsifier.** Analyze species, format, geography, price tier, sell-out and inventory where disclosed. Reconcile unallocated group costs before attempting earnings attribution. A product profit divided by group profit can be misleading when corporate costs are outside product totals. Unknown Purina sub-brand economics remain unknown rather than assigned equal weights.

### R2-C12 — Colgate-Palmolive / Hill's: specialty pet plus a private-label exit

**Source-established scope.** Q2 2026 Hill's Pet Nutrition sales were $1,195 of group sales $5,361. Hill's organic growth was 2.1%, combining organic volume of -1.8% and pricing of +3.9%; Prime100 contributed to the acquired-volume difference. The company separately identifies lower private-label pet sales and its exit from that business. Named pet brands include Hill's Science Diet, Prescription Diet and Prime100. [S15]

**Research interpretation.** The approximately 22.29% segment revenue ratio is broader than a pure premium-branded pet exposure. A private-label exit must not be mistaken for identical deterioration across the branded franchise.

**Indicators and falsifier.** Separate the exiting business, legacy brands and acquired formats; track evidence of repeat demand, channel access, price realization and contribution. Claims of professional recommendation or category positioning are not substitutes for attributable financial exposure. Operating profit should remain segment-specific with corporate charges separately reconciled.

### R2-C13 — Freshpet: fresh-format growth must pay for its operating system

**Source-established scope.** Q2 2026 sales were $305.6, with volume growth of 15.7% and price/mix of -0.2%. The company reported first-half operating cash flow of $84.8 and free cash flow of $27.4, separately from $100 of investment-sale proceeds. [S16]

**Research interpretation.** This supplies a cleaner company-level format exposure than a broad conglomerate, but it is not proof that all incremental growth has the same margin or capital intensity. No outlet, refrigerator or household count is invented here.

**Indicators and falsifier.** Research productive retail availability, within-location velocity, repeat feeding, manufacturing utilization, spoilage/service losses, logistics and maintenance/growth investment. A profitable-scale thesis fails if new capacity and distribution consume more cash than retained demand generates. Disposal proceeds cannot be counted as evidence of recurring customer cash generation. Compare cash after sustaining investment rather than use EBITDA alone.

### R2-C14 — General Mills: explicitly historical acquisition control

**Source-established scope.** In fiscal Q1 2026, reported on September 17, 2025, North America Pet revenue was approximately $610, up 6%. Whitebridge contributed about eleven growth points while organic sales declined 5%. The quarter's financial scope predates this research by a year; it is not claimed to be the company's latest result. [S17]

**Research interpretation.** This historical control proves why a label such as pet growth cannot bypass acquisition and brand-perimeter analysis. Blue Buffalo and acquired products should not share an assumed organic trend.

**Indicators and falsifier.** The next current-company refresh must establish the latest pet perimeter, comparable brands, category distribution and profit bridge from actual sources. R2 does not extrapolate this older observation into a present investment conclusion. The methodological lesson survives: changes to consolidated perimeter are not consumer adoption. A like-for-like exposure time series must preserve when the added business first became knowable and consolidated.

### R2-C15 — J.M. Smucker: licenses, snacks and pet under one issuer

**Source-established scope.** Fiscal Q1 2027, ended July 31, 2026, included $371.7 of U.S. Retail Pet Foods revenue within $2,219.3 group sales. Pet segment profit declined 2% despite sales growth of 1%. The release names Milk-Bone and Meow Mix and specifies that Dunkin' is licensed for defined packaged-coffee channels, not Dunkin' restaurants. It also discloses a material refund contribution to adjusted EPS. [S18]

**Research interpretation.** The approximately 16.75% pet revenue share does not make the company a pure pet exposure. Coffee licensing, frozen handheld products, pet and sweet snacks require different demand and margin bridges.

**Indicators and falsifier.** Maintain product/channel-specific commercial rights, recurring profit before exceptional contributions, and corporate financing costs. The license relationship must not generate restaurant ownership or revenue. A cash-generation thesis requires recurrence rather than extrapolation of a one-time benefit. Dividend and debt scenarios belong to common-share cash economics, not summed segment profits.

## 6. Household and personal care: six issuer structures

### R2-C16 — P&G: scale is not uniform category performance

**Source-established scope.** Fiscal 2026 Fabric & Home Care revenue was $30,314 of $87,032 total. In Q4, the segment's organic sales were flat, while the release distinguishes stronger Fabric Care from weaker Home Care. Beauty and Baby/Feminine/Family Care had different organic trends. The reported bridge explicitly warns about rounding. [S19]

**Research interpretation.** An approximately 34.83% annual Fabric & Home Care exposure is not a Tide-only or laundry-only purity measure. The combined segment can conceal category and region disagreement, including different promotion and mix effects.

**Indicators and falsifier.** Read regional volume, product tiers, merchandising, repeat purchase, productivity and brand reinvestment separately. A resilience thesis must survive both consumer demand and the cost of supporting the franchise. Do not mechanically add rounded price/volume/mix entries and overwrite the issuer's reported organic total. Underlying cash stability is a research question, not a label implied by company size.

### R2-C17 — Church & Dwight: demand growth can be reinvested rather than retained

**Source-established scope.** Q2 2026 organic sales grew 5.8%, with volume +4.3% and pricing/mix +1.5%. Adjusted EPS was $0.89 versus $0.94. The release distinguishes domestic/international operations and discusses brand and marketing investment. Its portfolio includes businesses such as household care, personal care and oral care; group organic growth is not one brand's growth. [S20]

**Research interpretation.** Increasing investment can rationally reduce current reported earnings, but it does not establish future returns. Conversely, simply penalizing reinvestment as deterioration can miss a durable-growth strategy. Separate the observed expense from the unproved expected benefit.

**Indicators and falsifier.** Track brand-level penetration and repeat usage where available, contribution after promotion, acquired perimeter and returns on incremental spending. Oral-care demand should not be inferred from household volume. A higher valuation requires evidence that reinvestment earns durable returns, not management's use of growth language alone.

### R2-C18 — Clorox: shipment timing and acquisition effects overlap

**Source-established scope.** Fiscal Q4 2026 sales were $1.95 billion, down 2%. GOJO contributed roughly ten growth points; organic sales declined 13%. The company attributed approximately 13.5 points of comparison pressure to lapping pre-ERP incremental shipments. Its portfolio includes multiple household, food and personal-care roles rather than cleaning alone. [S21]

**Research interpretation.** A severe reported organic decline can be dominated by a prior-period shipment distortion. The ERP project is relevant here as a commercial timing event, not an invitation to rebuild technology. GOJO's newly consolidated economics remain separate from the legacy comparison.

**Indicators and falsifier.** Examine consumer takeaway, order timing, service levels, inventory, price realization and integration costs. Do not automatically subtract 13.5 from 13 and label the result an accepted underlying-growth metric; retain the issuer's attribution alongside the reported observation until a valid reconciliation exists. A restored shipment base still does not prove improved consumer demand.

### R2-C19 — Kimberly-Clark: continuing operations and depreciation clocks

**Source-established scope.** The Q2 2026 release separates continuing operations from the discontinued International Family Care and Professional business. It describes pending Kenvue and Suzano-related transactions. It also explains that adjusted discontinued-operation profit benefited from cessation of depreciation on assets held for sale. [S22]

**Research interpretation.** An accounting perimeter can change before legal ownership ends, and accounting profit can improve without better factory economics. A pending combination cannot be incorporated as already-owned brands.

**Indicators and falsifier.** Retain the legal-close, held-for-sale, depreciation, consolidation and security dates separately. Analyze continuing-business volumes, cost conversion and reinvestment, then the common-share financing bridge. Do not compare full-company historical earnings with continuing-operation forecasts without a reconciliation. Current closing status and any future Kenvue brand exposure remain unverified in this research record.

### R2-C20 — Unilever: category detail matters inside portfolio simplification

**Source-established scope.** H1 2026 underlying sales grew 4.8%, with volume growth of 4.2%. Home Care grew 7.6%, predominantly volume, while Foods grew 1.2%. The results describe a planned Foods combination with McCormick; it is a future perimeter in that communication, not completed ownership. The cited results page is mutable, so its dated H1 context must be retained. [S23]

**Research interpretation.** A consumer-care growth thesis should not inherit the same demand or capital model as food. Portfolio simplification can alter future exposure and group costs, but a proposal is not a current comparable basis.

**Indicators and falsifier.** Build product/region rows for home care, personal care and beauty using actual disclosures, with brand investment and channel economics. Keep the proposed transaction in scenarios and future-event context. Current price effects and legally completed ownership need independent evidence; the source does not establish either.

### R2-C21 — Henkel: consumer exposure is not the whole issuer

**Source-established scope.** H1 2026 Consumer Brands revenue was EUR4,733 within EUR10,348 group sales. Consumer Brands organic growth was 1.7%; Adhesive Technologies grew 4.5%. The raised group sales outlook was accompanied by a higher adhesives outlook, while Consumer Brands guidance remained unchanged. [S24]

**Research interpretation.** Henkel's approximately 45.74% Consumer Brands revenue share prevents a group guidance upgrade from being mislabeled a universal household-consumption upgrade. Hair, laundry, home care and industrial adhesives have different end markets and profit transmission.

**Indicators and falsifier.** Preserve unit-level end markets, realization, volume, investment and cash, and reconcile group financing and corporate costs. A household theme cannot claim the full issuer response to industrial demand. Sum-of-business scenarios may be useful research, but neither all revenue nor all equity value should be assigned to the consumer theme.

## 7. Cross-sector mechanism anchor

### R2-C22 — Glanbia: the protein chain can contain opposite cost exposures

**Source-established scope.** H1 2026 Performance Nutrition like-for-like revenue grew 16.9% and Optimum Nutrition 25.2%. Performance Nutrition continued to face elevated whey costs, while Dairy Nutrition benefited from protein-solutions volume and pricing. The release separates three business segments. Its body is dated August 6, while page metadata displays August 5; the date discrepancy remains explicit. [S25]

**Research interpretation.** The same end-consumer theme can produce different effects for an ingredient supplier, a branded nutrition seller and a distributor. High ingredient prices may support one unit while compressing another. The consolidated effect depends on actual exposures, contracts and timing.

**Indicators and falsifier.** Map ingredient selling prices, contracted input costs, conversion, utilization, branded realization and cash investment. The transmission hypothesis fails if offsets, hedges or volume effects dominate the anticipated direction. Do not equate like-for-like volume conventions across suppliers and packaged-product companies without metric definitions. This is an economic-role link, not a newly admitted graph edge or automatic trade pair.
