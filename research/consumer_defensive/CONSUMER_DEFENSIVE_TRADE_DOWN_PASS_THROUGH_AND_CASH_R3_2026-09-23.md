## 1. The intelligence question

The system should identify a change in consumer behavior and explain where its economic benefit or harm is retained. It must not jump from an observed store-brand share gain to a supplier earnings forecast, or from a supplier's higher margin percentage to an attractive equity valuation.

The first question is what changed in the household's consumption decision. The second is which business has a documented commercial claim on that activity. The third is how the agreement, cost structure and capital requirements transmit it into recurring shareholder cash. Only then should existing expectations, valuation and market owners provide context.

The proposed output is not an abstention machine. Where evidence is sufficient, it should give a concise useful interpretation, state the principal competing explanation and identify the next discriminating observation. Missing evidence should narrow a claim rather than erase all useful business understanding.

## 2. Trade-down is a set of different behaviors

Use seven separate research dimensions, not a binary trade-down flag:

| Behavior | Economic question | Confound to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Switch brand within a comparable category | Is price-adjusted share moving between national and store brands? | Quality, format, pack and promotion differences |
| Switch retailer/channel | Is the same household reallocating spending or simply adding trips? | New footprint, geography and customer-income composition |
| Change package size | Is upfront affordability improving at a higher or lower price per standard unit? | A smaller pack is not an equal consumption unit |
| Reduce frequency or quantity | Is the category shrinking or just the transaction calendar shifting? | Stockpiling, pantry drawdown and calendar effects |
| Substitute category/occasion | Which meals or uses are replaced and which businesses capture them? | Similar calories or claims do not establish economic interchangeability |
| Move between foodservice and home preparation | Which product and service costs are removed or added? | Basket spending and meal counts use different denominators |
| Trade selectively, not uniformly | Does a household save in one category while paying a premium elsewhere? | Aggregate income buckets can hide heterogeneous choices |

A public share series cannot identify all of these mechanisms. PLMA's six-month-to-June14 observations distinguish unit and dollar performance; they do not identify the households that switched, their reasons or an individual manufacturer's contract capture. S22 is contextual evidence, not a causal label for each company.

The older academic evidence in S25 motivates testing persistence, asymmetry and brand-support behavior. R3 does not adopt historical coefficients or presume that every economic contraction produces the same response. A correlation between macro stress and store-brand share must be tested against prices, promotions, availability and retailer investment.

## 3. Separate three points at which money may be retained

### Consumer value

Compare total outlay and standardized quantity or service, retaining product quality and promotion scope. Unit price is spend divided by the declared standard quantity, not automatically the price of one package. Do not describe less spending caused by less consumption as a pure purchasing-power improvement.

### Retailer retained economics

For a specified sale, gross-profit dollars equal recognized net merchandise revenue minus recognized merchandise cost. The percentage margin divides those dollars by revenue. Net contribution then requires the relevant fulfillment, handling, shrink, rewards and service costs. Attribution of store overhead or customer acquisition must be explicit. A retailer can fund a lower price through better procurement, a different margin, a subsidy or reduced service; these mechanisms have different durability.

### Supplier retained economics

The manufacturer's result depends on contract scope, conversion compensation, input purchasing, quality requirements, minimum volumes, inventory ownership, deductions and capital obligations. Do not infer those contract terms from a private-label designation. Branded and private-label manufacturing are not mutually exclusive company identities.

A supplier can gain units and lose cash if it funds capacity or working capital without adequate compensation. Conversely, it can lose nominal revenue while protecting conversion profit when commodity savings are contractually passed through. The same retailer can win share while demanding that its suppliers absorb lower prices. These are research hypotheses to test with the actual agreements and financial results, not default accusations or conclusions about named companies.

## 4. Three synthetic contract cases

**All numbers in this section are invented educational inputs. They are not estimates of any named company's contracts, prices or margins.** Hold production volume, conversion cost, quality, geography and other terms constant unless explicitly changed.

### Case A — input pass-through plus a fixed conversion contribution

Suppose recognized input cost per unit falls from 8 to 6 and the contracted contribution above that cost stays 2. Revenue moves from 10 to 8, a 20% decline. Gross-profit dollars remain 2. The margin percentage rises from 20% to 25%.

There is no profit-dollar acceleration in this example. The higher margin percentage is a denominator effect. Falling reported price is also not evidence of lost conversion pricing power. Additional wages, energy or investment could still change the retained economics; they are held constant only for this demonstration.

### Case B — percentage markup rather than fixed compensation

Suppose the supplier receives a 25% markup on the same input cost. Revenue moves from 10 to 7.5 when cost falls from 8 to 6. Gross-profit dollars fall from 2 to 1.5, while gross margin stays 20%.

Cases A and B have the same raw-material movement but different profit outcomes. A universal commodity-relief score would misclassify at least one. Real contracts may index only part of cost, reset with lags, impose floors or use different quantity brackets. No company contract is assigned either synthetic formula without evidence.

### Case C — fixed selling price, lagged procurement and reset

With price initially fixed at 10, a recognized input-cost reduction from 8 to 6 would temporarily increase gross profit from 2 to 4. But a market spot-price drop is not necessarily a recognized-cost drop. Inventory, procurement terms and hedges can delay or alter the effect. A later customer-price reset can remove part or all of the increase.

A research scenario must show at least three clocks: purchase/hedge economics, recognition in cost of sales, and customer repricing. It must identify which assumptions are observed, contractual, management-guided or hypothetical. Do not assume one universal quarter lag or capitalize the temporary spread as permanent cash flow.

## 5. A synthetic retailer example: higher percentage margin, fewer dollars

Assume one comparable branded item sells for 5 and costs the retailer 4: gross profit1, margin20%. A store-brand alternative sells for4 and costs3.04: gross profit0.96, margin24%.

The consumer spends20% less on this assumed comparable item. The retailer's percentage margin improves by4 percentage points, but gross-profit dollars per item fall4%. Before any service-cost difference, unit volume must rise approximately4.17% to preserve the original gross-profit dollars. This is a counterexample, not a claim about typical private-label pricing.

Evaluate the whole shopping occasion as well as the item. Lower price could attract trips, improve loyalty or free spending for other categories; it could also substitute for existing profitable sales. None of those benefits is presumed. Preserve cannibalization, incremental traffic and contribution after costs as separate unknowns until measured.

The supplier's margin is a separate calculation. It cannot be recovered by subtracting the retailer's gross margin from the consumer saving, and a retail value-chain map must not visually imply that it can.

## 6. Working capital and cash-quality analysis

A simplified operating working-capital measure is `receivables + inventory - operating payables`. Its decrease can release cash without improving earnings. Classification and business scope matter: not all balance-sheet liabilities are operating payables, and acquired balances or currency movements are not operating cash flows.

**Synthetic illustration.** Receivables120, inventory80 and payables70 give net working capital130. Later balances110,70 and80 give100, releasing30 if the entire change is operational and all other reconciliation items are zero. That release does not become30 of additional recurring annual profit. Inventory depletion can eventually reverse or harm availability; extending supplier terms can transfer cash pressure to another business.

The proposed cash bridge should separately retain operating earnings, noncash accounting effects, ordinary working-capital change, exceptional disruption/settlement effects, asset disposals, financing and capital expenditure. Factoring, receivable sales and supply-chain-finance programs require their own disclosed accounting treatment. Do not claim their presence at a company without a source.

Bunge's R3-C16 is a real distinction between adjusted funds from operations and GAAP operating cash flow. Kerry's R3-C14 demonstrates two cash-conversion percentages using different working-capital conventions. They are not interchangeable comparators. An outcome model should prefer a consistently defined economic question over whichever reported ratio looks strongest. [S14, S16]

A healthy cash thesis needs a falsifier: poor conversion after normal inventory/service needs, reliance on repeated asset sales, rising financing for unchanged throughput, or inadequate returns on new capacity. However, planned growth investment can legitimately reduce near-term cash. Compare investment with subsequent productive use, not with a rule that every low-cash quarter is bad.

## 7. What would justify a valuation change?

Keep operating change, expectation change, valuation and market recognition separate. A mechanism becomes valuation-relevant only after specifying its duration, retained economics, investment need, financing consequences and uncertainty.

| Business role | Potential rerating mechanism to test | What would undermine it |
|---|---|---|
| Grocery/discount retailer | More repeat trips and comparable contribution with controlled service cost and capital | Share purchased through margin giveaways or costly fulfillment |
| Membership retailer | Durable member retention and profitable merchandise engagement | Fees rise while retention, usage or lifetime contribution weakens |
| Specialty-format retailer | New cohorts reproduce attractive mature-store returns | Cannibalization, poor maturation or rising build cost |
| Distributor | Denser routes and customer mix improve contribution and cash | Volume arrives at uneconomic delivery frequency or poor credit quality |
| Contract/private-label producer | Contract duration, productivity and capital compensation stabilize cash | Renegotiation captures all savings, excess capacity or concentrated customer risk |
| Functional-ingredient business | Repeat formulation demand produces retained value after support and investment | Innovation claims lack commercial conversion or buyers substitute more easily |
| Agricultural/biological processor | Sustainable through-cycle spreads and capital discipline | Peak profits or inventory marks are mistaken for a permanent base |

These are proposed analytical relationships, not assigned company valuation premiums. Improving operating economics can already be reflected in the stock price. Required current valuation inputs and forecast vintages must come from the existing owners; unavailable consensus or current prices stay unavailable rather than being invented.

### A transparent valuation illustration

Under a deliberately simplified steady-state model, using forward after-tax operating profit `NOPAT_next`, growth `g`, return on incremental investment `r`, and discount rate `k`:

`reinvestment rate = g / r`

`EV / NOPAT_next = (1 - g/r) / (k - g)`

Assume positive steady-state economics, `k > g`, consistent reinvestment/lease/tax treatment and no exceptional assets. This is a cash-flow identity under restrictive assumptions, not a universal sector model.

With growth2%, return on new investment12%, and discount rate10%, the forward operating-profit multiple is approximately10.42. At the same growth and discount rate, return20% gives11.25 because less reinvestment is needed for the same growth. Growth5% at return7% and discount10% gives approximately5.71: faster expansion at returns below the capital cost can destroy value.

The practical research question is not merely whether a business can grow. It is how much cash must be reinvested to obtain that growth and whether incremental returns remain adequate. Do not use the illustrative numbers as stock targets. Short-lived cost benefits require finite-duration scenarios, not this perpetual formula. For cyclical losses or near-zero earnings, a simple multiple is inappropriate; use a scenario consistent with the business and financing risk.

At equity level, enterprise-to-equity reconciliation must preserve debt, cash, leases where applicable, minority/preferred claims and dilution. Buybacks already reflected in per-share estimates cannot be added again as an independent return component. Dividends and total-return price adjustments must use a consistent convention in evaluation.

## 9. Six targeted empirical study designs

These extend the R2 research agenda; they are not six completed studies or a new evaluation platform. All outcomes and promotion gates belong to the existing evaluation/learning owners. Predeclare actual data availability and definitions before testing returns.

### R3-E01 — persistence of value-seeking behavior

Question: after a within-category price/availability change, do measured store-brand or channel gains persist beyond the initial promotion or stress period? Required data are consistent category/channel observations and, for household-switching claims, a licensed household panel. Compare matched periods, product definitions, distribution, promotions and category demand. The competing explanation is changes in availability or sampling rather than durable preference. Aggregate public shares permit descriptive work only, not a household causal conclusion.

### R3-E02 — who retains the economic benefit?

Question: when store-brand units improve, which documented suppliers or retailers subsequently improve contribution and cash? Join only source-backed commercial exposure, with effective dates. Compare manufacturers with different contracts and customer concentration, and retain negative cases. Exclude guessed retailer-supplier relationships. A category result without a supplier's exposure and profit bridge is insufficient evidence of issuer benefit.

### R3-E03 — input-price pass-through and lag

Question: does an identifiable input change precede recognized costs, customer pricing and retained margin in the predicted order? Start with transparent lag tables, contract/hedge evidence and falsifiers. Model estimation requires enough observations, appropriate seasonality and an identification strategy; common demand shocks can move both inputs and outputs. Do not call a time-series correlation causal or impose a universal three-month lag.

### R3-E04 — productive expansion versus footprint accumulation

Question: do new stores, plants or distribution investments generate adequate mature contribution and cash relative to investment? Use comparable cohorts, openings/closures, ramp periods, cannibalization and capital definitions. Announcement, construction, qualification, productive output and cash return are different events. Survivor-only cohorts exaggerate success; include closed locations and failed projects where available.

### R3-E05 — quality of cash improvement

Question: does lower working capital predict lasting returns or simply borrow from later periods? Separate ordinary operations from disposals, financing, acquisition scope and exceptional settlements. Compare normalized service/inventory needs and subsequent reversals. A cash-flow statement with missing reconciliation detail permits a narrower observation, not a fabricated recurring-cash estimate.

### R3-E06 — expectations, valuation and market incorporation

Question: which economic changes add information beyond existing financial/technical/theme signals when contemporaneous valuation and prior expectations are known? Keep same-fiscal-period forecast vintages, after-hours timing and corporate actions. A possible predeclared design separates1- and5-session reactions from63- and126-session follow-through, with multiplicity control and trading-cost/benchmark conventions. These horizons are study proposals, not new trading horizons or live signal authority.

Use acquired/delisted businesses when historically eligible to avoid survivor bias. Control duplicated source families and overlapping events. Train/test splits must separate time and correlated issuers/events. Do not use later restatements, current membership or corrected future exposures as as-known features. Without historical forecast and market data, the study remains unrun and no beat/mispricing score is published.

## 10. Measurement packet and source feasibility

The research proposes data requirements under existing owners, not a new registry or canonical store. Each usable observation needs: native identity or explicit unresolved binding; business/product/contract scope; territory/channel; external/internal and gross/net basis; unit/standard quantity; numerator/denominator; fiscal and comparison periods; actual/forecast/derived/attributed mode; adjustment and currency basis; source publication/availability and later correction clocks; confidence limitations; and rights/retention state.

Add mechanism-specific context only when supported: retailer versus manufacturer vantage; contractual index/reset terms; inventory owner; cost-recognition basis; stock versus flow; ordinary versus exceptional cash source; capital committed versus spent versus productive; and conditional counterparty relation. Unknown is legal. A hypothetical parameter belongs in a labeled scenario, not in a source fact.

The first feasible data layer uses issuer financial disclosures, agency statistics and already-entitled native market/financial services. Public websites can support small source-backed editorial assertions. They do not provide a complete historical panel, automatic redistribution rights or proven immutable retention. The PLMA member-login boundary is explicitly held. The full 2007 journal article's methods were not audited; its abstract is only a research lead.

The missing data inventory should be concrete: matched household switching; contract-level pass-through; product-level gross profit; real-time SKU availability; same-vintage consensus; historical security eligibility; and native private-source retention. A gap blocks only the claims depending on it, not all useful qualitative or financial synthesis. No new paid provider, scraping daemon or source warehouse is authorized by this document.
