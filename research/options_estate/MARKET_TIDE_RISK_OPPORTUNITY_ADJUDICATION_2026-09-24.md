## 3. Primary literature: the event premium is contested

Savor and Wilson (2013) report stronger scheduled-announcement returns over 1958–2009. Their publisher abstract was inspected, not their full methods replicated. This is evidence for a historical association, not a standing prohibition on conditional risk reduction. [S1]

Ghaderi and Seo, published online May 15, 2026, challenge the large-premium interpretation using joint return/volatility patterns. Their abstract attributes much of the observed average to policy surprises and finite-sample components, estimating a small premium. Full paper and appendix were not recovered, so this is an author-reported counterargument, not our replication or proof that the premium is zero. [S2]

Ruling: preserve both explanations in the claim map. Positive average return, a stable predictable premium and a profitable executable timing rule are three different claims. The existing calendar's context-only restriction stays intact; this report changes neither its code nor trading authority.

## 4. Distinct options candidate and its measurement boundary

Jacobs, Ke and Pan examine 207 scheduled meetings in 1996–2021. Their seven-day SPX option-implied, unstandardized third/fourth moments are measured at t−2 relative to median t−15:t−8. They report post-event return predictability; recursive out-of-sample results are referenced in Supplementary Table A4, which was not recovered here. The main methods were read, including rendered pages; selected table pagination differed between retrieval representations, so no table-performance coefficients are adopted. This is not a dealer-inventory feature or a validated pre-event exit rule. [S3]

Research distinction: an options-implied distribution reflects pricing as well as beliefs. It must not be silently relabeled as a real-world crash probability. Converting the former to the latter requires additional assumptions or empirical calibration; the Bank of England explicitly discusses this distinction. Good option-price fit also need not identify economically plausible risk premiums uniquely. [S4, S5]

Accordingly, three source measurements remain separate: the price of downside insurance; estimated signed dealer inventory/hedge sensitivities; and observed price/participation behavior. No generic 'smart money' label may collapse those different quantities into supposed knowledge of the next announcement.

## 5. A concrete event-attribution counterexample

The short horizon does not guarantee isolation of one event. Official records show CPI at 08:30 EDT and the FOMC statement at 14:00 EDT on March 15, 2017. [S6, S7]

Logical implication: an option spanning that day can price both uncertainties. Assigning all of its pre-event tail change to the FOMC merely because the contract is short-dated is not identified by maturity alone. This counterexample challenges exclusive attribution, not the existence of a predictive association. It does not establish how much either announcement contributed, or that either caused a market move.

A future options increment must bind the exact contract settlement/expiry interval to every known material event within it. Distinguish a single-event window from a CPI-plus-FOMC cluster and retain unknown coverage. Test cluster-adjusted and nonoverlap sensitivity rather than excluding inconvenient dates after results. Unscheduled actions and later revisions cannot acquire retrospective advance flags.

This is a new use of the already-reviewed March 2017 sequence, not a new calendar, event consumer or re-run of its tests.

## 6. Original decision analysis: risk reduction is not automatically alpha

For a simple research comparison, hold the same instrument and horizon fixed. Let w be benchmark exposure, w' < w reduced exposure, r_e the instrument's total return, r_c cash return and c the incremental trading cost. With constant weights and no leverage effects during that interval:

`R_reduced - R_benchmark = (w - w') * (r_c - r_e) - c`.

This is an accounting identity under stated assumptions, not a strategy estimate. It makes explicit the opportunity cost when the instrument outperforms cash. Lowering exposure can still satisfy a drawdown or risk-budget constraint even when average return falls. Conversely, lowering volatility alone does not establish a better user outcome.

Therefore the later policy study must evaluate return, adverse path, cost and re-entry together against the UNCHANGED incumbent risk policy. It must not compare a mostly-cash model with a fully invested benchmark, celebrate lower drawdown, and call the difference timing skill without exposure-aware controls. Exposure must be known before the assumed fill; a post-event reassessment cannot avoid a gap that already occurred.

Proposed research decision matrix — no live recommendations or new classifier:

| Observed/qualified risk assessment | Separately qualified opportunity assessment | Question the workspace should help answer |
|---|---|---|
| Elevated | Attractive | Is the potential reward compatible with the existing risk budget, and what confirmation or price makes participation acceptable? |
| Elevated | Weak or unavailable | Is reduced incremental risk justified, and what observable conditions allow reassessment? |
| Contained | Attractive | Does trend participation support continued exposure rather than an arbitrary calendar exit? |
| Contained | Weak or unavailable | Is apparent calm being mistaken for a good entry? |

'Unknown opportunity' must remain unknown, not become bearish. The same applies to unknown risk. The matrix supplies separate questions, not a weighted composite score or trade permission.
