## 2. Demand growth is only one side of the investment equation

The procurement thesis needs a supply-response model, not merely a shortage label. A production bottleneck may increase an incumbent supplier's importance, but qualification of an alternative supplier can improve a prime contractor's deliveries while changing the incumbent's future pricing or allocation. Neither effect can be inferred from sector demand alone.

Three distinct hypotheses should be tracked and later tested: demand-driven volume improvement; improvement in prime delivery/cash conversion as constraints ease; and the durability or erosion of supplier economics as alternative capacity becomes available. These are proposed hypotheses, not three admitted scores. The same event can support one while weakening another.

### Standard Missile: existing participation versus competing capacity

L3Harris's first-party product page identifies its Mk 104 motor with SM-2, SM-3 IB and SM-6, and its Mk 72 booster with SM-3 IB/IIA and SM-6. That establishes product-level participation, not a disclosed revenue share, exclusivity or program margin. [S1]

RTX's September 24, 2025 announcement describes up to $26 million of Avio USA engineering and qualification-related work for Mk 104 and explicitly frames the effort as second sourcing. Its November 10 announcement adds a proposed US facility relationship and preferred access to a portion of capacity. These are dated development/capacity observations; neither proves a completed factory or currently qualified production deliveries. [S2, S3]

Avio's March 12, 2026 annual-results release reports completion of a EUR400 million rights issue largely intended for its US factory and discusses the Mk 104 work and agreements with RTX and Lockheed. This is financing/capacity evidence, not a new program-specific government obligation. The study did not establish the latest September commissioning or qualification status. [S4]

**Research implication:** do not automatically assign the whole growth of Standard Missile demand to one supplier. Add exact program variants, the incumbent's evidenced role, alternative-source qualification stage, funded capacity, output actually achieved, contract pricing/allocation and customer guidance to the existing relationship and event owners. A generic supplier tag cannot express those distinctions.

A simple illustrative counterexample makes the logic clear: a market growing 30% with a supplier's share declining from 60% to 40% yields 52 units of that supplier's business per original 100 market units, versus 60 before. This hypothetical is NOT a forecast for L3Harris or Avio. It shows why growing procurement and growing company revenue are separate propositions.

### Northrop: distinguish development from near-term replenishment

Northrop's January 7, 2026 Navy announcement describes a $94.3 million development-and-qualification award for a new second-stage motor serving extended-range missile programs. The source does not establish a particular Standard Missile production allocation, so this record does not invent one. [S5]

RTX's May 26, 2026 release separately identifies Raytheon as prime on DARPA Burn n' Go phase two, collaborating with Northrop's Allegany Ballistic Laboratory. This is a documented research/development relationship, not proof of mature replenishment revenue or a production-margin uplift. [S6]

**Research implication:** Northrop is not simply outside the missile theme. The model must distinguish development exposure, prospective qualification, existing production and larger strategic-program economics. Those stages can have different financial horizons and risk even when a news classifier labels all of them missiles.

### L3Harris: program relevance is not consolidated shareholder sensitivity

Its July 27, 2026 THAAD framework explicitly connects the company with propulsion and control-system work and a capacity-expansion objective. A framework awaiting definitive terms is not an immediate conversion of its maximum value into earnings. [S7]

The July 29 Q2 release reports Missile Solutions operating income of $130 million alongside $522 million for Communications & Spectrum Dominance. It also reports a $14 million subsidiary preferred-stock deemed dividend in arriving at income available to common shareholders. These disclosed measures illustrate why the economic bridge must account for business mix and claims on subsidiary earnings; they do not justify a new purity score or a forecast. [S8]

The appropriate output remains a scenario of incremental cash attributable to the listed shareholder, with explicit units, scope, time, funding conditions, capital expenditure, working capital and ownership claims. Procurement amount, subsidiary sales, consolidated earnings and common-shareholder cash are not interchangeable denominators.
