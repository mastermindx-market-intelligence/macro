# SLR-P0 — source register and access ledger

Commission label: 2026-10-07. Sources checked during the research session extending into October 8 UTC. This is a directed primary-source review through the commission’s October 2026 horizon, not a systematic-review completeness claim. “Full paper” means an accessible original document with the stated targeted portions inspected; it does not mean every calculation was replicated. Abstract-only evidence is not silently promoted to a methods audit. Links identify external originals; versions and internal commits preserve provenance. No large verbatim excerpts are reproduced.

## External originals

### E01 — Moskowitz, T. J., and Grinblatt, M. (1999). Do Industries Explain Momentum?

Journal of Finance 54(4), 1249–1290. DOI: 10.1111/0022-1082.00146.

Original: https://spinup-000d1a-wp-offload-media.s3.amazonaws.com/faculty/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2019/09/DoIndustriesExplainMomentum.pdf

**Access:** Original full paper; targeted methods/conclusion and page images inspected.

**Use and limit:** Industry/common momentum is a necessary alternative explanation. Monthly portfolio evidence is not the proposed daily challenge estimand.

### E02 — Blitz, D., Huij, J., and Martens, M. (2011). Residual Momentum.

Journal of Empirical Finance 18(3), 506–521. DOI: 10.1016/j.jempfin.2011.01.003.

Original: https://repub.eur.nl/pub/22252/ResidualMomentum-2011.pdf

**Access:** Original repository full paper; rolling regression/formation methods and page images inspected.

**Use and limit:** Lagged factor-residual momentum is a strong baseline. Its multi-month formation window does not validate a one-day shock innovation.

### E03 — Gutierrez, R. C., Jr., and Pirinsky, C. A. (2007). Momentum, reversal, and the trading behaviors of institutions.

Journal of Financial Markets 10(1), 48–75. DOI: 10.1016/j.finmar.2006.09.002.

Original: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S138641810600053X

**Access:** Publisher abstract, author bibliography and working-paper record; full methods not inspected.

**Use and limit:** Distinguishes relative-return and abnormal-return persistence. Bibliographic spelling note: the publisher renders the coauthor as “Prinsky”; the author/working-paper record uses “Pirinsky.” This discrepancy is preserved, not silently treated as a correction to the handoff.

### E04 — Ehsani, S., and Linnainmaa, J. T. (2022). Factor Momentum and the Momentum Factor.

Journal of Finance 77(3), 1877–1919. DOI: 10.1111/jofi.13131; NBER Working Paper 25551.

Original: https://www.nber.org/papers/w25551

**Access:** Primary NBER abstract and publication metadata; full empirical methods not inspected.

**Use and limit:** Strong factor-attribution challenge to a firm-specific explanation. The competing result in E05 must be reported rather than reconciled by assertion.

### E05 — Graef, F., Höchle, D., and Schmid, M. (2025). Firm-specific versus systematic momentum.

Finance Research Letters 76, 106963. DOI: 10.1016/j.frl.2025.106963.

Original: https://irf.fhnw.ch/bitstreams/ba7cfaae-505c-4fbb-96be-3076cea663b4/download

**Access:** Nine-page published paper; methods, results and table images inspected.

**Use and limit:** A recent counterpoint to E04. Medium-term firm-specific momentum and short-term reversal coexist; no one-day leader-shock claim follows. Its missing-delisting-return convention is not imported into SLR.

### E06 — Cooper, M. J., Gutierrez, R. C., Jr., and Hameed, A. (2004). Market States and Momentum.

Journal of Finance 59(3), 1345–1365. DOI: 10.1111/j.1540-6261.2004.00665.x.

Original: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2004.00665.x

**Access:** Publisher/author abstract and metadata; full-paper fetch did not succeed.

**Use and limit:** Market-state dependence is a required challenge. No numerical state rule from this abstract is silently imported into SLR.

### E07 — Da, Z., Gurun, U. G., and Warachka, M. (2014). Frog in the Pan: Continuous Information and Momentum.

Review of Financial Studies 27(7), 2171–2218. DOI: 10.1093/rfs/hhu003.

Original: https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/Frog.pdf

**Access:** Author full paper; information-discreteness definition, methods and page image inspected.

**Use and limit:** Sign-based information discreteness is not synonymous with low volatility. SLR must control this path descriptor separately from volatility/extension.

### E08 — Daniel, K., and Moskowitz, T. J. (2016). Momentum Crashes.

Journal of Financial Economics 122(2), 221–247. DOI: 10.1016/j.jfineco.2015.12.002.

Original: https://spinup-000d1a-wp-offload-media.s3.amazonaws.com/faculty/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2019/09/Mom_crashes_JFE_final.pdf

**Access:** Published full paper; panic-state, beta and rebound methods/results inspected.

**Use and limit:** Loser rebound exposure is a serious negative-control species. A realized future rebound cannot be a challenge-time regime feature.

### E09 — Ang, A., Chen, J., and Xing, Y. (2006). Downside Risk.

Review of Financial Studies 19(4), 1191–1239. DOI: 10.1093/rfs/hhj035.

Original: https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/19/4/1191/1580531

**Access:** Primary publisher/NBER abstract and metadata; full methods not inspected.

**Use and limit:** Downside-risk pricing is an opposing interpretation, not an unconditional promise that protected stocks outperform. E11 and E12 qualify the literature.

### E10 — Frazzini, A., and Pedersen, L. H. (2014). Betting Against Beta.

Journal of Financial Economics 111(1), 1–25. DOI: 10.1016/j.jfineco.2013.10.005.

Original: https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~afrazzin/pdf/Betting%20Against%20Beta%20-%20Frazzini%20and%20Pedersen.pdf

**Access:** Author full published paper; introduction and beta/portfolio construction inspected.

**Use and limit:** Leverage and risk normalization matter. A leveraged low-beta portfolio result is not an unlevered stock-selection alpha claim.

### E11 — Wang, T. (2023 issue; online 2022). Bear Beta or Speculative Beta?—Reconciling the Evidence on Downside Risk Premium.

Review of Finance 27(1), 325–367. DOI: 10.1093/rof/rfac006.

Original: https://academic.oup.com/rof/article/27/1/325/6516548

**Access:** Full publisher HTML recovered on final verification; abstract, introduction and factor-decomposition/data methods inspected. Earlier access was intermittent.

**Use and limit:** Decomposes option-derived bear risk and challenges its protective interpretation. A statistically priced beta need not supply realized downside insurance.

### E12 — Bollerslev, T., Patton, A. J., and Quaedvlieg, R. (2022). Realized semibetas: Disentangling “good” and “bad” downside risks.

Journal of Financial Economics 144(1), 227–246. DOI: 10.1016/j.jfineco.2021.05.056.

Original: https://econ.duke.edu/~boller/Papers/BPQ_JFE_2022.pdf

**Access:** Twenty-page published paper; decomposition and empirical methods inspected; cover/abstract image verified.

**Use and limit:** The negative-market/positive-asset component is associated with lower subsequent returns. This is a direct conceptual falsifier, but repeated semibeta measurement is not a first-shock Detector-D experiment.

### E13 — Bollerslev, T., Patton, A. J., and Quaedvlieg, R. Granular Betas and Risk Premium Functions.

Author research page lists Journal of Econometrics, 2026 forthcoming. Inspected manuscript dated May 5, 2025; no final volume/pages or DOI asserted.

Original: https://econ.duke.edu/~boller/Papers/GranularBeta.pdf

**Access:** Full author manuscript; title/version image, methods and conclusion inspected.

**Use and limit:** Recent nonlinear/local exposure work strengthens the conditional-beta falsifier. A 2026 publication listing does not imply a 2026 sample or a one-day shock-return result.

### E14 — Fahlenbrach, R., Rageth, K., and Stulz, R. M. (2021). How Valuable Is Financial Flexibility when Revenue Stops? Evidence from the COVID-19 Crisis.

Review of Financial Studies 34(11), 5474–5521. DOI: 10.1093/rfs/hhaa134.

Original: https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhaa134

**Access:** Primary publisher abstract/indexed introduction; full methods not inspected.

**Use and limit:** Common-shock financial resilience is relevant. A persistent post-crisis price-level gap is not automatically additional return after the resilience landmark.

### E15 — Cella, C., Ellul, A., and Giannetti, M. (2013). Investors’ Horizons and the Amplification of Market Shocks.

Review of Financial Studies 26(7), 1607–1648. DOI: 10.1093/rfs/hht023.

Original: https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/publications/DP717.pdf

**Access:** Full original working paper and published metadata; common-shock, ownership-horizon, beta and reversal methods inspected.

**Use and limit:** Short-horizon institutional ownership can amplify declines followed by reversal. Independent ownership evidence, not OHLCV cause labeling, supports the mechanism.

### E16 — Coval, J., and Stafford, E. (2007). Asset fire sales (and purchases) in equity markets.

Journal of Financial Economics 86(2), 479–512. DOI: 10.1016/j.jfineco.2006.09.007; NBER Working Paper 11357.

Original: https://www.nber.org/papers/w11357

**Access:** Primary NBER abstract; attempted full-paper access failed.

**Use and limit:** Observed fund pressure provides a fire-sale mechanism. It does not license reviving the killed Mastermind OHLCV forced-versus-information classifier.

### E17 — Demers, E., Hendrikse, J., Joos, P., and Lev, B. (2021). ESG did not immunize stocks during the COVID-19 crisis, but investments in intangible assets did.

Journal of Business Finance & Accounting 48(3–4), 433–462. DOI: 10.1111/jbfa.12523.

Original: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jbfa.12523

**Access:** Primary publisher indexed text; complete methods not inspected.

**Use and limit:** An example of a protective-characteristic interpretation changing with controls. No ESG or intangible factor is added to production here.

### E18 — Chordia, T., Roll, R., and Subrahmanyam, A. (2000). Commonality in liquidity.

Journal of Financial Economics 56(1), 3–28. DOI: 10.1016/S0304-405X(99)00057-4.

Original: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X99000574

**Access:** Primary publisher abstract/introduction; full methods not inspected.

**Use and limit:** Liquidity has a common component. This motivates market/sector liquidity controls, not identification of hidden buyers.

### E19 — Chordia, T., and Subrahmanyam, A. (2004). Order imbalance and individual stock returns: Theory and evidence.

Journal of Financial Economics 72(3), 485–518. DOI: 10.1016/S0304-405X(03)00175-2.

Original: https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/documents/areas/fac/finance/36-00.pdf

**Access:** Full October 7, 2002 author working paper; data, theory and return/imbalance timing methods inspected; publication metadata verified separately.

**Use and limit:** Autocorrelated flow/order splitting and contemporaneous conditioning change the interpretation of lagged flow. No modern feed equivalence or institutional identity is assumed.

### E20 — Anand, A., Irvine, P., Puckett, A., and Venkataraman, K. (2013). Institutional trading and stock resiliency: Evidence from the 2007–2009 financial crisis.

Journal of Financial Economics 108(3), 773–797. DOI: 10.1016/j.jfineco.2013.01.007.

Original: https://cpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/people.smu.edu/dist/6/414/files/2020/07/Institutional-trading-and-stock-resiliency-Evidence-from-the-2007-2009-financial-crisis-2013.pdf

**Access:** Full published paper; institution-specific data, trading-cost resiliency and results inspected.

**Use and limit:** Liquidity recovery is distinct from next-month residual-price continuation. The data support a stronger participant interpretation than anonymous prints.

### E21 — Campbell, J. Y., Ramadorai, T., and Schwartz, A. (2009). Caught on Tape: Institutional Trading, Stock Returns, and Earnings Announcements.

Journal of Financial Economics 92(1), 66–91. DOI: 10.1016/j.jfineco.2008.03.006.

Original: https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120378-7e71-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b

**Access:** Primary Harvard repository summary; PDF retrieval failed.

**Use and limit:** Quarterly ownership-linked inference is not a simple large-print identity rule. No claim of full-method replication.

### E22 — Cont, R., Kukanov, A., and Stoikov, S. (2014). The Price Impact of Order Book Events.

Journal of Financial Econometrics 12(1), 47–88. DOI: 10.1093/jjfinec/nbt003.

Original: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.6402

**Access:** Full author paper; OFI construction, contemporaneous impact methods and page images inspected.

**Use and limit:** Quote-event OFI includes limit-order/cancellation effects, not just signed trades. Contemporaneous impact fit is not future alpha or beneficial-owner identification.

### E23 — Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System (November 3, 2025). Order Flow Imbalances and Amplification of Price Movements: Evidence from U.S. Treasury Markets.

FEDS Notes; official research note.

Original: https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/order-flow-imbalances-and-amplification-of-price-movements-evidence-from-u-s-treasury-markets-20251103.html

**Access:** Official indexed article text; no numerical figure result is reproduced here.

**Use and limit:** Stress, imbalance and depth interact. Treasury evidence is a mechanism check, not US-equity first-challenge validation.

### E24 — Chen, J., Hsieh, P.-F., and Yang, J. J. (2026). Order spoofing, price impact, and market quality.

Pacific-Basin Finance Journal 97, 103077, March 2026. DOI: 10.1016/j.pacfin.2026.103077.

Original: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0927538X26000235

**Access:** Publisher indexed methods/abstract and author publication record; full-page retrieval failed.

**Use and limit:** The absence of investor identifiers limits linking candidate order/trade patterns to a common participant. The non-US market setting and inferred-pattern design do not prove US institutional absorption.

### E25 — FINRA (May 10, 2019). Understanding Short Sale Volume Data on FINRA’s Website.

Official Information Notice; read alongside FINRA’s Short Interest—What It Is, What It Is Not.

Original: https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2019-07/information-notice-051019.pdf

**Access:** Official notice/FAQ text; semantic use, not a new regulatory or entitlement claim.

**Use and limit:** Short-marked volume is not short interest. It does not establish aggressor demand, beneficial ownership, net inventory or institutional accumulation.

### E26 — Nasdaq. TotalView-ITCH 5.0 specification.

Official technical specification; inspected available version 5.0, no unverified revision date asserted.

Original: https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/content/technicalsupport/specifications/dataproducts/NQTVITCHspecification.pdf

**Access:** Official 36-page specification; order-reference, attributed/unattributed add and related fields inspected, including a page image.

**Use and limit:** Order IDs and optional MPIDs are feed-level identifiers, not universal beneficial-owner or metaorder identities. Venue/session scope must be preserved.

### E27 — Petersen, M. A. (2009). Estimating Standard Errors in Finance Panel Data Sets: Comparing Approaches.

Review of Financial Studies 22(1), 435–480. DOI: 10.1093/rfs/hhn053.

Original: https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/petersen/htm/papers/standarderror.html

**Access:** Primary author method overview and publisher metadata; full-paper methods not inspected.

**Use and limit:** Panel dependence can invalidate ordinary standard errors. The precise SLR block length and thresholds are this report’s design choices, not recommendations attributed to Petersen.

### E28 — Cameron, A. C., Gelbach, J. B., and Miller, D. L. (2011). Robust Inference With Multiway Clustering.

Journal of Business & Economic Statistics 29(2), 238–249. DOI: 10.1198/jbes.2010.07136.

Original: https://cameron.econ.ucdavis.edu/research/JBESpaper2009version.pdf

**Access:** Original author working-paper abstract/method overview and publication metadata; not a complete proof review.

**Use and limit:** Supports multiway dependence treatment under its assumptions. Two-way clustering does not by itself solve arbitrary cross-date contagion or few-cluster inference.

### E29 — Harvey, C. R., Liu, Y., and Zhu, H. (2016). … and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns.

Review of Financial Studies 29(1), 5–68. DOI: 10.1093/rfs/hhv059; NBER Working Paper 20592.

Original: https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592

**Access:** Primary NBER/publisher abstract and framework summary; full methods not inspected.

**Use and limit:** Factor-search multiplicity raises the evidence bar. SLR’s single primary and Holm secondary family are explicit design choices; no generic t-statistic threshold is treated as validation.
