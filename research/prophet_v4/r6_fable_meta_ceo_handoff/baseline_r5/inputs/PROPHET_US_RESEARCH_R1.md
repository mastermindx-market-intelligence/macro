## 2. Architecture choice: three plausible approaches

### A. Tune the existing V3 composite

Advantages: the smallest serving change; retains a familiar interface; cheap to compare as a control. Disadvantages: cannot recover opportunities eliminated before scoring, does not create strategy-specific holding laws, and leaves discovery, timing, selection and performance attribution intertwined. Retain it as an incumbent/control, not the destination.

### B. Replace the product with one large end-to-end model

Advantages: permits nonlinear interactions and representation learning across broad data. Disadvantages: an end-to-end loss can obscure which subsystem failed, learn publication artifacts rather than opportunity, and confuse missing evidence with economic weakness. It also risks bypassing deterministic Availability and laundering source or timing errors into model confidence. Research ambitious models, but not as a universal authority replacement at birth.

### C. One canonical platform with strategy-specific decisions and shared evidence — recommended

Use the same security identity, candidate episode, evidence lineage, market-data and quote owners, deterministic Availability, plan lineage, outcome owners and publication/auth stack. Expose separate strategy/horizon projections and test models over them. A shared representation may be useful; a single interchangeable scalar for every horizon is not.

This recommendation extends the accepted one-platform/multiple-sleeve architecture [I1] and Conditional Fusion's family, router and multi-head design [I2]. It is not a new program, model registry, episode system or parallel ranking service.

## 3. The sleeve structure

### 3.1 What a sleeve is

A sleeve is a falsifiable economic opportunity mechanism with an explicit horizon, candidate population, entry policy, hold/exit law and evaluation contract. It is not a market label, an indicator, a display tab, or a collection of famous winners.

Every research sleeve must state the economic claim; which securities and instruments it supports; which evidence is required versus optional; decision-time data availability; entry and abstention rules; what the thesis predicts; what would invalidate it; how price incorporation is approximated; its expected holding interval and time stop; the relevant benchmarks and costs; and which claims remain unearned.

A four-hour reset, daily confirmation and weekly structural trend can be different observations within one sleeve. Conversely, a tactical rotation entry and a long-cycle accumulation thesis may describe the same security while requiring different actions. Shared episode identity does not imply shared capital allocation. Existing plan-lineage restrictions remain binding until separately reconciled; the design does not automatically license concurrent duplicate positions.

### 3.3 Early Leadership / Rotation

The economic question is not simply whether a group rose. It is whether a change in demand, expectations or positioning creates remaining opportunity in a member, at a price and time a user can actually use.

The proposed evidence decomposition is group strength; breadth and persistence; the stock's relative role within that group; price/volume response; catalyst proximity; fragility and entry geometry. Group leadership, stock selection and trade availability must remain independent. A favorable group cannot turn a stale quote or broken structure into an entry.

Industry-momentum research establishes that industry effects can account for important parts of measured equity momentum in historical samples [R1]. Its intermediate-horizon results do not validate a two-day rotation rule. The research implication is to compare group allocation and within-group selection explicitly, not merely add a bullish theme point to every member.

Potential entry tactics include an accepted early technical event, a lawful continuation setup, and a reset/pullback tactic. Each must use the existing technical/Radar/Setup Species owners, preserve exact prior kills, and be tested as a mechanism. This is not permission to remove the incumbent not-topped veto or rename the rejected naive leader-pullback construction.

### 3.4 Quality Earnings / Expectation Revision

A positive earnings surprise is not the whole thesis. The proposed causal chain for research is: newly available economic evidence changes plausible earnings expectations; the market's response may be incomplete, excessive or appropriate; a strategy may or may not retain useful risk-adjusted opportunity.

Candidate inputs are owner-issued revenue/margin/cash-flow facts, guidance changes, analyst revision timing, estimate dispersion where lawfully licensed, repeatability, capital needs, and related-company effects. The stock's pre-event run-up and post-event response are separate evidence. A price-incorporation feature is a proxy to test, not direct knowledge of what every investor has priced in.

Novy-Marx's fundamental-momentum research finds a substantial relationship between earnings and price momentum in its historical construction [R2]. That motivates an independent revision sleeve; it does not justify the current SUE flag, a 20–90-session horizon, or any proposed coefficients without testing.

Known future binary events belong in a research/risk calendar before the result. They must not become a prediction that the result will be favorable. Post-result continuation is a different experiment with a different information set.

### 3.5 Cycle Capture

Cycle Capture must remain a flagship research priority, not a distant reward after tactical trading is solved. It should begin in parallel with a data/source feasibility study, while tactical research proceeds faster on its own clocks.

The first proving domain should be selected before its future performance is inspected, using economic interpretability, lawful point-in-time source coverage, full investable-universe history and identifiable failure cases. Do not select semiconductors, miners, energy or another industry merely because its last rebound was spectacular.

The proposed mechanism requires more than a large drawdown: an eligible economic cycle; evidence about supply, demand, inventories, utilization or capacity; issuer survivability; funding and dilution risk; a defensible base/pivot; a declared response hypothesis; and a core-hold/invalidation law. Company distress and industry distress should be measured separately.

Historical distress research cautions that financially distressed stocks can combine low returns with high risk [R3]. A July 2026 working paper specifically studies industry distress and competitive feedback [R4]. The latter is preliminary evidence considered at abstract level here, not a basis for promoting a new signal. Both argue against defining opportunity as distance below a prior high.

The controls should include deep-drawdown alone, simple trend continuation, industry exposure, buy-and-hold/cash where appropriate, and failure-inclusive point-in-time universes. Delistings, mergers, recapitalizations, dilution and businesses that never recover must remain in scope.

A tactical failed trade cannot quietly become a six-month investment. A long-cycle core cannot automatically be closed by one daily oscillator reversal. The strategy version chosen before entry governs that distinction.

## 4. Three clocks, not one universal confirmation ladder

The proposed model separates:

**Economic clock:** how quickly the thesis could plausibly work — event days, earnings quarters, inventory/capacity years.

**Structure clock:** the evidence that the price process is forming, confirming, persisting or deteriorating on its relevant sampling grid.

**Execution clock:** whether the current quote, spread, session, gap, geometry and risk permit an entry now.

These are research roles, not new state stores. They must be represented through the existing owners and B3/B4 projections.

Research on short-term returns finds that continuation versus reversal can depend on turnover and information conditions [R5, R6]. In particular, the cited short-term momentum study uses prior-month returns, not a universal four-hour indicator. It supports conditioning by mechanism and testing timeframe roles, not assuming that a shorter chart always enters better.

### The decisive early-versus-late experiment

Start with the same episode population as it first becomes observable through a preserved expert. Compare a small frozen set of policies: enter at the first lawful early opportunity; wait for the registered slower confirmation; or wait for a registered pullback. All policies use decision-time information only.

Retain episodes that never confirm, never offer the pullback, invalidate before entry, cannot be priced, or expire. Do not evaluate an early-entry policy only on the subset that later became successful 3D confirmations. That would answer a hindsight-conditioned question.

First hold the exit policy and evaluation ruler fixed to isolate entry timing. Then run a separately frozen entry-by-management experiment. Do not compare early entries under one favorable horizon with late entries under another.

Preserve both an episode-origin evaluation and an entry-clock evaluation. The former measures complete opportunity-policy value; the latter describes the trade actually filled. Different meanings require separate columns and denominators.

The native exchange/session/bar owner must define four-hour anchoring and partial bars. Reconstructed intraday candles or a completed bar assumed before its close cannot provide historical early-entry proof. Source-to-visible latency is part of the input, not an implementation detail to subtract away after testing.

## 5. The proposed prediction object

The final product should not ask one model for a universal BUY score. For an episode, strategy, decision time and horizon, the proposed research object includes:

- expected absolute net return and expected broad-market/sector-relative return, separately;
- favorable and adverse return quantiles;
- probability of a useful target arriving before invalidation;
- time-to-payoff distribution;
- severe-loss, gap and liquidity risk;
- confidence in those forecasts, including evidence coverage and historical applicability;
- present Availability and its deterministic blockers, received from B4 rather than predicted into authority.

The probability of making money, expected payoff, upper-tail opportunity and downside risk are not interchangeable. A high chance of a small gain with a rare large loss may be unattractive. A high upper-tail forecast can simply identify lottery-like risk. Historical research on extreme daily-return stocks provides a relevant warning against equating upside potential with expected value [R7].

### Remaining opportunity, not just technical maturity

A key proposed extension is to forecast the opportunity **remaining from the current lawful decision**, rather than treating confirmation stage as a substitute for future value. A mature trend can still offer an attractive reset; an early pattern can already be unenterable after a gap.

Time-to-event methods with competing events provide a useful statistical formulation: target first, invalidation first, or neither by a specified horizon. DeepHit is one published method for estimating competing-event distributions [R8], but its published applications do not prove financial trading edge. The initial Prophet baseline should be a simpler regularized discrete-time competing-risk model, with nonlinear challengers compared afterward or in parallel.

Use one joint first-event law, not separately fitted event probabilities that can sum to more than one. Unresolved observations are censored when time truly has not elapsed. Missing prices, confirmed halts, delistings, and a policy that legitimately never entered require different handling. Ambiguous target/stop order in a coarse bar must remain bounded or follow a preregistered conservative convention, not invented tick history.

### Conditional trade value versus complete policy value

An entry policy should be assessed over the pre-policy opportunity cohort:

`V(policy | information at decision) = P(entry) × E(net trade outcome | entry) + P(no entry) × E(cash/wait outcome | no entry)`

This is an accounting decomposition for research, not an identified estimate or new live score. A no-entry cash outcome can be zero under a declared excess-to-cash numeraire; it is not equivalent to missing data, an unresolved horizon or a zero-return filled trade.

An especially selective policy may have excellent filled-trade statistics while being a poor opportunity product. Report coverage, waiting, false alarms and foregone opportunities alongside conditional trade results. No policy may improve its denominator by deleting difficult cases.

## 6. Model research: transparent references and ambitious challengers

### 6.1 Reference family

Preserve exact incumbent V3 and its existing controls. Add preregistered, competitive reference models: regularized linear/logistic models, hierarchical partial pooling, and sparse additive models with a small declared interaction set. Baselines should include simple own-stock price, industry/group, earnings and combined evidence where inputs are lawful.

A transparent model is a scientific reference, not an assertion that markets are linear. Its intercept, shrinkage, feature access, turnover, missingness and capital assumptions must be fairly specified.

### 6.2 Nonlinear family

Research grouped gradient-boosted ranking, return regression, quantile regression and calibrated probability models on identical decision-date populations. Gu, Kelly and Xiu show that nonlinear interactions can be valuable in historical asset-pricing prediction [R9]. Their broad monthly-equity experiment and long sample are not direct validation for Prophet's short tactical or intraday product.

LambdaMART is a useful ranking challenger, but it uses pairwise learning with a ranking-metric-aware objective; a relevance margin is not a calibrated expected return or win probability. XGBoost's official documentation describes the query-group requirement and objective [R10]. Maintain separate calibrated outcome heads rather than renaming the rank margin “confidence.”

### 6.3 Conditional experts

Test whether a strategy/fingerprint/context interaction improves results beyond a strong global model. Begin with the allowed owner-native mechanisms: global cross-sectional expert fit, behavioral-neighbor pooling and empirical-Bayes shrinkage. Do not create a per-ticker best-historical-expert selector or broad self-authorizing macro router.

Thin regime/sector cells should shrink toward broader estimates. Context uncertainty should increase uncertainty or abstention, not select whichever expert recently won. Regime context may change an interaction; a night-constant bullish label cannot directly order every stock in that cross-section.

Momentum crash research concerns particular historical portfolio constructions and states [R11]. It is a reason to test state-conditioned failure behavior, not a universal instruction to avoid every leader in a volatile market.

### 6.4 High-capacity representation and graph research

Permit controlled experiments with strongly regularized high-dimensional features, sequence encoders and temporal graph representations when the data contract can support them. Do not insist that all such research wait until every simple model has failed. Equally, do not allow nominal model size, a fashionable architecture or a single impressive chart to establish product value.

The complexity literature is contested. Kelly, Malamud and Zhou provide theory and experiments favoring high complexity under their conditions [R12]. Buncic's March 2026 working paper challenges the empirical comparison, especially intercept restrictions and performance aggregation [R13]. The authors' research page also presents their response [R14]. This round does not adjudicate that dispute. It changes our experimental design: strong fair baselines, identical capital constraints, true ensemble returns, and all model-selection trials counted.

Never average separate simulated Sharpe ratios and call that the performance of a realizable ensemble. Form the actual combined prediction/portfolio first under the declared policy, then evaluate its realized series. The downstream Portfolio/Risk owner continues to control capital deployment.

### 6.5 Complexity admission gates

A challenger needs incremental outcome value, stable top-of-queue behavior, acceptable adverse tails, coverage-stratified robustness, correction safety, repeatable exact-vintage inference and a usable explanation. Extra inference cost or slower publication must be charged against the capability it improves.

Research complexity may run in parallel; live promotion remains sequentially gated. None of this round's proposed models has been fitted or licensed to alter the product.

## 7. Intelligence composition and the potential moat

The moat should not be more indicator names. It should be the cumulative, decision-time connection between an opportunity's economic mechanism, observed technical development, issuer-specific evidence, available entry, user-visible delivery and subsequent path.

### 7.1 Base coverage plus incremental specialist evidence

Every supported candidate should receive a base view from identity, price, structure, liquidity, group context and evidence freshness. Richer evidence extends that view. Missing options or transcript coverage should not silently make a company bad; missing required price/basis/identity facts can correctly make the entry unavailable.

For every rich family, measure incremental value over both the base view and its correlated siblings. Preserve complete-case and sparse-coverage cohorts separately. A coverage band is a disclosure; it is not proof that a model's uncertainty is calibrated.

### 7.2 Economic response and price incorporation

For a material event, the proposed research separates the economic response hypothesis from the observed market response. Was the event new to the system? Which businesses are plausibly exposed? Was a favorable outcome already expected? Did price gap beyond a feasible risk budget? Did peers respond while the directly exposed name did not?

These questions should consume owner-issued facts and typed relationships. They should not be answered by an LLM declaring that a stock is “underpriced.” Price incorporation is inferred imperfectly from available evidence and requires validation.

### 7.3 Typed relationship propagation

Customer/supplier predictability research motivates testing bounded economic-link features [R15]. It does not establish that any graph neural network or inferred edge is useful. Require valid-time and observed-time edges, direct versus proxy relationships, direction and strength uncertainty, and lawful source rights.

The first graph contest should compare simple dated relationship features with no-graph, industry-only, relationship-shuffled and time-shifted controls. High graph degree and popular-theme membership must not masquerade as causal discovery. Only after these tests should a temporal graph challenger compete.

### 7.4 Living thesis without narrative authority

A future dossier should identify supporting facts, counterevidence, expected response, observable invalidation and the next review condition. A readable summary may help a user understand the evidence. It cannot create facts, grant entry, repair a missing source or explain away a failed thesis.

A model contribution explanation is not a causal explanation. Family-level attribution stability and intervention controls are separate requirements. Correlated news, theme, analyst and graph views of the same underlying event should not get four independent votes.

## 8. Two distinct product priorities

**Research attention priority** answers which unresolved or changing opportunities deserve inspection. A blocked or sparse-evidence candidate can legitimately be important here.

**Actionable-opportunity priority** compares candidates inside a particular strategy/horizon and Availability lane. It must not display a research-interest score as permission to enter.

Neither needs a second ranking authority or new queue database. They are purpose-labeled projections through the existing V4 and Fusion owners. Initially, show explicit strategy groups rather than a misleading global number comparing a ten-session trade with a two-year cycle thesis.

A future actionable ranking policy can compare expected net payoff, adverse-tail exposure and uncertainty under a declared utility. The penalties are not frozen in this round. Model confidence cannot be treated as a convenient multiplier without testing its meaning.

The combined product should be able to communicate: strong economic thesis; meaningful remaining opportunity; currently outside the entry zone; next lawful review condition; confidence limited by specified missing evidence. This is more useful than a green card that conceals all those distinctions.

## 9. Calibration, selection and uncertainty

Calibration must be tested for the decisions actually shown: top-ranked candidates, each sleeve, each horizon and relevant coverage strata. Good full-universe probability calibration need not survive selection into a small action queue.

Research on calibration supplies useful methods [R16]. Adaptive conformal work addresses dependence and changing distributions [R17]. Neither grants automatic per-stock, per-strategy or top-K coverage guarantees. A conformal interval for future outcomes is not a confidence interval for a conditional mean, and neither automatically establishes positive expected trade value.

Report calibration error and proper scoring rules together with discrimination, net outcome, tail risk and abstention. Estimate ranking uncertainty where practical; do not imply that rank 1 is meaningfully better than rank 2 when their evidence is indistinguishable.

A useful fallback is not fabricated neutrality. Depending on the missing contract, it can be a base-evidence view, an explicitly older snapshot, an unscored research row or unavailable entry. Each must preserve the fact it actually represents.

## 10. Scientific safeguards that materially change the plan

### 10.1 Two temporal integrity problems

There is source lookahead: a fact was not published, observed or captured by the system at the decision time. There is also model lookahead: a later-trained model may contain knowledge of events beyond that decision.

Chronologically consistent LLM research explicitly examines training-data vintage and finds application-dependent effects [R18]. A 2026 finance-evaluation position paper stresses lookahead, survivorship and other biases [R19]. These sources do not show every historical LLM experiment is invalid. They do show that filtering retrieval by date is not by itself a clean backtest certificate.

For evidence extraction, demand source-span faithfulness and evaluate extraction separately from forecasting. Masking the ticker is a useful control, not proof against latent knowledge. Forecasting experiments need an admissible model vintage or a clearly labeled retrospective status; prospective frozen-model evidence remains the clean path where training provenance cannot be established.

### 10.2 Separate discovery, ranking and management experiments

Discovery experiments compare complete supported-universe opportunity processes and quantify false alarms, recall and review burden. Ranking experiments use the same predeclared candidates and change only ordering. Timing experiments share the origin episode population and compare lawful decision policies. Management experiments freeze their entry and vary the hold/exit policy. An eventual whole-product contest combines them, but does not replace the attribution tests.

### 10.3 Dependence, costs and multiple testing

Random row splits are not acceptable for this product. Splits must respect decision chronology, overlapping labels, repeated episode observations, issuer continuity and model-feature selection. Long-cycle labels need long-horizon isolation; the tactical study should not be incorrectly credited with long-cycle evidence or indefinitely blocked by unrelated long labels.

Institutional transaction-cost studies demonstrate that implementation cost depends on trading circumstances and can materially alter anomaly viability [R20]. They do not supply a free retail Prophet cost schedule. Use measured lawful NBBO/fill information where available, explicitly labeled conservative floors otherwise, and refuse capacity claims without size/participation evidence.

Multiple-testing research in asset pricing motivates a finite registered experiment family and rigorous selection accounting [R21]. A fixed 300-row floor, a p-value threshold or a high backtest Sharpe is not a substitute for effective sample size, economically meaningful effect size and out-of-sample validation.

### 10.4 Non-inferiority is a positive requirement

Suppose a proposed policy's net-excess difference has an interval from -3 to +1 percentage points. The interval overlaps zero, but it does not establish that the policy avoids a economically unacceptable loss. This is an illustrative mathematical example, not Prophet evidence.

A future non-inferiority rule should predeclare its acceptable degradation margin and require a sufficiently informative bound. The margin is a research/business decision to freeze before outcomes, not a number selected after inspecting a result.
