# RD1: the yield-only shortcut did not earn directional authority

## Decision

Reject this exact construction for promotion. Preserve the research utility and observed rates context. This does not reject real yields as explanatory variables, all momentum methods, conditional confluence or the broader rates-direction program. Independent review is still required; no predictive advantage, production integration or prospective validation is claimed.

The frozen 10Y/five-observation-interval primary test lost to the no-change forecast in every primary year. Adding real-rate/breakeven information made essentially no difference versus the curve-only model. Beating naive linear momentum extrapolation is not sufficient when a simpler no-change benchmark is better.

## Jump-risk result

There were 65 overlapping primary-panel jump labels under the frozen definition: absolute yield change greater than max(10 bp, twice trailing volatility scaled by sqrt(horizon)). At the predeclared 0.50 alert-probability threshold, the richer candidate made zero alerts and caught none. The naive momentum comparator made 54 alerts, 51 false, and caught 3 of the 65 labels. This is not an accepted jump-warning engine.

The no-change distribution's approximate interval coverage is useful diagnostic evidence, not proof that its conditional tails, event-day behavior or future coverage are reliable. A no-change point benchmark must not be rendered as a confident prediction of a quiet or flat market.

## Known September 23 case: no hindsight rescue

For the September 22 origin, the one-observation-horizon richer model reconstructed a +0.7935 bp point change, about 49.41% probability of a rise beyond +1 bp, and 1.96% probability of an upside jump beyond +10 bp. That is not a strong advance warning. This reconstruction was made after the event; it was not a forecast issued September 22. The frozen cache has no September 23 outcome, so its target stays null rather than importing a later quote into the source series.

The earlier conversational claim that this setup would likely have been in a very high upside-vulnerability percentile is therefore **unproven**. The current experiment neither established that claim nor tested the later macro-surprise/policy-pricing hypotheses. We must not rewrite this failure into a successful prediction.
