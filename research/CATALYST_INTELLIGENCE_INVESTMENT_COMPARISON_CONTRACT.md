## 3. Entry-price sensitivity without changing the recommendation

The same view answers a practical question: under these fixed example assumptions, what reference price corresponds to a chosen expected net-return requirement?

For weighted terminal equity price E, friction c expressed as a fraction of entry price, and required net return r:

`illustrative reference price = E / (1 + c + r)`.

The default requirement is 10%, adjustable from 0% to 50%. At 20%, the prices are approximately $29.82 for Vector and $26.20 for Nova. The zero-expected-return prices are separately identified inside the calculation disclosure. Altering the draft requirement changes only this local sensitivity calculation and its export; it does not alter a specialist probability, valuation input, published stance, rank, source timestamp or account state.

This inversion is conditional on fixed probabilities, terminal equity values, cash-distribution assumptions and proportional costs. Production finance may make dilution, financing, costs or scenario weights dependent on the entry price. Then the algebraic shortcut is not sufficient: the existing financial owner must solve and qualify the complete dependency, identify non-unique or infeasible solutions, and preserve uncertainty.

These displayed prices are not fair-value targets, executable limit orders, Buy boundaries or safe-loss levels. A zero expected return does not mean zero probability of loss. The common fixture omits dividends; a real total-return model must include all relevant cash distributions and corporate actions consistently.
