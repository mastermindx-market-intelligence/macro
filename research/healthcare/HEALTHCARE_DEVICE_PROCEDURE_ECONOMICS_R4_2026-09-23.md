# Healthcare R4 — devices, procedures and the economics of actual use

Research date: 2026-09-23. Operation: `gmi-healthcare-deep-research-20260923-sol-001`. Carrier: Macro draft/HOLD PR #7788. Principal-led original research and synthesis for the existing GMI Healthcare program. This is not an accepted production schema, final implementation design, complete device universe, clinical recommendation, security ranking or trade authorization. Fable's implementation-orchestration handoff remains deferred.

## 1. Research result and boundaries

R1 established the Healthcare research foundation; R2 separated therapeutic progress from economic rights; R3 mapped supplier activity to earnings, cash investment and ownership. R4 tests the next connection: **when a device becomes available or more widely used, which activity increases, who pays, who earns, and what must change before shareholder expectations improve?**

The research spans surgical systems and orthopaedics, cardiac intervention, glucose monitoring and insulin delivery, imaging equipment, diagnostic services, and a first ambulatory-provider comparison. These are deliberately discriminating cases, not a census. The principal contribution is an evidence-backed method for distinguishing an installed machine from useful utilization, an authorized indication from paid access, a clinical result from a financial result, and a system-wide business measure from economics retained by common shareholders.

The proposed extension to the existing model is:

`company/business → device family and version → clinical indication and patient context → jurisdiction and authorization → care workflow/site → activity actually delivered → payment/contract → retained economics → expectation change`

This is a relationship proposal for existing GMI/BioCatalyst/identity/evidence/publication owners. It does not establish a second registry, evidence graph, ingestion service, medical decision system or page template. The Robotics work remains the owner of overlapping physical technology and BOM research. Healthcare should explain the care workflow and economics rather than research the same motors, optics or robotic components again.

## 2. Five linked but non-interchangeable questions

A device story should answer five questions separately. First, what clinical or technical evidence changed for the specified population and comparator? Second, what regulatory permission now exists for the exact version and jurisdiction? Third, which patients and providers can actually access the workflow, including coverage, staffing and facilities? Fourth, what is the commercial mechanism: capital sale, operating lease, usage payment, implant, disposable, service, subscription or a combination? Finally, how do the resulting revenue, costs, reinvestment and financing compare with the prior expectations for the business and security?

An answer at one layer cannot stand in for another. The proposed page should offer a useful explanation when a link is unknown instead of hiding the missing link behind a green score. A research-priority flag may highlight an unresolved, consequential link; it must not become a stock recommendation or trade input by itself.

### Working measurement dictionary

| Measure | What it can describe | What it does not establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Placement | A system placed during a period | Upfront sale, installed-base utilization, or successful patient outcome |
| Ending installed base | Systems counted at a date | Average available system-days, active sites, or procedures per comparable cohort |
| Procedure count | Defined procedures performed in a period | Unique patients, all market demand, manufacturer revenue or physician profit |
| Shipped units | Product entering a channel | Dispensing, active use, paid patient-days, adherence or additional patients |
| Recurring revenue | Revenue under a stated accounting/contract definition | Guaranteed renewal, constant margin, or absence of replacement costs |
| Regulatory decision | Scoped permission or restriction | Payer authorization, local readiness, completed treatment or payment |
| Orders/backlog | Contractual demand under issuer definitions | Current revenue, cash receipts, non-cancellability or clinical value |
| ARR | Annualized contract metric under a stated definition | Revenue recognized in the quarter or cash collected |
| Same-facility growth | Change within a defined comparable operating set | Growth of the whole portfolio or a constant case/payer mix |
| Revenue per case | Revenue divided by specified cases | Pure unit-price change, identical clinical complexity or equal profitability |

These are analytical distinctions, not universal accounting definitions. Preserve the issuer's definition and make any normalization explicit.

## 3. Surgical and orthopaedic platforms: installed hardware is only one economic layer

### Intuitive: the lease categories are nested

Intuitive reported 468 da Vinci placements in Q2, including 254 operating leases, of which 131 were usage-based. Its da Vinci procedure growth was approximately 15%; combined da Vinci/Ion growth was approximately 16%. Instruments/accessories revenue covers both platforms. [R4-S01]

Those disclosures create a precise modeling requirement. Usage-based leases are a subset of operating leases, not another category to add to them. The remaining 214 placements are only established as outside the disclosed operating-lease category; this research does not label all of them outright purchases. The reported ending installed base is not a substitute for a same-cohort, average-period utilization denominator.

The investment question is therefore not merely whether placements are accelerating. It is whether new and existing installations generate the procedure mix and contractual revenue expected, and how much customer financing, depreciation, service and training the supplier must support. Lower upfront customer cost could help adoption while shifting supplier cash timing. That is a hypothesis to investigate, not a conclusion that operating leases are economically superior.

The proposed consumer should link placements, relevant installed cohorts, utilization and the disclosed revenue streams, retaining unknowns when the company does not provide a complete reconciliation. A ratio made from instruments revenue and only one platform's procedures must be blocked unless its scope is reconciled.

### Orthopaedics: keep implant adoption, robotic assistance and reporting changes distinct

Stryker's SEC-hosted Q2 release attributes Orthopaedics organic growth of 8.6% to volume. It also describes an organizational change combining orthopaedic instruments with Other Orthopaedics in Ortho Tech while moving other portfolios. [R4-S03] A newly enlarged reporting line is not organic expansion of the old line. A robotic-system association does not show that all implant sales came through that platform, or that implant outcomes improved.

Zimmer Biomet's August release reports ROSA Shoulder clearance and first cases, while its growth definitions separately account for acquisition timing. [R4-S04] Clearance, early cases, installed systems, routine utilization and economic contribution are different stages. Treat the product statement as a release-date observation; exact version-level letters and a September-wide authorization census were not acquired.

For future research, record procedure family, primary versus revision setting where disclosed, implant attachment, implant price/mix, equipment arrangements, surgeon training and operating-site readiness. Do not copy a robot's exposure weight onto every implant or procedure. The stronger thesis is not the longest product list; it is a defensible explanation of where adoption changes earnings, what can slow the change, and how quickly competitive substitution can occur.

### Medtronic: organic growth is not necessarily equal-calendar growth

Medtronic's fiscal first quarter ended July 31, 2026. It estimated that an additional fiscal week benefited first-quarter organic growth by approximately $570 million. [R4-S02] That is not the same period as a June-quarter peer. The issuer's organic definition and a calendar-normalized comparison must be stored separately. This dossier does not invent a corrected growth rate by subtracting that estimate without the corresponding comparable denominator and business-week allocation.

## 4. Cardiac intervention: evidence, population, access and commercial rights

### PFA: the comparator determines what was learned

ADVENT randomized 305 patients to pulsed-field ablation and 302 to thermal ablation in drug-refractory paroxysmal atrial fibrillation. The reported one-year efficacy comparison met non-inferiority; its difference interval included zero. [R4-S06] This is not a demonstration that all PFA systems are superior to all alternatives, nor evidence for a different untreated persistent-AF population. It does not rule out uncommon or longer-term harms.

Boston Scientific's July release discusses AVANTGUARD against antiarrhythmic drugs in previously untreated persistent AF. It also reports a minority investment of approximately 34% in MiRus and an exclusive option concerning the TAVR business. [R4-S05] These belong in different records: a sponsor-reported clinical comparison, and an economic-rights relationship. Neither establishes a PFA-versus-PFA clinical ranking or completed full ownership of MiRus.

The proposed PFA view should explain technology/platform version, clinical population, comparator, follow-up, formal endpoint conclusion and care-site constraints. Commercial research should then examine the relevant catheter or system contract, procedure adoption, competing workflows, customer training, utilization and retained margin. Faster execution of one procedure stage may be commercially helpful, but a full-room throughput or provider-profit gain needs its own measurement.

### TAVR: three clocks must not collapse into one event

Edwards' July Q2 report describes TAVR sales of $1.26 billion, up 11.3% reported and 10.5% constant currency, and anticipated a September CMS decision. [R4-S07] The July expectation must remain historical once a September decision exists. A 2024 clinical publication, a later device authorization and a September 2026 coverage decision are not three independent demonstrations of clinical efficacy.

EARLY TAVR compared early intervention with clinical surveillance in 901 patients. The reported primary endpoint was a composite of death, stroke or unplanned cardiovascular hospitalization. It occurred in 26.8% versus 45.3%; 87.0% of surveillance-assigned patients underwent valve replacement during median 3.8-year follow-up. [R4-S08]

The useful inference is narrower than a broad survival claim. The composite result does not establish a separate statistically demonstrated mortality benefit, and the comparison is not simply treatment versus never receiving treatment. Intervention timing, eligible population, comparator evolution and follow-up affect both the clinical interpretation and the economic opportunity. No patient-level treatment recommendation follows here.

CMS's September 10 final decision distinguishes symptomatic severe AS from asymptomatic severe AS. The latter requires a CMS-approved coverage-with-evidence-development study; the final text also retains heart-team and hospital requirements. [R4-S09] A general ASC-expansion narrative cannot silently move this hospital-scoped pathway into a freestanding center.

On September 23, the CMS study page displayed no entries under its new September-10-to-present CED heading, followed by a separate historical list. It also describes transition arrangements for prior studies. [R4-S10] This is an observed page state, not proof that all coverage is unavailable or that existing trials lost coverage. The future system must distinguish new-period study availability, prior-study continuity and the individual's applicable coverage path. Counting historic entries as immediately available new access would misstate the opportunity.

### Site of care: a rule change is an input, not a completed business forecast

CMS's CY2026 OPPS/ASC final-rule fact sheet describes expansion of the ASC covered-procedure list. [R4-S11] This wave did not acquire the code-level addenda or compute individual facility payments. It therefore does not assert that a particular procedure code is newly covered, that every clinically eligible patient can use an ASC, or that a specific provider earns a quoted margin.

Before estimating a site-of-care shift, research the final effective code, payer, clinical scope, provider accreditation/readiness, physician arrangements, contracted payment, patient cost-sharing and local capacity. A proposed future rule must not overwrite an effective rule. Coverage eligibility, service provision, clean claim submission, adjudication and collection need separate dates where material.

## 5. Diabetes technology: permission, compatibility, channel and persistence

### Monitoring and delivery are related but distinct workflows

FDA's June 12 Stelo announcement extends OTC clearance to age two and older for people not using insulin, with additional scope limitations. [R4-S13] The prior adult-only announcement is not a complete current label. That expansion does not by itself establish automated-insulin-delivery compatibility, paid patient adoption or Medicare eligibility.

CMS LCD L33822 has separate diabetes, prescription/training, label-consistency, treatment/history and visit requirements; continued coverage includes recurring clinical follow-up. [R4-S14] Preserve the observed policy version and payer scope. Do not turn one OTC clearance into an automatic reimbursement expansion, and do not treat a coverage criterion as individualized medical guidance.

The proposed compatibility relationship must identify the exact sensor, pump/controller, algorithm/software version, geography and effective date. A broad company partnership or shared technology category is insufficient. Lack of an acquired compatibility record is unknown, not proof of incompatibility.

### Dexcom: revenue channels are not patient counts

Dexcom's Q2 revenue table reports $1,102.4 million through distribution and $206.0 million direct, totaling $1,308.4 million. [R4-S12] The distribution share is a revenue-channel measurement. It is not a percentage of patients, the share reimbursed through a particular benefit, or evidence of patient adherence. The missing links are dispensing, active users, actual wear, replacement and discontinuation, channel inventory, net realization and collection.

For a recurring monitoring thesis, the proposed economic unit is paid patient-time reconciled to units and reported revenue. A longer nominal wear duration can change units per patient without changing the patient population. Free replacements and channel fill can change shipment counts without creating new paid users. These are modeling possibilities; no real-world persistence estimates were acquired here.

### Tandem: pay-as-you-go changes the revenue interpretation

Tandem reports more than 33,000 pump shipments and says the pharmacy channel generated 10% of US sales in Q2. Its table separately reports an operating loss of $13.792 million, adjusted EBITDA of $6.418 million and free cash outflow of $38.673 million. [R4-S15]

The channel percentage describes sales, not patient or shipment share. A shipment-to-revenue ratio is not a clean selling price when upfront pumps, recurring supplies and pay-as-you-go arrangements coexist. Positive adjusted EBITDA does not establish positive operating profit or cash generation. Product submissions and version-specific interoperability claims in the release must remain dated observations until their later status is checked.

The future research should compare contract economics over an appropriate customer cohort: acquisition cost, initial placement/payment, recurring margin, persistence, support and replacement obligations. A lower upfront hurdle can be an adoption opportunity and a financing/cash-timing obligation simultaneously.

### Insulet: two incidents must not be merged or spread to unaffected components

FDA's March Omnipod 5 alert identifies a device/model and particular lots; subsequent page updates change information about that incident. [R4-S17] The May announcement explicitly describes a separate correction involving specified lots across additional Omnipod product families and states that the issue does not affect CGM systems/readings. It is a company announcement hosted by FDA, not a second independent investigation. [R4-S18]

Insulet's earnings footnote identifies estimated warranty costs associated with the March and May corrections. [R4-S16] This does not justify attributing the entire GAAP-to-adjusted earnings difference to the incidents, or treating the exclusions as economically irrelevant.

The proposed event representation separates initiating notice, scope expansion, corrected publication, replacement activity and later resolution evidence. It preserves event dates, affected versions/lots, jurisdiction, source role and unresolved exposure. Do not equate replacement shipments with new paid customers or propagate one component defect to compatible sensors. No failure-rate estimate is justified from report counts and a shipment headline alone.

## 6. Imaging and diagnostics: installed equipment, orders, use and software are different layers

### GE HealthCare: preserve the recast business perimeter

In Q2, GE HealthCare combined Imaging and Advanced Visualization Solutions into Advanced Imaging Solutions and recast historical segments. It reported $168 million operating cash flow, $100 million capex and $68 million free cash flow; operating cash included a $107 million tariff refund. [R4-S20]

A comparison to the old Imaging line is not automatically like-for-like. A mechanical subtraction of the cash refund produces a different cash subtotal, but is not an accepted normalized earnings or future-cash forecast. The correct research response is to display the bridge and specify remaining timing/accounting assumptions, not attribute all cash improvement to device demand or AI adoption.

Keep product sales, services, radiopharmaceutical/contrast-related activities, order commitments and installed-machine utilization separate where disclosures permit. The manufacturer and the imaging-center operator participate in related but different revenue pools.

### Siemens Healthineers and Philips: order and earnings definitions can diverge

Siemens Healthineers' fiscal Q3 ended June 30, 2026. Its highlighted 1.27 book-to-bill refers to equipment; its guidance update lowered expected revenue growth while increasing expected EPS, with tariff refunds affecting earnings. [R4-S21] A higher EPS forecast is therefore not sufficient evidence that the underlying procedure or diagnostic-volume outlook improved. A narrower equipment ratio is not directly rankable against another issuer's total-company order ratio.

Philips reported comparable order intake down 1% and identified a 4.2-percentage-point refund contribution within a 16.4% adjusted EBITA margin. [R4-S22] A 12.2% subtraction is only a mechanical illustration using rounded disclosed measures, not a fully normalized or forward margin. Preserve operating demand, booked orders, accepted installations, adjusted reporting and unusual cash effects separately.

### Abbott: ownership changes and internal transfers can masquerade as theme acceleration

Abbott's report states that Exact Sciences was acquired on March 23, 2026; its comparable-sales calculation includes prior and current acquired-business sales. It also identifies a transfer of Amulet-related sales from Structural Heart to Electrophysiology, including a recast $55 million in Q2 2025. [R4-S19]

Those disclosures support two different controls. The acquired operating business should not silently remain an independent company exposure with duplicated revenues. An internal transfer must not look like an economic loss from one clinical theme or newly won demand in another. This research does not provide a complete current instrument/delisting audit; the existing security owner must reconcile any eventual basket change.

Organic, comparable and constant-currency labels are not interchangeable across issuers. The consumer should retain the formula, period, acquired perimeter and recast series. Treat relevant corporate revenue growth as one observation, not proof that a particular sensor, software product or clinical application drove it.

### RadNet: a real provider comparison, not an equipment proxy

RadNet reports aggregate advanced-imaging volume growth of 21.2% versus 9.6% on a same-center basis. Its $32.4 million Digital Health revenue includes intersegment revenue; $105.5 million ARR is a defined annualized active-contract metric. [R4-S23]

The distinction matters for interpreting healthcare AI. Internal deployment can create operating value but is not independent third-party revenue validation. ARR is not quarterly recognized revenue. Total growth and same-center growth answer different questions. Neither proves that AI caused incremental throughput without an appropriate counterfactual.

A future operator view should inspect appointment availability, completed scans, staffing, hours, modality mix, referral access, authorization, contracted collection and equipment cost. A software-speed claim should identify which stage accelerated and whether another stage remains binding. No independent causal study of RadNet's AI productivity was acquired in this wave.

## 7. Provider bridge: why increasing revenue need not mean increasing cases

Tenet's Ambulatory report shows same-facility system-wide revenue up 5.0%, cases down 1.2%, and revenue per case up 6.3%, attributed to acuity and service mix. System-wide measures include unconsolidated facilities. Adjusted EBITDA of $542 million is separately presented as $330 million after NCI. [R4-S24]

This is a concrete counterexample to using provider revenue growth as a direct estimate of device demand or procedure growth. The approximate multiplicative bridge is `(1 - 0.012) × (1 + 0.063) - 1 = 5.0244%`; rounding explains its proximity to the reported 5.0%. Revenue per case is not pure price. The EBITDA/NCI difference is not automatically current cash distributed, and EBITDA after NCI is not free cash flow to common equity.

The next provider/payer research wave should preserve consolidated, system-wide, same-facility and equity-accounted scopes. The same care episode can generate a device sale, facility payment, physician payment and payer expense; adding those together is not an unduplicated healthcare market size.

## 8. Original economic models and counterexamples

The following models are analytical proposals. Illustrative numbers are invented to test logic, not measured operating times, reimbursement rates, device prices or clinical effects.

### A. Capacity gains are constrained by the whole workflow

For a simplified single-room day, use the minimum of booked demand, qualified team capacity, recovery capacity and the integer number of complete room cycles that fit. A more realistic implementation must handle overlap, cancellations, case complexity and multiple resources; the simple model is only a falsifier for naive proportional forecasts.

Assume 480 available minutes and a 120-minute whole-room cycle: four cases fit. Reducing that cycle to 100 minutes still permits only four whole cases. Reducing it to 90 minutes permits five by room time, but still only four if staffing/recovery can support four. The time saving may have other value, such as reducing overtime or variability; it is not automatically monetized as more cases. These are not estimates for any named procedure.

### B. Supplier and provider economics can move in opposite directions

In an illustrative case, net collection of $5,000 and variable cost of $3,500 yields $1,500 contribution per case, or $6,000 at four cases. If a new workflow raises variable cost to $3,900, four cases generate $4,400; five generate $5,500. Faster throughput alone has not recovered the original contribution. The example omits fixed capital, financing, complications, quality, patient benefits and differences in payment, so it cannot decide whether a real technology is worthwhile.

Its purpose is to force the product to ask whose economics changed. The manufacturer may earn more per procedure while the provider needs a different payment, volume or cost structure. A payer's avoided downstream spending and a patient's clinical benefit are distinct outcomes with potentially different horizons. Do not silently count all hypothetical benefits as cash earned by the device company.

### C. Patient-time and shipped units can diverge

Assume a hypothetical 300 paid patient-days with complete wear and no replacements. Ten-day units require 30 sensors; fifteen-day units require 20. This is a unit-count identity, not a revenue forecast. Price, realized wear, channel inventory, adherence, reimbursement and replacement can each change. Record those assumptions before extrapolating a longer-wear product's economics.

### D. Growth decomposition needs interaction and comparable populations

A case-volume change and revenue-per-case change combine multiplicatively, not by simple addition. Same-center and aggregate growth should not be combined as independent drivers. A new facility, reporting transfer or acquisition changes the population being measured. A current-period revenue forecast needs a defined starting population and explicit bridge rather than a sum of headlines.

### E. Financial measurements need tolerances, not cosmetic correction

GE HealthCare's rounded product and service rows sum to $5,294 million versus displayed total revenue of $5,295 million; its release explicitly warns that rounding can prevent sums. [R4-S20] The consumer should preserve the source and tolerate a documented rounding residual rather than overwrite a value or manufacture a missing business. A larger unexplained discrepancy should remain a failed reconciliation.

### F. Authorization and current ownership are not inferred from convenient registries

FDA explains that 510(k) clearance involves substantial equivalence; it is not a generic finding of clinical superiority. Its predicate-search guidance warns that the database retains original applicant/trade-name information rather than updating it for current ownership. [R4-S25, R4-S26]

FDA's UDI guidance distinguishes model/version identifiers from production identifiers, and GUDID stores the former, not actual lot/serial values. [R4-S28] Consequently a model match alone cannot determine that an individual product belongs to an affected lot. A current labeler or original applicant also needs a separate, dated corporate-ownership relationship before assigning public-equity exposure.

## 9. Safety evidence without false precision

FDA states that MAUDE reports cannot alone establish adverse-event rates, changes in rates, cross-device comparisons or causation. [R4-S27] A count is not a rate without valid exposure and reporting assumptions. Zero retrieved reports is not proof of safety. A public warning can affect reporting intensity; that does not independently establish a change in true incidence.

For the proposed product, represent reported concern, formal recall/correction, regulatory action and validated clinical findings separately. Explain source role and preserve supplement/correction relationships. Clinical priority and severity should follow appropriate evidence and expert review, not stock sentiment. A device-safety alert may raise an investment research question about replacement expense, adoption or liability; it is not a validated equity-return forecast.

Keep the user-facing output non-clinical: what evidence changed, the specified affected scope, uncertainty, financial exposure questions and links to authoritative source information. Do not generate personal treatment instructions from an investment-intelligence page.

## 10. What could change valuation expectations — research framework, not price targets

The proposed investment model should identify whether new information changes the size, growth, durability or risk of a cash-flow stream. It must also identify the pre-event expectation. A business improvement that was already expected need not increase estimated security value; a temporary accounting benefit need not improve the recurring business. R4 has not acquired point-in-time consensus or a defensible market-implied operating baseline.

| Workflow/business | Input that could change a thesis | Evidence needed before translating it to security value | Contrary case |
|---|---|---|---|
| Surgical platform | Active procedure growth, mix, lease utilization and recurring attachment | Comparable installed cohorts, net revenue mechanism, service/capital cost and expected penetration | Idle systems, lower-value mix, financing burden or competition |
| Cardiac intervention | Scoped clinical evidence, access expansion and adoption | Population/comparator fidelity, final coverage, ready sites, net catheter/implant economics | Conditional access, treatment substitution, slow site readiness or weaker net realization |
| Monitoring/delivery | New paid users, persistence, supported versions and channel access | Patient-time, returns/replacements, contracts, acquisition/support cost | Channel fill, churn, free units, compatibility gaps or correction costs |
| Imaging equipment | Orders converting to accepted installations and subsequent service | Backlog definition, cancellation/acceptance timing, funding and mix | Deferred projects, constrained staffing or weak diagnostic business |
| Imaging software/provider | External recurring contracts or measurable operating productivity | External versus intersegment revenue, renewals and a credible causal comparison | Internal accounting growth without external adoption, staffing bottleneck or implementation cost |
| Ambulatory provider | Acuity/service mix and retained contribution | Comparable cases, contracted collection, cost mix, NCI and investment | Payer-mix deterioration, facility additions mistaken for organic growth or partner cash leakage |

These are original thesis variables, not scored securities. Future work must incorporate balance-sheet obligations, dilution/buybacks, lease exposure, investment needs and investor expectations through existing financial owners. Do not build a new trade-selection or sizing policy from research summaries.

## 11. Proposed product consumers and bounded future slices

These proposals are intended for the shared `state_of_themes.html` architecture. They are not yet approved implementation tasks or dispatch packets.

**Usage-and-economics panel.** From a subtheme and company, show the activity measured, period, population, contract basis, related revenue and missing conversion links. A da Vinci placement should lead to lease/usage distinctions without pretending that every installation supplies a measured utilization denominator.

**Evidence-to-access timeline.** Show the clinical result, version-specific regulatory event, payer decision and operational access as distinct dated events. The TAVR case should preserve the July expected decision and September final outcome, with the new-period study-list observation separate from the historic list. Empty data should be visibly unknown/not observed, not converted into universal denial.

**Version/compatibility and safety view.** Show device families, specific versions and known supported combinations with jurisdiction/time. A lot-scoped correction should not flag an entire compatible ecosystem. Source corrections should update one event rather than inflate independent incident counts.

**Provider/supplier comparison.** Let the user inspect different participants in the same workflow without adding their revenues into an unduplicated total. Preserve facility, physician, vendor, payer and patient perspectives. Use illustrative scenarios only when inputs are visibly labeled; do not fill missing contracted payment or real throughput with model guesses.

**Measurement-quality explanation.** Show why two apparent peers are not directly comparable: extra fiscal week, recast segment, equipment-only versus total-company orders, system-wide versus consolidated, ARR versus recognized revenue, or differing cash definitions. This explanation should accompany the investment synthesis, not become a separate provenance-only product.

A future vertical slice is useful only if a persona can traverse a real theme to a real dated observation and understand the economic implication and uncertainty. Each slice needs source-backed fixtures, correction/unknown states, a real consumer, focused tests and browser proof on the actual path. None of those production proofs has occurred in R4.

## 12. Data feasibility, ownership and acquisition limits

Issuer releases and filings can support selected revenue, contractual, scope and accounting observations. They do not establish patient counts, private net pricing, whole-market shares or causal clinical benefits. Original trial abstracts supply bounded evidence; they do not replace protocols, analysis plans, supplements and later follow-up. CMS/FDA pages supply specific authorization/coverage context; snapshot dates and later corrections matter.

The AccessGUDID documentation describes a v2 lookup using a device identifier. A single web-tool request using the FDA-listed Omnipod identifier returned `Internal Error`. [R4-S29] No successful record resolution, API readiness or historical join is claimed. This is a path/tool observation, not a provider-wide outage, and documentation availability is not production integration proof. The DI/PI distinction remains supported independently by FDA documentation.

Preserve source publication time, effective date, reporting period, observation time and later correction time separately through existing evidence owners. Historical research must not use a current recast, current owner or expanded label as if it was available at a prior date. Store original synthesis and permitted public references, not restricted commercial claims corpora or patient data. Commercial licensing, collection terms, cost, refresh reliability, point-in-time completeness and production ingestion remain dedicated obligations.

Public source absence is not proof that a product, contract, customer or study does not exist. An empty displayed section, a failed API read and a provider's legal exclusion are three different states. The proposed UI and machine consumers must preserve that distinction.
