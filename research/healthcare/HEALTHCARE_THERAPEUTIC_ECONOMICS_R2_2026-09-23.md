# Healthcare R2 — therapeutic progress, economic ownership and investability

Research date: 2026-09-23. Operation: `gmi-healthcare-deep-research-20260923-sol-001`. Carrier: Macro draft/HOLD PR #7788. Principal-led original synthesis; no foundational research delegated. This is a research proposal and evidence dossier, not an approved implementation contract, financial recommendation, clinical recommendation or production dataset.

## 1. What this wave resolves

R1 established the structural research foundation. R2 investigates a harder question: **when a therapeutic theme advances, what exactly improves for which company, and what could prevent the improvement from reaching shareholders?**

The proposed minimum analytical unit is an asset, indication, patient context, territory, economic right and observation time. A ticker or technology tag is only a navigation entry. The same product can create product sales for one company, alliance revenue for another, royalties for an inventor and manufacturing revenue for a supplier. Those are different claims on an overlapping economic pool, not additive estimates of the market.

Three comparisons drive this dossier: metabolic/obesity therapies; advanced oncology; and cell/gene therapies. They are deliberately not a complete Healthcare universe. Provider/payer, device, tools, manufacturing-services and broader therapeutic dossiers remain owed.

This wave supports five distinct judgments. It does not compress them into one sentiment score:

1. What clinical evidence changed, and how strong is the comparison?
2. What regulatory permission exists for which patients and jurisdiction?
3. Can eligible patients actually receive and continue the treatment?
4. Which company recognizes revenue, bears costs and retains economics?
5. What expectation was already embedded in the security price?

The last question requires contemporaneous valuation, consensus and financing evidence not acquired in R2. No stock recommendation or claim of underpricing follows from this dossier.

## 2. Metabolic therapies: volume, formulation and price must be separate

### A. Lilly: strong demand is not an unqualified pricing-power signal

Lilly's Q2 2026 release reports worldwide revenue of $22.974 billion, with company-attributed volume growth of 60% and a 13% negative realized-price effect. It reports $9.943 billion for Mounjaro, $4.928 billion for Zepbound and $98 million for Foundayo. These are period-specific company disclosures, not a prescription-derived revenue estimate. Brand, molecule, indication and territory must remain separate: brand sales alone do not partition diabetes from obesity demand. [R2-S02]

**Research implication:** compare paid treatment volume, net realization, mix, capacity cost, persistence and commercial spending. Demand growth and price pressure can coexist. Do not convert a volume-growth headline into an assumed proportional margin or earnings increase.

### B. Foundayo: a concrete inventor-to-commercializer economic link

FDA approved Foundayo/orforglipron on April 1, 2026 for adult chronic weight management within the specified label. Lilly's June 30, 2026 filing identifies worldwide development/commercialization rights licensed from Chugai. Chugai receives tiered royalties on worldwide net sales in a disclosed range from mid-single-digit percentages to low teens; Lilly recognizes these as cost of sales. The precise tier schedule is not supplied. [R2-S01, R2-S03]

**Research implication:** Chugai is a source-backed economic participant, not a speculative supplier inferred from a technology basket. Record the right and range without inventing a midpoint royalty, annual earnings contribution, exclusivity expiry or parent-company look-through. A drug's inventor, license holder and commercial revenue reporter can be different entities.

### C. Novo: test the evergreen capacity-shortage narrative

Novo's Q2 report attributes gross-margin pressure partly to around DKK 3 billion of one-time costs for right-sizing manufacturing-capacity agreements. Its adjusted Q2 gross margin was 78.2%, versus 82.7% a year earlier. The report separately discusses realized-price pressure and distinguishes reported from adjusted figures affected by rebate-provision comparisons. [R2-S04]

At the September 21, 2026 capital-markets day, management emphasized network optimization and productivity after capacity expansion. Its oral-capacity roadmap separates active-ingredient output, tableting and cost competitiveness. The deck labels the margin/cost illustrations as illustrative, not financial guidance. [R2-S05]

**Inference, not an industry verdict:** these disclosures invalidate an unconditional thesis that every additional GLP-1 prescription perpetually increases the scarcity rent earned by every capacity builder. They do not prove all manufacturing is abundant, identify cancelled suppliers, or establish industry-wide oversupply. The next tools/manufacturing wave must identify where constraints remain and where capacity relief or internalization changes supplier economics.

### D. Oral is a route, not a complete manufacturing taxonomy

The research model should separate route, molecular/platform type, formulation, presentation, production process and actual vendor relationship. Lilly describes orforglipron as a small molecule; Novo's manufacturing roadmap separately discusses peptide synthesis, oral drug product, formulation/bioavailability and injectable presentations. [R2-S05, R2-S20]

An oral formulation should not automatically inherit an injector, cartridge or sterile-fill dependency. Equally, oral administration does not by itself establish cheap manufacturing, unconstrained ingredient supply, better adherence or superior outcomes. These require distinct evidence.

### E. A strong number can accompany a failed trial

Novo's February 23 REDEFINE 4 topline release reports an 84-week, randomized, open-label comparison of CagriSema with tirzepatide involving 809 people. The primary non-inferiority endpoint was not met. The efficacy-estimand weight changes were 23.0% versus 25.5%; the treatment-regimen estimand yielded 20.2% versus 23.6%. [R2-S06]

**Research implication:** preserve the formal endpoint conclusion, estimand and comparator together. A large absolute change is not automatically comparative success. Failure to establish non-inferiority is not synonymous with no benefit, nor a substitute for the full confidence interval and prespecified margin when making an inferiority claim. Those details remain unacquired here.

For contrast, the original SURMOUNT-5 report studied 751 adults with obesity without diabetes, at 72 weeks, comparing specified maximum-tolerated weekly injectable doses. It reported 20.2% versus 13.7% weight reductions. [R2-S07] Its comparator, population, horizon and treatment versions must travel with those numbers. Do not compare its best headline with another trial's different estimand, newer formulation or different follow-up and call the result a head-to-head ranking.

## 3. Advanced oncology: indication-specific evidence and layered ownership

### A. Pluvicto: an earlier treatment setting is not proof of longer survival

FDA's July 31, 2026 expansion concerns PSMA-positive metastatic androgen-pathway-modulation-naive or sensitive prostate cancer, with an androgen-receptor pathway inhibitor. The PSMAddition primary radiographic progression-free-survival analysis had hazard ratio 0.72; overall-survival data were immature. Patient selection can use Locametz or another approved PSMA PET product. [R2-S08]

**Research implication:** preserve the combination relationship and the eligible diagnostic alternatives. Do not infer that a companion-imaging need is an exclusive revenue right for one tracer, that an added combination necessarily displaces the backbone therapy, or that a progression endpoint establishes overall-survival benefit.

### B. Different analyses of the same study are not independent discoveries

Novartis's same-day announcement reports both the original PSMAddition analysis and a subsequent updated analysis: updated rPFS HR 0.67, with an OS trend HR 0.80 and 95% interval 0.63–1.01. It also describes five US sites as operational **or under construction**. [R2-S09]

**Resolved apparent disagreement:** FDA's 0.72 and the company's 0.67 refer to different analysis snapshots, not necessarily inconsistent results. Keep the study identity, endpoint and analysis version. Never average the hazard ratios, count two corroborating trials, or turn the OS trend into a demonstrated survival conclusion. Exact data-cutoff dates still need the underlying reports.

**Capacity implication:** distinguish site announced, built, qualified, operational and delivering product. A combined operating/construction count is not an operating-site census. Manufacturer delivery claims are attributed operational statements, not independent throughput or service-level proof.

Novartis reported Q2 2026 Pluvicto sales of $651 million. [R2-S10] This demonstrates commercial activity, but neither product sales growth nor a label expansion establishes Novartis-wide earnings sensitivity, supplier pricing power or a stock-price forecast.

### C. Enhertu: avoid counting the same market twice

AstraZeneca's H1/Q2 2026 report gives combined Enhertu sales of $2.961 billion in H1, compared with AstraZeneca's own Enhertu product revenue of $1.719 billion. For Q2, its $888 million of Enhertu product revenue includes $550 million of alliance revenue. The report separately identifies Daiichi's US sales recognition and a Japan royalty. [R2-S15]

AstraZeneca's US approval announcement explicitly describes its gross-profit share as alliance revenue while Daiichi records US product sales. That announcement is dated January 2025; use it for the stated accounting relationship, not as an exhaustive current clinical label. [R2-S16]

**Research implication:** market sales, consolidated company revenue, gross-profit sharing and territorial royalties are separate measures. Build product-market totals from deduplicated external sales, not the sum of every partner's reported line. Do not apply one US accounting rule to every territory, or label all alliance revenue as a sales royalty.

### D. CARVYKTI: the reporting bridge is observable, not guessed

Legend's Q2 2026 release reconciles $657 million of CARVYKTI net trade sales to $328.5 million at a 50% reporting share, then $326.1 million of Legend collaboration revenue after a $2.4 million foreign-exchange variance. It separately reports $136 million of cost of collaboration revenue. [R2-S14]

**Research implication:** do not call the collaboration-revenue line profit or infer a contract change from a slightly sub-50% observed ratio. The exact current territory-by-territory contractual exceptions were not fully acquired in this wave. Do not extend this quarter's reporting bridge into a universal worldwide rights rule.

The same release's early LB2501 in-vivo CAR-T result reports responses in six of six higher-dose patients and complete responses in five. [R2-S14] A small early cohort is not evidence equivalent to randomized late-stage efficacy, nor proof that autologous commercial infrastructure has already been displaced.

## 4. Cell/gene therapies: approval can expand or contract; economics are not modality-wide

### A. Casgevy: the regulatory record needs application identity and time

FDA's current Casgevy page includes an age-two-and-older indication under STN 125787 while an older entry under STN 125785 retains age twelve and older. The July 1, 2026 FDA bulletin confirms the pediatric expansion. [R2-S11, R2-S19]

**Research implication:** preserve the application, indication, jurisdiction, effective date and supersession relationship. A current page may contain multiple application records. A historical age limit cannot be treated as the universal current label. Conversely, a US expansion does not establish foreign approval or payer coverage.

### B. Vertex and CRISPR: shared costs matter as much as shared upside

CRISPR's Q2 update confirms Vertex's lead role and a 60/40 division of program costs and profits. It reports net collaboration expense of $40.3 million despite increased Casgevy sales. Vertex separately reports Casgevy product revenue of $76 million for Q2. [R2-S12, R2-S13]

**Research implication:** the CRISPR right is not a 40% sales royalty. An analyst must distinguish product revenue, commercialization cost pools, R&D responsibilities and the partner's reported expense/income. Company-name or platform tags do not authorize extending this collaboration to its other pipeline assets.

For the future product, a one-time-therapy funnel must ask about referral, eligibility, payer authorization, treatment-center capacity, patient-specific processing where applicable, release, treatment and revenue recognition. These are proposed measurement stages, not a claim that counts for every stage are public or that every modality shares the same workflow.

### C. Elevidys: post-approval risk belongs inside the economic model

FDA's November 2025 action added a boxed liver-injury warning and limited Elevidys to ambulatory patients aged four and older with a confirmed DMD mutation. The current product page retains that scoped indication. [R2-S17, R2-S18]

Sarepta's Q2 2026 release reports $98.1 million of Elevidys net product revenue and attributes lower volume to the updated patient scope. It separately describes increased manufacturing deliveries to Roche and non-recurring collaboration effects. [R2-S21]

**Research implication:** approval is not an irreversible terminal state. Distinguish patient-scope contraction, clinical use, product shipments, recognized manufacturing revenue and partner milestones. Do not extend one product's safety outcome indiscriminately to all AAV, all gene therapies or gene editing. Do not use total corporate revenue as a clean proxy for patient demand when partner accounting and milestones are material.

The current full Roche territory/royalty/supply contract remains a follow-up acquisition item. This dossier establishes a disclosed supply relationship, not an invented contractual rate or supplier margin.

## 5. Proposed economic-transmission model

The following is original modeling guidance, not a calibrated forecasting system.

### Chronic therapies

Use a cohort/period view: paid patient-months multiplied by realized revenue per patient-month, reconciled to reported revenue with channel inventory, gross-to-net estimates, foreign exchange and recognition timing. Separate new starts, refills, switches, discontinuation and persistence. Prescriptions are events, not automatically unique patients; a cumulative prescription count cannot be substituted for point-in-time treated population.

### One-time and patient-specific therapies

Use mutually understood funnel states and period flows: eligible referrals, completed evaluations, authorizations, manufacturing starts where applicable, successful releases, treatments, and recognized sales. A referral pool is a stock; treatments in a quarter are a flow. Avoid multiplying prevalence by list price and presenting the result as annual obtainable revenue. Preserve conversion delays, attrition, release failures and treatment-site limits as unknown until measured.

### Licensors and collaborators

Use the actual contractual basis: net sales, specified gross profit, defined net commercial profit/loss, milestones, cost reimbursement, supply transactions or a combination. Store responsibility for costs and the reporting treatment. Do not replace undisclosed terms with zero, inferred percentages or the midpoint of a qualitative range. Milestone eligibility is neither cash receipt nor recurring revenue. Counterparty amounts require currency and period reconciliation.

### Suppliers

Ask which customer/product/process the supplier actually serves, whether the relationship is disclosed, and whether the contract rewards volume, reserved capacity, technology access or another deliverable. Track cancellations, internalization, qualification, yield, utilization and substitution. A new plant or broad capability statement is not enough to assign customer revenue or pricing power.

### Security-level implications

Keep separate views for current revenue materiality, retained contribution economics, pipeline option value, balance-sheet/financing exposure and cross-theme dependencies. None is a complete proxy for the others. A small current product can have large future option value; a large reported revenue stream can carry extensive shared costs. A positive therapeutic result can coexist with a disappointing security response when prior expectations were higher. This is a logical possibility, not a claim about an observed event return in R2.

The investment-research question should be: **which input changed relative to the prior thesis, how does it transmit to cash flow, and what was already expected?** No evidence-backed ranking is possible until those baselines exist.

## 6. Clinical-evidence comparison requirements

Before offering a comparative conclusion, retain: study identity; randomized versus uncontrolled design; blinding; treated and analysis populations; comparator and dose/formulation; disease stage and prior therapy; endpoint and hierarchy; estimand; missing-data handling; follow-up; absolute effect; uncertainty interval; safety; data cutoff; publication date; and sponsor/source limitations.

Require explicit abstention or a qualified description when a material field is absent. A research result can be decision-useful without being reducible to a score. Proposed comparison flags should explain the mismatch, not silently discard one trial.

Separate formal trial success, clinical magnitude, safety/tolerability, generalizability, regulatory action and commercial consequence. Do not transfer proof across those layers. A secondary or exploratory endpoint cannot silently replace a failed primary endpoint. An updated analysis should update a versioned study record, not create a new independent evidence count.

## 7. Falsifiable bottleneck hypotheses for the next wave

| Hypothesis to investigate | Evidence that would support it | Evidence that would weaken or falsify it |
|---|---|---|
| A specific manufacturing step constrains treatment growth | Product-specific qualified capacity, lead times, utilization and unmet orders at a dated geography | Qualified alternative capacity, lower lead times, contract right-sizing, internalization or demand below capacity |
| Diagnostic access constrains a defined label expansion | Testing coverage, turnaround, eligible-site gaps and missed treatment opportunities | Multiple accepted tests, adequate coverage and timely access; downstream constraint dominates |
| Patient-specific processing limits one-time therapy delivery | Cohort-linked starts, releases, failures, delays and infusions | Available slots with payer/referral bottlenecks instead; workflow substitution supported by sufficient evidence |
| Convenience creates incremental paid adoption | Cohort-adjusted new-to-class starts and persistence with net-price evidence | Predominantly cannibalized existing patients, high discontinuation or price erosion exceeding contribution benefit |
| A platform supplier benefits across competing therapeutic winners | Verified multi-customer rights/contracts and product/process relevance | Exclusivity, customer insourcing, pipeline cancellations, competing modality or immaterial contracted revenue |

The bottleneck record must specify its falsifier and next observation. Do not infer concentration from the number of vendors mentioned in press releases. Public supplier silence is missing information, not proof that a relationship does not exist.

## 8. Product implications to carry into the shared template design

Reuse existing GMI, BioCatalyst, evidence, company/security and publication owners. These are proposed consumers and fields, not a new graph, registry or ingestion service.

A useful Healthcare detail view should let the user move from theme to subtheme to company to asset, then inspect the indication, evidence snapshot, access constraints and economic right without losing context. The central explanation should answer: what changed; who earns what; which assumptions are unverified; what could reverse the thesis; and which observation comes next.

Proposed views are an economic-rights overlay, a treatment/access funnel, an evidence-comparison panel and a dated thesis-change view. They should share the adjacent template's navigation and components. Avoid a visually impressive but unqualified global supply graph.

Research-priority ordering may elevate a thesis with a large unresolved causal link. It must remain separate from stock desirability, portfolio weight or trade execution. A model-generated summary cannot originate or size trades from these records.

Suggested research acceptance examples: recover Foundayo's licensor without assigning an invented royalty rate; explain Legend's reported revenue reconciliation without calling it profit; preserve both PSMAddition analyses without double counting; show the Casgevy age change without globally rewriting historical records; reject the unconditional GLP-1 scarcity story when contrary evidence exists.
