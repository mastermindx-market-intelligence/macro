## 3. Central design hypothesis

**Healthcare needs linked clinical, physical, payment, and economic views—not one universal linear BOM.**

A practical analytical sequence is:

clinical need / care task → intervention or service → evidence → authorization where applicable → qualified delivery capacity → access/payment → actual utilization → economic rights → issuer cash-flow exposure.

This is a research model, not a claim that every healthcare business passes through an identical sequence. Hospitals, insurers, tools suppliers, investigational therapies, and approved products require different paths. Scientific/regulatory distinctions are grounded in S01–S05; payment distinctions in S09–S12; economic distinctions in S13–S15.

Three approaches were considered. A flat stock basket is simple but cannot preserve product/indication or partner economics. A new Healthcare graph could be richly structured but duplicates current GMI, BioCatalyst, identity, and evidence owners. The working preference is typed Healthcare facets and source-scoped observations composed through those existing owners. Exact schema extensions remain to be reconciled and approved; this document does not enroll new types.

The moat hypothesis is not more drug headlines. It is a continuously corrected explanation of which constraint is binding, what evidence would change that assessment, and how a specific entity's rights and economics transmit the change. This must be tested, not marketed as already achieved.

## 5. Minimum research object and relationship requirements

Treat these as questions that the incumbent data owners must be able to answer, not a newly implemented database schema.

**Program/product:** source-native name and aliases; exact formulation/model; disease/use; modality; target/biomarker where relevant; stage and jurisdiction; source-scoped identifiers; validated canonical binding or explicit unresolved state. Trial, regulatory application, marketed package, billing code, facility, and security identifiers must remain separate namespaces.

**Evidence:** source publisher and document; original versus copied announcement; exact locator; reported statement versus independent observation; study design/population/comparator; prespecified endpoint and timepoint; effect estimate and uncertainty where disclosed; safety context; publication and event dates; limitation; correction/supersession. A result claim must not be reduced to the word positive. No clinical treatment recommendation is produced.

**Commercial rights:** source-reported legal entities; product/indication/territory; exclusive/nonexclusive/co-commercialization role where supported; licensor/licensee; profit-share or royalty basis; manufacturing obligations; milestones and contingent development balances; announcement date versus effective date. Unknown rights remain unknown. A corporate filing can be evidence of the contract without exposing confidential undisclosed terms.

**Capacity:** product/process/site scope; qualification status; nameplate versus validated capability; announced versus available versus output; unit and time basis; yields/release constraints only where sourced; replacement/substitution evidence; delivery and care-site constraints. No sensitive operational manufacturing instructions are required for this investor-facing model.

**Economics:** numerator, denominator, fiscal period, currency, entity/consolidation basis, gross/net basis, booked versus forecast, product versus segment, and overlap/containment. Amounts shown in separate company accounts are not automatically additive market demand. Missing disclosure is not zero.

**Time:** retain business/event date, expected versus actual status, source publication, first public availability when established, observation/retention, and dataset vintage separately. Preserve partial precision. A month-only date must not become a specific-day catalyst. A build timestamp does not refresh the underlying study or spending year.

## 6. Source-backed stress cases and deductions

### A. Trial milestone is not a results catalyst

ClinicalTrials.gov defines primary completion around final primary-outcome data collection, not later analysis (S01). Proposed behavior: display registry completion and a separately evidenced public-readout window as different events. Unknown readout stays unknown. This is especially important before any retrospective event study: later registry revisions cannot be treated as historically known dates. The existing legacy clinical context must not silently acquire PIT status.

### B. Identifier and regulatory status are different facts

NDC listing does not establish FDA approval or payer coverage, and a labeler need not be the physical manufacturer (S03). Device 510(k) clearance and PMA approval must retain their actual pathway labels (S05). The Purple Book distinguishes licensed biological-product relationships (S04). Proposed behavior: resolve each status from the appropriate official source, preserving the specific subject/jurisdiction rather than assigning a company-wide approved flag.

### C. Surgical robotics: exposure is not one revenue bucket

FY2025 Intuitive Surgical reported, in USD millions, instruments/accessories 6,018.9, systems 2,473.7 and service 1,572.1, totaling 10,064.7 (S13). Derived mix is approximately 59.80%, 24.58% and 15.62%. Product revenue 8,492.6 is a subtotal, not a fourth additive category. Its separate recurring-revenue presentation includes 874.3 of operating leases, reaching 8,465.3, approximately 84.11% of total. Recurring status overlaps product categories. Proposed behavior: show multiple labeled, internally consistent views; never sum them into a larger market size or count Robotics/Healthcare memberships as independent company revenues. These are FY2025 facts, not a latest-quarter performance claim.

### D. Shared drug economics can change without a new trial

Regeneron's June 2026 filing reports Q2 Sanofi-collaboration share of profits of USD 2,032.6 million plus 141.7 manufacturing reimbursement, totaling 2,174.3 collaboration revenue. The profit bridge is 2,285.6 less 253.0 development reimbursement. It states that the development balance was repaid by June 30 and would no longer reduce collaboration revenue from Q3 (S14). Proposed behavior: capture this dated economic-term change separately from trial events and end-product sales. The disclosed collaboration spans Dupixent and Kevzara; do not relabel its aggregate as Dupixent-only. The filing's forward statement does not prove realized Q3 results or a stock return.

### E. A historical manufacturing expansion is not current patient throughput

Novartis's January 5, 2024 release described a four-site radioligand network and 250,000-dose capacity for 2024 and beyond (S15). It does not establish capacity per plant, patients treated, doses actually released, or current September 2026 supply. Proposed behavior: retain the dated corporate claim and require new evidence for current qualified output, delivery and care-center throughput. A sector-wide shortage narrative should expire when its precise supporting evidence no longer supports it.

### F. Scarcity does not automatically confer pricing power

FDA's 2019 shortage analysis, revised in 2020, identified weak incentives for less-profitable products, insufficient reward for mature quality systems, and recovery difficulties (S07). Our inference: scarcity and shareholder value require different analyses. Research must identify who can economically relieve a constraint, at what contractual price and cost, with what replacement risk. Do not rank a generic manufacturer as an attractive bottleneck owner solely because a medicine is scarce.

### G. Coverage and claims are useful, but are not net company sales

CMS distinguishes coverage policies and supporting coding documents (S09). Part B spending excludes Medicare Advantage (S10). The annual Part D dataset is gross claims cost, omits manufacturer rebates/concessions, and its retrieved page identifies 2024 as the latest annual data; the quarterly series is preliminary and revisable (S11–S12). Proposed behavior: show population, period and data maturity, separate coding/coverage/payment/realized claims, and refuse to label those gross figures manufacturer net revenue. Publicly suppressed cells must remain suppressed, not be reconstructed.

### H. Safety report counts are not comparative incidence

FDA notes that MAUDE reports can be incomplete, unverified and affected by reporting behavior, with no sufficient utilization denominator for incidence estimation (S06). Proposed behavior: use these as safety-review inputs, not failure-rate rankings. A rise in reports is a question to investigate; it is not proof of a causal deterioration.

## 7. Bottleneck and value-capture research protocol

For each proposed constraint, answer five separate questions: what is constrained; how is that known; what would relieve it; who controls the relief; who retains the economics. A critical component can be unconstrained; a constrained component can be unprofitable; increased upstream capacity can leave downstream access unchanged.

| Constraint family | Discriminating evidence to seek | Falsifier or competing explanation | Economic question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clinical evidence | Exact endpoint/population, result maturity, comparative evidence | Apparent advantage disappears with comparable populations, safety, or follow-up | Which rights holder benefits within the supported indication? |
| Manufacturing/quality | Qualified product-specific output, release interruptions, validated expansions | Nameplate capacity is available but demand, distribution, or quality is the real issue | Does additional output generate incremental profit after ramp costs? |
| Inputs/logistics | Substitution, procurement or delivery evidence, product-specific timing | Shortage is local or caused by ordering behavior | Are prices contractually fixed; who bears waste or expedite costs? |
| Diagnostic/referral | Eligible/diagnosed/tested/treated pathway evidence | Low use reflects clinical preference or access rather than test capacity | Who is paid per test, referral, procedure or course? |
| Care delivery | Staffed capacity, training, scheduling, throughput | Equipment exists but staffing or reimbursement prevents use | Which provider or service vendor can monetize relief? |
| Payment/access | Specific coverage decision, policy version, payer/site scope, utilization | Authorization exists but no appropriate coverage, affordability or delivery | Who captures incremental demand versus pays additional claims? |
| Commercial/financing | Supported adoption, payer/customer concentration, runway/capex | Promising pipeline lacks funding or viable demand | Is benefit offset by dilution, licensing concessions or financing cost? |

No universal numerical bottleneck score is proposed in this pass. Present evidence dimensions and falsifiers first. Any later score requires a defined use, calibration data, point-in-time evaluation, and acceptance by existing research/evaluation owners. Research priority, forecast probability and trade authority remain separate.

To test transmission, pair a claimed catalyst with a measurable downstream consequence and alternative hypotheses. Examples: qualified capacity expansion followed by actual output; improved access followed by utilization; increased utilization followed by disclosed issuer economics. A missing link remains missing. Co-membership, narrative similarity or a stock-price move alone cannot prove causality.
