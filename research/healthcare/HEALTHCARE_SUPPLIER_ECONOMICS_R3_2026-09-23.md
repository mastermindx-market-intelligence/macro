# Healthcare R3 — supplier economics, ownership and cash conversion

Research date: 2026-09-23. Operation: `gmi-healthcare-deep-research-20260923-sol-001`. Carrier: Macro draft/HOLD PR #7788. Direct principal research; no foundational research delegated. Original synthesis for a later reviewed Healthcare design, not an accepted implementation specification, production dataset, investment recommendation or clinical recommendation.

## 1. The question this research resolves

The Healthcare equivalent of a Robotics bill of materials cannot be a list of suppliers with a scarcity label. The useful question is: **when a therapy or healthcare workflow grows, which supplier activity changes, under which contract, with what retained earnings and cash-flow consequences?** R3 compares contract manufacturing, recurring production supplies, instruments, cell-engineering technology, packaging, logistics and isotope services. These are selective, discriminating cases, not a census of all vendors or countries.

The proposed explanatory chain is:

`clinical/commercial demand → relevant manufacturing or care activity → qualified supplier involvement → revenue mechanism → contribution economics → reinvestment and financing → per-share expectations`

Each arrow is a research question. A validated relationship at one arrow must not silently validate the others. This extends R2's asset/indication/patient/territory/right/time unit; it does not replace it with a rival Healthcare graph. Existing GMI, BioCatalyst, identity, evidence and publication owners remain the intended implementation homes.

Three distinctions become particularly important. Operational capacity is not the same as profitable utilization. Revenue growth is not the same as recurring earnings or cash conversion. A business capability is not the same as an independently investable security. The evidence below contains both promising operating developments and counterexamples to simplistic bullish and bearish conclusions.

## 2. Seven economic archetypes for research, not seven exclusive baskets

| Archetype | What to establish | Economic question | Principal false inference to reject |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contract development/manufacturing | Actual product/process, clinical or commercial service, contract, qualified site, customer concentration | Fee, volume, reserved capacity, cost pass-through, ramp expense and maintenance/growth investment | A new building immediately creates high-margin revenue |
| Production consumables | Qualified recurring use, quantity per batch or treatment, purchasing channel and inventory | Consumption, price/mix, customer stocking, substitution and process intensity | Drug sales and supplier sales grow at the same rate |
| Instruments and production equipment | Installation, acceptance, installed base, service and replacement demand | Project conversion, backlog quality, deferred acceptance, utilization-linked pull-through | A large installed base proves current order growth |
| Platform license and technology participation | Specific permitted uses, milestones, royalties, shared obligations and limits | Recurring access fees versus uncertain downstream participation | A platform agreement conveys economics on every partner therapy |
| Containment and delivery | Relevant formulation and presentation, approved customer configuration, units per treatment | Premium mix, component attachment, customer contracts and factory ramp | Every GLP-1 product needs the same pen, cartridge or elastomer |
| Logistics and storage | Services actually sold, program stage, shipment/storage units, service quality | Commercial scale-up, recurring storage, network utilization and working capital | Number of supported trials equals commercial success or market share |
| Isotope and radiopharmaceutical services | Exact isotope/application, supplied material or service, qualification, buyer and timing | Deliverable supply, production and transport reliability, contracts and capital intensity | Isotope capabilities establish drug approval, monopoly or interchangeable capacity |

A company may participate in several archetypes. The proposed product should display the applicable economic mechanism per relationship rather than impose one company-wide label. The archetypes are original research organization; they do not constitute new canonical taxonomy IDs.

## 3. Peptide and complex-molecule manufacturing: stage, mix and cash matter

### Bachem: commercial production can precede earnings leverage

Bachem reported H1 revenue of CHF326.4 million and EBITDA of CHF82.8 million, versus CHF313.0 million and CHF91.0 million a year earlier. Building K's ramp affected costs. The company says K is producing commercial GMP products, while Sisslerfeld is a later capacity project. These statements establish different observed stages; they do not identify unnamed customers, a drug-specific supply share or utilization across the entire network. [R3-S01]

**Inference:** a useful expansion thesis must describe the interval between production beginning and fixed costs being absorbed. The question is not simply whether a facility exists, but whether qualified demand arrives at adequate price and yield. A margin decline during a ramp is not proof the project is structurally uneconomic; conversely, expected future scale does not erase current capital requirements. Test subsequent output, accepted orders, contribution, mix and cash against the original ramp assumptions.

### PolyPeptide: earnings acceleration and cash absorption coexist

PolyPeptide's H1 disclosures report revenue of EUR236.6 million, EBITDA of EUR49.1 million and operating cash flow of EUR25.2 million. The business review identifies EUR9.2 million from intangible-asset sales in EBITDA and greater receivables as a cash-flow headwind. Metabolic treatments represented 68.4% of sales, a disclosed company-specific exposure rather than an inferred industry percentage. [R3-S02, R3-S03]

The cash-flow table permits a useful reconciliation, in EUR thousands:

`25,219 operating cash − 43,718 cash purchases of PPE − 603 cash purchases of intangibles = −19,102`

This agrees with approximately EUR19.1 million negative free cash flow. The separately reported EUR41.318 million capital-expenditure measure is not the same input as these cash purchases. Substituting it produces a different result. Customer prepayments and collection timing need separate treatment; neither is automatically a new recurring profit stream. [R3-S02, R3-S04]

**Research consequence:** store accrual additions, cash investment, customer funding, receivables and one-off earnings effects independently. The hypothesis of profitable scale-up is strengthened by collections and durable utilization, not merely by a higher EBITDA multiple applied to a quarter containing unusual income. The opposite mistake is to reject a growing business solely because working capital absorbs cash before the customer pays.

### Samsung's proposed acquisition adds a financing layer

Samsung's August 31 prospectus announcement offers CHF44.31 cash per PolyPeptide share, subject to the transaction's conditions. Its August 28 financing announcement proposes KRW3 trillion of rights-offering proceeds, with approximately KRW2.71 trillion intended for the acquisition. These are announcement/prospectus evidence, not receipts of acquisition closing or financing proceeds. No closing proof was acquired in this wave. [R3-S05, R3-S06]

The proposed investment view must distinguish a target's operating prospects from its transaction-linked payoff, and the buyer's strategic gain from the financing burden and per-share consequences. Cash consideration does not mean the buyer funds the acquisition without new equity. An attractive operating asset does not, by itself, demonstrate accretive capital allocation.

### Lonza: qualified output, contract evidence and comparable perimeter

Lonza's H1 reporting is for continuing CDMO operations excluding CHI. Its announced Stein ADC filling expansion is expected in 2030 and is backed by a long-term agreement with an unnamed major pharmaceutical customer. The customer identity and product economics remain undisclosed in the acquired announcement. A current agreement is stronger evidence than a generic capability page, but it is not current production. [R3-S07]

The H1 presentation distinguishes commercial production beginning at Visp from a multiyear ramp. It also describes H1-weighted Vacaville sales and approximately flat expected full-year sales. Its operational-FCF bridge includes EBITDA, trade working capital, capital expenditure and an additional reconciling category; this is not automatically a standardized levered cash-flow measure. Printed slides 15 and 18 were visually checked. [R3-S08]

**Research consequence:** preserve the reporting perimeter, the stage of each site and the evidence supporting a named versus anonymous customer edge. Do not resolve an undisclosed customer by choosing the most prominent ADC developer. Do not merge future Stein capacity into a current qualified-capacity map. Capacity is specific to a product/process, not an interchangeable company-wide pool of liters or square meters.

## 4. Bioprocess supplies: growth rates need definitions and inventory context

### Sartorius and Sartorius Stedim Biotech: related exposures, different securities

Sartorius's H1 release separates recurring-business growth from a modest equipment recovery and distinguishes reported, currency-adjusted and operational growth. The Stedim release has its own reporting perimeter and growth figures. Neither should be silently substituted for the other. [R3-S09, R3-S10]

The current issuer investor-relations page describes Sartorius AG as the parent and reports approximately 71.5% of Stedim's share capital and around 83% of voting rights. It also distinguishes the parent's lab business. [R3-S11]

**Proposed treatment:** the company map can show both listed entities, but the economic-exposure view must identify the overlap. Share-capital ownership is not the same as voting control, consolidated revenue attribution or minority-adjusted equity value. Adding all parent and subsidiary revenues as independent supplier demand would double count activity. A verified security master, current share classes and historical ownership dates remain integration requirements.

### Danaher and Cytiva: business identity must survive brand changes

Danaher's Q2 Biotechnology revenue grew 4.0% reported and 2.5% on its core measure. The release separately explains changes in its disclosed growth metrics. Danaher's current Cytiva profile places Pall's Life Sciences business with Cytiva. [R3-S12, R3-S13]

The proposed ontology should retain operational brands as research entities but connect them to their current owning business and investable parent. Historical GE, Pall or Cytiva labels should not create independent contemporary stock exposures. Do not compare Danaher's Q2 core growth directly with a competitor's H1 constant-currency or adjusted-operational growth and call the result market-share change. A like-for-like product, region and customer-demand denominator would be required.

### Repligen: organic growth and margin improvement, but no invented backlog

Repligen's Q2 release reports approximately USD204 million of revenue, 12% reported and 13% organic growth, with adjusted operating margin of 16.7%. Its portfolio spans multiple bioprocessing activities, and guidance excludes pending acquisitions. A customer-level order book and supplier pricing-power series were not acquired. [R3-S14]

This is useful operating evidence, but it is not proof that every product is supply-constrained. The next discriminating questions are whether purchasing reflects durable production, restocking, new-platform qualification or an instrument/project cycle. Without those inputs, an estimated growth elasticity to biologic-drug sales should remain uncalibrated.

### Merck KGaA: strong production-related demand includes a timing component

Merck KGaA's Q2 announcement reports Process Solutions organic growth of 14.7%, supported by downstream processing and single-use demand, while also identifying temporarily higher Asia-Pacific customer purchasing. The company distinguishes Process, Discovery and Advanced Solutions. This is Merck KGaA, Darmstadt, Germany, not Merck & Co. in the United States. [R3-S26]

**Inference:** the acceleration is evidence to investigate, not a run rate to extrapolate unchanged. The product should expose an inventory/timing explanation alongside underlying demand. A shared company name is insufficient identity resolution; the risk is wrong corporate ownership, not just a cosmetic label error.

### Thermo Fisher and Avantor: breadth can obscure the mechanism

Thermo Fisher's Q2 results report approximately USD11.99 billion consolidated revenue and USD2.815 billion for Life Sciences Solutions. Segment reporting and eliminations make it inappropriate to treat the whole company as one bioproduction exposure. The disclosed bioprocess-design-center opening is evidence of a capability expansion, not customer-specific materiality. [R3-S29]

Avantor reports VWR Distribution & Services separately from Bioscience and Medtech. Its Q2 consolidated revenue growth was 0.5% reported versus a 0.4% organic decline, while VWR's organic growth was positive. [R3-S34]

The analytical implication is to separate distribution, owned products, services and capital equipment. Revenue scale can reflect channel throughput rather than proprietary economic capture. A broad supplier relationship does not reveal margin, exclusivity or pass-through treatment. Distributor and manufacturer sales also cannot be summed as if both represent distinct final end-market demand.

## 5. Cell engineering, preservation and logistics: platform success is uneven

### MaxCyte: downstream participation can grow while the core business contracts

MaxCyte's Q2 table separates core revenue of USD6.503 million from USD0.004 million in milestones and USD0.764 million in royalties, totaling USD7.271 million. Core revenue declined while royalties increased. The Genentech agreement grants access across selected ex-vivo workflows; financial terms are not disclosed in that announcement. [R3-S16, R3-S17]

A platform relationship therefore needs multiple economic edges: instrument or access revenue, consumables, milestone eligibility and downstream royalty participation where verified. Do not assign an undisclosed rate or assume every program under the counterparty earns the same economics. Ex-vivo access is not evidence of participation in all in-vivo cell therapy. A small growing royalty stream does not establish that the entire business has inflected.

### BioLife–Repligen: the transaction changes the exposure question before closing

The July joint announcement proposes USD11.25 cash plus 0.1442 Repligen shares for each BioLife share, with closing conditional and expected in Q4. The described CryoStor commercial footprint is an issuer operating claim, not an independently measured market share. No closing receipt was acquired. [R3-S15]

The consideration is not a permanently fixed dollar payoff: the share component varies with the buyer's stock price. This is a logical property of the stated formula, not a forecast of either stock. The proposed data model should preserve a pending ownership edge, the consideration terms, conditions, source date and an unresolved closing check. A target can remain a separate security while its investment thesis already contains transaction risk.

### Bio-Techne and Wilson Wolf: an ownership interest is not consolidated product sales

Bio-Techne's FY2026 filing reports a 19.9% equity-method interest in Wilson Wolf, maker of cell-culture devices including G-Rex. It describes a triggered forward-contract obligation to purchase the remaining interest by December 31, 2027, potentially earlier if specified milestones are met. The June 30 filing says the second milestones had not been met. [R3-S28]

This is more precise than describing a generic optional future acquisition. The remaining obligation, timing conditions, accounting and financing matter. The initial minority holding should not become 100% current ownership in the graph. Neither partner revenue nor equity-method income should be recast as Bio-Techne's consolidated G-Rex sales.

Bio-Techne itself is subject to an announced USD73-per-share cash acquisition by Merck KGaA, expected in late 2026 or early 2027 subject to conditions. Funding is described as cash and new debt. No closing proof was acquired. [R3-S27]

**Research consequence:** nested corporate actions require dated, distinct relations. A proposed acquirer does not immediately own the target's minority investee, and an eventual obligation to buy an investee does not make it fully owned today. The system must explain this chain rather than flatten it into one bullish cell-therapy label.

### Cryoport: commercial support, trial support and network scope differ

Cryoport's Q2 results distinguish services and products, report USD9.4 million of commercial cell/gene-therapy revenue, and describe support for 779 trials and 22 approved therapies at June 30. CRYOPDP's 2025 divestiture is excluded from continuing results. [R3-S18]

The trial count is a supported-workflow indicator, not an approval probability or equal-weight revenue opportunity. Commercial therapy count is not unique patients or a denominator for market share. A service network should be mapped using actual service and ownership scope; divested facilities must not remain in the current investable footprint merely because older material names them.

## 6. Containment, delivery and analytical workflows: the attachment depends on the product

### West: components, devices and contract services are different activities

West's Q2 table separates high-value components, delivery devices, standard components and West Vantage. It attributes some component strength to GLP-1 demand. A footnote describes an agreement involving SmartDose 3.5mL and AbbVie and modifies future organic-growth comparisons for the affected revenue. This wave did not acquire a closing receipt or drug-specific component volumes. [R3-S19]

**Proposed treatment:** preserve presentation, platform, current ownership and reporting-comparison adjustments. An autoinjector, wearable delivery system and vial component are not interchangeable. Removing revenue from a future organic-growth base is an accounting-comparison event, not proof every associated operating asset has already transferred.

### Stevanato: denominator and cash-definition problems are demonstrably solvable

Stevanato reports high-value solutions at EUR135.9 million: 45% of group revenue, but 51% of BDS revenue. Its Alina/cartridge example is tied to an unnamed customer's liraglutide therapy. The company also reports Balda C. Brewer sold on June 30. These details prevent denominator substitution, guessing a famous different molecule, and retaining a divested business in a current map. [R3-S20]

Its cash-flow reconciliation resolves an apparent discrepancy, in EUR millions:

`31.9 operating cash + 1.8 interest paid − 0.2 interest received − 57.6 PPE purchases + 0.2 PPE sale proceeds − 8.1 intangible purchases = −32.0`

A simple `31.9 − 65.7` yields −33.8, not the issuer's FCF. The table supplies the missing adjustments; no data correction or claim of an issuer arithmetic error is warranted. [R3-S20]

The broader requirement is a traceable metric-definition view. Preserve the issuer's measure, the normalized analyst measure, the reconciling items and the distinction between them. A common display label must never erase different treatment of interest, asset sales, acquisitions or leases.

### SCHOTT Pharma: comparable periods before comparable growth

SCHOTT Pharma's fiscal Q3 release covers a fiscal year beginning October 1. Its nine-month figures therefore cannot be compared as though they were a calendar company's nine months through September. The release distinguishes reported and constant-currency sales growth and high-value-product mix. [R3-S21]

The proposed comparison view must carry period start, period end, duration, currency basis and denominator. Fiscal labels are navigation, not standardized time identities. A calendar-Q2 peer and an April–June fiscal-Q3 company may actually cover similar months, while another fiscal-Q3 report ends in July.

### Agilent: manufacturing, analytics and research collaboration need separate edges

Agilent's March launch brings BIOVECTRA and Nucleic Acid Solutions into Agilent Advanced Therapeutics. The announced portfolio includes oligonucleotides and other manufacturing capabilities. Its separate MAM workflow combines instruments, software, consumables and standards for analytical quality-control work. A May NATi collaboration is a research agreement, not proof of commercial therapy supply. [R3-S31, R3-S32, R3-S33]

Agilent's fiscal Q3 ends July 31. Its release identifies a non-GAAP margin benefit from tariff refunds, which is a discrete financial effect rather than proof of structural product pricing power. [R3-S30]

The synthesis is to distinguish making therapeutic material from measuring it, enabling discovery and selling service support. New analytical software or instrument capability does not prove regulatory adoption, paid installed base or a particular drug customer's dependence. Analytical methods can become economically important without making their vendor a therapeutic-rights owner.

## 7. Radiopharmaceuticals: scarce-input narratives require product and contract evidence

### Telix and ITM: acquisition economics, not immediate ownership

The September 21 announcement proposes a Telix–ITM combination with approximately USD1.65 billion upfront consideration on a cash-free/debt-free basis, including components for seller shares, assumed net debt, and other stated items, plus up to USD700 million contingent consideration. The expected closing remains conditional. ITM's market-position and pro-forma performance descriptions are company claims, not independent supply/concentration studies. [R3-S22]

Do not add debt again if it is already included in the stated transaction bridge. Do not treat contingent consideration as certain, or pro-forma revenue/income as the same thing as historical consolidated sales. ITM belongs on an operating dependency map without inventing a standalone public ticker. A pending deal can provide a future ownership path, not a completed present one.

### Eckert & Ziegler: rapid subtheme growth can be hidden in flat group sales

The H1 report states that Lu-177 revenue more than tripled and CDMO revenue nearly doubled, while group sales remained close to the prior year. It also identifies a prior-year licensing contribution absent in H1 2026 and weaker engineering activity. The report's relevant financial pages were visually checked. [R3-S23]

**Inference:** consolidated growth can hide an important subtheme acceleration; the converse is that a rapidly growing subtheme need not yet dominate corporate earnings. This is precisely why the future system needs measured business exposure rather than a binary sector assignment. Neither the rate of increase nor the vendor's presence establishes a monopoly, pricing power or current drug-market share.

### Named counterparties give stronger but still bounded evidence

A 2025 Eckert & Ziegler announcement names AtomVie in a Lu-177 supply agreement. A May 2026 agreement with Thor Medical explicitly begins with preclinical Pb-212 supply. These support distinct counterparties and development-stage edges, not a blanket claim of commercial therapeutic supply or interchangeable isotope capacity. Current unit volumes, economics and amendments remain unacquired. [R3-S24, R3-S25]

The proposed evidence view should distinguish supplied isotope/material, manufacturing services, drug authorization, release/delivery, and treatment. An additional supplier announcement can weaken an unqualified exclusivity narrative without proving that an alternative is qualified for a particular commercial product. No technical isotope-production instructions are needed for this investment-intelligence requirement.

### A named pharmaceutical partner can still be non-investable directly

The indexed Business Wire announcement from Resilience names Lilly and KwikPen in an expanded manufacturing relationship. The complete source page did not open, so only the limited issuer-indexed facts are retained. It does not establish an accessible public Resilience security, unit economics or present output of the expansion. [R3-S35]

The system should allow a useful private-company dependency without converting it into a phantom stock basket. Missing supplier financial disclosure is a real limit, not permission to substitute another listed company with a similar capability.

## 8. The proposed supplier-to-cash-flow model

This section is original modeling guidance, not a calibrated forecast or observed investment return.

### Separate operating activity from purchases

For a recurring production supply, a useful starting identity is:

`qualified production activity × input consumed per activity × supplier attachment × realized price`

Recognized supplier revenue can differ because orders, shipments, customer stocking, qualification batches, returns, recognition timing, currency and contract terms intervene. Drug revenue also contains its own pricing, reimbursement and channel effects. Therefore, the ratio of supplier growth to drug-sales growth is not automatically a causal elasticity.

The attachment variable is particularly important. Broad capability means the vendor could participate; a contract means a specified relationship exists; actual purchases mean economic activity occurred. A company can support competitors yet have concentrated revenue. A sole publicly named supplier may not be the sole actual supplier. Missing information must remain missing.

### Model economic capture rather than label quality

For each relation, first establish the unit earning revenue: a batch, released material quantity, component, shipment, storage period, service acceptance, access fee, sales royalty or other contractual basis. Then identify the variable costs, reserved capacity, utilization, quality costs, corporate allocation and cash investment necessary to earn it.

A gross-margin percentage alone is not enough to distinguish a defensible franchise from an temporarily favorable mix. Nor does a high research intensity prove a durable moat. Proposed moat evidence includes validated switching difficulty, exclusive rights, demonstrable process performance, customer retention, multi-program repeat use, integration into regulated workflows, installed-base pull-through and competitive alternatives. Each requires source support and a falsifier, not an analyst adjective.

### Maintain a separate bridge for cash and financing

The analyst should choose a definition before calculating free cash flow, then reconcile to reported measures. Keep cash investment and accrual capital additions separate; distinguish growth investment from maintenance only when defensibly disclosed. A project funded by a customer prepayment still carries delivery obligations. A rights issue improves resources but changes share count and may change the relevant per-share baseline. A future acquisition commitment belongs in the funding assessment before it becomes consolidated revenue.

Avoid making every adjustment favorable. Label one-offs symmetrically: gains, losses, refunds, restructuring charges, acquisition effects and asset-sale items. The recurring cost of running a growing platform should not disappear merely because a company labels a measure adjusted. The research product should show management's bridge and the analyst's alternative rather than silently choosing one.

### Connect to valuation through explicit revisions, not a sentiment score

A supplier thesis can re-rate through several different channels: a higher expected demand path; larger retained margin; improved utilization; better cash conversion; longer durable earnings; lower concentration or financing risk; or changed discount-rate assumptions. These can offset each other. Revenue growth accompanied by heavier capital intensity need not increase value per share at the same rate. Equally, lower growth with reduced capital needs may strengthen cash generation.

The future scenario view should record the previous dated assumption, new observation, revised assumption, causal explanation, uncertainty range, falsifier and affected horizon. It should separately identify what the market or consensus already expected. Current consensus histories, valuation snapshots and point-in-time forecast skill were not acquired in R3. No underpricing, target price, security ranking or transaction-arbitrage recommendation is established.

## 9. Six scenarios to test before calling anyone a beneficiary

**Oral substitution.** Ask which molecule and formulation changes, whether adoption is new-to-class or replacement, and which physical presentation is avoided. Do not remove every injector dependency from the Healthcare map or assume all oral products share one manufacturing process. The result should be a conditional change in specific relationships, with patient and economics uncertainty visible.

**Manufacturing insourcing.** Test whether a drug company moves a named outsourced activity internally. A CDMO may lose a future fee stream while demand for some qualified supplies remains. Do not claim that either effect exists without evidence of the relevant products, contracts and vendor involvement. An announced factory alone is insufficient.

**Capacity relief.** Lower lead times and more qualified supply can increase delivered treatments while reducing scarcity premiums. Separate volume, utilization, price and reinvestment. A supplier can grow revenue yet earn a lower return on its incremental capital; this is a scenario to model, not a finding about every company in R3.

**Process intensification or workflow simplification.** More output per batch can change the number and type of consumables used per treatment. A higher-throughputput platform may create adoption but cannibalize older products. Without a documented change in consumption and attachment, do not assume a one-for-one increase in supplier sales.

**Cell-therapy workflow change.** Distinguish ex-vivo equipment, patient-specific processing, centralized or distributed activities and logistics. Early clinical results for another approach do not prove commercial displacement of existing suppliers. Conversely, a broad label such as cell therapy does not protect every legacy revenue stream from substitution.

**Transaction and financing change.** Test the target's standalone scenario, pending consideration, conditions and the buyer's financing separately. Model current minority holdings and future purchase obligations without inventing completion. A map should explain conditional exposure, not erase the operating entity or prematurely consolidate its revenue.

For each scenario, the useful output is the strongest evidence for transmission, the strongest opposing evidence, missing discriminating observations and the next dated test. This is research-priority work, not portfolio sizing.

## 10. Data and product requirements emerging from R3

### Minimum research record

The proposed supplier record should contain company/business identity; exact capability and economic archetype; product/process and clinical/commercial stage; named or anonymous counterparty status; evidence kind; contract basis; geography/site where relevant; period and observation dates; current ownership relation; materiality with explicit unknown reason; confidence limitations; competing alternatives; falsifier; and next observation.

Metrics additionally need unit, currency, period start/end, measure definition, accounting perimeter, reported/organic/constant-currency basis, stock-versus-flow classification, original value, correction/version history and any analyst normalization. Do not use a single freshness timestamp to conceal an old contract or an undated corporate-profile page.

Corporate-action records need announcement, conditions, actual effective date when proved, current versus proposed owner, consideration formula, and separate capital commitments. A source saying expected to close does not supply an effective date. These are proposed fields for existing owners, not a new corporate-actions service.

### Evidence classes without a false universal score

A useful UI can distinguish a marketing capability, named collaboration, qualified activity, recognized revenue and measured materiality. Those are evidence descriptions, not a global numerical confidence ladder. A specific contract may be old or commercially small; a recent revenue disclosure may be broad and not customer-specific. Show both what is established and what remains uncertain.

Company operating claims should remain attributed. Readable public pages do not establish complete lawful bulk-ingestion rights, historical version availability or a refresh service-level agreement. Neither current web searches nor research JSON constitute production point-in-time reconstruction.

### User workflow inside the shared template

The user should be able to start at a Healthcare subtheme, inspect the activity that grows, see participating companies and business units, trace the contractual and financial mechanism, and compare the assumptions against negative evidence. The page should preserve context across theme, company and asset views rather than launching unrelated dashboards.

Four proposed panels would serve that workflow: an economic-exposure map with current/proposed ownership; a site/process qualification and utilization view; a normalized operating/cash-flow comparison with definitions; and a dated thesis-change explanation with falsifiers. The separate template owner should decide the reusable presentation architecture. This research does not authorize edits to that owner's components.

Required degraded states include undisclosed customer, anonymous contract, unverified closing, absent materiality, different fiscal periods, changed reporting perimeter, source unavailable, conflicting source snapshots and insufficient comparable evidence. The UI should remain useful while abstaining from unsupported rankings.

### Candidate vertical slices, not an accepted implementation plan

The first slice should let an analyst trace one measured supplier exposure through its sources and cash-flow bridge, including one negative case and one missing-data state. A second could add current versus proposed ownership and prevent parent/subsidiary double counting. A third could add a single scenario with explicit assumptions and fiscal/metric comparison checks. Each would need backend consumer, shared-template projection, tests and real-path browser proof before acceptance. The exact paths and shared owner compatibility must be refreshed at design time.

This sequencing is research advice for the later design review. It is not permission to implement, merge or create a parallel ingestion/identity/evidence system.

## 11. What has not been established

No sector-wide bottleneck winner, supplier market-share census, company-wide calibrated materiality, accepted basket membership, production source acquisition, historical forecast skill or valuation advantage is established. Selected strong contracts coexist with anonymous customers and undisclosed economics. Future capacity and pending acquisitions remain conditional. The dataset is a selective research sample and must not be labeled complete Healthcare coverage.

This wave does not replace deeper coverage of medical devices and procedures, providers/payers/distribution, remaining therapeutic families, cross-sector and animal-health opportunities, or lawful source feasibility/cost/refresh. It adds a supplier-economic and normalization foundation that those waves should reuse. The next domain corpus should investigate devices and procedure economics, then the treatment-to-payment/provider bridge; final Fable handoff remains held until the research and reviewed design are mature.
