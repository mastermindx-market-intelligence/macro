# Healthcare R5 — payment, risk transfer and retained economics

Research date: 2026-09-23. Operation: `gmi-healthcare-deep-research-20260923-sol-001`. Carrier: Macro draft/HOLD PR #7788. Principal-led original research for the existing GMI Healthcare program. This is not an approved production schema, exhaustive security universe, clinical recommendation, current valuation or trade instruction. Fable's final implementation-orchestration handoff remains deferred.

## 1. The investment question, not another sector directory

R1 established the structural foundation, R2 examined therapeutic economic rights, R3 examined supplier economics, and R4 linked devices to actual use. R5 asks **who receives the payment, who bears the cost risk, and who retains the improvement when healthcare activity changes**. Its cases are predominantly United States businesses and payment arrangements. They must not be generalized into a global payer model.

The principal result is an economic transmission framework and a set of falsifiable research opportunities. A rise in medical activity is not a uniform sector tailwind. An administrative service contract, an insured product, a provider paid per service, a capitated provider and a specialty distributor can respond differently. Even inside one group, the effects can offset. The proposed product should explain those connections before offering a theme-level synthesis.

The minimum proposed research unit extends R2/R4 rather than creating a parallel registry:

`business or asset + patient/product cohort + payment/contract type + care setting + economic right/risk + service period + information date`

Company names are navigation, not proof of a current security or approved basket membership. Public-company look-through, contract rights, consolidation and materiality remain separate joins through the existing owners. A model's account of an economic mechanism is a hypothesis until the decisive input and counterevidence are inspected.

Three user jobs drive the work. First, explain why apparently similar companies react differently to the same operating development. Second, identify which subtheme and part of a business actually changed, rather than extrapolate a consolidated headline. Third, select the next observation that could change the thesis. The machine job is to maintain those relationships, clocks and uncertainties consistently, not to manufacture a single healthcare sentiment score.

## 2. Seven overlapping payment and risk archetypes

These are original research categories for later mapping to existing taxonomy, not new canonical IDs or mutually exclusive baskets.

| Relationship | Proposed economic unit | Question that can change the thesis | Invalid shortcut |
|---|---|---|---|
| Insured health plan | Covered member-month, premium, incurred claims and retained contractual margin | Does the earned premium and benefit/cohort mix adequately cover current and developing costs? | More members necessarily means more profit. |
| Administrative services | Paid service/member-month, fee and delivery cost | Do retention and operating efficiency improve contribution without taking new clinical-cost risk? | All administered medical spending is company revenue. |
| Fee-for-service or case-paid provider | Comparable service/episode and net collection | Does payment and case mix outpace incremental staffing, supplies and capacity cost? | More billed charges means more collected profit. |
| Capitated or other risk-bearing care | Attributed covered time, contractual budget, covered costs and risk-sharing terms | Are care economics improving after settlement, risk adjustment, quality conditions and partner sharing? | A clinical-cost reduction belongs entirely to the provider. |
| Physician/platform enablement | Contracted services, retained care/platform margin and cash conversion | What is retained after payments to practices, suppliers and participating partners? | Practice collections equal consolidated revenue. |
| Pharmacy benefit/specialty services | Contract, dispensing/service activity, pricing basis and retained fees/margin | How do mix, contract terms, generics/biosimilars and service intensity change contribution? | Higher drug spending automatically improves every participant's margin. |
| Distribution and site-of-care supply | Product units/value, service scope, gross profit and working capital | Is growth coming from pass-through product value, service intensity, acquisitions or better economics? | Low gross-margin percentage proves poor incremental returns. |

A company can occupy multiple rows. Coverage, economic ownership and consolidation do not have to coincide. For example, a service provider may influence the care of people whose claims it does not insure. A listed group can contain both insurers and service businesses. Research must establish the contractual position instead of relying on a name such as technology, platform, care or insurance.

## 3. Insured economics: current experience, estimates and liquidity

### UnitedHealth — a favorable estimate revision is not a newly treated patient

The Q2 release reports $860 million of favorable prior-period development, mostly for 2026 service dates. UnitedHealthcare and Optum segment revenues overlap at consolidation. Operating cash also reflects payment timing. These are issuer observations, not independently established causal effects. [R5-S01]

**Research inference:** retain separate service-date and reporting-period fields. Prior-period does not necessarily mean a prior calendar year. Do not attribute all margin improvement to current utilization or treat a cash-timing benefit as a permanent increase in earnings. The discriminating follow-up is the comparable cohort's later claims development and earned-pricing relationship. Group-level analysis must eliminate internal transactions; adding business revenues can inflate both the apparent market and the economic exposure.

### CVS — identical-looking ratios can use different denominators

CVS defines its benefit ratio using premium revenue, while the operating-expense ratio uses total revenue. The quarter's comparison also reflects the absence of a prior-year premium-deficiency provision; its $1.2 billion favorable prior-years development disclosure covers six months. [R5-S02]

**Research implication:** the product should reconstruct a claimed bridge in common units before comparing margins. It must not subtract two percentages with different bases, put a first-half amount into Q2 alone, or call the absence of an old provision a new cash receipt. Portfolio exits and mix changes need a comparable-business view. This does not dismiss an improvement; it distinguishes what actually improved and what must recur to sustain the new expectation.

### Molina — cost reductions are subject to the contract

Molina's June filing presents equal premium-tax revenue and expense and discusses minimum medical-loss-ratio and cost-corridor provisions. It says much of favorable prior-year Medicaid reserve development was absorbed by those provisions. Parent liquidity and consolidated balances are also distinct. [R5-S03]

**Research implication:** a gross saving is not automatically retained profit. The amount available to owners may depend on reconciliation, refunds, quality gates and the agreement's allocation of risk. An observed fall in claims is not sufficient to size an earnings revision. The missing terms should be visible and the modeled retained benefit should remain unknown, not default to 100%. Tax pass-through must not be treated as member growth or pure service demand.

### Humana — seasonality is not an error in the guidance

Humana's Q2 release reports $7.61 adjusted EPS and $17.91 year-to-date while maintaining a full-year floor of $9.00. Its quarterly benefit ratio and full-year ratio guidance are different-period measures. [R5-S04]

**Research implication:** do not annualize a strong quarter or assume a full-year floor below year-to-date earnings is internally impossible. A useful research model asks about the remaining year's claims, benefit design and seasonal timing. The required observation is a source-backed full-year bridge, not a straight-line extrapolation. No conclusion about a current share valuation follows from the arithmetic.

### Elevance — a raised earnings outlook can coexist with a worse benefit ratio

Elevance's Q2 benefit expense ratio was 89.7%, up 80 basis points, while its adjusted EPS included about $0.80 of below-the-line benefits and guidance increased. [R5-S05]

**Research implication:** direction of reported earnings, underwriting experience and investment/financing effects must be independently inspectable. An improved headline need not mean that every operating input improved. The stronger thesis test asks which component accounts for the revision and whether that component repeats over the relevant horizon. Do not mechanically label the whole company as repaired or deteriorating from one metric.

### Centene — portfolio mix and accessible capital cannot be flattened

Centene reports different Q2 benefit ratios for Medicaid, Commercial and Medicare. Its $44.8 billion of cash, investments and restricted deposits is not the same measure as $715 million available for general corporate use. [R5-S06]

**Research implication:** the proposed underwriting-repair subtheme needs product-cohort records, not a blended ratio. A broad cash balance cannot simply be subtracted from enterprise value as surplus cash. Claims obligations, operating capital, subsidiaries and restrictions must be understood first. Likewise, a better aggregate ratio could reflect mix as well as within-product improvement. The economic thesis needs that decomposition before being promoted into a company comparison.

## 4. Services and care models: who carries the medical-cost risk?

### Cigna — pharmacy-benefit and specialty economics diverge

Cigna's Q2 Pharmacy Benefit Services revenue rose while adjusted pretax income fell; Specialty and Care Services income increased. The two businesses have different reported mixes. Its customer-relationship counts also include distinct service categories, not a guaranteed count of unique people. [R5-S07]

**Research implication:** map benefits to the relevant contract and activity. A biosimilar or specialty-volume thesis must identify whether the company earns distribution margin, dispensing income, service fees, another contractual payment, or some combination. Do not assume that product-price reductions are automatically negative for every intermediary or that every saving passes through equally. The counterevidence is a change in contract economics, customer retention, product mix or costs, not merely a slower top line.

### Evolent — a technology-enabled company can have risk-sensitive revenue

Evolent separates administrative-services-only arrangements from its Performance Suite and states that people may be counted across product categories. Q2 revenue increased while adjusted EBITDA decreased. An expected new contract launch was conditional and its quoted $300 million was annualized revenue, not Q2 revenue. [R5-S08]

**Research implication:** a software label is not enough to assign software-like economics. The model must preserve the agreement's risk, the reported expense-ratio denominator and the difference between launch, annualized opportunity and revenue actually earned. More demand for cost-management services can coexist with financial pressure on a business that bears part of the underlying medical risk. A future contract's protections need evidence; a phrase about improved terms does not disclose the retained profit or eliminate downside.

### Privia — retained platform economics and collections are different layers

Privia's reported adjusted EBITDA margin uses Care Margin, not revenue. Practice collections include non-owned groups. Its release separately presents provider expense, gross profit, Care Margin and a medical-claims roll-forward. [R5-S09]

**Research implication:** retain the company's terminology and then offer an explicitly labeled common-basis calculation. A platform's gross activity is not its retained revenue. An attributed patient is not automatically a fully capitated patient, and one contract's claims risk does not apply to every arrangement. Future research should reconcile the mix of fees, capitation and shared savings, while separating estimates and paid claims. The layer at which a percentage is calculated is essential to comparing business models fairly.

### agilon — influenced lives are not the revenue denominator

agilon's Q2 release separates end-period and average Medicare Advantage membership from ACO-model membership, and says its ACO-model partnered entities are not consolidated. Medical margin is also distinct from gross profit. [R5-S10]

**Research implication:** the revenue denominator must correspond to the consolidated contract, covered period and actual measure. A total platform count cannot be substituted for average consolidated member-months. A growth thesis needs cohort entry, retention, funding and subsequent medical experience, not just an enlarged footprint. A provider's operating influence, accounting perimeter and economic rights can differ without the disclosure being inconsistent.

### HCA — a current-quarter recognition can cover several service periods

HCA recognized $1.372 billion of revenue and $829 million of related operating expense for a Florida payment program spanning October 2024–June 2026. Of that, $980 million and $557 million respectively related to periods before 2026. [R5-S11]

**Research implication:** connect the service period, recognition decision, associated expense and settlement. A current-quarter headline does not turn the whole amount into current-quarter organic activity or an annual recurring run rate. The arithmetic identifies a $543 million net amount, of which $423 million relates to the pre-2026 portion; the residual must still not be described as a Q2-only run rate. The next research question is repeatable payment and case economics, with uninsured exposure and collection risk separately examined. This is financial analysis, not a judgment on the merits of the payment policy.

### DaVita — treatment contribution is not all-in profit

DaVita reports treatment volume, treatment days, revenue and patient-care cost per treatment, with additional administrative and depreciation expenses. It separately reports risk-based integrated kidney-care patients and annualized medical spend. [R5-S12]

**Research implication:** do not call revenue less patient-care cost net profit, or add annualized medical spend to consolidated revenue. The proposed recurring-care model needs comparable treatment days, site/cohort scope, payer mix and cost categories. A clinical workflow change may affect treatments, care coordination and payment contracts differently. Model each connection rather than assign the whole company a single utilization coefficient.

### Option Care — care-site change is a workflow thesis, not just a cheaper address

Option Care describes home and alternate-site infusion services in its Q2 release. That establishes an operating role, not proof that every therapy, patient or reimbursement arrangement can shift to that setting. [R5-S13]

**Research implication:** investigate clinical eligibility, staffing, preparation, delivery, monitoring, payer authorization and net economics together. The useful hypothesis is that a defined, safely deliverable cohort can receive effective care through a more attractive workflow. Counterevidence includes inadequate access, higher total episode cost, delivery failures, poor payment terms or insufficient staffing. A patient benefit must remain visible even when it does not directly become supplier cash flow.

## 5. Distribution and specialty platforms: separate the value passing through

### Cencora — acquisition and product mix can pull margins in different directions

Cencora's fiscal Q3 report attributes gross-margin mix pressure to GLP-1 products and identifies OneOncology as acquired in February 2026. Acquisition-related financing also affects interest expense. [R5-S14]

**Research implication:** track organic activity, operating perimeter, service mix and financing separately. An acquired specialty business may change the group's economics, but the acquisition price and funding are part of the shareholder analysis. Falling gross-margin percentage does not establish falling gross-profit dollars or poor returns on working capital. Neither does stronger gross profit prove better per-share cash generation. The company-specific reconciliation, not an industry slogan, must connect the two.

### McKesson — reporting scope determines which theme is visible

McKesson's fiscal Q1 2027 report, for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, separates North American Pharmaceutical from Oncology and Multispecialty activities and their adjusted operating profit. The latter's reported growth is not a second independent measure of all group pharmaceutical demand. [R5-S15]

**Research implication:** preserve fiscal period and reporting perimeter before trend comparisons. A reorganization, acquisition or change in disclosed segment can improve visibility without creating new underlying demand. The research should map operating businesses and services to the company while avoiding duplicate market totals. Historical segment labels are not safe substitutes for current reporting boundaries.

### Cardinal — adjusted results can still include a one-time benefit

Cardinal's fiscal Q4 report includes a $100 million one-time tariff-refund profit impact in Global Medical Products and Distribution; the segment reports $150 million profit. The item is included in the reported non-GAAP earnings presentation. [R5-S16]

**Research implication:** adjusted is not synonymous with recurring. Subtracting the identified item produces a specific ex-item comparison, not a complete normalized earnings model. Other timing, tax, cost and business-mix effects remain separate. The eventual system should show the reported value, the defined adjustment and the remaining uncertainty rather than overwrite the issuer's number or claim a definitive normalized margin.

## 6. Six falsifiable subtheme dossiers to develop within the shared template

These are principal research priorities, not security rankings, trading signals or finalized taxonomy. The company examples establish relevant questions; they do not prove favorable valuation.

### A. Underwriting repair and contract repricing

**User job:** distinguish an insurer whose comparable economics are improving from one whose aggregate result benefits primarily from portfolio exit, seasonal comparison or reserve development. Relevant evidence includes the payer cases above. [R5-S01–S06]

**Mechanism to test:** earned pricing, benefits and risk/cohort mix improve relative to developing medical costs. Potential value is more sustainable retained earnings and greater confidence in future estimates. The framework must separate the timing of rate changes from the period they are earned, and separate new cohorts from renewals. A nominal price increase can coexist with worse margins when covered risk or cost intensity changes.

**Falsifiers:** adverse subsequent development, weak renewal retention, unfavorable cohort mix, premium growth below comparable costs, contract sharing that returns apparent savings, or an improvement caused mostly by denominator/perimeter changes. **Next observation:** a comparable service-period bridge with subsequent run-out, not another management adjective about discipline. Unknown contract-level data blocks a quantified company benefit, not qualitative research.

### B. Specialty medication and physician-service intensity

**User job:** identify where a more complex treatment ecosystem increases retained service economics, not merely the dollar value of drugs moving through it. Cigna, Cencora, McKesson and Option Care provide different roles to investigate. [R5-S07, R5-S13–S15]

**Mechanism to test:** verified activity requires services, dispensing, coordination or infrastructure for which a business retains compensation. Product mix, customer terms and working capital may dominate a headline sales figure. Scale is valuable only if it produces attractive retained contribution after funding and operating requirements.

**Falsifiers:** fee compression, loss of customer scope, supplier or customer insourcing, greater funding needs, unremunerated complexity, service failures or acquisition costs that consume the apparent benefit. **Next observation:** contribution/cash growth per comparable activity, with actual agreement evidence where available. Do not assign speculative supplier or specialty-drug affiliations from a generic capability page.

### C. Care-site and episode redesign

**User job:** trace how a clinically appropriate shift changes the economics of a patient episode across provider, payer and suppliers. It extends R4's procedure analysis rather than repeats it.

**Mechanism to test:** a defined cohort can receive care through a workflow with better quality, access or total resource use, and the contract determines who receives economic benefit. This could include alternate-site infusion or other bounded care shifts, but requires evidence for the particular service. [R5-S11–S13]

**Falsifiers:** unsuitable patients, capacity or workforce constraints, offsetting downstream utilization, greater total episode cost, insufficient payment or clinical evidence, or substitution that does not produce incremental activity. **Next observation:** comparable episode outcomes and retained economics, not a claim that lower facility cost guarantees lower patient payment. Preserve patient outcomes as a first-class result even when financial gains accrue elsewhere.

### D. Risk-bearing care enablement versus administrative services

**User job:** distinguish a company selling tools or administration from one accepting material clinical-cost risk through the same broad care platform. Evolent, Privia and agilon demonstrate why names and platform counts are insufficient. [R5-S08–S10]

**Mechanism to test:** improved care coordination, operating leverage or contractual terms translates into recurring retained economics. A platform can improve efficiency while its risk-bearing contracts deteriorate. Conversely, higher revenue per member can represent changed risk or covered services rather than higher pure software pricing.

**Falsifiers:** deteriorating medical experience, unfavorable attribution or settlement, more risk taken for insufficient payment, unsustainable guarantees, staff/service cost growth, partner churn or poor cash conversion. **Next observation:** contract-cohort contribution and reconciliation, then quality/access evidence. A savings benchmark is not a causal evaluation by itself.

### E. Cash realization, capital availability and financing

**User job:** determine whether reported earnings can become cash available for reinvestment or owners on the expected timetable. This is cross-cutting, not a rival sector classification.

**Mechanism to test:** claims/payments settle as expected, operating capital requirements remain manageable and funding supports growth without excessive dilution or risk. Group cash, subsidiary assets, restricted balances and current liquidity are not interchangeable. A temporary working-capital release may be useful but is not recurring unit profit. [R5-S01, R5-S03, R5-S06, R5-S09, R5-S14]

**Falsifiers:** slower collections, reserve revisions, cash trapped behind obligations, lower return on incremental investment, adverse refinancing or unpriced acquisition requirements. **Next observation:** source-backed cash and capital bridge. Do not assume all cash is surplus or all debt is an identical financing burden.

### F. Administrative productivity with verified economic capture

**User job:** separate a measurable productivity improvement from an AI narrative. R4's imaging/software distinction and R5's administrative-versus-risk-bearing contracts are complementary.

**Mechanism to test:** comparable work is completed accurately with lower total cost, and retained contribution improves after deployment, oversight and contract repricing. Case mix and quality must remain comparable. Higher platform counts or lower reported administrative expense alone do not establish causality. [R5-S08–S09]

**Falsifiers:** more errors or downstream rework, selective measurement, costs moved outside the reported category, savings ceded at renewal, or unmeasured quality/access harm. **Next observation:** a bounded before/after or suitable comparison with actual cost, outcome and contract data. Until then, use a research hypothesis and explicit unknowns, not an automation-derived earnings forecast.

## 7. Original economic models and falsifiers

The following models are analytical proposals. Numerical examples in the companion are expressly hypothetical unless marked issuer-reported. They do not describe actual reimbursement, clinical outcomes, investment returns or company valuation.

### 7.1 Insured covered-time model

For a consistent cohort and period, investigate earned premium and other appropriately allocated consideration, incurred medical costs, administrative costs and contractual settlements. Incurred costs require estimates and later development; cash paid is a different measure. Do not substitute ending membership for covered member-months. Investment income and tax can matter to earnings but are not proof of better claims economics.

The companion's toy insurer receives 500 of premium, incurs 450 of claims and 30 of administration, leaving 20 before other items. A 2% increase in claims costs 9, reducing the result to 11 even though the headline change is only 2%. A pure administrator with unchanged fee and delivery cost does not inherit that claims loss by assumption. Conversely, if a contract returns savings to another party, the retained benefit is conditional rather than the gross change in medical spend.

This sensitivity is an identity under the stated assumptions, not a prediction. The actual research problem is discovering the inputs, contractual allocation and expectation baseline.

### 7.2 Distribution model

Separate product value, units, retained compensation and working capital. In a toy fixed-fee case, 100 units priced at 100 each with a fee of 2 per unit produce 200 of fees. A product-price drop to 90 reduces product value by 10% without changing those fees. The fee-to-product-value ratio rises mechanically. In a percentage-fee contract the answer differs. Neither model can be assigned to a real company without evidence.

A real economic bridge should explain price, mix, unit activity, purchasing/customer terms, funding and costs; it should not treat gross reported revenue as a universal demand denominator.

### 7.3 Claims roll-forward and cash clocks

The accounting identity is beginning liability plus current and prior-period incurred estimates minus paid claims, adjusted for any disclosed additional movements, equals ending liability. The Privia example checks an issuer's displayed bridge; it does not validate actuarial adequacy or infer hidden reserves. [R5-S09]

Every major observation needs at least its applicable service period, recognition period and publication date. Add cash settlement, contract effective/renewal and revision times when relevant. The model should explicitly preserve overlaps: a Q2 adjustment may concern Q1 service, a first-half disclosure is not a Q2 amount, and a new catalog update can contain older observations.

### 7.4 Financing and per-share expectations

Model recurring operating economics before financing, then connect tax, investment requirements, restricted/available capital, debt, partner interests and shares. A toy constant 100 of earnings divided by 100 shares gives 1.00 EPS; reducing shares to 90 gives 1.11 before any financing/opportunity cost. That arithmetic does not create free value or establish that a real repurchase is attractive.

Similarly, at an explicitly illustrative 10% discount rate, cash of 100 in one year is worth about 90.91, versus 82.64 in two years. Timing can matter without a change in the nominal total. The rate is not a recommendation or a calibrated cost of capital. Actual valuation must use evidence and sensitivity, not the toy parameters.

### 7.5 Missing information is an analytical result

A lower medical cost with unknown risk-sharing terms does not warrant a precise retained-benefit figure. A contract's annualized opportunity before launch is not current revenue. A provider's improved throughput without measured paid demand does not justify incremental earnings. The user should see the unresolved link and the exact evidence needed to resolve it; the model should not silently substitute a industry-average margin or confident language.

### What implementation must prove before using these sources

Reuse existing company, provider and evidence owners. Map contractual entities to parent businesses and securities with effective dates, without equating names or treating an NPI/contract ID as a ticker. Preserve suppressions and unknowns rather than zero-fill them. Record gross/net basis, plan population, member-time, geographic scope and observation dates. A county/plan slice, regulatory reporting scope or submitted provider list is not the entire addressable market.

For historical tests, reconstruct what was available at the decision time. Revised claims or current ownership must not be joined backward without a dated basis. Archive permitted source snapshots through existing evidence/versioning owners; do not create another archive service or truth plane. A page update and a downloadable file are not proof that the historical version existed in its current form.

A commercial dataset may still be required for timely patient activity, contracts, consensus, net prices or historical ownership. R5 does not establish vendor price, entitlement, retention rights or completeness. Public documentation is not a license for unrelated commercial feeds or a promise that all necessary inputs are free.

## 9. Valuation and research-priority requirements

The target is a decision-support system, not an industry encyclopedia or a provenance browser. A high-quality Healthcare thesis should specify the changing economic input, the mechanism of transmission, the likely horizon, the opposing explanation and what investors previously expected. Provenance makes that explanation inspectable; it cannot replace it.

### 9.1 Keep three judgments separate

**Business evidence:** what changed in comparable activity, contractual economics and cash requirements? **Forecast revision:** which explicit assumption should change, by how much if supportable, and over which period? **Security valuation:** how does that revised expectation compare with a contemporaneous price and prior expectations? A strong result can fail to exceed an already-high expectation. Conversely, a difficult operating quarter can be less adverse than a prior scenario. Those are possibilities, not observed event-return conclusions in R5.

The model should present estimates as ranges only when the underlying inputs support ranges. Unknown risk-sharing should not become a numeric range chosen merely to look useful. An investment thesis may be decision-relevant qualitatively while remaining unsuitable for sizing, target prices or security ranking.

### 9.2 Proposed bridge to earnings and cash

For each material thesis, retain the prior assumption and its information date, new observation and source, comparable cohort/scope, changed volume/price/risk/cost/capital input, explicit calculation if any, retained-economic mapping, horizon and falsifier. Then connect consolidation, tax, financing and share count only where evidence permits. Show both issuer-defined and analyst-defined metrics rather than replace the source.

Do not assign a universal valuation multiple by a word such as platform, AI or specialty. Separate recurring service economics from claims risk, one-time settlements, pass-through revenue, acquired earnings and financing. Cash availability and liquidity are not interchangeable with theoretical asset value. Counterparties' overlapping revenues are not additive market size.

### 9.3 Research priority is not stock desirability

A high research priority can mean a material uncertain link, a new contradiction or a near-term information event. It need not mean the security is attractive. Suggested dimensions are decision consequence, uncertainty that can be resolved, evidence quality and cost of the next observation. This is a proposed research workflow, not a new production score or an authorization to trade.

A useful next-observation packet includes an exact question, the source capable of answering it, observation versus publication timing, a threshold defined before seeing the new result when appropriate, and what would change under each possible answer. It should allow an analyst to reject a thesis without losing the evidence that produced it.

## 10. Proposed shared-template user workflows

These are design inputs; no product component was changed and no final design approval is inferred.

**Workflow A — explain an earnings surprise.** From the Healthcare theme page, choose a business, its product/cohort and a reporting period. The result should separate comparable current activity, estimate revisions, contract/perimeter effects and cash timing. The visible output is a short causal explanation with its alternative interpretation and next observation. Evidence remains one step away, not the entire page.

**Workflow B — trace a cross-participant event.** Select a defined increase in treatment use, care-site shift or product-price change. Show participants by actual role: who receives revenue, bears claims or delivery cost, shares savings and finances inventory/capacity. Where terms are missing, the edge is conditional. Do not assume offsetting group effects cancel exactly, or sum the graph into market demand.

**Workflow C — compare business economics.** Normalize units and periods where justified and retain issuer metrics alongside explicit analyst bridges. Block misleading comparisons such as a care-margin denominator against total revenue, an ending patient count against member-months, annualized pipeline against booked sales, or restricted group assets against parent cash. A blocked comparison should explain the missing field, not simply disappear.

**Workflow D — maintain a subtheme thesis.** The user sees the mechanism, relevant businesses, latest support, strongest counterevidence, unresolved materiality and next discriminating observation. Historical versions remain available through existing owners. Research priority may change with new evidence, but no model summary originates or sizes a trade.

### Candidate acceptance situations for the later build

A user should be able to explain the UNH service-date distinction, the CVS denominator, the HCA retrospective period and the Cigna within-group divergence. They should be able to inspect a risk-bearing versus administrative contract without calling both pure software; distinguish Privia collections, revenue and retained economics; and understand why the September enrollment source is located but not yet ingested. The structured companion specifies additional negative cases. None has been executed against the application.
