## 1. Outcome and governing thesis

The user job is not to browse a flat list of Finance stocks. A serious investor should be able to begin with the Finance sector, follow how money, collateral, risk, information and legal responsibility move through a financial workflow, identify the institutions and infrastructure that control each step, understand how each company is paid and what risk it carries, measure the relevant macro and operating drivers, inspect bottlenecks and substitution paths, and trace every material statement to dated evidence.

The machine job is to preserve those distinctions over time without collapsing unlike businesses into a generic Financials label or creating another canonical graph, score, queue, publisher, identity master or evidence ledger.

The Finance equivalent of a robotics bill of materials is therefore a **financial-system flow-and-control map**:

```text
source of funds / risk
  → regulated or contractual intermediary
  → product / instrument / transaction workflow
  → execution or authorization
  → clearing / netting / risk transfer
  → settlement / custody / servicing / record
  → end owner, beneficiary or risk bearer
```

Every useful company record must explain at least:

1. the exact role played;
2. whether the firm acts as principal, agent, fiduciary, utility, data/trust provider or software supplier;
3. the revenue mechanism;
4. the risk actually retained;
5. the regulatory and funding perimeter;
6. the workflow and counterparties involved;
7. the measured exposure and denominator;
8. the observation, business-validity and source clocks;
9. the evidence and limitations;
10. the issuer/security binding, where validated.

## 3. Executive design rulings

### R-FIN-1 — Finance is a system overlay, not one canonical theme

Finance spans sectors, instruments, legal entities and market utilities. The product starts at the Financials sector/system level and connects canonical themes, source-local subthemes, baskets, companies and infrastructures. It does not force the entire financial system into a single theme node.

### R-FIN-2 — Three maps remain distinct

The product must expose selectable views rather than one deceptive graph:

1. **Contractual money/risk flow** — who owes, owns, guarantees, insures, services or bears risk.
2. **Regulated infrastructure/access** — which rails, venues, memberships, licenses, custodians and records make the workflow possible.
3. **Public-equity exposure/economics** — which business/issuer/security earns what revenue and retains what risk.

A similar label, shared customer or price correlation cannot create a contractual or causal edge.

### R-FIN-3 — Role is more important than sector label

A company may simultaneously be a balance-sheet principal, agency intermediary, fiduciary, utility, data vendor and software supplier in separate businesses. Each role is recorded at business/workflow grain. “Bank,” “fintech,” “exchange,” “insurer” or “asset manager” alone is insufficient.

### R-FIN-4 — Exposure requires a denominator

Revenue, volume, assets, loans, premiums, deposits, AUM, custody, transaction count, open interest, ratings, policies, members or users must carry a matching denominator, period, entity, geography, currency and consolidation basis. “Exposure” without those fields is a research lead, not a measured fact.

### R-FIN-5 — Stock, flow, gross, net and notional remain different

AUM, deposits, loans, reserves and backlog-like balances are stocks. Originations, payment value, premiums, transactions and net flows are flows. Gross assets, net assets, notional derivatives, net current exposure and revenue are not interchangeable. The UI and data contract must identify the measurement class.

### R-FIN-6 — No universal Finance bottleneck score

Finance bottlenecks may be regulatory permission, balance-sheet capital, deposits, liquidity, collateral, clearing membership, distribution, proprietary data, benchmark licensing, identity trust, integration or operational resilience. These are separate observations. No fused bottleneck number may conceal which constraint is binding.

### R-FIN-7 — Primary-source hierarchy is explicit

- Regulatory/statutory data anchor system state and prudential facts.
- Issuer filings anchor company segment economics and reported operating measures.
- Network, venue and infrastructure statistics anchor workflow activity.
- Contract/rule documents anchor legal roles and settlement mechanics.
- Secondary research and model extraction generate candidates; they do not self-ratify facts.

### R-FIN-8 — Regulatory status is not a quality endorsement

A license, registration, designation or membership proves a legal/system status at a date. It does not prove economic moat, solvency, performance, safety, product quality or investability.

### R-FIN-9 — Announced capability is not realized economics

A product launch, partnership, protocol support, network connection or future target is not automatically current volume, revenue, adoption, market share or earnings exposure. Statement mode and realization state must survive to the UI.

### R-FIN-10 — Membership, dependency and price expression remain distinct

A company can belong to a sector/basket, depend on a rail, and exhibit price sensitivity to a macro driver. Those are three different claims. Basket membership and co-movement cannot create `CLEARS_THROUGH`, `FUNDS`, `UNDERWRITES`, `CUSTODIES_AT` or another economic edge.

### R-FIN-11 — Research context has no trading authority

The Finance map may explain drivers, beneficiaries, losers, toll collectors, fragility and watch conditions. It may not originate, rank, size, gate or execute a trade. Existing selection, Prophet, portfolio and execution owners retain authority.

### R-FIN-12 — Existing owners remain canonical

GMI remains the identity/evidence/semantic spine. Specialist bank, credit, payments, sector, company, earnings, options and macro engines keep their facts. The Finance dossier is an owner-preserving read projection, not a replacement state machine.

### R-FIN-13 — Current full-fidelity research remains protected

The Robotics precedent generalizes: source-scoped detailed assertions use the incumbent accepted evidence and authenticated publication path. No full-fidelity current payload is embedded in public static HTML, public Git artifacts, localStorage, IndexedDB, service-worker caches or a new public mirror.

### R-FIN-14 — First vertical proves a real workflow

The first delivery must let a signed-in reader trace a real transaction/system workflow to company economics and exact evidence. A schema, empty graph or static taxonomy does not qualify.

### R-FIN-15 — Fable is the later integration principal, not the research substitute

Sol performs the difficult research, ontology and plan construction. Fable CEO is reserved for architecture adjudication, collision resolution, orchestration and acceptance after the blueprint is mature. Routine evidence population, engineering, tests and bounded research expansion should later route through the least-scarce capable avenues.

### 4.2 Economic modes

**Balance-sheet principal** — earns spread, retains assets/liabilities, capital and loss risk.  
**Agency intermediary** — earns commissions or transaction fees while arranging rather than owning the core exposure.  
**Fiduciary** — manages or safeguards client assets under a duty, commonly earning AUM/AUA-linked fees.  
**Utility / market infrastructure** — provides access, netting, settlement, record, custody or network coordination.  
**Information / trust provider** — sells data, benchmarks, ratings, identity, models or decision infrastructure.  
**Software / workflow provider** — supplies operating technology without automatically owning the financial exposure.

## 7. Nine foundational workflow maps

### 7.1 Card payments

```text
consumer / commercial payer
  → merchant / platform
  → gateway or payment orchestrator
  → acquirer / merchant processor
  → card network
  → issuer processor
  → issuing bank
  → clearing and settlement
```

Cross-cutting providers: tokenization, fraud scoring, identity, chargeback management, FX and data.

Do not collapse:

- network assessment with interchange;
- acquiring with issuing;
- authorization with settlement;
- payment volume with processor revenue;
- credential issuance with credit underwriting;
- merchant relationship with network ownership.

Key metrics: payment volume, transactions, cross-border volume, credentials/accounts, merchant locations, authorization rate, fraud and chargebacks, take rate/yield, net revenue, incentive/rebate expense and settlement exposure.

### 7.2 ACH, instant payments and wires

```text
originator
  → originating depository institution
  → payment operator / rail
  → receiving depository institution
  → beneficiary
```

Message, authorization, fraud control, clearing and final settlement remain separate stages. Fedwire, ACH, instant-payment and correspondent/cross-border rails have different finality, availability, economics, limits and participant requirements.

Key metrics: value, transaction count, average size, same-day/instant share, participating institutions, reach, exception/fraud rates, availability and settlement clock.

### 7.3 Public securities issuance and trading

```text
issuer / borrower
  → adviser / underwriter / ratings / legal
  → venue or ATS
  → broker / order router / dealer / market maker
  → clearing agency / CCP
  → CSD / settlement
  → custodian / transfer agent / beneficial owner record
  → market and regulatory reporting
```

Listing, execution, price discovery, clearing, novation, settlement, custody and record ownership are different functions. A vertically integrated operator receives multiple role records.

Key metrics: issuance, ADV, contracts/shares, open interest, rate per contract, clearing value, netting, margin/collateral, fails, assets under custody/administration, listings and market-data revenue.

### 7.4 Bank credit

```text
deposits / wholesale funding / capital
  → underwriting and origination
  → balance-sheet retention or warehouse
  → servicing and collections
  → loss recognition / reserves / capital consumption
  → optional distribution or securitization
```

Key metrics: deposits and beta, funding mix, loan commitments and balances, yield, spread, NIM/NII, underwriting standards/demand, delinquencies, nonaccruals, charge-offs, ACL/provision, risk-weighted assets, CET1, liquidity and concentration.

### 7.5 Mortgage finance

```text
borrower
  → broker / originator
  → underwriting, appraisal and title/data
  → warehouse funding
  → agency / aggregator / private sponsor
  → guarantee and securitization
  → MBS investor
  → primary servicer / subservicer
  → default, claims, foreclosure and loss allocation
```

Applications, locks, funded originations, securitization, unpaid principal balance, servicing rights, prepayment and delinquency are separate stocks/flows.

### 7.6 Asset management and ETF plumbing

```text
asset owner / wealth client
  → distributor / adviser / platform
  → asset manager / sponsor
  → index, benchmark, ratings, data and risk systems
  → broker / execution
  → custodian / fund administrator / transfer agent / recordkeeper
  → authorized participant / market maker / venue for ETF creation-redemption
```

AUM change must separate market appreciation, FX, acquisitions and external net flows. Fund assets, manager AUM, custodian AUC and administrator AUA are not interchangeable.

### 7.7 Insurance and reinsurance

```text
policyholder
  → agent / broker / MGA / distributor
  → primary carrier
  → reinsurer / retrocession / insurance-linked capital
  → claims, TPA and specialist vendor
  → investment portfolio and capital
```

Premium growth, rate, insured exposure, retention, loss trend, reserve development, catastrophe loss, reinsurance cost and investment income must remain distinct.

### 7.8 Private credit and private markets

```text
LP / pension / insurer / wealth capital
  → alternative manager / fund / BDC
  → sponsor, originator or direct borrower
  → warehouse, subscription or NAV facility
  → servicing, valuation and portfolio monitoring
  → refinancing, sale, repayment or restructuring
```

Fundraising, commitments, dry powder, invested capital, deployment, gross assets, net assets, leverage, spread, nonaccrual, PIK, realized loss and fee-related earnings are separate.

### 7.9 Stablecoin and tokenized settlement

```text
fiat or bank-deposit source
  → issuer and reserve arrangement
  → reserve custodian / manager
  → wallet / exchange / broker / payment provider
  → blockchain / validator / settlement layer
  → merchant, institution or off-ramp
```

Token issuer, reserve asset, custodian, wallet, exchange, validator and payment use are separate objects. Token supply or on-chain transfer does not automatically equal merchant settlement, revenue, reserve profit or regulated deposit substitution.

## 8. Bottleneck and moat intelligence

Finance bottlenecks should be modeled as typed constraints with a denominator and substitution path.

### 8.1 Constraint classes

1. **Regulatory permission** — charter, license, designation, registration or approved activity.
2. **Capital and balance-sheet capacity** — regulatory capital, risk limits, leverage, reserve or insurance capital.
3. **Funding and liquidity** — deposits, secured funding, wholesale access, collateral and liquidity buffers.
4. **Network membership and acceptance** — rail, venue, clearing, custody, correspondent, merchant or institutional reach.
5. **Settlement and record finality** — required depository, ledger, transfer, custody or legal record.
6. **Data and benchmark control** — proprietary observations, reference prices, ratings, indices and embedded workflow data.
7. **Distribution** — advisers, merchants, issuers, employers, platforms, agents and institutional relationships.
8. **Integration and switching cost** — core systems, certification, data history, compliance, operational change and retraining.
9. **Risk and identity trust** — underwriting, fraud, KYC/AML, claims, model validation and reputation.
10. **Operational resilience and cyber** — uptime, disaster recovery, security and regulatory continuity.
11. **Specialist talent and market trust** — underwriting, market making, claims, restructuring, advisory and risk expertise.

### 8.2 Required evidence for a bottleneck claim

- constrained activity and unit;
- affected workflow and geography;
- numerator and denominator;
- qualified participants or alternatives;
- capacity, utilization or access status where measurable;
- switching/substitution route and time/cost;
- price, take-rate, margin or service evidence;
- observation and effective dates;
- source type and coverage;
- what would ease or migrate the constraint.

Concentration can indicate a structural choke point, but does not alone prove durable pricing power or investment attractiveness.

| Credit-spread widening | reprices risk and reduces issuance while raising new-money yields | lenders, insurers, exchanges/data | mark losses, defaults, capital constraints |
| Higher unemployment | consumer/SMB credit stress, claims and lower activity | card/consumer lenders, insurers, payments | reserve coverage, underwriting vintage, mix |
| Equity/credit issuance recovery | underwriting, listings, trading, ratings and data demand | investment banks, exchanges, ratings, data | fee competition, mix and market share |
| Volatility increase | trading/hedging/clearing demand and margin needs | venues, market makers, brokers, CCPs | inventory loss, liquidity withdrawal, client deleveraging |
| Asset-price appreciation | raises AUM and wealth activity | managers, custodians, platforms | fee compression and net flows |
| Housing turnover/refi change | origination, servicing and prepayment economics | mortgage lenders/servicers, title/data | rates, home prices, credit and capacity |
| Regulation/capital change | alters permitted activity, capital usage and compliance demand | banks, brokers, insurers, regtech | implementation cost, substitution, delayed effect |
| Payment modernization | changes message richness, speed, treasury workflow and fraud surface | banks, processors, software, identity/fraud | cannibalization, pricing, adoption and interoperability |
| Tokenization/stablecoins | changes issuance, custody, collateral and settlement design | exchanges, custodians, networks, data/compliance | legal status, reserve economics, demand quality |

Every transmission statement remains a mechanism hypothesis until supported by owner observations and outcome evaluation.

## 12. Investable basket admission methodology

A research slice becomes a price basket only after satisfying all of the following:

1. **Mechanism:** explicit economic driver and workflow.
2. **Role:** principal, agent, fiduciary, utility, data/trust or software role.
3. **Exposure:** measured numerator/denominator or an explicit `not separately disclosed` state.
4. **Relationship:** direct, enabling, proxy or adjacency classification.
5. **Sensitivity:** expected direction and lag to the named driver, with uncertainty.
6. **Identity:** legal issuer, security/listing and ownership resolution.
7. **Eligibility:** geography, liquidity, history, currency/FX and data coverage.
8. **Membership time:** point-in-time history or an honest current-membership/non-PIT label.
9. **Weighting:** transparent rule; no hidden conviction score.
10. **Benchmark:** appropriate sector/market/driver comparison.
11. **Rebalance:** rule, review owner and correction process.
12. **Evidence:** source rights and dated receipt.

Recommended basket-role labels:

- `DIRECT_PURE_OR_HIGH_EXPOSURE`
- `DIRECT_DIVERSIFIED`
- `ENABLER_OR_TOLL_COLLECTOR`
- `SECOND_ORDER_BENEFICIARY`
- `PROXY_OR_ADJACENCY`
- `AT_RISK_OR_DISRUPTED`
- `HEDGE_OR_OFFSET`

Do not pad a sparse cohort with weak proxies. Do not add overlapping basket returns as independent exposure. Company membership remains many-to-many and time-bound.

## 14. Primary-source scale anchors from this tranche

These examples justify a system model; they do not imply attractiveness.

- The FDIC reported 4,278 insured institutions in Q1 2026, with industry NIM at 3.31%, domestic deposits up 2.1% quarter over quarter and loan growth of 1.6%; some CRE and consumer portfolios still showed elevated delinquency. This supports institution-level and portfolio-specific views rather than one bank-cycle label. `[F02]`
- The OCC reported that four banks held 79.1% of banking-industry derivatives notional in Q1 2026; interest-rate products were 68.5% of the total. Notional, net current exposure and revenue must remain separate. `[F03]`
- Fedwire reported about $4.83 trillion average daily value in Q2 2026, while Federal Reserve commercial ACH processed about $203.4 billion average daily value. Rails differ materially in workflow and economics. `[F04,F05]`
- Nacha reported 35.2 billion on-network ACH payments worth $93 trillion in 2025, including about 1.45 billion Same Day ACH payments worth $3.92 trillion. `[F06]`
- DTCC states that its subsidiaries processed $4.7 quadrillion of securities transactions in 2025 and its depository subsidiary provided custody/asset servicing for about $114 trillion. This is company-reported infrastructure scale, not a regulator-certified moat measure. `[F07]`
- SEC Form PF statistics reported 58,891 private funds, $29.6 trillion gross assets and $19.0 trillion net assets in 2025Q4; the reporting population excludes smaller/non-covered advisers, so it is not a complete private-markets census. `[F08]`
- SEC NRSRO statistics show 10 registered NRSROs and about 2.19 million outstanding ratings as of 31 December 2025. Registration and outstanding-rating counts do not by themselves prove pricing power or rating quality. `[F09]`

## 16. Recommended first vertical

Prove the reusable Finance model with **Financial Rails & Market Infrastructure**, using two facets:

1. **Money Movement** — card, ACH, instant, wire and cross-border roles.
2. **Securities Infrastructure** — venues, market makers, clearing, CSD/settlement, custody, data and reporting.

Why this vertical first:

- it connects the existing Fintech & Payments theme to the broader Finance sector without inventing a universal Finance theme;
- roles and workflow boundaries are explicit and testable;
- official and infrastructure-level operating statistics are unusually strong;
- it exposes structural toll collectors and dependencies while preserving legal/economic distinctions;
- it can show principal-versus-agent and transaction-versus-balance-sheet economics;
- it exercises the owner-preserving graph, evidence, clock, identity and protected-publication requirements before more ambiguous credit and insurance modeling.

Proposed user journey:

```text
state_of_themes.html
  → Financials sector/system dossier
  → Money Movement or Securities Infrastructure
  → workflow stage and company/business role
  → measured economics and constraints
  → exact source assertion
  → existing basket/stock/sector workflow
```

First-release acceptance means a signed-in reader can:

- distinguish issuer, acquirer, gateway, processor and network in a payment flow;
- distinguish venue, broker/dealer, clearing, settlement, custody and data in a securities flow;
- see who earns transaction, network, spread, custody, data or software revenue;
- see who retains credit, liquidity, inventory, counterparty or operational risk;
- inspect current dated operating measures and limitations;
- avoid mistaking announced support, membership or partnership for realized material economics;
- return to existing market and company pages without a second truth plane.
