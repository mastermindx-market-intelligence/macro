### 1.1 Interpretation law

- AUM growth is not organic growth.
- Ending AUM is not average fee-paying AUM.
- AUA is not AUM.
- ETF AUM is not index-provider linked assets and is not automatically high-fee AUM.
- Client assets are not advisory assets.
- Net new assets are not organic net new assets when recruiting/acquisitions are included.
- Written premium is not earned premium or underwriting profit.
- A low combined ratio is not reserve certainty.
- Premium growth is not value creation without rate adequacy.
- Adjusted operating income is issuer-defined and does not replace GAAP/statutory evidence.
- Broker revenue growth is not organic growth when acquisitions dominate.
- A data subscription is not underwriting exposure.
- Current market price and expectations remain separate.

## 3. BlackRock, Inc. / `BLK`

### 3.1 Roles and business grain

- fiduciary asset manager;
- ETF sponsor;
- active and index manager;
- private-markets manager;
- cash manager;
- portfolio/risk technology and data provider;
- retirement and outsourced-solution provider.

The legal issuer contains businesses with different fee rates, duration and capital needs. BlackRock cannot be represented by one AUM number.

### 3.2 Source-backed 2025 observations

BlackRock reported:

- total year-end AUM of approximately `$14.0415 trillion`;
- total net inflows of approximately `$698.3 billion`;
- long-term net inflows of `$567.5 billion`;
- cash-management net inflows of `$130.8 billion`;
- ETF AUM of approximately `$5.4677 trillion` and ETF net inflows of `$526.7 billion`;
- institutional index AUM of approximately `$3.6962 trillion` and net outflows of `$119.3 billion`;
- active AUM of approximately `$3.4327 trillion` and net inflows of `$136.1 billion`;
- market appreciation of approximately `$1.4836 trillion`, FX impact of `$220.5 billion`, acquisitions of `$121.0 billion`, and realizations of `$33.1 billion` reducing the bridge;
- approximately `$91 billion` of non-fee-paying, unfunded/uninvested private-market commitments outside AUM.

Institutional index represented a large portion of long-term AUM but a much smaller portion of base-fee revenue, while active, private and technology businesses carried different economics.

### 3.3 Earnings bridge

```text
average fee-paying AUM by product/mix
× effective base-fee rate
+ performance fees
+ securities lending
+ technology/subscription revenue
+ advisory/other
− compensation, distribution, integration and operating expense
= operating income and FCF
```

### 3.4 Valuation lens

- normalized P/E and FCF;
- recurring base-fee growth and margin;
- technology ACV/subscription growth;
- SOTP for traditional management, private markets, data/technology and balance-sheet investments.

### 3.5 Rerating conditions

- organic base-fee growth exceeds organic asset growth through favorable mix;
- ETF growth broadens beyond a few low-fee flagships;
- private-market commitments convert into fee-paying AUM;
- technology revenue and ACV compound independently of market levels;
- acquired businesses integrate and expand per-share FCF;
- operating leverage and capital return remain credible.

### 3.6 De-rating conditions/falsifiers

- market and acquisition effects dominate organic flows;
- lower-fee index/ETF mix dilutes effective fee growth;
- private-market realizations or fundraising weaken;
- integration cost, debt or compensation absorbs revenue growth;
- technology/data growth disappoints;
- valuation assumes durable double-digit fee growth without flow confirmation.

### 3.7 Candidate research slices

`ETF_INFRASTRUCTURE`, `INDEX_AND_CUSTOM_BETA`, `OUTSOURCED_CIO`, `TARGET_DATE_RETIREMENT`, `PRIVATE_MARKETS_CONVERGENCE`, `FINANCIAL_DATA_AND_RISK_TECH`.
