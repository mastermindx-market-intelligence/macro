## 3. Visa Inc. / V

### 3.1 Identity and roles

**Legal issuer:** Visa Inc.  
**Security:** Class A common stock, ticker `V`.  
**Economic modes:** network utility, transaction processor, money-movement platform, data/services provider. Visa is not the cardholder's deposit-taking issuing bank and does not receive issuer interchange merely because a credential carries the Visa brand.

**Workflow roles**

- payment credential/network rules;
- authorization, processing, clearing and settlement support through VisaNet;
- domestic and cross-border transaction routing;
- Visa Direct money movement across cards, accounts and wallets;
- advisory, issuer, acceptance, risk, identity and other value-added services.

### 3.2 Source-backed operating scale

Visa reported for fiscal 2025:

- approximately `$17 trillion` of total payments and cash volume;
- `329 billion` payments and cash transactions carrying the Visa brand, of which approximately `258 billion` were processed by Visa;
- nearly `5 billion` payment credentials;
- more than `175 million` merchant locations;
- nearly `14,500` financial-institution clients;
- Visa Direct processed more than `12.5 billion` transactions for more than `650` partners.

These populations have different denominators. Branded transactions include transactions processed by Visa and by other networks; Visa-processed transactions are a narrower population.

### 3.3 Reported revenue bridge

Fiscal 2025 reported drivers included:

- service revenue: primarily nominal payments volume and pricing;
- data-processing revenue: processed transactions and pricing;
- international-transaction revenue: cross-border volume, currency volatility and mix;
- other revenue: advisory and other value-added services, licensing and account-holder services;
- client incentives: long-term arrangements designed to grow volume, acceptance and use, recorded as an offset to gross revenue.

Reported fiscal-year growth included nominal payments volume of `7%`, processed transactions of `10%`, and nominal cross-border volume excluding intra-Europe transactions of `13%`. Visa reported `257.5 billion` processed transactions for the fiscal-year earnings presentation, reflecting the precise quarterly aggregation convention; its Form 10-K describes approximately `258 billion`.

### 3.4 Economic and valuation model

```text
payments volume / transactions / cross-border
× applicable service, processing and international economics
+ value-added services and other licensing
- client incentives
- personnel, technology, litigation and other operating expense
= operating income and free cash flow
```

**Primary anchor:** normalized EPS and free cash flow per share.  
**Secondary cross-checks:** net revenue growth by category, processed-transaction growth, cross-border mix, value-added-service growth, incentive ratio, operating margin, share count and capital return.

### 3.5 Upward rerating conditions

- durable nominal payment and transaction growth;
- cross-border activity growing faster than domestic activity;
- value-added services becoming a larger, durable contributor;
- money-movement expansion across additional endpoints and use cases;
- incentives growing more slowly than supported gross economics over a suitable period;
- operating leverage and cash conversion;
- regulatory and legal outcomes that preserve network economics;
- per-share growth after acquisitions, litigation and capital return.

### 3.6 De-rating conditions and falsifiers

- cross-border or consumer/commercial volume slows more than expected;
- incentives/rebates absorb a rising share of economics;
- regulation or litigation materially changes fees or network rules;
- account-to-account, real-time or stablecoin rails disintermediate profitable use cases faster than Visa monetizes them;
- value-added-services growth is acquisition-heavy or low margin;
- platform investment rises without durable revenue conversion;
- network scale is mistaken for issuer credit exposure or interchange revenue.

### 3.7 Research-slice candidates

- Card Network Toll Collectors;
- Cross-Border Payments;
- Money-Movement Networks;
- Payment Risk, Identity and Tokenization;
- Value-Added Payment Services.

These are research candidates, not admitted baskets.

## 4. Mastercard Incorporated / MA

### 4.1 Identity and roles

**Legal issuer:** Mastercard Incorporated.  
**Security:** Class A common stock, ticker `MA`.  
**Economic modes:** network utility, switch/processor, settlement guarantor for many transactions, data/security/services provider.

Mastercard's own disclosures distinguish:

- core payment-network economics;
- domestic and cross-border switched transactions;
- commercial and new payment flows;
- value-added services and solutions.

### 4.2 Source-backed operating scale and drivers

For 2025 Mastercard reported:

- gross dollar volume growth of `9%` on a local-currency basis;
- cross-border volume growth of `15%`;
- more than `175 billion` facilitated transactions;
- payment-network net revenue growth of `12%`;
- value-added services and solutions net revenue growth of `23%`, or `21%` currency neutral;
- `$20.522 billion` of payment-network rebates and incentives, up `16%`.

Mastercard reports that it switches a substantial majority of Mastercard and Maestro-branded transactions, including nearly all cross-border transactions, while guaranteeing settlement for many transactions. Network role, switching population and settlement exposure remain distinct.

### 4.3 Economic and valuation model

```text
domestic and cross-border GDV
+ switched transactions
→ network assessments / processing economics
+ security, authentication, insights, engagement and other VAS
- rebates and incentives
- operating and acquisition-related costs
= EPS and free cash flow
```

**Primary anchor:** normalized EPS and free cash flow per share.  
**Secondary cross-checks:** GDV, cross-border, switched transactions, payment-network revenue, VAS organic versus acquired growth, incentive ratio, margins and share count.

### 4.4 Upward rerating conditions

- cross-border and switched-transaction growth stays durable;
- VAS expands faster than the core network with high retention and margin;
- services deepen security/data integration and increase client wallet share;
- organic services growth remains strong after acquisition effects;
- rebates/incentives are supported by profitable network and services growth;
- network investment broadens flows without impairing margins;
- legal/regulatory risk remains contained.

### 4.5 De-rating conditions and falsifiers

- gross volume and transaction growth decelerate below expectations;
- incentives rise faster than net economics;
- VAS growth relies materially on acquisitions or low-margin services;
- domestic networks, instant payments or regulation reduce profitable routing/acceptance economics;
- settlement or operational failures raise risk and cost;
- multiple expansion assumes a permanence of cross-border growth that the data no longer support.

### 4.6 Research-slice candidates

Same core slices as Visa, with a separate VAS/security and new-payment-flows view. Visa and Mastercard overlap economically and must not be counted as independent confirmations merely because both rise.

**Security:** Class A common stock, ticker `CME`.  
**Economic modes:** derivatives venue, designated clearing organization/central counterparty, cash-market matching platform, market-data licensor, benchmark and collateral-service provider.

### 5.2 Source-backed 2025 scale

CME reported:

- total annual futures/options contract volume of approximately `7.060 billion`;
- clearing and transaction fees of `$5.281 billion`, up `6%`;

CME also reported substantial cash performance-bond and guaranty-fund balances and interest earnings/distributions. Gross interest earned on customer collateral must not be confused with retained operating profit because much is distributed back under applicable arrangements.

### 5.3 Economic bridge

```text
contract volume × effective rate per contract × product mix
+ cash-market matching fees
+ market-data subscriptions and licenses
+ access, communications, collateral and related services
- volume incentives and operating expense
± retained collateral / treasury economics
= operating income and free cash flow
```

**Primary anchor:** normalized EPS/FCF.  
**Secondary cross-checks:** ADV, open interest, RPC, product/geographic/client mix, options share, data subscriptions, market-data pricing, capital return and tail-risk expense.

### 5.4 Upward rerating conditions

- structural increases in hedging, participation and open interest across asset classes;
- new products add durable volume rather than cannibalizing existing contracts;
- international and client-type breadth improves;
- RPC remains resilient after mix and incentives;
- market-data growth adds recurrence;
- clearing and cross-margin efficiencies deepen customer dependence;
- capital return persists without weakening resilience;
- Treasury clearing or adjacent infrastructure creates attractive incremental economics after capital/cost.

### 5.5 De-rating conditions and falsifiers

- volatility-driven volume is annualized as structural growth;
- interest-rate product concentration or a few clearing firms create unrecognized dependence;
- incentives, mix or competition reduce RPC;
- regulation changes access, market-data pricing or clearing economics;
- product expansion raises capital/operational risk faster than revenue;
- collateral income is capitalized as permanent without net-retention analysis;
- an operational or clearing loss challenges the assumed utility-quality risk profile.

### 5.6 Research-slice candidates

- Derivatives Exchanges;
- Clearing and Collateral Infrastructure;
- Market Data Toll Roads;
- Treasury/Repo Market Modernization;
- Options and Futures Volume Growth.

```text

+ recurring fixed-income, exchange-data, connectivity and listings
```

## 11. State Street Corporation / STT

### 11.1 Identity and roles

**Legal issuer:** State Street Corporation.  
**Security:** common stock, ticker `STT`.  
**Lines of business:** Investment Servicing and Investment Management.

Roles include custody, accounting, fund administration, middle/back-office outsourcing, recordkeeping, foreign exchange, securities finance, software/processing and asset management.

### 11.2 Source-backed 2025 scale and revenue sensitivity

State Street reported:

- AUC/A of `$53.80 trillion` at December 31, 2025, up `16%`;
- AUM of `$5.67 trillion`, up `20%`, primarily from higher markets and net inflows;
- approximately `79%` of total revenue from fee sources;
- total fee revenue up `8%`;
- NII up `1%`, with average interest-earning assets up `11%` and NIM down `10 bps`;
- servicing-fee revenue of `$5.324 billion`, up `6%`;
- newly announced and onboarded 2025 servicing mandates of approximately `$2.12 trillion` each;
- approximately `$2.50 trillion` of servicing assets remaining to be installed at year-end;
- approximately `$2.1 billion` returned through dividends and buybacks.

State Street estimated, based on historical samples:

- approximately `65%` of servicing fees were variable with asset valuations;
- approximately `20%` were influenced by fund activity volumes;
- approximately `15%` tended not to vary with markets/values;
- pricing pressure reduced servicing fees by roughly `2%` annually on average over the five years through 2025.

AUC/A wins therefore cannot be converted mechanically into revenue. Mandate service breadth, asset type, installation timing, activity and pricing all matter.

### 11.3 Economic bridge

```text
AUC/A by asset and service mix × servicing fee economics
+ client transaction / FX / securities-finance / software economics
+ AUM × management fee rate
+ balance-sheet NII
- pricing concessions
- technology, servicing and personnel cost
= earnings, capital generation and per-share return
```

**Primary anchors:** normalized P/E and P/TBV.  
**Secondary cross-checks:** servicing-fee organic growth, mandate installations, AUM flow/market bridge, fee sensitivity, NII/NIM, expense productivity, ROTCE, dividends and buybacks.

### 11.4 Upward rerating conditions

- mandates install and generate attractive revenue per serviced asset/activity;
- net new business and activity outrun pricing pressure;
- Alpha/platform breadth increases service scope and retention;
- AUM growth includes organic net flows;
- NII is resilient after cash/deposit repricing;
- productivity offsets technology investment without operational weakness;
- capital return grows per-share book and earnings value.

### 11.5 De-rating conditions and falsifiers

- AUC/A growth is predominantly market beta;
- fee sensitivity and pricing pressure produce weak monetization;
- installed assets carry lower-than-assumed service breadth or fee yield;
- expense growth absorbs revenue;
- NIM or deposit/cash behavior deteriorates;
- investment-management flows or fee rates weaken;
- operational errors undermine the utility-quality thesis.

### 11.6 Research-slice candidates

Same custody/servicing slices as BNY, plus ETF/index asset management and outsourced operating-platform research.
