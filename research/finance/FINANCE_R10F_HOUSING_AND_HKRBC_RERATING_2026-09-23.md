## 1. Executive conclusions

### R10F-1 — Housing easing has both volume-support and bank-margin channels

On 17 May 2024, China:

- lowered nationwide minimum commercial mortgage down payments to 15% for first homes and 25% for second homes;
- removed the nationwide policy floor for first- and second-home commercial mortgage rates;
- allowed local differentiated floors;
- lowered housing provident-fund loan rates by 25bp effective 18 May;
- announced a RMB300bn relending facility intended to support purchases of completed but unsold homes for affordable housing, with an indicated RMB500bn potential bank-loan mobilization.

The bank transmission is not simply `HOUSING_EASING_POSITIVE`.

```text
lower down payment / lower mortgage price
→ potentially higher affordability and mortgage demand
→ possibly more transaction activity and lower property tail risk

but

lower loan pricing
→ lower new mortgage asset yield
→ NIM pressure unless deposit/funding cost falls enough

and

weaker collateral / property prices
→ mortgage, developer and collateral credit risk
→ provisions / capital / required return
```

The beneficial and adverse channels can operate simultaneously.

- individual mortgage-loan funding for developers' funding statistics fell materially.

Therefore:

> The policy can have a valid stabilization mechanism without producing immediate or complete property-market recovery.

### R10F-4 — China Merchants Bank shows the core shareholder-economics conflict

CMB's 2024 results are a useful business-level witness.

Reported/derived observations from issuer material and later sourced comparatives include:

- NIM fell from roughly 2.15% in 2023 to 1.98% in 2024;
- return on average equity fell from 16.22% to 14.49%;
- residential mortgage balance increased from RMB1,376.8bn at end-2023 to about RMB1,403.8bn at end-2024, roughly +2%;
- residential mortgage NPL ratio rose from 0.37% to 0.48%;
- mortgage special-mention ratio rose from 0.95% to 1.28%;
- weighted-average residential mortgage LTV was still conservative at 37.15% at end-2024;
- corporate real-estate NPL ratio improved from 5.26% to 4.94%.

This is exactly why a property-support policy needs several clocks.

```text
mortgage volume modestly up
+ corporate property stress somewhat better
but
mortgage credit indicators modestly worse
+ NIM lower
+ ROE lower
```

No one measure captures the shareholder effect.

The NIM decline cannot be attributed only to the May mortgage-floor change; broader LPR and policy-rate reductions, loan demand, deposit-cost changes and asset mix all matter.

### R10F-6 — Hong Kong RBC changes the measurement basis, not merely the capital ratio

Hong Kong's Risk-based Capital regime took effect for its quantitative pillar on 1 July 2024.

The regime changes solvency measurement from the prior rule basis toward:
- economic valuation of eligible capital resources;
- a prescribed capital amount reflecting risk;
- quantitative capital requirements;
- governance/risk-management requirements;
- disclosure requirements.

A pre-RBC solvency margin and an HKRBC solvency ratio are not the same denominator.

Therefore:

> A ratio falling mechanically when the regulatory basis changes is not evidence of deteriorating capital strength.

### R10F-7 — AIA Company proves the comparability problem directly

AIA Company Limited's 2024 financial statements state:

- HKRBC became part of the Hong Kong Insurance Ordinance effective 1 July 2024;
- 2024 local capital was reported under HKRBC;
- 2023 was reported under the prior HKIO basis.

The table reports:
- 2024 eligible capital: USD9.144bn;
- 2024 prescribed capital amount: USD3.011bn;
- 2024 HKRBC solvency ratio: 304%;
- 2023 available capital: USD8.924bn;
- 2023 regulatory minimum capital: USD1.952bn;
- 2023 old-basis solvency ratio: 457%.

The apparent 457% → 304% ratio decline is **not a 153 percentage-point deterioration in like-for-like solvency**.

The numerator definition, denominator definition and valuation framework changed.

This must be represented as:

```text
REGIME_BREAK
prior_metric ≠ new_metric
```

not a time-series percentage change.

### R10F-8 — Group capital, local capital and shareholder distributable value remain separate

AIA Group's shareholder capital framework is not identical to AIA Company Limited's local HKRBC ratio.

AIA's group reporting showed strong operating/per-share economics in 2024:
- VONB +18%;
- OPAT per share +12%;
- underlying free-surplus generation per share +10%;
- EV equity per share +9%;
- shareholder capital ratio 236% at year-end;
- additional dividends and buybacks.

These do not prove that HKRBC caused value creation.

The correct hierarchy is:

```text
local regulated insurer capital
→ local dividend/remittance capacity
→ group free surplus
→ group capital allocation
→ per-share distributions / reinvestment
→ valuation
```

Each arrow requires evidence.

### R10F-9 — Prudential is not a July-2024 newly treated control

Prudential Hong Kong received approval to early-adopt the Hong Kong RBC framework from 1 January 2022.

Therefore its listed parent, Prudential plc, is useful as:
- an example of an insurer already operating with HKRBC economics before industry-wide implementation;
- a comparator for the absence of a new July 2024 regime shock.

It is not an untreated insurer in a simple July-2024 event study.

This materially improves the control design.

### R10F-11 — AIA's operating trajectory argues against confusing regulatory transition with operating disruption

Before the July 2024 implementation, AIA reported:
- Q1 2024 VONB +31%;
- Hong Kong VONB +43%;
- enhanced group capital-management policy and additional buyback authorization.

For 1H24:
- VONB +25%;
- OPAT/share +10%;
- UFSG/share +10%;
- shareholder returns continued.

For FY24:
- VONB +18%;
- OPAT/share +12%;
- UFSG/share +10%.

The transition therefore occurred during strong operating growth and substantial capital return.

It is not valid to read the lower local post-HKRBC solvency percentage as business deterioration.

### R10F-12 — RBC's investable mechanism is through capital efficiency and product economics

The relevant questions are:

- Which assets and liabilities consume more or less prescribed capital?
- Which insurance products become more or less capital efficient?
- Does reinsurance become more valuable?
- Does ALM behavior change?
- Does product pricing or mix change?
- Does local surplus/remittance capacity change?
- Does the parent alter dividends, buybacks, debt or growth investment?
- Does greater disclosure reduce the required return?

The regime's value relevance comes through these channels, not from the regulatory label itself.

### 2.4 System falsifier

A durable housing-stabilization interpretation should eventually show some combination of:
- improving transaction volumes;
- less severe price declines;
- inventory absorption;
- mortgage demand;
- improving developer cash flows;
- lower developer/property credit losses.

If only mortgage rates fall while activity and collateral quality remain weak, the policy may support borrowers yet still compress bank asset yield without eliminating credit risk.

## 7. Evidence limitations

- The bank and developer cohorts are purposive research cohorts, not causal controls.
- Hong Kong price files are yfinance-derived auto-adjusted historical series; they are return proxies, not quote-price valuation inputs.
- One bank has a different +252 observed date because of the non-null observation schedule; the bank cohort +252 is the mean of each security's own 252nd post-anchor non-null observation.
- Later China property policy packages materially confound long-horizon returns.
- CMB NIM/ROE and mortgage-credit changes reflect the full 2024 macro/rate/credit environment, not the May policy alone.
- AIA Company's 2024 HKRBC ratio and 2023 old-regime ratio have different measurement bases.
- AIA Company Limited's local solvency table was text-extracted from the audited PDF; screenshot retrieval of the exact table page encountered a cache failure after a successful nearby PDF screenshot. Do not call it independently visually verified through the web screenshot path.
- Prudential's early adoption means it is not a July-2024 untreated control.
- China Life is a non-HKRBC listed-reference security, not a business-model-matched control.
- Historical analyst consensus remains unestablished.
- No causal outcome, canonical source admission, identity binding, basket or trading signal is created.

## 8. Material hypotheses and falsifiers

### H-CN-1 — Housing easing can stabilize risk while compressing loan pricing

Support:
- transaction/mortgage demand eventually improves;
- property-risk indicators improve;
- bank losses/revisions improve;
- but new mortgage yields decline.

Falsifier:
- no measurable housing-demand/risk response and no bank pricing effect.

### H-CN-2 — Developer equity should not be assumed to benefit durably from policy relief

Support:
- immediate positive announcement return but later reversal if cash flow/inventory/property prices remain weak.

Falsifier:
- sustained sales, cash flow and credit improvement accompanies the initial repricing.

### H-HKRBC-1 — The capital regime changes comparability before it changes economic value

Support:
- large numerical break in reported solvency ratio due changed basis while the insurer remains strongly capitalized and operating trends stay robust.

Falsifier:
- a valid like-for-like bridge shows the apparent ratio change represents equivalent economic capital deterioration.

### H-HKRBC-2 — Value impact must pass through product/capital/remittance economics

Support:
- observable changes in product mix, asset allocation, reinsurance, free surplus, local remittance or parent capital return.

Falsifier:
- no material change in those channels and no evidence that disclosure/capital framework changes required return.
