## 1. Executive conclusions

### R10E-1 — A legal close is not the first-information date

UBS completed the Credit Suisse acquisition on 12 June 2023, but the economically relevant information sequence began materially earlier:

```text
19 Mar 2023 rescue / merger agreement
→ 5 Jun expected 12 Jun closing communicated
→ 9 Jun Swiss loss-protection agreement signed
→ 12 Jun legal completion
→ client stabilization and retention
→ restructuring / cost saves / NCL runoff
→ legal-entity and platform migrations
→ normalized capital return
```

A return window beginning at the June close therefore measures a **post-announcement execution period**, not a clean acquisition-discovery shock.

### R10E-2 — Forced bank consolidation creates several valuation bridges at once

The UBS/Credit Suisse transaction affects:

1. client retention and deposit stabilization;
2. scale and cross-selling;
3. cost take-out;
4. inherited loss-making businesses;
5. non-core asset wind-down;
6. litigation and integration expense;
7. funding and liquidity;
8. regulatory capital and future capital rules;
9. per-share distributions;
10. the discount investors apply for execution uncertainty.

The valuation question is not "did AUM rise?" or "was the acquisition large?" It is:

> Did retained client economics + cost synergies + capital release exceed attrition + integration/legal loss + capital burden on a per-share basis, and did the market reduce the required-return discount as execution evidence accumulated?

### R10E-3 — Negative goodwill makes headline profit unusable as the primary rerating anchor

UBS reported 2Q23 PBT of about USD29.2bn, overwhelmingly reflecting roughly USD28.9bn of negative goodwill. Underlying PBT excluding negative goodwill, integration-related expense and acquisition cost was about USD1.1bn.

The Finance system must therefore keep:

```text
purchase accounting
reported profit
underlying operating profit
integration expense
PPA / pull-to-par effects
capital ratio
client-flow economics
```

as separate observations.

An acquisition accounting gain can inflate reported ROE or EPS without proving recurring earning power.

### R10E-4 — UBS's post-close evidence shows a risk-discount normalization mechanism, not a one-day deal catalyst

UBS's first post-close reporting showed the inherited Credit Suisse franchise remained economically weak while capital stayed strong. By FY2023 UBS reported:

- USD77bn of net new assets in Global Wealth Management since close;
- USD77bn of net new deposits across GWM and Personal & Corporate Banking since close;
- approximately USD4bn of exit-rate gross cost savings versus the combined 2022 base;
- NCL RWA reduction and leverage-denominator reduction;
- CET1 ratio of 14.5%;
- a proposed ordinary dividend of USD0.70/share, +27% YoY.

Later 2024 reporting showed further integration milestones, USD7.5bn cumulative gross cost savings versus the 2022 baseline and substantial NCL RWA runoff.

These data describe a plausible **execution-risk compression** path:

```text
franchise panic
→ legal close
→ client/deposit stabilization
→ cost and non-core execution
→ lower integration uncertainty
→ restored capital-return capacity
→ lower required-return discount
```

They do not establish that the June 12 event caused the subsequent stock return.

### R10E-6 — TSE's March 2023 request is an ongoing management process, not a one-day capital-return mandate

On 31 March 2023 the Tokyo Stock Exchange requested Prime and Standard companies to:

1. understand cost of capital and profitability;
2. analyse current market valuation;
3. formulate and disclose improvement policies/targets;
4. execute them;
5. engage investors and update progress.

The TSE explicitly framed this as sustainable value creation, not simply a request for buybacks or dividends.

The correct mechanism is:

```text
cost-of-capital awareness
→ board-level diagnosis
→ low-return asset / policy-share reduction
→ business portfolio change
→ improved ROE / RORA / per-share compounding
→ clearer growth expectations and lower information asymmetry
→ capital return when economically appropriate
→ valuation recognition
```

### R10E-7 — Disclosure participation is endogenous and cannot define the pre-event treatment group

By mid-2023 and into 2024, disclosure participation expanded substantially. TSE later published price-performance comparisons by disclosure status.

Those comparisons are useful **ex-post associations** but are not valid untreated-control designs because:

- management chooses whether and when to disclose;
- better-governed or already-improving firms may self-select;
- companies may disclose after stock performance or business conditions change;
- later disclosure status is future information relative to March 2023.

Therefore a "discloser versus non-discloser" return spread must not be used as a causal TSE-policy effect without a time-varying selection design.

### R10E-9 — SMFG provides a source-backed bridge from TSE's abstract request to actual financial-company economics

SMFG's FY2023/FY2024 disclosures explicitly connect corporate value to:

```text
ROE
+ expected growth
− cost of capital
→ PER
→ PBR
```

Issuer disclosures report:

- PBR recovering from 0.58 at FY2022 end to 0.86 at FY2023 end and later reaching around 1.0 in July 2024;
- FY2023 profit attributable to owners of parent of JPY962.9bn;
- FY2023 ROE under the TSE definition of 7.0% versus 6.5% a year earlier;
- JPY134bn of policy/equity holdings reduced during FY2023;
- a roughly 10% management CET1 target framework;
- progressive dividends and a JPY100bn share-buyback program announced in May 2024.

The issuer also identifies equity-holding reduction and capital-efficient portfolio allocation as mechanisms for improving ROE, while describing higher expected growth and lower capital cost as mechanisms for improving PER.

This is the Finance architecture in real company language:

```text
business profitability / RORA
→ capital allocation
→ ROE
→ expected growth and information asymmetry
→ cost of capital / PER
→ PBR
```

### R10E-10 — The policy-shareholding mechanism can improve ROE without automatically creating value

Selling strategic shareholdings can:

- reduce equity-price risk;
- shrink excess or low-return capital usage;
- realize gains;
- release capital for buybacks/dividends/growth;
- improve capital discipline.

But value creation depends on what happens next.

A sale followed by:
- low-return reinvestment;
- expensive acquisition;
- temporary earnings gains without higher sustainable return;
- or distributions that weaken needed capital

does not necessarily deserve a lasting higher multiple.

The product must therefore follow **capital recycling**, not merely the amount of stock sold.

## 4. Product implications

These two cases demonstrate two different Finance rerating archetypes.

### Forced integration / restructuring

User question:

> Is the acquirer reducing the inherited risk discount fast enough for retained client economics, cost savings and capital release to become visible in per-share returns?

Required path:

```text
deal/rescue
→ inherited balance sheet and franchise
→ client retention
→ cost/integration/non-core execution
→ normalized earnings
→ capital headroom
→ distributions
→ valuation / price
```

### Cost-of-capital governance

User question:

> Is management actually converting low-return capital into higher sustainable per-share returns, or merely publishing a plan?

Required path:

```text
cost-of-capital diagnosis
→ quantified plan
→ capital/business actions
→ ROE/per-share results
→ growth expectations / required return
→ valuation
→ price
```

The latter should not become a "shareholder friendly" badge.
