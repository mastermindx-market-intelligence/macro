## 1. Executive ruling

### R-FIN-GR-1 — Global Finance is a federation of local financial operating systems

The product must not translate a U.S. bank, insurer, exchange, asset manager or payment taxonomy into another country by changing the currency and ticker.

A local financial operating system includes:

```text
monetary regime
+ bank-funding structure
+ credit-allocation mechanism
+ accounting and provisioning
+ prudential capital
+ resolution and deposit-insurance design
+ payment and settlement rails
+ market-access and ownership rules
+ insurer capital and contract accounting
+ tax and shareholder-distribution rules
+ listing / security / currency identity
+ local investor base and price-discovery channels
```

Only after that system is established may Mastermind map a company or subtheme into the common Finance research grammar.

### R-FIN-GR-2 — The common analytical chain remains stable, but every input is local

The global research chain is:

```text
local system and policy regime
→ company business role and source-native metrics
→ revenue / spread / fee / loss / reserve / capital bridge
→ per-share economic anchor
→ local expectations and valuation convention
→ security-level price recognition
```

The chain is common. The definitions, clocks, denominators and valuation anchors are not.

### R-FIN-GR-3 — Cross-market comparison requires two outputs, not one normalized number

Every cross-market comparison must preserve:

1. **Source-native observation** — the local reported metric with exact framework and period.
2. **Comparison view** — a bounded normalized interpretation carrying the residual non-comparability.

A normalized label such as `CET1_RATIO`, `NPL_RATIO`, `SOLVENCY_RATIO`, `ROE`, `VALUE_OF_NEW_BUSINESS` or `PAYMENT_VOLUME` may group observations for navigation. It may not assert that the underlying calculations are economically identical.

### R-FIN-GR-4 — Regional rerating is driven by changes in the local contract with capital

The most important regional rerating mechanisms are not all the same:

- Europe: return on capital versus CRR3/CRD6 output-floor and capital-return capacity.
- United Kingdom: structural-hedge reinvestment, deposit competition, ring-fenced balance-sheet economics and capital distribution.
- Japan: normalization of yen rates, deposit pass-through, JGB duration/valuation, cross-shareholding release and capital efficiency.
- Mainland China: policy-directed credit, margin compression, asset-quality opacity, property/LGFV exposure, state ownership and dividend durability.
- Hong Kong: currency-board rates, HIBOR and liquidity, China-linked credit/wealth, Stock Connect and cross-border market activity.
- India: high nominal credit growth, deposit mobilization, local GAAP and prudential ratios, secured/unsecured mix, NBFC funding and digital payment scale.
- Singapore: regional wealth and transaction franchises, deposit/hedge economics, cross-border credit and high-return fee businesses.
- Australia: mortgage concentration, deposit competition, APRA capital, serviceability/LVR/DTI regulation and franked capital return.
- Korea: household leverage, bank-holding-company capital return, credit-cost normalization, K-IFRS 17/K-ICS insurer transitions and value-up policy.

### R-FIN-GR-5 — State ownership and policy role are contextual dimensions, not quality scores

A state-owned or policy-linked financial institution can have:

- lower funding cost or privileged distribution;
- policy-directed asset growth;
- constrained pricing or capital return;
- implicit support;
- opaque or non-economic allocation;
- a different required return.

Mastermind must model those channels separately. “State-backed” cannot be encoded as automatically safer, cheaper, higher quality or more investable.

### R-FIN-GR-6 — Currency and listing identity are first-class

The following remain distinct:

```text
legal issuer
operating subsidiary
home-market ordinary share
A share
H share
ADR / GDR / sponsored receipt
local currency
reporting currency
functional currency
price currency
dividend currency
hedged and unhedged return
```

A/H dual listings are two securities on different venues and investor-access channels. An ADR is not a second issuer. Currency translation is not organic growth. A local-currency rerating and a dollar investor’s total return may diverge materially.

### R-FIN-GR-7 — Regional research has no direct trade authority

Regional system context may explain operating economics, valuation conventions, risk and price conflicts. It may not rank or size securities, originate trades, override Prophet, or turn regulatory capital into an automatic investment conclusion.

## 3. Europe and the United Kingdom

### 3.1 System map

```text
ECB / national central banks / Bank of England
→ deposit-taking and capital-market banks
→ corporate / household / sovereign / market-finance assets
→ EU CRR3/CRD6 or UK PRA capital and liquidity
→ EBA / ECB / PRA supervisory disclosure
→ national and EU deposit-insurance / resolution structures
→ exchanges, clearing, custody, benchmarks and data
→ Solvency II / Solvency UK insurers and reinsurers
→ asset managers, pension pools and wealth distributors
```

### 3.2 Banking rerating clocks

1. **Policy and market rates** — policy-rate direction, deposit pass-through and wholesale funding.
2. **Structural hedge and reinvestment** — especially important for UK deposit franchises.
3. **Loan demand and mix** — household, mortgage, corporate, commercial real estate and cross-border.
4. **Asset quality** — NPL formation, Stage 2/Stage 3 migration, cost of risk and overlays.
5. **CRR3/CRD6 / PRA capital** — output-floor and RWA effects, management buffers and distribution.
6. **Fee and capital-market earnings** — wealth, transaction banking, advisory, markets and securities services.
7. **Currency and geographic mix** — group results can move materially without local organic change.
8. **Capital return** — dividends, buybacks and acquisition/disposal allocation.
9. **Price recognition** — P/TBV or P/E versus normalized RoTE and required return.

The EBA’s CRR3/CRD6 dashboard accompanies the package effective from 1 January 2025 and projects capital metrics across the output-floor phase-in. The relevant company question is not simply whether CET1 is high, but how the fully loaded RWA regime changes excess capital and sustainable per-share return.

### 3.3 Bank archetypes

#### Pan-European universal bank

Economic bridge:

```text
local retail/commercial NII
+ transaction / wealth / market fees
+ cross-border diversification
− credit loss
− operational and regulatory cost
− capital consumed across jurisdictions
= distributable per-share earnings and tangible-book compounding
```

Primary anchors:

- normalized RoTE versus cost of equity;
- tangible book value per share;
- CET1 generation and distribution;
- segment sum-of-the-parts where mix is material.

Examples for research: Santander, BNP Paribas, HSBC.

#### UK deposit-led bank

Key distinctions:

- structural-hedge reinvestment;
- deposit migration and pass-through;
- mortgage and consumer-credit mix;
- ring-fenced bank versus group scope;
- conduct/remediation and motor-finance or legacy charges;
- PRA capital and distribution.

Example: Lloyds Banking Group.

#### Wealth-led Swiss bank

Key distinctions:

- invested assets versus revenue and profit;
- net new money quality;
- recurring versus transaction-based income;
- credit exposure to wealth clients;
- integration and cost-removal;
- Swiss capital-rule uncertainty and group buffers.

Example: UBS.

### 3.4 Europe/UK insurer and reinsurer rerating

Do not compare premium growth with value creation without the underwriting, contract-service-margin, investment and capital bridges.

#### P&C and diversified insurer

```text
earned premium / insurance revenue
− claims and loss adjustment
− acquisition / expense
= underwriting result
+ investment result
+ asset-management / life contribution
− capital and tax
= shareholder earnings and solvency generation
```

Required clocks:

- price versus loss-cost trend;
- catastrophe and reserve development;
- reinsurance purchase and attachment;
- investment yield and duration;
- Solvency II capital generation;
- cash remittance to holding company;
- one-offs and disposals;
- IFRS 17 versus regulatory capital.

Example: Allianz.

#### Reinsurer

Key variables:

- rate and terms at renewal;
- exposure and aggregate limits;
- large-loss and catastrophe experience;
- prior-year reserve development;
- retrocession;
- investment result;
- foreign exchange;
- solvency capital and dividend/buyback capacity.

Example: Munich Re.

The EIOPA July 2026 dashboard describes stable medium-level overall risk with strong premium growth, some loss-ratio deterioration, private-credit monitoring and high cyber/digital risk. Those are sector observations, not company valuation conclusions.

### 3.5 European market-infrastructure rerating

LSEG illustrates a mixed recurring/transactional utility:

```text
subscription ASV and retention
+ index / data / workflow growth
+ clearing and post-trade activity
+ capital-markets activity
− data/content and technology cost
− integration / restructuring
− leverage and capital allocation
= FCF and per-share compounding
```

A clearing or data franchise deserves a different anchor from a bank or insurer. Recurring subscription economics, clearing volumes, rate sensitivity, customer ownership and market structure should be visible separately.

## 4. Japan

### 4.1 System map

```text
Bank of Japan
→ megabanks / regional and shinkin banks
→ yen deposits, domestic and overseas loans, JGBs and cross-shareholdings
→ FSA capital / liquidity / conduct supervision
→ insurers and economic-value solvency / ICS transition
→ broker-dealers and wealth channels
→ JPX / JSCC cash, derivatives and clearing
→ domestic households, corporates, pensions and foreign investors
```

### 4.2 Japan is a rate-normalization and capital-efficiency system

Japan’s financial rerating requires at least six separate clocks:

1. **Yen rate clock** — asset yield and deposit pass-through.
2. **JGB clock** — reinvestment benefit versus duration valuation loss.
3. **Loan-volume clock** — domestic, overseas, real-estate and NBFI exposure.
4. **Cross-shareholding clock** — sale gains, market risk and capital release.
5. **Expense/demography clock** — wage, technology and shrinking domestic demand.
6. **Capital-return clock** — dividend, buyback, policy stakes and RWA allocation.

The BOJ April 2026 Financial System Report states that PPNR has improved with higher yen rates, while expenses, bond valuation losses, real-estate lending and foreign NBFI/private-fund exposures warrant attention. This rejects a one-variable `HIGHER_RATES_HELP_JAPANESE_BANKS` state.

### 4.3 Megabanks

Economic bridge:

```text
yen spread normalization
+ loan growth and international banking
+ fee / market / wealth income
+ cross-shareholding gains or release
− deposit repricing
− JGB / securities valuation and hedge cost
− overseas credit and FX risk
− operating expense
− regulatory capital
= normalized group ROE and book-value growth
```

Primary anchors:

- normalized ROE;
- book value per share;
- CET1 and RWA efficiency;
- total payout and cross-shareholding reduction;
- segment SOTP for trust, securities, asset management and leasing.

Research examples:

- Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group;
- Sumitomo Mitsui Financial Group;
- Mizuho Financial Group.

### 4.4 Securities and wealth

Nomura must be separated into:

- recurring wealth-management economics;
- wholesale markets and investment banking;
- investment/merchant exposure;
- compensation and operating leverage;
- capital allocated to volatile businesses.

A rise in market activity can improve current earnings without changing the durable recurring franchise. Conversely, stable client assets can support a higher-quality mix even when one quarter’s trading income falls.

### 4.5 Insurance

Japan’s insurer rerating must preserve:

- domestic life new-business value or source-native equivalent;
- protection/savings product mix;
- interest-rate and duration mismatch;
- foreign-currency assets and hedge cost;
- cross-shareholding reduction;
- economic-value capital / ICS;
- catastrophe and claims experience for P&C;
- accounting transition and source-native adjusted profit.

Examples:

- Tokio Marine;
- Dai-ichi Life, held for exact primary-source transcription in the structured packet if not fully sourced.

### 4.6 Market infrastructure

JPX/JSCC should be mapped as:

- cash-equity venue;
- derivatives venue;
- clearing utility;
- listing and issuer-service platform;
- data/index provider.

Japan’s rate normalization can raise interest-rate derivatives and clearing activity without guaranteeing recurring revenue quality. Product, ADV, open interest, clearing notional, data and listing metrics remain separate.

## 5. Mainland China and Hong Kong

### 5.1 Two linked but distinct systems

Mainland China and Hong Kong cannot be represented as one market.

#### Mainland China

```text
PBOC / NFRA / CSRC / MOF
→ state-owned, joint-stock, city and rural banks
→ policy-directed credit and bond holdings
→ property / LGFV / industrial / household / SME exposures
→ domestic payment and clearing rails
→ A-share / interbank / exchange markets
→ C-ROSS II insurers and local accounting
```

#### Hong Kong

```text
currency-board and HKMA liquidity
→ local and cross-border banks
→ HKD / USD / RMB funding and wealth
→ Hong Kong RBC insurers
→ HKEX cash, derivatives, clearing and Stock Connect
→ H shares / red chips / international listings
→ Mainland and global capital flows
```

### 5.2 Mainland bank rerating clocks

1. **Policy credit** — aggregate growth and target sectors.
2. **Loan pricing** — LPR and new-loan rate; margin can compress despite volume.
3. **Deposit pricing and mix** — administratively and competitively influenced.
4. **Property/LGFV and legacy asset quality** — reported NPL is not full economic loss.
5. **Fee and wealth-management regulation**.
6. **Bond holdings and policy implementation**.
7. **Capital and state ownership**.
8. **Dividend durability and payout policy**.
9. **A/H security and investor-access valuation gap**.

The PBOC reported that loans and bonds are large parts of bank assets and that banks hold major shares of government and corporate credit bonds. This means a China bank cannot be analyzed only as a loan spread franchise.

### 5.3 China insurer rerating

Required distinct planes:

- life new-business value;
- value margin and channel mix;
- persistency;
- insurance-service and operating profit;
- investment portfolio and property/credit exposure;
- C-ROSS II or source-native regulatory capital;
- bank/wealth/health ecosystem;
- dividend/remittance capacity;
- A/H listing and currency.

Ping An is a conglomerate, not a pure insurer. Life, P&C, bank, wealth/asset management, health/eldercare and group capital must remain separate.

### 5.4 Hong Kong rate and liquidity transmission

Under the Linked Exchange Rate System, U.S. rate moves transmit through HKD liquidity and HIBOR with local timing and balance-sheet effects. A bank’s Hong Kong spread economics can therefore differ from its Mainland, UK or global franchises.

Required fields:

- HKD/USD/RMB funding mix;
- HIBOR and prime-rate sensitivity;
- aggregate-balance and liquidity context;
- mortgage and property exposure;
- Mainland wealth and cross-border flows;
- insurance and asset-management distribution;
- local versus group capital.

### 5.5 Stock Connect and HKEX

Stock Connect is a cross-border access and liquidity system, not merely a trading-volume series.

Separate:

- Northbound and Southbound ADT;
- eligible stocks and ETFs;
- quota/access and holiday/calendar effects;
- cash-equity turnover;
- clearing and settlement;
- derivatives and hedging ecosystem;
- listings and capital formation;
- product/index/data economics;
- investment income on corporate/clearing balances.

HKEX reported 2025 Northbound ADT of RMB212.4 billion and Southbound ADT of HK$121.1 billion, both records, with ETF activity also increasing. That is an operating-volume fact, not proof of the exchange’s current valuation or future profit growth.

## 6. India

### 6.1 System map

```text
RBI / SEBI / IRDAI / PFRDA
→ public and private banks
→ NBFCs and housing-finance companies
→ deposits, wholesale funding, securitization and co-lending
→ UPI / IMPS / RuPay and bank/payment-app ecosystem
→ mutual funds, insurers, pensions and capital markets
→ local accounting, prudential and priority-sector rules
```

### 6.2 India bank and NBFC rerating

India often combines high nominal volume growth with meaningful funding and credit-cost dispersion.

Required clocks:

1. Deposit and CASA mobilization.
2. Loan/AUM growth by secured/unsecured product.
3. NIM and funding-cost change.
4. GNPA/NNPA, Stage or regulatory asset quality.
5. Provision and overlays.
6. capital adequacy and growth capacity.
7. branch/distribution and digital customer acquisition.
8. operating cost and cross-sell.
9. local fiscal year and Indian GAAP/Ind AS scope.
10. valuation expectations for high growth.

HDFC Bank is a bank plus a group of insurance, asset-management and finance subsidiaries. Bajaj Finance is an NBFC with a different liability, product, capital and credit contract. They must not be compared as one `India financials growth` model.

### 6.3 UPI and payment rails

NPCI reported August 2026 UPI volume of 24.509 billion transactions across 752 live banks, with value reported in crore rupees. UPI activity should be decomposed into:

- rail/system volume;
- payer and payee bank roles;
- third-party-app activity;
- merchant acquiring and acceptance;
- fraud/dispute;
- monetization source;
- cross-sell and deposit effects;
- infrastructure cost.

Payment volume is not processor or bank revenue. A public rail’s scale can be economically valuable while the monetization accrues to different participants.

## 7. Singapore, Australia and Korea

### 7.1 Singapore

Singapore’s leading-bank rerating can combine:

- SGD and foreign-currency deposit franchises;
- hedging and structural-rate management;
- regional corporate credit;
- wealth AUM and fee income;
- bancassurance;
- treasury/customer flow;
- high capital return;
- cross-border asset quality.

DBS reported 2025 total income of SGD22.9 billion, profit before tax of SGD13.1 billion and ROE of 16.2%. Its own disclosure says lower rates reduced commercial-book NII while deposit growth, hedging, wealth fees and markets income offset the pressure. This is a regional universal/wealth bank, not a pure NIM exposure.

### 7.2 Australia

Australia’s system is mortgage-heavy and prudentially distinct.

Required clocks:

- owner-occupied versus investor mortgage growth;
- variable/fixed and offset-account behavior;
- deposit pricing and wholesale funding;
- mortgage competition and NIM;
- arrears, NPL, serviceability, LVR and DTI;
- APRA CET1 / harmonized comparison;
- operational and conduct cost;
- franked dividends and buybacks;
- housing macro and regulatory limits.

APRA’s June 2026 data show A$2.5585 trillion of residential credit, 31.2% investor share and 1.01% non-performing loans. These aggregate statistics inform the local system; they do not substitute for company underwriting or valuation.

CBA should be modeled as a domestic deposit/mortgage/transaction bank with franchise and capital-return economics. Macquarie is a diversified global asset manager, market/commodities intermediary, bank and investment firm; its group AUM, markets-facing profit and bank capital require SOTP treatment.

### 7.3 Korea

Korea requires:

- household-credit and housing sensitivity;
- bank-holding-company diversification;
- credit-card and nonbank subsidiaries;
- insurer K-IFRS 17/K-ICS;
- CET1-linked shareholder-return policy;
- currency and wholesale funding;
- governance and value-up policy.

KB Financial’s CET1-linked shareholder-return framework is a separate observable from earnings. A higher payout can rerate the security only if credit, capital, RWA and per-share book economics remain durable.

## 8. Regional rerating archetypes

### 8.1 Rate normalization

Applies differently:

- Japan: positive asset repricing versus deposit pass-through and bond marks.
- Europe/UK: structural hedge and deposit competition versus lower-rate pressure.
- Hong Kong: HIBOR/liquidity timing under a currency board.
- Singapore: hedge and wealth offset to commercial-book NII.
- Australia: mortgage/deposit competition.
- China: policy easing may support volume while compressing margins.

### 8.2 Capital-release rerating

Potential triggers:

- fully loaded regulatory clarity;
- lower RWA intensity;
- disposal or cross-shareholding release;
- reserve or risk normalization;
- surplus solvency capital;
- explicit payout framework.

Falsifiers:

- rising required capital;
- stress loss;
- acquisition consumption;
- weak organic generation;
- distribution restriction;
- currency or holding-company remittance friction.

### 8.3 Business-mix-quality rerating

Examples:

- bank toward wealth/transaction fees;
- exchange toward data/index/workflow;
- insurer toward protection and capital-light fee income;
- conglomerate toward transparent SOTP;
- asset manager toward permanent or fee-paying capital.

The quality claim requires revenue, margin, retention and per-share evidence, not segment labels.

### 8.4 Policy or market-access rerating

Examples:

- Stock Connect expansion;
- Basel/CRR3 clarification;
- Solvency II review;
- Japan corporate-governance and cross-shareholding changes;
- India digital rails;
- Korea value-up policy.

A rule change creates option inventory. Realized economics require adoption, volumes, pricing, cost, capital and persistence.

### 8.5 Risk-discount normalization

Examples:

- property/LGFV stabilization;
- mortgage/consumer loss normalization;
- catastrophe reserve confidence;
- acquisition/integration de-risking;
- duration and hedge stabilization.

A falling reported NPL or combined ratio does not suffice if definitions, write-offs, reserves or exposure mix changed.

## 13. Evaluation implications

R10 historical cases must be stratified by:

- jurisdiction and regulatory era;
- accounting era;
- monetary regime;
- business model;
- security/listing and currency;
- capital framework;
- local market-access regime;
- source availability.

Do not pool Japan rate-normalization cases with U.S. bank cases, or Chinese policy-credit cases with European spread cases, merely because all are called “banks.”

Minimum historical nulls:

- local fundamentals improve but currency offsets the investor return;
- reported profit rises from reserve release or disposal while normalized value does not;
- capital ratio rises because RWA contracts and growth deteriorates;
- payment volume grows without monetization;
- insurer premium/VONB grows while free surplus or solvency deteriorates;
- Stock Connect or market turnover rises but fee yield/mix weakens;
- policy announcement creates a temporary rerating with no realized earnings.
