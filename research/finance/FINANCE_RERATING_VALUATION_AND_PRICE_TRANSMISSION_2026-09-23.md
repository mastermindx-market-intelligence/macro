## 1. Executive ruling

### R-FIN-RR-1 — Rerating is the core Finance intelligence job

The core investor question is not merely:

> Where is a financial bottleneck?

It is:

> What has to change in the earnings, book-value, free-cash-flow, risk and capital-return path for this financial theme or company to be worth a different multiple—and has the stock begun to recognize that change?

Finance intelligence must therefore explain three distinct sources of shareholder return:

1. growth in the appropriate per-share economic anchor;
2. cash distributions and net share-count change;
3. change in the valuation multiple applied to that anchor.

### R-FIN-RR-2 — The valuation anchor is subtheme-specific

There is no universal Finance valuation multiple.

- A deposit-taking bank is not valued like a card network.
- A P&C insurer is not valued like an insurance broker.
- A transaction exchange is not valued like a recurring-data vendor.
- A traditional asset manager is not valued like an alternative manager.
- A mortgage originator is not valued like a mortgage servicer.
- A BDC is not valued like the private-credit manager sponsoring it.
- A financial-software vendor is not valued like the institutions running on its software.

Every research slice must declare its primary economic anchor, secondary cross-checks and the circumstances under which those anchors become unreliable.

### R-FIN-RR-3 — Fundamentals, expectations, valuation and price remain separate planes

The product must preserve four independently clocked planes:

1. **Operating economics** — what the business is earning, losing, retaining and compounding.
2. **Expectations** — what analysts and the market expected, and how that path changed.
3. **Valuation** — what multiple or required return investors apply to the expected path.
4. **Price recognition** — whether price, relative strength, breadth, volume and flows have begun to express that interpretation.

No fused Finance rerating score is authorized. A conflict between planes is often the most valuable information.

### R-FIN-RR-4 — Improving fundamentals do not guarantee a rising stock

A business may report stronger current earnings while the stock falls because:

- the result was already expected;
- forward estimates decline;
- earnings quality or durability weakens;
- the business is overearning relative to normalized conditions;
- required returns rise;
- risk or capital intensity rises;
- valuation was already extreme;
- positioning and flows reverse.

Conversely, a stock may rerate before reported earnings improve because the market sees stabilization, reduced tail risk, better future margins, capital release or a changed terminal-growth path.

### R-FIN-RR-5 — A bottleneck is admitted only through an economic bridge

A constraint enters the rerating view only when the research can state:

```text
constraint or access advantage
  → affected workflow and quantity
  → revenue, pricing, cost, loss, capital, duration or market-share effect
  → estimate-path implication
  → valuation implication
  → observable confirmation / falsifier
```

A concentrated clearing utility, scarce charter, proprietary dataset or large merchant network may be strategically important. It is not automatically growing, profitable, underappreciated or attractively valued.

### R-FIN-RR-6 — Existing owners remain canonical

The Finance program will consume existing earnings, revisions, valuation, sector, rates, credit, company-intelligence, Leader Radar, ThemeState and GMI evidence owners where accepted. It must not create a second generic rerating engine, consensus history, valuation service, price-leadership plane or security identity master.

### R-FIN-RR-7 — Rerating research is descriptive and evaluative, not trade authority

This framework may describe mechanisms, states, historical outcomes and forward watch conditions. It may not originate or size trades, alter Prophet eligibility, or convert a valuation state directly into action.

## 2. Shareholder-return decomposition

### 2.1 General identity

For a suitable per-share economic anchor `A` and valuation multiple `M`:

```text
Price_t = Anchor_t × Multiple_t
```

A useful approximation for total shareholder return is:

```text
TSR
≈ growth in the per-share economic anchor
 + change in the valuation multiple
 + cash distributions
 - economic dilution not already captured per share
```

The exact multiplicative identity is preferred when data permit:

```text
Price_1 / Price_0 = (Anchor_1 / Anchor_0) × (Multiple_1 / Multiple_0)
```

Dividends and other distributions are added separately. Buybacks affect both cash and per-share quantities and must not be double-counted. Acquisitions, divestitures, accounting changes and security conversions require explicit bridges.

### 2.2 Anchor law

The anchor must represent the business's actual value-creation mechanism.

Possible anchors include:

- tangible book value per share;
- book value per share excluding specified OCI distortions;
- normalized EPS;
- distributable or fee-related earnings;
- free cash flow per share;
- net asset value per share;
- recurring revenue or ARR with a credible margin path;
- segment-level sum-of-the-parts value.

Revenue, AUM, payment volume, deposits, premiums and transaction counts are usually **drivers of the anchor**, not substitutes for per-share value.

### 2.3 Bank and insurer residual-income lens

Under restrictive stable-growth and clean-surplus assumptions, a book-value business can be described by:

```text
P / B ≈ (ROE - g) / (required return - g)
```

For banks, tangible book and ROTCE may be the more useful form. This is an explanatory identity, not a forecasting shortcut. It becomes unreliable when:

- current ROE is cyclically abnormal;
- reserve adequacy is uncertain;
- capital requirements are changing;
- OCI or duration marks obscure economic value;
- substantial off-balance-sheet or fee businesses are present;
- acquisitions, minority interests or intangibles dominate;
- expected growth requires dilutive capital.

The practical lesson is robust even when the formula is not: **a durable positive spread between normalized return on equity and the required return supports a higher price-to-book multiple; a fragile or negative spread supports a discount.**

## 3. Four-plane Finance state

### 3.1 Plane A — Operating economics

This plane answers:

- What is growing?
- What is the unit economics bridge?
- What losses or risks are being absorbed?
- How much capital is required?
- What per-share economic value is being created?

Common fields:

```text
revenue and mix
yield / fee rate / take rate / rate-on-line
funding cost
credit or underwriting cost
operating expense and operating leverage
capital intensity
per-share earnings / book / NAV / FCF growth
cash conversion
share count
```

### 3.2 Plane B — Expectations and revisions

This plane answers:

- What did investors expect before the new evidence?
- Which period changed?
- Did the long-term path change or only one quarter?
- Are estimates rising because of genuine volume/margin durability or a temporary factor?
- Is the revision broad across the subtheme or concentrated in one company?

Required distinctions:

- level versus change;
- current quarter versus next year versus terminal path;
- reported beat versus forward guidance;
- earnings revision versus valuation-target revision;
- same-period comparable estimate history;
- analyst dispersion and coverage;
- model-derived estimate versus external consensus.

### 3.3 Plane C — Valuation and required return

This plane answers:

- What is the appropriate anchor?
- What normalized profitability and growth does the current multiple imply?
- What risk premium is embedded?
- Is the company being treated as a cyclical, utility, compounder, platform, credit vehicle or distressed balance sheet?
- Has the business mix changed enough to justify a different framework?

Valuation is not only cheap versus expensive. It is the market's joint view of:

```text
normalized profitability
growth duration
cyclicality and tail risk
capital intensity
balance-sheet leverage
accounting confidence
regulatory risk
liquidity and governance
capital return
```

### 3.4 Plane D — Price and flow recognition

This plane answers:

- Is the stock outperforming its sector and relevant subtheme?
- Is leadership broad or concentrated?
- Is price confirming estimate revisions?
- Is the stock rising on improving fundamentals or only factor/market beta?
- Is ownership becoming crowded?
- Has the market already advanced farther than the evidence?

Applicable existing-owner outputs may include relative strength, lifecycle, breadth, participation, volume, flow and crowding context. This plane does not overrule the other three.

### 3.5 High-value conflict states

| Conflict | Interpretation to investigate |
|---|---|
| Operating improves; revisions do not | evidence may be backward-looking, one-off or not material enough |
| Revisions improve; valuation compresses | required return or perceived risk is rising; prior expectations may be too high |
| Valuation expands; revisions do not | narrative, factor or option value is leading; proof burden rises |
| Price leads; operating evidence lags | market may be anticipating an inflection, squeezing or mispricing |
| Operating improves; price lags | under-recognition, low confidence, poor capital allocation or hidden risk |
| Price falls while revisions rise | multiple compression, crowding, higher rates or falling terminal-growth assumptions |
| Price rises while risk worsens | short-covering, liquidity or delayed recognition; never auto-rationalize |

## 4. Finance rerating clocks

Financial businesses often rerate because several clocks turn at different speeds. The product must preserve them.

### Clock 1 — Macro and funding

- policy rates;
- curve shape and term premium;
- credit spreads;
- liquidity and reserves;
- deposit and wholesale funding conditions;
- asset prices and volatility;
- unemployment, nominal spending and housing activity.

### Clock 2 — Activity and volume

- loans, originations and commitments;
- payment value and transactions;
- trading volume, open interest and issuance;
- AUM, client assets, net flows and fundraising;
- premiums, insured exposures and policy count;
- mortgage applications, locks and servicing balances.

### Clock 3 — Unit economics and margin

- deposit beta and funding cost;
- asset yield and NIM;
- fee, take-rate or revenue-per-contract changes;
- client incentives, rebates and distribution cost;
- loss-cost trend versus pricing;
- expense and compensation leverage;
- software gross margin and implementation burden.

### Clock 4 — Risk and loss recognition

- delinquencies and migration;
- reserve and provision changes;
- charge-offs, nonaccruals and restructurings;
- catastrophe and reserve development;
- counterparty, liquidity and margin requirements;
- duration, prepayment and lapse behavior;
- cyber, conduct and operational events.

### Clock 5 — Capital and distribution

- regulatory capital and buffers;
- leverage and liquidity;
- dividend and buyback capacity;
- debt reduction;
- acquisition funding and integration;
- capital trapped in subsidiaries;
- rating-agency or regulator constraints.

### Clock 6 — Expectations and revisions

- estimate direction and magnitude;
- guidance change;
- estimate dispersion;
- long-term growth and normalized margin assumptions;
- perceived earnings quality;
- risk-premium and cost-of-equity changes.

### Clock 7 — Valuation and price recognition

- multiple change;
- peer-relative and history-relative valuation;
- relative strength and breadth;
- investor flows and ownership;
- factor exposure;
- crowding and narrative maturity.

### Regulatory and market-structure overlay

A rule, court decision, capital standard, fee cap, clearing mandate, product approval or accounting change can affect several clocks at once. Announcement, adoption, effective date, implementation and economic realization are separate events.

## 5. Rerating archetypes

A company or subtheme may participate in more than one archetype. Each must be evidenced separately.

### 5.1 Cyclical earnings inflection

The activity, margin or loss cycle turns from deterioration toward improvement.

Examples:

- capital-markets issuance recovers;
- NIM stops falling;
- payment cross-border activity accelerates;
- mortgage volume and production margins recover;
- insurance pricing again exceeds loss-cost trend.

### 5.2 Risk-discount normalization

The market requires less compensation for a feared balance-sheet, credit, liquidity, reserve, legal or regulatory outcome.

The stock can rerate before EPS rises if the probability or severity of the downside state falls.

### 5.3 Quality and durability rerating

The market reclassifies earnings as more recurring, less capital-intensive, less cyclical or more defensible.

Examples:

- a transaction business develops a meaningful recurring data/software layer;
- an asset manager grows perpetual-capital and base-fee earnings;
- a lender proves deposit-franchise durability;
- an insurer improves reserve confidence and underwriting consistency.

### 5.4 Structural growth and market-share gain

The addressable market, penetration or company share grows faster than previously assumed.

The research must distinguish total-market growth from company-specific share, price, mix and acquisition effects.

### 5.5 Capital release and return

Excess capital, lower required capital, reduced uncertainty or divestiture proceeds enable dividends, buybacks, debt reduction or higher-return reinvestment.

A buyback is not automatically accretive. Price paid, capital need, share-count change and foregone alternatives matter.

### 5.6 Business-mix rerating

A conglomerate shifts toward a business with a different growth, margin, recurrence or capital profile.

Sum-of-the-parts logic is appropriate only when segment economics and allocation are credible.

### 5.7 Operating-leverage rerating

Revenue grows faster than the cost base or a fixed platform absorbs more activity. It can reverse when volume falls, incentives rise, compensation resets or compliance costs increase.

### 5.8 Regulation and market-structure rerating

A change expands or narrows permitted economics, competition, capital requirements, fee pools, product access or settlement design.

Regulation can create both a moat and a cost burden. The net effect must be measured.

### 5.9 Consolidation and scale rerating

A company gains distribution, data, clients, product breadth or cost leverage through M&A or industry consolidation. The research must preserve purchase price, funding, integration, divestitures, dilution and organic versus acquired growth.

### 5.10 Discount-rate and factor rerating

Long-duration, high-growth or high-dividend financial businesses can rerate because required returns change even if operations do not. Rate sensitivity differs by business and must not be inferred from the sector label.

### 5.11 Narrative or option-value rerating

AI, tokenization, stablecoins, real-time payments or a new distribution channel may create option value before material earnings. The product must say when revenue, capital need and adoption remain unproven.

## 6. Rerating lifecycle

The following lifecycle is a research grammar, not a new canonical state machine or trading signal.

```text
IMPAIRED / DISCOUNTED
  → STABILIZING
  → OPERATING INFLECTION
  → ESTIMATE RECOGNITION
  → MULTIPLE RERATING
  → PER-SHARE COMPOUNDING
  → OVER-EARNING / CROWDING RISK
  → DE-RATING OR RESET
```

### Impaired / discounted

The market prices a material probability of weak profitability, dilution, losses, funding stress, reserve inadequacy, regulatory damage or structural decline.

### Stabilizing

The negative rate of change slows. Balance-sheet, liquidity, loss, flow or volume evidence stops worsening, but a durable recovery is not yet proven.

### Operating inflection

A relevant operating driver improves: volume, spread, pricing, losses, flows, margin, capital efficiency or market share.

### Estimate recognition

Point-in-time expectations begin to rise or downside dispersion falls. A single post-event price target is not sufficient evidence.

### Multiple rerating

The market applies a higher multiple because normalized profitability, growth durability, risk or capital return is believed to have improved.

### Per-share compounding

The business repeatedly grows the appropriate anchor per share and distributes capital without undermining resilience.

### Over-earning / crowding risk

Current margins, loss experience, volatility, issuance, spreads or asset prices may be better than a sustainable norm while the multiple assumes persistence.

### De-rating or reset

Revisions, risk, required return or price recognition reverses. The operating business may still be growing in absolute terms.

## 8. Subtheme rerating maps

### 8.1 Universal and money-center banks

**Economic bridge**

```text
asset balances × asset yield
- funding balances × funding cost
= net interest income
+ investment banking / trading / payments / wealth / service fees
- operating expense
- credit cost
- tax
= earnings available to common
```

**Upward rerating conditions**

- sustainable ROTCE rises relative to cost of equity;
- TBVPS grows through the cycle;
- deposit funding proves durable and appropriately priced;
- credit losses and reserve uncertainty remain controlled;
- fee pools recover without excessive compensation leakage;
- capital buffers permit distributions after regulatory needs;
- business mix becomes less volatile or more capital-efficient.

**Leading and coincident evidence**

- deposit growth/mix and deposit pricing;
- asset repricing and loan mix;
- lending standards and demand;
- issuance, M&A, trading and wealth activity;
- delinquencies, criticized assets and early-stage migration;
- CET1/RWA, liquidity, AOCI and capital actions;
- estimate revisions and TBVPS expectations.

**De-rating traps**

- treating high policy rates as automatically positive when deposit beta rises;
- annualizing a trading or provision benefit;
- ignoring regulatory capital changes;
- treating derivatives notional as either revenue or loss exposure;
- mistaking nominal EPS growth for per-share value creation after dilution or M&A.

### 8.2 Regional and community banks

**Primary rerating mechanism**

The stock often rerates when a feared funding, duration, CRE or capital outcome becomes less likely and normalized ROTCE/TBVPS growth becomes visible.

**Upward conditions**

- deposits stabilize or grow without expensive wholesale substitution;
- deposit beta and funding cost peak;
- NIM troughs with a credible asset-repricing bridge;
- securities marks and duration become manageable through time/capital;
- CRE or concentrated-credit uncertainty falls;
- capital headroom and liquidity improve;
- consolidation creates credible scale without destructive dilution.

**De-rating conditions**

- uninsured or concentrated deposits remain flight-prone;
- wholesale funding masks weak franchise deposits;
- CRE, construction or consumer concentrations deteriorate;
- held-to-maturity and OCI economics pressure capital confidence;
- regulation raises the required capital or cost base;
- apparent EPS recovery relies on reserve release or security gains.

### 8.3 Card and consumer lenders

**Economic bridge**

```text
receivables × revenue yield
- funding cost
- rewards / acquisition / servicing cost
- net credit losses
- operating expense
= risk-adjusted earnings
```

**Rerating conditions**

- purchase volume and receivables grow with stable or improving risk-adjusted margin;
- new vintages perform as expected;
- delinquency and charge-off paths stabilize relative to pricing and reserves;
- funding mix and cost improve;
- rewards and acquisition spending generate durable primary relationships;
- capital remains sufficient after growth.

**Trap**

Loan or customer growth that raises future losses faster than revenue should not read as improving growth quality.

### 8.4 Specialty finance, BNPL, SMB and equipment finance

Required distinctions:

- principal lender versus originator/distributor;
- on-balance-sheet receivable versus serviced or sold asset;
- warehouse funding versus deposits;
- gross originations versus retained exposure;
- gain-on-sale revenue versus recurring servicing income;
- credit-adjusted contribution margin.

Rerating requires proof that growth survives funding, losses, securitization markets and customer-acquisition cost.

### 8.5 Investment banks and broker-dealers

**Drivers**

- equity and debt issuance;
- M&A backlog and completion;
- client trading and hedging activity;
- prime brokerage and securities finance;
- wealth/client asset flows;
- compensation ratio and operating leverage;
- capital usage and regulatory constraints.

**Rerating conditions**

- fee pools broaden across products;
- announced pipelines convert to revenue;
- market share rises;
- compensation and expense do not absorb the cycle recovery;
- wealth or recurring businesses reduce cyclicality;
- normalized ROE exceeds the required return.

A single volatile trading quarter cannot prove a new earnings regime.

### 8.6 Wealth platforms and online brokers

**Economic bridge**

```text
client assets × asset-based fee rate
+ client cash / balances × net interest economics
+ trading and order economics
+ lending / margin / banking products
- servicing, acquisition and platform costs
```

**Rerating conditions**

- durable net new assets and household acquisition;
- primary-account penetration and retention;
- better monetization without customer attrition;
- stable cash-sorting behavior;
- operating leverage and capital return;
- product expansion that raises lifetime value.

**Trap**

Rising client assets from market appreciation are not the same as organic net new assets. Higher policy rates can raise NII while accelerating cash migration into higher-cost products.

### 8.7 Exchanges, clearinghouses and market infrastructure

**Economic bridge**

```text
transaction / clearing revenue
= volume or open interest × effective revenue per unit × product mix

plus recurring data, connectivity, listings, technology and services
plus or minus collateral / treasury economics
```

**Rerating conditions**

- structural growth in participation, hedging, products or open interest;
- recurring revenue becomes larger and more durable;
- pricing or revenue per unit holds after rebates and mix;
- cross-sell and distribution improve;
- operating leverage and capital return persist;
- clearing or post-trade expansion earns an acceptable return on required capital.

**De-rating conditions**

- peak volatility/volume is annualized;
- rebate and incentive growth erodes effective monetization;
- collateral interest is treated as permanent operating growth;
- regulation changes access, data or clearing economics;
- a concentrated product or client weakens.

### 8.8 Market makers and liquidity providers

A rerating should rest on durable market share, technology, product breadth, client relationships, capital efficiency and risk control—not one period of unusually wide spreads or volatility.

Track:

- normalized trading income by product;
- volume, spread and volatility regime;
- market share;
- inventory and balance-sheet use;
- loss days and tail behavior;
- compensation and technology expense;
- regulatory capital.

### 8.9 Card networks

**Economic bridge**

```text
payments volume / transactions / cross-border activity
× applicable service and processing economics
+ value-added services
- client incentives and rebates
- operating expense
= operating profit and free cash flow
```

**Rerating conditions**

- nominal consumer/commercial volume remains durable;
- cross-border mix improves;
- processed transactions and credentials expand;
- value-added services grow faster and deepen client dependence;
- incentives remain controlled relative to gross revenue;
- margins and cash conversion hold;
- legal/regulatory outcomes preserve economics.

**Trap**

Gross payment volume does not directly equal net revenue. Issuer interchange is not the network's assessment revenue, and authorization is not settlement.

### 8.10 Merchant acquirers, processors and gateways

**Economic bridge**

```text
merchant volume × net processing/acquiring yield
+ software / subscription / value-added services
- network, interchange and pass-through items where applicable
- servicing, sales, fraud and platform costs
```

**Rerating conditions**

- organic merchant and volume growth;
- stable net take rate after mix;
- higher value-added-service attach;
- retention and cohort economics improve;
- integration lowers cost without harming service;
- free cash flow supports deleveraging and capital return.

**De-rating conditions**

- acquisition growth masks organic weakness;
- gross TPV rises while net revenue yield falls;
- churn or customer concentration rises;
- debt and integration risk dominate;
- commoditization erodes pricing.

### 8.11 Cross-border, remittance and B2B payments

Track corridor mix, active customers, volume, net take rate, FX economics, compliance cost, working capital, losses, network reach and customer retention. Volume growth with lower take rate and higher compliance/fraud cost may not improve value.

### 8.12 Traditional asset managers

**AUM bridge**

```text
ending AUM
= beginning AUM
+ market / performance movement
+ FX movement
+ external net flows
+ acquired AUM
- divested AUM
```

**Economic bridge**

```text
average AUM × effective fee rate
+ performance and other fees
- distribution and operating expense
= earnings / free cash flow
```

**Rerating conditions**

- sustained organic long-term net flows;
- organic base-fee growth, not merely market appreciation;
- fee-rate stabilization or favorable mix;
- differentiated performance and distribution;
- margin and capital return improve;
- product expansion does not dilute economics.

**Trap**

AUM growth from a rising market can coexist with negative flows, falling fee rates and lost market share.

### 8.13 Alternative asset managers and private markets

**Separate economics**

- fee-related earnings;
- performance-related or distributable earnings;
- realized versus accrued carry;
- balance-sheet investments;
- fee-earning versus total AUM;
- perpetual versus finite-duration capital;
- fundraising, deployment, realizations and credit performance.

**Rerating conditions**

- fee-related earnings grow with durable fee-earning AUM;
- fundraising remains strong across strategies;
- deployment earns attractive economics;
- realization markets reopen;
- perpetual capital and insurance/wealth channels deepen;
- credit outcomes and marks remain credible;
- capital-light earnings receive a higher mix weight.

**De-rating conditions**

- accrued carry is mistaken for realized cash;
- marks or NAV lag public markets and credit reality;
- nonaccruals, PIK or amendments rise;
- realization drought persists;
- fee compression or fundraising concentration rises;
- leverage and contingent obligations are ignored.

### 8.14 BDCs and listed private-credit vehicles

**Primary anchor**

P/NAV plus sustainable distributable income and dividend coverage.

**Rerating conditions**

- NAV remains stable after realized and unrealized losses;
- nonaccruals and PIK remain controlled;
- asset yields exceed funding and management costs through the cycle;
- leverage is appropriate;
- dividend coverage is cash-supported;
- sponsor origination access adds value after fees.

High dividend yield alone is not an investment-quality indicator.

### 8.15 Custody banks, fund administrators and recordkeepers

Drivers include AUC/AUA, market levels, client wins/losses, servicing fee rates, NII, FX, securities finance, fund complexity, transaction volumes and technology cost.

Rerating requires evidence that organic servicing growth, client retention, scale and recurring fees outweigh market beta and fee compression.

### 8.16 P&C insurers

**Economic bridge**

```text
earned premium
- current accident-year losses
- catastrophe losses
- prior-year reserve development
- underwriting expense
= underwriting result

plus investment income
plus/minus capital and tax effects
= growth in book/tangible book value and earnings
```

**Rerating conditions**

- rate and exposure growth exceed loss-cost trend;
- current accident-year combined ratio excluding catastrophe improves;
- reserve confidence strengthens;
- catastrophe volatility is appropriately priced and capitalized;
- reinvestment yield improves without poor credit quality;
- BVPS/TBVPS compounds after dividends and buybacks;
- capital return does not weaken resilience.

**De-rating conditions**

- premium growth is driven by rate alone while exposure or retention weakens;
- social or claims inflation outruns pricing;
- adverse reserve development emerges;
- catastrophe assumptions prove inadequate;
- the underwriting cycle softens while the multiple assumes persistence.

### 8.17 Reinsurance

Key clocks:

- rate-on-line and terms;
- insured exposure and attachment points;
- primary-carrier retention;
- traditional and ILS capital supply;
- retrocession cost;
- catastrophe and reserve experience;
- investment yield;
- capital return.

A hard market can create exceptional earnings and attract new capital, which eventually contributes to softening. The product must distinguish structural franchise improvement from cycle overearning.

### 8.18 Life and annuity insurers

**Economic bridge**

- spread between asset yield and credited/guaranteed rate;
- new business volume and margin;
- lapse/surrender behavior;
- credit and duration performance;
- hedging and guarantee costs;
- capital generation and distribution.

**Rerating conditions**

- higher new-money yields improve spread economics without credit deterioration;
- lapse behavior remains manageable;
- asset-liability management is credible;
- capital and distributable cash become more visible;
- legacy guarantees or opaque exposures decline.

### 8.19 Insurance brokers and MGAs

Organic growth should be decomposed into:

```text
premium-rate movement
+ insured exposure / payroll / revenue growth
+ new business
- lost accounts
+ changes in retention and mix
```

Rerating rests on durable organic growth, retention, margin, free cash flow, data/distribution advantages and disciplined M&A. A hard insurance-pricing market can temporarily flatter organic revenue.

### 8.20 Ratings agencies

**Two-speed model**

1. transaction-sensitive ratings tied to issuance and refinancing;
2. recurring analytics, data, KYC, software and monitoring.

Rerating can occur when issuance recovers and the recurring business simultaneously proves pricing, retention and margin durability. Regulatory/legal risk and issuer concentration remain explicit.

### 8.21 Market data, indices and benchmarks

Drivers include ARR/subscription growth, pricing, retention, terminals/users, dataset breadth, index-linked assets, net flows, derivatives/products, licensing and distribution.

Rerating conditions:

- recurring organic growth and pricing;
- embedded workflow and low churn;
- index-linked assets grow from both markets and net flows;
- cross-sell and new products expand without large capital need;
- concentration and regulatory risks remain controlled.

### 8.22 Core banking, issuer processing, financial software and RegTech

**Drivers**

- ARR/ACV and organic revenue;
- gross and net retention;
- recurring revenue percentage;
- implementation duration and backlog;
- transaction/usage volumes;
- cloud conversion;
- cross-sell;
- gross margin, operating margin and FCF;
- SBC, dilution, debt and acquisition effects.

**Rerating conditions**

- durable ARR plus margin/FCF inflection;
- high retention from mission-critical integration;
- organic growth accelerates;
- implementation and service burden falls;
- product breadth increases wallet share.

**Trap**

High reported recurrence does not prove high organic growth, pricing power or low switching risk. Acquisitions can manufacture growth while debt and integration reduce per-share value.

### 8.23 Mortgage originators

**Production bridge**

```text
funded origination volume × gain-on-sale margin
- production and fulfillment cost
- hedging / fallout / funding effects
= production earnings
```

Rerating depends on mortgage rates, housing turnover, refinancing incentive, capacity, market share, pull-through and production margin. Volume growth with collapsing gain-on-sale economics may not create value.

### 8.24 Mortgage servicers

Track servicing UPB, servicing fee, ancillary revenue, prepayment/retention, delinquency, advance requirements, MSR valuation/hedges and financing.

Servicing and origination can offset one another across rate regimes, but the hedge is imperfect and company-specific.

### 8.25 Mortgage REITs

Primary state:

```text
asset yield
- repo / funding cost
± hedge economics
- credit / operating cost
× leverage
→ distributable earnings and book-value change
```

Rerating requires stable book value, prudent leverage, adequate liquidity and attractive risk-adjusted spreads. Dividend yield without book-value preservation is a classic trap.

### 8.26 Digital-asset exchanges, custody and stablecoin infrastructure

Separate:

- trading volume and take rate;
- custody assets and fees;
- subscription/services revenue;
- staking or validator economics;
- reserve assets, reserve yield and distribution arrangements;
- stablecoin supply and actual monetized use;
- institutional versus retail mix;
- regulatory status and capital/liquidity.

Token transfer volume, stablecoin supply or assets on platform do not automatically equal revenue, profit or durable adoption.

### 8.27 Neobanks, embedded finance and banking-as-a-service

Rerating requires proof that user/account growth converts into:

- primary-account behavior;
- low-cost, durable funding;
- risk-adjusted lending or fee profit;
- cross-sell and retention;
- positive contribution and corporate margins;
- adequate compliance and capital.

Subsidized account openings, interchange-only engagement or partner-bank concentration can make headline growth fragile.

## 9. Derived metric library

All derived metrics require exact inputs, period, unit, formula, source references and revision. They remain separate observations rather than a fused score.

### 9.1 Banks

```text
Sustainable return spread
= normalized ROTCE - estimated cost of equity
```

```text
Cumulative deposit beta
= change in average deposit cost over a defined rate cycle
  / change in the selected policy or market short rate
```

```text
Credit cost
= net charge-offs or provision / average loans
```

```text
Per-share economic compounding
= change in TBVPS + dividends per share
```

Buybacks, issuance, acquisitions, OCI and accounting changes require a reconciliation.

### 9.2 Consumer and specialty credit

```text
Risk-adjusted revenue yield
= revenue yield - funding cost - net credit loss rate
```

Compare vintages at like seasoning rather than pooling new and mature loans.

### 9.3 Payments

```text
Net monetization yield
= applicable net revenue after incentives/pass-through treatment
  / compatible payment or transaction denominator
```

Network, processor, acquirer and issuer denominators are not interchangeable.

### 9.4 Exchanges and FMI

```text
Effective revenue per unit
= transaction and clearing revenue / compatible contract, share or value unit
```

Product mix, rebates, tiers and collateral income remain separate.

### 9.5 Traditional asset managers

```text
Organic base-fee growth
= change in recurring base fees attributable to net flows, fee rate and mix
  excluding market appreciation, FX and acquisitions where measurable
```

### 9.6 Alternative managers

Track separately:

- FRE growth and margin;
- fee-earning AUM growth;
- perpetual-capital share;
- gross and net fundraising;
- deployment;
- realized and accrued performance revenue;
- credit losses, nonaccrual and PIK;
- share-count and capital-allocation changes.

### 9.7 Insurance

```text
Pricing adequacy proxy
= premium rate change - loss-cost trend
```

```text
Current accident-year underwriting margin ex-cat
= 100 - current accident-year combined ratio excluding catastrophe
```

```text
Shareholder-value compounding
= BVPS or TBVPS growth + dividends per share
```

Reserve development and OCI are separately shown.

### 9.8 Software and data

Track ARR/ACV growth, gross/net retention, recurring mix, organic growth, gross/operating/FCF margin and SBC dilution separately. A “Rule of 40” combination may be displayed only as a transparent derived comparison, never as an authority-bearing score.

### 9.9 Mortgage

```text
Production gross revenue proxy
= funded volume × gain-on-sale margin
```

```text
Servicing revenue base
= average servicing UPB × servicing fee rate
```

Prepayment, recapture, delinquency, advances, hedges and MSR marks remain separate.

## 10. Expectations and surprise law

Stock reaction is driven by change relative to expectations, not the absolute quality of one reported number.

A conceptual decomposition is:

```text
price response
= surprise in the earnings / book / FCF path
+ surprise in growth duration
+ surprise in risk and required return
+ surprise in capital return
+ positioning / liquidity / technical recognition
```

This is not an additive forecast equation. It is a checklist that prevents “good result = stock up” reasoning.

### Required expectation controls

- point-in-time estimate before the evidence;
- exact comparable period;
- estimate vintage and publication clock;
- analyst count and dispersion;
- guidance and management definition;
- one-off and accounting adjustments;
- price and valuation state before the event;
- relevant peer and macro moves;
- ownership/crowding where an accepted owner exists.

### Common false conclusions

- “Beat” without forward estimate improvement;
- higher AUM without organic flows;
- higher payment volume without net monetization;
- higher premium without exposure/loss-cost context;
- higher NII without deposit/funding durability;
- higher trading revenue without normalized volatility context;
- higher dividend without book-value or capital preservation;
- lower P/E caused by falling forward earnings quality.

## 11. Regime interaction map

### Falling policy rates

Potential positives:

- funding relief for some borrowers and lenders;
- mortgage/refinancing and capital-market recovery;
- higher asset valuations and AUM;
- lower required returns for duration-sensitive compounders.

Potential negatives:

- lower cash and float income;
- NIM pressure depending on asset/liability repricing;
- weaker reinvestment yields;
- signal of deteriorating growth or credit.

### Steepening yield curve

Can improve spread economics and expectations, but interpretation depends on whether the steepening reflects growth, inflation, term premium or policy easing. Credit and funding effects can dominate.

### Tightening credit spreads and reopening issuance

Supports underwriting, ratings, exchanges, alternatives, realizations and borrowers. It may simultaneously compress lender spreads and encourage weaker underwriting.

### Rising volatility

Can increase trading, hedging, clearing and market-data activity. It can also cause client deleveraging, inventory loss, collateral stress and lower asset values.

### Asset-price appreciation

Raises AUM, AUC/AUA, wealth activity and performance fees. It can conceal negative organic flows, falling fee rates or weak operating leverage.

### Inflation and nominal growth

Can support payment and premium volume while raising claims, compensation and operating costs. Real activity and unit economics remain separate.

### Recession / unemployment

Hurts credit, payment volume, issuance and flows, but timing differs. Provisions and equities often turn before realized charge-offs peak; this lead/lag must be evaluated rather than hardcoded.

## 14. Historical research and evaluation design

### 14.1 Research question

For each financial subtheme:

> Which dated changes in operating drivers, expectations, risk, capital and valuation preceded or accompanied changes in relative stock performance—and under what macro regime?

### 14.2 Required point-in-time panel

At minimum:

- historical membership and issuer/security identity;
- price, total return and peer/sector benchmark;
- valuation multiple using then-known fundamentals/estimates;
- operating-driver observations at their publication dates;
- estimate and guidance vintages;
- capital actions and share count;
- macro/regime variables;
- source and correction receipts;
- coverage and missingness.

Current membership must not be backfilled as historical truth.

### 14.3 Return decomposition

For every eligible episode, separate:

- growth in the selected per-share anchor;
- change in the applicable multiple;
- dividends/distributions;
- net dilution or buyback accretion;
- market, sector and subtheme relative return;
- material acquisition/divestiture effects.

### 14.4 Event and horizon discipline

Research windows should be preregistered by question. Candidate horizons may include immediate event response and 1/3/6/12-month follow-through, but the final design must account for reporting frequency, overlap and subtheme-specific lag. Do not shop horizons after seeing returns.

### 14.5 Controls

- sector and style factors;
- rates, curve, credit, volatility and asset-price regime;
- size and liquidity;
- historical versus current membership;
- concurrent earnings and corporate events;
- survivorship and delisting;
- acquisition and accounting regime changes;
- multiple testing and overlapping observations.

### 14.6 Forward evaluation

Historical results may justify a display/shadow hypothesis. They do not authorize a current rank or trade. Promotion requires a prospective, point-in-time evaluation with predeclared eligibility, nulls, corrections, benchmarks and failure criteria.

## 17. Primary-source findings retained in this tranche

The following findings inform the framework; they are not ratings or recommendations.

1. Bank valuation research from the BIS links P/B differences with expected profitability/ROE and capital-management buffers, supporting a sustainable-return and capital-headroom lens rather than a generic bank multiple. `[FR01]`
2. Visa and Mastercard disclosures separate payment/GDV, processed/switched transactions, cross-border activity, value-added services and client incentives, demonstrating that gross volume alone cannot explain revenue or rerating. `[FR04,FR05]`
3. CME, ICE and Nasdaq disclosures show that exchange economics combine transaction/clearing sensitivity with recurring data, listings, index, connectivity and technology revenue; the correct valuation depends on mix and normalized volume. `[FR06–FR08]`
4. BlackRock disclosures separate market movement, net flows, fee rates, base fees and technology revenue; AUM growth alone cannot establish organic earnings growth. `[FR09]`
5. Blackstone disclosures distinguish fee-related earnings, fee-earning AUM, deployment, realizations and performance revenue; total AUM is not a complete valuation anchor. `[FR10]`
6. Chubb disclosures preserve premium growth, current accident-year underwriting results, catastrophe loss, reserve development, investment income and book-value growth as separate clocks. `[FR11]`
7. Charles Schwab disclosures show why client assets, net new assets, client cash, cash sorting, funding, NII, asset-management fees and trading must remain distinct. `[FR12]`
8. Rocket disclosures separate origination volume/gain-on-sale from servicing UPB, MSR economics, retention and delinquency. `[FR14]`
9. Moody's disclosures distinguish transaction-sensitive ratings from recurring analytics, KYC and software, creating different rerating clocks inside one issuer. `[FR15]`
10. FIS, Fiserv, SS&C and Guidewire disclosures illustrate that “financial technology” spans recurring processing, transaction-linked merchant economics, AUM/account-based administration and ARR/cloud software; they require different anchors. `[FR16–FR19]`
