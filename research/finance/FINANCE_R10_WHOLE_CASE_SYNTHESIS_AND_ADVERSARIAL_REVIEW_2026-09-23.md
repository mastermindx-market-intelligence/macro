## 1. The main finding

Across twelve very different Finance events, the reusable analytical object is:

```text
dated new information
→ exact business exposure
→ operating / balance-sheet transmission
→ per-share earnings / book / cash-flow / capital path
→ what was already expected
→ change in required return / valuation
→ price recognition
→ later confirmation or falsification
```

An event label by itself has little analytical value.

The casebook repeatedly demonstrates that:

- good operating news can coexist with a falling multiple;
- rising stock price can coexist with P/E compression;
- higher regulatory capital can improve resilience while reducing marginal growth;
- lower mortgage rates can support borrowers while compressing bank asset yield;
- a lower solvency ratio can be a measurement-regime break rather than deterioration;
- a buyback can improve per-share metrics without fixing poor operating returns;
- product adoption can be real while financially immaterial to a diversified issuer;
- a major acquisition can generate accounting profit while underlying businesses remain loss-making;
- a stock can recover before reported earnings recover because tail-risk expectations improve.

The Finance product therefore needs **mechanism + expectation + valuation + recognition**, not “catalyst happened”.

## 3. Evidence maturity — what is actually strong

### Tier A — direct accounting / regulatory transmission

These are the strongest reusable Finance observations because the parameter-to-balance-sheet bridge is source-measurable.

Examples:

- RBI risk-weight change → disclosed Bajaj/SBI Cards capital-ratio impact;
- CRR3 → bank-specific RWA / CET1 impact;
- HKRBC → new numerator/denominator and local solvency regime;
- APRA serviceability buffer → explicit borrower-capacity underwriting parameter;
- acquisition accounting → UBS negative goodwill / acquired RWA;
- mortgage rate/down-payment policy → explicit loan-price/affordability parameters.

These are good candidates for durable structured facts.

### Tier B — company operating response

Examples:

- NII/liability margin;
- NIM;
- client/deposit retention;
- fee-paying assets;
- cost saves;
- credit losses/provisions;
- ROE/ROTCE;
- book or tangible book per share;
- VONB/free surplus;
- share count, dividends and repurchases.

These can establish whether the mechanism entered company economics.

### Tier C — expectation state

This remains the weakest common layer.

Historical analyst consensus is missing or incomplete in many cases. Management guidance exists for some cases but is not analyst consensus. Central-bank communication is not market-implied policy probability.

The product must explicitly say `HISTORICAL_CONSENSUS_UNAVAILABLE` instead of estimating surprise from hindsight.

### Tier D — valuation recognition

Useful only with exact denominator and information date.

The casebook demonstrates:
- ING: price up with historical P/E compression and P/B expansion;
- MUFG: accounting book/share growth plus P/B expansion;
- HKEX: measured P/E compression depends heavily on denominator definition;
- Korea: book/share can rise while plan-date-to-year-end multiple falls;
- TSE issuer examples: ROE, growth and capital allocation must be connected rather than inferred from price.

### Tier E — causal event outcome

Not established for the casebook.

Every case has material anticipation, macro, company, sector, selection or overlapping-policy issues. Formal R10 causal labels remain `UNMEASURED`.

## 4. Eight reusable Finance rerating archetypes

### A. Funding/rate normalization

Cases: ECB/ING, BOJ/MUFG.

Core bridge:

```text
asset repricing
− funding repricing
± securities / hedge / duration
− credit / expense
→ normalized returns
→ book/share and capital return
→ valuation
```

Critical variables:
- deposit beta;
- NIM/NII;
- fixed/floating mix;
- securities reinvestment;
- duration losses;
- credit;
- capital.

### B. Regulatory capital intensity

Cases: CRR3, RBI, HKRBC.

Bridge:

```text
regulatory rule
→ RWA/PCA/economic capital
→ capital headroom
→ pricing / growth / mix / reinsurance
→ return on allocated capital
→ distributions and required return
```

Key law: a ratio may be non-comparable across regime changes.

### C. Governance and capital efficiency

Cases: TSE, Korea Value-Up.

Bridge:

```text
cost-of-capital diagnosis
→ portfolio/cross-share actions
→ ROE/ROTCE
→ growth expectations
→ payout / share-count
→ P/B or P/TB recognition
```

Key law: issuer execution matters more than participation/disclosure.

### D. Forced integration / restructuring

Case: UBS/Credit Suisse.

Bridge:

```text
acquired franchise
→ client retention
→ cost takeout
→ non-core runoff
→ normalized earnings
→ capital headroom
→ lower execution discount
```

Key law: acquisition accounting and underlying earnings remain separate.

### E. Market-access / infrastructure expansion

Case: ETF Connect.

Bridge:

```text
eligibility / access
→ actual adoption
→ incremental volume
→ retained take rate / fees
→ issuer materiality
→ valuation
```

Key law: infrastructure importance is not revenue materiality.

### F. Operating permission / trust shock

Case: Paytm.

Bridge:

```text
regulatory restriction
→ product continuity / migration
→ customer retention
→ revenue / incentive / remediation cost
→ normalized earnings
→ tail-risk discount
```

Key law: stock recovery can precede earnings recovery when catastrophic-risk probability falls.

### G. Underwriting-capacity tightening

Case: APRA.

Bridge:

```text
qualification constraint
→ approved amount / borrower mix
→ origination volume
→ future credit quality
→ loss / capital
→ normalized bank returns
```

Key law: safer future earnings can require less near-term volume.

### H. Policy easing with shareholder offsets

Case: China housing.

Bridge:

```text
lower borrower hurdle / rate
→ demand / transaction support
→ collateral/property stabilization
but
→ lower asset yield / NIM
→ credit and provision consequences
→ net shareholder economics
```

Key law: policy support for the economy is not automatically accretive to the intermediary's shareholders.

## 6. Adversarial review — ten ways the research could fool us

### 1. Event selection bias

The 12 cases were purposively selected after the historical period. They illustrate mechanisms; they do not estimate unconditional forecasting performance.

### 2. First-information leakage

Implementation dates frequently follow:
- earlier legislation;
- central-bank guidance;
- transaction announcements;
- company planning;
- draft rules.

A clean event study cannot assume implementation date equals surprise date.

### 3. Outcome leakage

Later company success, disclosure status, survival or adoption must not define the pre-event cohort.

### 4. Current-membership hindsight

Several descriptive peer/cohort analyses use the incumbent current universe. They are context, not PIT basket history.

### 5. Adjusted-price misuse

Adjusted closes support return descriptions but cannot be divided directly by unadjusted historical EPS/book to create valuation.

### 6. Missing expectations

Without dated analyst consensus:
- no true surprise;
- no claim that results exceeded/failed "the market";
- management guidance remains separately labelled.

### 7. Accounting denominator drift

Examples:
- historical vs forward EPS;
- book vs tangible book;
- regulatory capital vs common equity;
- old HK solvency vs HKRBC;
- group capital vs local subsidiary capital.

### 8. Macro co-treatment

Examples:
- BOJ change + yen + Japan governance + broad rally;
- APRA buffer + later RBA hikes;
- China May housing + later September stimulus;
- European-bank results + rate/credit/political cycle.

### 9. Diversified issuer attribution

Examples:
- HKEX investment income versus ETF Connect;
- UBS purchase accounting versus underlying operations;
- Ping An property exposure versus life/asset-management factors.

### 10. Narrative asymmetry

Positive mechanisms are easier to narrate after a stock rises. The product must require:
- ex-ante falsifier;
- explicit missing state;
- negative/null cases;
- prospective accumulation.

## 7. Research findings that should become implementation law

### LAW-R10-1 — No event-to-stock shortcut

Never:

```text
event → bullish/bearish stock
```

Require the full economic bridge.

### LAW-R10-2 — Four planes are mandatory

Every dossier preserves:
1. operating economics;
2. expectations;
3. valuation;
4. price recognition.

### LAW-R10-3 — Every valuation has an anchor

Examples:
- banks → P/TBV + normalized ROTCE;
- networks/data/software → P/E/FCF;
- insurers → BV/EV/free-surplus/earnings depending model;
- asset managers → fee-related/distributable earnings;
- BDCs → NAV/NII/dividend coverage.

### LAW-R10-4 — Event clocks are multi-stage

At minimum:
- first credible information;
- announcement;
- legal/effective;
- operational implementation;
- first comparable results;
- later revision/correction.

### LAW-R10-5 — Regime breaks are first-class

Accounting, solvency, capital, corporate-action or identity changes can make a numeric time series invalid.

### LAW-R10-6 — Per-share is the investor bridge

Company growth without:
- share count;
- distributions;
- dilution;
- book/share;
- EPS/share;
- NAV/share;
- capital return

is incomplete.

### LAW-R10-7 — Retrospective evidence cannot self-promote

R10 can define features, visualizations and evaluation hypotheses. It cannot originate a production predictive factor or trade state.

## 9. Evaluation ladder required after implementation

R10 argues for a staged evaluation program:

### E0 — semantic validation
Can every input be interpreted correctly?

### E1 — source/clock validation
Was the observation actually known at the claimed date?

### E2 — mechanism validation
Does the hypothesized financial transmission appear in operating data?

### E3 — valuation validation
Did the appropriate valuation anchor change, with correct denominator?

### E4 — descriptive price recognition
Did price recognize the change, including drawdown and relative context?

### E5 — prospective prediction
Do states observed going forward predict later outcomes without hindsight?

### E6 — promotion
Only accepted prospective evidence may support any downstream ranking/trade proposal.

R10 stops before E5/E6.
