### 1.1 Seven-clock recap

1. Funding.
2. Asset repricing.
3. Origination vintage.
4. Credit performance.
5. Provision and reserve.
6. Capital and per-share book value.
7. Expectations, valuation and price recognition.

A record is incomplete if it describes only one of these clocks.

### 1.2 Cross-company interpretation law

- A large balance sheet is not a high-quality funding franchise.
- Deposit growth is not favorable when it is expensive, brokered, acquisition-driven or required to replace runoff.
- Net interest income growth is not enough without credit, capital and normalization.
- Lower provision is not favorable if allowance coverage becomes insufficient.
- EPS growth is not per-share value creation if TBVPS, NAV/share or economic capital deteriorates.
- AUM growth is not manager growth when acquired, non-fee-paying or offset by fee compression.
- High BDC NII is not sustainable dividend power when PIK, nonaccruals, marks or leverage deteriorate.
- Acquisition scale is not organic growth.
- Current valuation and consensus are separate planes and must be joined later through accepted owners.

## 3. JPMorgan Chase & Co. / JPM

### 3.1 Identity and business roles

**Legal issuer:** JPMorgan Chase & Co.  
**Security:** common stock, ticker `JPM`.  
**Economic modes:** balance-sheet principal, deposit institution, lender, card issuer, payment/treasury provider, broker-dealer, market maker, adviser, custodian, asset/wealth manager and data/technology operator.

The issuer contains businesses with different clocks:

- Consumer & Community Banking;
- Commercial & Investment Bank;
- Asset & Wealth Management;
- Corporate/other and funding/capital management.

A consolidated bank multiple cannot explain every segment. The research record must preserve both consolidated return/book economics and material segment contributions.

### 3.2 Source-backed scale anchors

JPMorgan reported for 2025:

- return on tangible common equity of approximately `20%`;
- tangible book value per share of approximately `$107.56` at year-end;
- material consumer, commercial, investment-banking, markets, payments, custody and asset-management operations.

The reported ROTCE and TBVPS are consolidated observations. They do not establish normalized returns for each segment or the market’s current expectations.

### 3.3 Earnings bridge

```text
consumer/commercial loans and securities × spreads
+ card balances × yield and fees
+ payments/treasury/custody economics
+ investment banking and markets
+ asset/wealth management fees
− deposit/wholesale funding cost
− credit losses and provision
− compensation/technology/control expense
− capital and regulatory burden
= common earnings and TBVPS compounding
```

### 3.4 Primary valuation lens

- normalized ROTCE relative to cost of equity;
- TBVPS growth after dividends and repurchases;
- P/TBV and normalized P/E;
- segment SOTP where market/IB, payments/custody and asset-management economics are material.

### 3.5 Rerating conditions

- ROTCE remains above required return through a less favorable credit/rate environment;
- TBVPS compounds after capital return;
- deposit franchise and wholesale access remain durable;
- fee and market businesses reduce dependence on one spread outcome;
- capital uncertainty falls or excess capital can be redeployed/returned;
- investment-banking recovery is accompanied by broad client activity rather than one episodic quarter;
- credit and reserve performance confirm that current earnings are not peak-cycle overearning.

### 3.6 De-rating conditions and falsifiers

- higher capital/RWA requirements reduce normalized per-share returns;
- fee/market strength masks worsening consumer or commercial credit;
- deposit or funding costs rise faster than asset repricing;
- market/trading earnings normalize below assumptions;
- operational, legal or control risk raises required return;
- buybacks occur at an uneconomic valuation or weaken resilience;
- reported ROTCE remains high but TBVPS growth, reserve quality or per-share capital deteriorates.

### 3.7 Leading indicators

Deposit mix and cost; card payment rate and delinquency; commercial demand; investment-banking pipeline; trading/client activity; asset-management flows; expense guidance; RWA/CET1 and stress constraints; forward EPS/TBVPS revisions.

### 3.8 Source limitations

Consolidated metrics do not reveal the marginal return or capital intensity of every business. Current valuation and consensus must be supplied later by their accepted owners.

### 18.2 Capital and credit change the meaning of growth

- Servicing UPB can be valuable while creating advance, prepayment and operational exposures.

- Buybacks can raise EPS while harming TBVPS or resilience.
- Acquisition can raise every balance without producing organic per-share growth.

```text
reported operating change

→ per-share economic consequence

```
