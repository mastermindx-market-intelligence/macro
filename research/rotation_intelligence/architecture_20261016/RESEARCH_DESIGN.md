# Scientific design: selection, migration increment and earlier action

Date: 2026-10-16. **Candidate design for the existing scientific owner. This is not canonical PREREG V3 and grants no P1/P2/P3 outcome-reading authority.** The existing `LEADERSHIP-ROTATION-INTELLIGENCE` workstream retains registration, size/power and first-read gates. Earlier failed or underpowered studies remain unchanged.

## 1. Separate five claims

| Claim | Appropriate evidence | What does not establish it |
|---|---|---|
| Measurement is correct | Source-clock, arithmetic, population, return-basis and consumer tests | A profitable-looking chart |
| A future outcome is predictable | Frozen target/features and held-out predictive evaluation | Same-day correlation or an explanatory narrative |
| Donor information adds value | Matched receiver-only versus receiver-plus-donor comparison | A paired story compared with a weaker receiver baseline |
| Earlier action is economically better | Executable origin, common terminal date, waiting return and costs | A detector that mechanically fires a fixed number of days earlier |
| A portfolio/stock expression adds value | Mandate-aware net policy outcomes and group-controlled stock comparison | A high-beta stock rising more than its sector |

The target question is economic value with controlled uncertainty. Do not retune until a preferred claim passes; record which claims fail and retain useful measurement even when predictive authority is withheld.

## 2. Information set and valid labels

At origin t, predictors and eligibility must be measurable using the admitted information available by t. A training example's outcome must also have matured and become available by that fold's training cutoff. A forecast target may legitimately use future returns: **future labels are normal in forecasting; future predictors, immature training labels and hindsight-selected origins are the leakage problems.**

Distinguish the three dates for every row: forecast origin t; next executable entry e(t); and terminal u(t,h). Define them through the existing calendar/quote owner. No fill at a future minimum, retrospectively chosen pivot, or an unobservable close. Same-close entry is not the default merely because an indicator uses that close.

A causal signal need not show monotonically increasing or decreasing performance across horizons. A 20-session economic effect can exist without a next-day effect. Horizon sensitivity is informative, but an invented monotonicity requirement can incorrectly reject real mechanisms.

## 3. Primary population and baselines

### Initial population

Use the broad US sector ETF universe as the first investable research population, with real instrument inception and source availability. Never create pre-inception XLRE/XLC observations to force an eleven-sector panel. Fix rules for the eligible universe at each date and disclose coverage. Industries and overlapping themes are separate transfer studies with dated constituents and weights; they do not inflate the effective sample of the sector study.

The initial policy study is long-only and unlevered. Bill exposure is a feasible alternative. Shorts, borrow, option overlays and account-specific position sizing are excluded until independently scoped. Macro/China/HK transfer requires its own clocks, universes and qualification, not a pooled assertion that the US result generalizes.

### Comparator catalogue to freeze before confirmatory use

| Comparator | Proposed definition | Why it is needed |
|---|---|---|
| B0 | Broad equity benchmark and a separately specified bill investment | Distinguish relative selection from absolute opportunity |
| B1 | Equal-weight eligible sector instruments with a frozen rebalance schedule | Test whether modelling adds value over diversification |
| B2 | Transparent trailing-relative-momentum selection; candidate 63-session lookback, top three, monthly rebalance | A reproducible simple selector that cannot be weakened after observing the challenger |
| B3 | A frozen absolute-trend/bill rule using the same eligible instruments and execution convention | Compare against simple de-risking, not just fully invested equities |
| B4 | Receiver-only regularized model with the same receiver/macroeconomic inputs, folds and complexity budget as the paired model | Isolate the incremental contribution of donor evidence |

B2/B3 parameters above are **candidate defaults**, not a claim of optimal settings or an override of existing preregistration. Canonical registration must resolve all method choices and immutable code/data references before first confirmatory use. Tuning alternatives are counted, not hidden in a footnote.

## 4. Three candidate confirmatory families

The following are a proposal for the existing registration owner. They do not alter the native H1/H2/H3 definitions or significance budgets. Any new family allocation and relation to those hypotheses must be explicit in the dated registration.

### F1 — Receiver selection at an actionable horizon

**Question:** does the proposed receiver-selection method improve future group opportunity beyond frozen simple and receiver-only baselines?

Candidate primary horizon: 20 trading sessions; 5 and 60 sessions are secondary unless separately registered. The primary statistic should be the paired net policy/opportunity difference across the same origins and eligible populations, not whichever rank or hit metric looks best. A rank statistic can be primary for a purely predictive-ranking claim, but is not universally mandatory for an economic claim.

For a fixed holding convention, define a group excess-return label relative to the explicit benchmark. For the policy comparison, compute actual wealth paths under the stated rebalances and costs. Do not call the average of overlapping horizon returns a continuous portfolio CAGR.

Secondary diagnostics: cross-sectional rank association, top-set excess, probability calibration, downside, turnover, cost sensitivity, and supported versus unsupported population. Report rank uncertainty and no-selection frequency. Improvement in rank correlation without economic benefit does not qualify an allocation policy.

**Falsifier:** the same method with new features removed performs equivalently or better under matched origins and tuning. A policy that wins only before realistic costs or only by changing the eligible population fails the intended claim.

### F2 — Incremental information in the donor side

**Question:** conditional on the same receiver information and shared context, does adding donor evidence improve forecasting or decisions?

The reduced and full models use identical receiver features, macro context, training cutoffs, eligibility, cost/fill assumptions and evaluation dates. Match model flexibility/tuning resources or report the complexity difference. The donor model cannot win by quietly importing an improved receiver signal or an extra future-known variable.

Evaluate paired differences at the origin/population level. Do not count each donor–receiver combination as an independent observation when receivers or market shocks are shared. Cross-validation and bootstrap units preserve the common date and relevant episode dependence.

Same-day return differencing does not remove common-market exposure when betas differ. Fit nuisance exposures using only admitted training data, retain uncertainty and compare with a simple beta-only residual control. Residual predictability is not causal proof that dollars traveled from donor to receiver.

**Null and challenge cases:** no donor information conditional on receiver/context; shared factors with unequal betas; receiver-only drift; donor-only drift; overlapping members; stale/missing donor data; shuffled residual donor blocks preserving the declared conditioning structure. Arbitrary shuffling that destroys temporal dependence is not a valid null by default. The null-generation mechanism itself needs native size qualification.

**Falsifier:** incremental value disappears under matched receiver features, common-factor controls or chronological validation. This invalidates the donor-increment claim, not the usefulness of identifying receivers alone.

### F3 — Acting on formation versus waiting for confirmation

**Question:** does a frozen causal early-action policy beat a frozen delayed/confirmed policy and no action, after the cost of false starts?

Eligibility is defined at origin from past information. Retain **all eligible origins**, including candidates that never confirm, failed transitions, missing subsequent confirmation and rejected observations. Scoring only eventual confirmed winners conditions on the future and answers a different question.

Both policies start from the same initial capital at t and end on the **same terminal date u(t,h)**. The waiting policy earns the specified bill return before its actual entry. A candidate never confirmed follows its predeclared no-entry/cash rule; it must not disappear from the sample. Costs are charged once for every actual entry, exit and rebalance. No borrow is charged to a long-only policy; no fictitious round trip is charged in addition to the trades already counted.

A common end date makes the comparison fair; it does not guarantee the early policy wins. Early entry can suffer adverse excursion before a cheaper delayed entry. Evaluate the distribution of the paired wealth difference, false-start loss, drawdown and opportunities missed by waiting.

A lead-time statistic can be descriptive when the confirmation rule and matching are fixed in advance. It is not sufficient evidence of economic value. If detector B equals detector A delayed by five sessions, observed lead is five by construction even under a no-edge world.

**Falsifier:** the early policy's benefit is mechanical from incomparable end dates, conditioning on future confirmation, omitted waiting returns or missing trade costs. Economic failure after correcting those issues remains a valid negative result.

## 5. Additional information: ablation before complexity

Test source families in a predeclared order, with their actual admitted use: receiver price/volatility; breadth and concentration; continuous macro/real-rate context; genuine fund activity; earnings-expectation revisions; industry supply/capital-cycle evidence. Multiple correlated transforms from one underlying price series are not independent confirmations.

Separate **data quality contribution** from **predictive contribution**. A better flow collector may fix measurement and still add no forecast skill. A revisions feature must distinguish actual dated analyst consensus from management guidance. A supply-cycle feature must be evaluated against price/revision baselines rather than just narrated after the move.

Candidate interactions are economically constrained, for example financing sensitivity × real-rate change or input-cost exposure × commodity-price shock. Pool/shrink estimates rather than fitting every combination of sector, quad, breadth state and rate state independently. The number of specifications is registered and included in multiplicity accounting.

## 6. Development, calibration and confirmation are different samples

Much of the available historical period has already been explored by prior Mastermind studies and public research. **A newly named split is not automatically an untouched holdout.** Record prior exposure and analysis decisions. Treat known/explored data as development unless the existing scientific owner establishes an independent unused sample.

Use chronological development folds with train-contained preprocessing, beta estimation, feature selection and tuning. Purge training observations whose label intervals overlap the held-out evaluation interval. Apply an embargo where the actual geometry/serial dependence requires it; a single fixed gap is not a universal cure. Forward-only folds do not become valid if their training labels have not matured by the training cutoff.

Calibration uses only matured labels and maintains its own declared role. For a 20-session target, feedback arrives later than the next forecast; an online algorithm assuming instant labels cannot be transplanted unchanged. Prospective frozen forecasts are stored through the existing owner with their original issue time; later corrections never rewrite the prediction that was actually issued.

A final confirmatory result requires the native registered protocol and eligible untouched/prospective evidence. This package does not open that evidence, set look counts or infer admission from a copied artifact.

## 7. Dependence, multiplicity and power

Eleven sector observations on one day are not eleven independent market experiments. Shared risk, overlapping holdings and overlapping forward windows can severely reduce information. Retain the full cross-section within time blocks; use paired resampling of candidate and baseline outcomes. Where episodes overlap, the unit must reflect that dependence rather than multiply pair counts.

A small sample of macro cycles cannot support precise probabilities for every regime cell. Use partial pooling or withhold claims. No universal minimum detectable basis-point effect follows from a generic effective-N formula; it depends on the declared generating process, costs, signal variance and actual dependence.

Before real-data confirmation, the native test must demonstrate false-positive control and power at economically meaningful margins. Required synthetic worlds include common factors, unequal betas, serial correlation, structural changes, delayed releases, changing/missing constituents, selection, confirmation never occurring and overlapping horizons. The actual statistic, data-dependent selection and nuisance fitting must be run—not merely compared with an oracle formula.

This supplement's 20,000-replication Gaussian witness samples the exact distribution of a panel mean under *known* covariance. Its purpose is to expose an invalid IID approximation. It does **not** establish that a practical estimated-covariance test or the native LR statistics are sized/powered. With 11 sectors and 60 dates, cross-sector correlation 0.6 inflates the mean variance sevenfold; adding AR(1)=0.5 inflates it about 20.53-fold. The exact executed results are in [synthetic_design_results.json](synthetic_design_results.json).

The canonical registration should allocate family-wise error across the actual confirmatory claims, horizons and model search. Holm-style family control is a candidate, not an override of existing native thresholds. Exploratory false-discovery control and confirmatory family-wise control answer different questions. Report all registered outcomes, including unsupported, inconclusive and negative results.

## 8. Calibration after selecting the winner

A model may be marginally calibrated across all sectors and poorly calibrated for the sectors selected as best. Evaluate the selected population, the full eligible population, rank uncertainty and no-selection cases separately. Preserve the selection policy in the calibration identity.

Adaptive conformal and selection-aware calibration are research candidates, not automatic guarantees. The conditions in the source papers—exchangeability/symmetry or particular long-run adaptive guarantees—must be reconciled with serial sector data, overlapping labels and delayed feedback. Wide or infinite intervals may be honest but economically unhelpful; coverage alone is not allocation skill. See [LITERATURE](LITERATURE.md).

## 9. Qualification rubric

A positive predictive/economic claim requires all of: valid as-known inputs; executable target/accounting; native statistical size and power qualification; frozen protocol and comparable baseline; corrected confirmatory evidence; robustness within declared support; and prospective normal-path evidence at the level required by the owner.

For candidate margins, publish a sensitivity grid before outcome access rather than claim an unmeasured universal precision target. Economic thresholds must be tied to the mandate and empirically supported cost uncertainty before final registration. A smaller effect discovered after the fact cannot silently replace a failed registered margin.

Possible outcomes are distinct: measurement correct; prediction inconclusive; prediction failed; predictor qualified for a limited population/horizon; policy not worthwhile after costs; or policy eligible for a separately authorized implementation. No single PASS badge replaces those distinctions.
