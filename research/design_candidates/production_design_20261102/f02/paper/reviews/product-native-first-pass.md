## Observed defects and reference regressions

### NPRC-001 — Numeric alignment is declared but not realized

**Severity:** major. **Type:** observed rendering/product regression. **Tasks:** T01, T06, T07.

**Before evidence:** `evidence/company-before.png`, lower statement table, has numeric amounts and their subordinate changes aligned to the ends of the year lanes. Different magnitudes such as revenue `26.91B` and cost of goods sold `9.44B` can be scanned vertically.

**After evidence:** `paper/screenshots/company-dark.jpg` and `company-light.jpg`, the FY2022–FY2026 table, visibly start `12.00`, `6.00`, `2.00`, and `16.7%` at the left of each numeric lane. Their decimal points do not form a common vertical edge. `paper/screenshots/options-dark.jpg` and `options-light.jpg` show the same issue in Strike and Contracts: `120.00` versus `85.00`, and `1,500` versus `940`, begin at the same left boundary. This matters more than whether the written style calls the column right-aligned.

Corroborating native export evidence: `paper/exports/company-light.jsx`, cells `5MI-0` (`12.00`) and `5M0-0` (`6.00`); `paper/exports/options-light.jsx`, cells `6GS-0` (`120.00`), `6G3-0` (`85.00`), `6GM-0` (`1,500`), and `6B2-0` (`940`). The text children have `width: 'max-content'` alongside `textAlign: 'right'`; the rendered screenshots show that this has not moved the child to the numeric lane's trailing edge. The core Metrics board does use visibly aligned numeric lanes, so consumer integration has not preserved that pattern.

**User consequence:** comparison by magnitude requires extra eye movement, and the current Company table is preferable for scanning annual rows. The proposed grammar itself says to right-align numeric values (`paper/screenshots/grammar.jpg`, Table section; export node `4NL-0`).

**Required closure:** correct the actual rendered alignment in both themes, including unequal digit counts, negative values, percentages, currency/share units, and missing values. Supply recaptured consumer rows. A declaration of `textAlign` alone does not close this finding.

### NPRC-003 — Participation fixtures do not reconcile with the count definition and displayed precision

**Severity:** major. **Type:** observed data/semantic consistency problem in illustrative fixtures. **Tasks:** T01, T02, T04, T12.

**Definition evidence:** `paper/screenshots/controls.jpg`, “How participation is calculated,” defines a numerator of covered securities above their 50-day average and a denominator of covered securities with both valid observations. The same board presents `74.20%` with `178 / 184 covered`. Native nodes are `4L2-0` (definition), `4KR-0` (percentage), and `4KS-0` (coverage). `metrics.jpg` and `metrics-light.jpg` repeat the value and count in the Information technology row and evidence block; light nodes include `4IJ-0` and `4IF-0`.

Under the displayed count interpretation and ordinary rounding, these examples do not reproduce:

| Visible fixture | Count denominator implied by the coverage/definition | Reproducible nearby count result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Information technology `74.20%` | 178 covered | 132 / 178 = 74.157…%, which rounds to **74.16%** at two decimals; 133 / 178 rounds to 74.72%. |
| Macro Technology `72.4%` | 42 / 42 | 30 / 42 rounds to **71.4%**; 31 / 42 rounds to **73.8%**. |
| Macro Consumer discretionary `63.5%` | 47 / 47 | 30 / 47 rounds to **63.8%**; 29 / 47 rounds to **61.7%**. |
| Macro Utilities `58.3%` | 28 / 28 | 16 / 28 rounds to **57.1%**; 17 / 28 rounds to **60.7%**. |

Macro evidence is in both theme screenshots, with light export nodes `53P-0` / `53L-0` (Technology), `52H-0` / `52D-0` (Consumer discretionary), and `51V-0` / `51R-0` (Utilities). A bounded integer check also found no numerator/denominator pair with denominator at most 178 that rounds to 74.20% at two decimals, nor one with denominator at most 47 that rounds to 63.5% at one decimal. Thus merely assuming that the valid subset is smaller does not resolve those two examples.

**Qualification:** this finding concerns the packet's displayed calculation contract and fixture precision, not the truth of a real market observation. A weighted measure, a different denominator, or pre-rounding to fewer decimals could change the interpretation, but none is disclosed here. For example, first rounding 132 / 178 to one decimal and then printing `74.20%` would explain the string while implying greater precision than the underlying rounded result. The reference must make that choice explicit. I did not aggregate the independent Macro universe sample from sector rows; the packet explicitly separates those cohorts.

**User consequence:** the exact-evidence pattern currently cannot explain how some of its own headline values follow from its count semantics. This weakens a core promise of the shared kit even when the examples are illustrative.

**Required closure:** use coherent numerators, valid denominators, and rounding rules across metric, row, and inspector, or visibly disclose a distinct weighted/quantized measure. Show the actual numerator and calculation once so a reader can reproduce the result. Do not resolve the issue only with a “fixture” label.
