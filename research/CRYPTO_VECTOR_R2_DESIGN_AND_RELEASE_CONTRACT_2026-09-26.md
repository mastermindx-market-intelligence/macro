## Direction: sophisticated charts, simple questions

The old R1 card-wall omitted much of the analytical depth already described in our earlier research. R2 restores charts as the core research instrument.

Use a quiet shared shell; one short plain-language read; one dominant analytical view; a compact interpretation column; progressive detail through a source/snapshot inspector. Thin precise chart strokes, low-contrast grids, aligned time axes, explicit units and benchmarks, direct series labels, restrained regime colors, and generous spacing matter more than ornamental glass or extra tiles. Light and dark are separate contrast compositions. Mobile must reorganize the task and preserve usable labels/targets.

Bitcoin: Overview → Cycle & risk → Strategy → On-chain → Derivatives.
Crypto: Market → Assets → Capital & leverage → Events.
Cross-links carry asset, selected snapshot and return context. Preserve useful existing Cycle Lab, strategy comparison, derivatives/on-chain research and crypto-equity dossiers rather than flattening the product into an introductory brief.

Glance answers what is happening and what the model says. Context explains what supports it, what could invalidate it and whether its evidence is available. Study depth retains full methods, original observations, model version, counterevidence and evaluation. Provenance must support—not replace—the user workflow.

### 09 — inspect a past decision without hindsight

The user selects an exact date and sees one recorded target, the preceding valid target, the explicit comparison dates and the source record. The example changes from 100% to 60% Bitcoin on 12 September; 40% cash is the complement, not a personal account balance. Price, risk and allocation align to the same selected date. Previous/next date controls and Back to latest keep historical context unmistakable.

The example inspector deliberately withholds a causal explanation when the decision record does not contain one. A coincident rise in risk cannot itself prove why allocation changed. The existing decision owner must supply the rule version, evaluated inputs and resulting action; explanation text may summarize that record but may not reconstruct authority from a chart or LLM narrative.

A dated record is not proof that its inputs were available on that day. The screen says that publication timing is unverified. Only an accepted point-in-time owner receipt may enable an as-available-then claim. A revised-input view must retain the original record and label the revision; it must not replace historical decisions or change the displayed cutoff silently. The chart stops at the selected date in this view. Absence of a previous valid decision must suppress the change amount and use the existing first-target semantics, never assume an earlier zero.

### 10 — compare a saved review with a later observation

The hero is deliberately capable of saying "Same allocation. Different evidence." The worked example compares 12 and 26 September, preserves both dated records at 60% Bitcoin, and shows risk 61 to 43 as an 18-point change, not a loss-probability change. Synthetic daily Bitcoin prices of 87,600 and 94,400 produce +7.8% rounded price return; this is not strategy or account performance. The authored chart coordinates and axis labels were checked for those endpoints.

Each comparison field requires matching entity, denomination, measurement definition, observation grain, model/measurement version and qualified endpoints. Model changes, source revisions, new observations and newly available coverage are different changes. A new funding observation cannot be subtracted from a missing prior value. A corrected on-chain observation gets its own revision disclosure and does not silently restate the saved review. Disallow a headline delta when its necessary field comparison is unavailable; retain unrelated qualified fields.

Saving this comparison creates a new review through the existing snapshot/case owner; it never overwrites the original baseline. The production implementation must bind both snapshot revisions, dates, source references, qualified comparison basis and notes to the saved review. Reopening the review must reproduce its original basis even after a newer market publication. The UI must not create a second review store or use a browser-only success flag as evidence of server persistence.

### 11 — saved review and optional alert are separate effects

The 390-pixel Chinese design shows a successful save as an explicit synthetic state, then says the alert is not yet enabled. Confirmation describes the model-allocation change, comparison baseline, channel and start behavior. Its illustrative trigger is an absolute change of at least 10 percentage points from the previous valid published target; 60% to 70% is an example, not a live instruction. Price and risk-only changes do not satisfy that model-allocation trigger. This threshold is a design example pending validation against the existing Alert Center capabilities, not a new alert engine.

Before implementation, resolve the existing subscription schema, supported triggers, entitlement and channel. Unsupported terms are unavailable with a reason; never simulate support. Activation requires an explicit user confirmation of the exact terms and an owner receipt whose readback agrees on the subscription and active state. A save receipt is not an activation receipt; an active subscription is not proof of notification delivery. The example does not backfill historical alerts. Model-version changes and noncomparable targets must follow existing owner policy rather than generating a false market-change notification.

For both actions, preserve drafts and the original request identity across double taps, navigation and reload. Display saving/checking/confirmed failure/confirmed success distinctly. An ambiguous response must reconcile the original request before another modifying submission. Explicitly confirmed failure may offer a retry governed by the existing owner. Canceling the optional alert leaves the saved review intact. Opening a record or source must not perform an implicit save or activation.
