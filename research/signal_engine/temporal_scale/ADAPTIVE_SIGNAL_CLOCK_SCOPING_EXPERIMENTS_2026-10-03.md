## Leakage, dependence and stability controls

Every clock estimate must have instrument and signal-family identity, source/session/rights/adjustment vintage, measurement and publication times, decision cutoff, kernel-state reference, estimator version, lower/central/upper band or modes, uncertainty, drivers, contrary evidence, falsifiers, abstention reason and a separate evaluation-target reference.

Forbid: full-sample spectral periods, future-confirmed pivots, retrospectively smoothed regime labels, revised macro data used before publication, future session corrections, outcome-selected examples, post-reveal tuning, per-name argmax over grains or kernels, and variable evaluation horizons selected from observed returns.

Treat overlapping events as dependent: effective independent block/episode N is not nominal signal count, and TrialLedger's multiple-testing effective_n is not the same as statistical-power N. Use time-block bootstrap/permutation, purge/embargo or equivalent, era splits, preregistered familywise decisions, practical margins and **joint power** for the complete acceptance rule.

Study hysteresis, persistence, update cadence, compression/expansion transitions, shock overrides, recovery and the cost of a wrong clock. If turnover is too high, return UNSTABLE rather than changing a user's signal horizon every bar.

## Falsifiers and failure register

Kill or narrow adaptation if no stable ex-ante scale exists; measurement noise dominates; instrument-disjoint transfer fails; gains vanish after A/K matching or PIT corrections; B4/B5 equal or beat it with lower instability/cost; apparent gains are volatility targeting, signal-frequency or activity artifacts; clock turnover is unusable; per-name historical outcome tuning is necessary; risk/localization trade-offs violate preregistered limits; rights/corrections cannot be qualified; or consumer value fails to justify complexity.

Risk classes: hindsight leakage, overlapping effective N, multiple testing, microstructure noise, session/DST/overnight grammar, vendor/roll identity, future-resolved structural features, scale thrashing, nonstationarity, entitlement gaps, interpretation as directional certainty, and unauthorized downstream gate/rank/size effects.
