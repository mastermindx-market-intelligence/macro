## 1. The investment question this pass resolves

The principal synthesis is that **the owner of a scarce mineral occurrence, the operator able to recover it, the customer entitled to its output, and the supplier improving that operation are different economic roles**. The proposed Mining product must explain the connection between them without assigning every participant the same commodity exposure.

For an accessory metal, a higher price does not specify the response of its host operation, the available recovery circuit, or the terms under which someone gets paid. For a mining supplier, a stronger commodity market does not identify whether revenue comes from new equipment, replacement parts, service work, software, acquired businesses, or a price adjustment. The proposed analysis therefore follows two linked questions: which activity creates incremental usable output, and how is the resulting value divided after capital and operating costs?

This pass contributes eleven focused dossiers and sixteen primary-source records. It also adds a host-dependency research map, five explicitly hypothetical mechanisms, four investor workflows, and twenty-eight prospective requirements. It is not a global supply forecast, a complete company census, a stock ranking, or a validated return model. Only limited factual observations are taken from the sources; interpretations and future product requirements are our proposals.

The separate coverage/frontier document reconciles these findings with canonical Passes 01–05. It deliberately excludes the parallel local Pass 05 identified by the existing DO_NOT_PUBLISH_DUPLICATE ruling. No earlier report or historical check receipt is changed or described as newly rerun.

## 2. Host relationships: a starting point, not a company exposure weight

USGS's by-product life-cycle research identifies the importance of recovery through host-commodity systems and uncertainty about where accessory elements partition into products or waste. Its 2018 project description is methodological context, not a measured 2026 recovery rate for an issuer. [S01]

The USGS host-commodity table published in November 2025 provides the following selected research relationships. They are broad associations, not a claim that every deposit or facility produces the accessory metal, or that all supply follows one route. The source itself distinguishes by-products and co-products. [S02]

| Material to investigate | Selected host/feed association from the source | Proposed economic question |
|---|---|---|
| Gallium | Bauxite; zinc | Which processing stage separates a saleable product, and who owns that product? |
| Germanium | Zinc; coal fly ash | Does the specific feed contain recoverable material under an accepted process and contract? |
| Indium | Zinc | Is output constrained by host throughput, recovery, or commercial allocation? |
| Rhenium | Molybdenum; copper | Which source and processing route actually contribute to attributable sales? |
| Tellurium | Copper and other base/precious-metal hosts | Which producer/refiner has an economic recovery route rather than only geological presence? |
| Cobalt | Nickel; copper | How does the host operation's plan influence supply independently of cobalt demand? |
| Hafnium | Zirconium | Which separation/product specification and customer qualification define the market? |
| Vanadium | Relevant steel slag; spent catalysts | Is the opportunity primary extraction, recovery from an industrial stream, or recycling? |

The map helps formulate questions; it cannot answer materiality. A company is not added to an investable basket merely because it mines one of the listed host commodities. The proof needed is asset- and product-specific: a supported material path, recoverable or contracted quantity, and the issuer's economic entitlement. Unknown materiality remains unknown rather than an invented zero or an arbitrary weight.

A useful distinction is between **host-dependent production**, **dedicated production**, **recovery from finite historical stock**, and **recovery from ongoing external feed**. These are proposed research classifications. One issuer may span several, and the classifications can change as a process becomes economically important. Do not permanently call an output a negligible by-product when its contribution becomes material, or assume a dedicated mine can ignore its own co-products and capital requirements.

## 3. Eleven source-grounded dossiers

### SE-C01 — Trail: maximizing host-metal tonnes is not necessarily the economic objective

**Evidence.** Teck's October 2025 review prioritizes residue processing rather than maximum refined-zinc output at Trail, reducing concentrate purchases in a low-treatment-charge environment. Its 2026 refined-zinc guidance changed from 260–300 kt to 190–230 kt on that strategy. [S03] A July 2026 agreement separately describes a conditional investment framework that could double germanium/antimony capacity and add gallium capability; definitive documentation and approvals remain conditions. [S04]

**Interpretation.** The operational strategy and the potential investment address different decisions. A lower host-metal output plan may reflect a more attractive feed mix rather than a failure to use available equipment. A potential recovery expansion is not current additional supply. Neither conclusion provides a plant-level valuation by itself.

**Proposed research output.** Present the feed alternatives, the costs or proceeds associated with them, and the scarce capacity they use. Explain why a throughput-maximizing interpretation could conflict with a contribution-maximizing plan. Keep the possible expansion in a separate, dated investment case until actual funding, construction, recoveries, and sales are supported. The missing quantity is often not ore in the ground but a profitable, available processing route.

**Falsifier and remaining gap.** Better feed economics could be offset by limited residue availability, recovery costs, capital needs, or commercial rights. This pass does not establish a complete feed inventory, investment close, current plant cash-flow reconciliation, or incremental metal output.

### SE-C02 — Kipushi: a metal can be in the concentrate without being a demonstrated revenue stream

**Evidence.** Ivanhoe's July 29 release reports Q2 zinc in concentrate of 70,177 tonnes versus 65,044 in Q1; payable zinc sold declined from 54,940 to 43,424 tonnes. The company attributes unsold accumulation to logistics. It separately says it is examining how to recover value from gallium and germanium in the concentrate. Its zinc recovery figure is explicitly a zinc measure. [S05]

**Interpretation.** The calculated sequential changes are +7.89% for zinc output and −20.96% for payable sales. Those are different observations, not an unexplained single growth rate. Nothing in the cited accessory-metal statement establishes current payable gallium/germanium quantities or margins. Zinc recovery cannot be applied to those elements.

**Proposed research output.** A company can have supported operating zinc exposure and a separately labeled accessory-metal opportunity. The dossier should identify what converts the opportunity into an economic assertion: a relevant process result, product specification, recovery or payability term, and evidence of ownership or commercial entitlement. It should not estimate that value by multiplying total concentrate mass by a metal price.

**Falsifier and remaining gap.** A change in shipping performance might improve cash without any new recovery process; a successful accessory-metal recovery process might improve future economics without increasing zinc output. Actual accessory assays, partitioning, recovery costs and contract payability remain unverified here. The two mechanisms must not be merged.

### SE-C03 — Freeport: primary and by-product molybdenum do not share one operating equation

**Evidence.** Freeport's Q2 filing separates its wholly owned Climax and Henderson molybdenum mines from by-product molybdenum at copper operations. Primary-mine output was 8 million pounds versus 9 million a year earlier; primary unit net cash cost was US$19.20/lb versus US$14.20. The filing explains that primary-mine segment sales use an internal sales company, whereas consolidated realization reflects external contract terms. [S06]

**Interpretation.** On those rounded observations, output fell 11.11% while unit cost rose 35.21%. That does not establish the response of the group's by-product output or the full sensitivity of consolidated earnings. A calculation combining mine-level tonnes, group realized prices and an internal segment margin would mix accounting scopes.

**Proposed research output.** Preserve the primary route and the host-linked route as separate contributions under one issuer. Show consolidation and intercompany boundaries before aggregation. A customer asking about molybdenum leverage should see what is produced deliberately at the molybdenum mines, what depends on a copper mine plan, and what portion of the financial result remains unallocated.

**Falsifier and remaining gap.** A favorable price change can coexist with declining output or higher input costs. Product mix, contracting, conversion costs and the underlying production plan may dominate a simple spot-price sensitivity. This pass is a focused extension of the earlier Freeport work, not a repeated copper dossier or a complete molybdenum business valuation.

### SE-C04 — Almonty: recognize the September operating milestone without mistaking accounting gains for mine cash

**Evidence.** The Q2 release reports C$181.8 million of net income including C$173.1 million of net noncash instrument-revaluation gains; adjusted EBITDA was C$17.6 million. The three disclosed revaluation components are +204.4, −30.7 and −0.6 million. At that reporting point Sangdong remained in commissioning/ramp-up. [S07] A September 21 update reports certificates issued September 17 authorizing commercial operation and sales from the processing/crushing facilities. [S08]

**Interpretation.** The later disclosure is a meaningful positive state change. Continuing to describe the plant solely by its earlier commissioning status would miss it. Conversely, permission to operate and sell does not demonstrate sustained nameplate output, realized customer payments, or the margin on every future tonne.

**Proposed research output.** Retain separate event-effective, issuer-publication and observation dates. Show the newly supported capability and the next operating evidence needed. The revaluation components sum to 173.1, but subtracting them from net income does not create a fully normalized earnings or cash-flow measure; other reconciliation items remain. Do not annualize a large instrument valuation gain as mine cash generation.

**Falsifier and remaining gap.** The milestone can narrow one uncertainty while ramp reliability, shipment evidence, contracted product specifications, reinvestment and financing claims remain material. This is an issuer-reported certificate event, not an independently retrieved regulator certificate or proof of commercial deliveries at forecast rates. The dossier should say what improved without erasing what has not yet been established.

### SE-C05 — Los Santos: a route to market can help restart economics, but the tailings stock is finite

**Evidence.** Almonty's September 17 announcement describes a multi-year take-or-pay agreement with Sandvik subsidiary WBH for a minimum of approximately 1,720 tonnes of contained WO3 from existing Los Santos tailings. It includes a conditional US$3 million upfront payment for offtake rights and anticipates restarting processing. WBH combines its own mine feed, purchased concentrate and secondary material in its broader business. [S09]

**Interpretation.** The agreement connects recoverable stock, a processor/customer, and financing. It does not turn the contracted lifetime quantity into annual output, the conditional payment into received operating cash, or an expected restart into a completed operating record. The material has already been mined, but recovering it still consumes capital, operating inputs and processing capacity.

**Proposed research output.** Model the finite feed source, expected recovery path, product basis, contract period, funding conditions and remaining stock separately. The same transaction gives one party a potential sales channel and the other a prospective input supply. Those are distinct economic claims on one material flow, not two additions to global supply.

**Falsifier and remaining gap.** Recoveries, restart costs, full contract schedules, net pricing and payment realization are not established by the headline quantity. The process could extend useful supply while eventually exhausting the stock. Do not infer perpetual terminal growth from a finite tailings project, or copy promoter claims of being the first or only solution without separate evidence.

### SE-C06 — Sibanye-Stillwater: a recycled-ounce headline is not an element-specific growth rate

**Evidence.** For H1 2026, the disclosed recycled/sold precious-metal total rises from 1,152,079 to 2,788,340 ounces. Its components are gold 95,188, five-metal PGMs 194,926 and silver 2,498,226 ounces; the prior PGM component was 155,375. Copper is reported separately in pounds. The report identifies contributions from an acquired operation as well as existing-site growth. [S10]

**Interpretation.** Total precious-metal ounces increase 142.03%, while the PGM component increases 25.46%; silver represents 89.60% of the current physical-ounce total. These calculated shares do not measure revenue, value, profit or an equal-metal economic exposure. A large low-value-unit component can dominate a physical sum.

**Proposed research output.** Preserve each metal, the composite definition, operating site, acquisition perimeter and period. Keep an issuer's reported physical sum available, but do not use it as a PGM supply or profitability signal. A value-equivalent conversion requires identified prices and a valuation date; an accounting-sales comparison requires the appropriate realization and rights rather than a spot-price shortcut.

**Falsifier and remaining gap.** Increasing recycled volume can be driven by acquisition, feed composition, market prices that induce collection, or actual processing gains. These are candidate explanations to test, not a causal attribution established by the total. Neither complete feed ownership nor metal-specific margins are reconciled in this pass.

### SE-C07 — Umicore: purchased-metal value and contractual accruals can obscure industrial activity

**Evidence.** Umicore distinguishes H1 revenues excluding purchased metals of approximately EUR1.9 billion from turnover of EUR13.9 billion. It reports a EUR132 million working-capital increase associated with activity and metal prices. Its battery-cathode discussion attributes part of improved performance to take-or-pay accruals, with invoices calculated after year-end from actual versus committed annual volume. [S11]

**Interpretation.** Different reporting denominators answer different questions. Gross turnover is not the same measure as the retained industrial revenue basis. Likewise, compensation recognized under a contract is not necessarily physical shipments or cash already collected. The arithmetic can be correct while the implied operating story is wrong.

**Proposed research output.** Label the purchased-metal exclusion, reporting definition, and contract status. Separate product delivery, accrued compensation, invoicing, payment and any later adjustment. A processor may have more money tied up in material as prices rise without a proportionate gain in industrial contribution. That is a funding question, not automatically a defect in the business.

**Falsifier and remaining gap.** Stronger contract protection may improve the earnings floor but leave collection timing and counterparty conditions relevant. More turnover might result from metal prices rather than productive activity. The actual accrual-to-invoice-to-cash bridge, metal financing arrangements and normalized economics are not fully available here. The proposed answer should name the missing bridge rather than substitute one revenue label for another.

### SE-C08 — Weir: acquired growth and aftermarket revenue are not proof of organic subscription growth

**Evidence.** In Weir's H1 issuer RNS, ESCO aftermarket orders are GBP360 million versus GBP329 million at constant currency, with reported rounded growth of 9%; organic growth is 1%. Its bridge identifies acquisition effects separately. Group aftermarket revenue is GBP1,047 million of GBP1,269 million. Software businesses are discussed using their own recurring-revenue measures. [S12]

**Interpretation.** The rounded table gives a calculated 9.42% constant-currency change and an 82.51% aftermarket share of group revenue. Neither means organic mining activity grew by that amount or that 82.51% of revenue is a software subscription. Rounding accounts for the small table-versus-headline difference; no issuer error is alleged.

**Proposed research output.** Distinguish original equipment, replacement parts, consumables, service, upgrades and recurring software, preserving disclosed aggregation rather than inventing unavailable splits. Retain organic, acquisition, currency and portfolio-restatement bridges. The question is whether growth reflects more customer activity, market share, price/mix, newly acquired revenue, or a combination.

**Falsifier and remaining gap.** A large installed base can be attractive, but its earnings depend on utilization, wear, maintenance practices, capture of third-party replacement business and customer contracts. A high aftermarket percentage by itself establishes neither guaranteed renewal nor software-like unit economics. The research does not estimate a full subscription valuation or customer-retention series from this disclosure.

### SE-C09 — Metso: preserve restated comparators and distinguish orders from utilization

**Evidence.** Metso's Q2 2026 sales of EUR1,334 million compare with re-presented Q2 2025 sales of EUR1,257 million; the release explains the business reclassification. Orders are EUR1,462 million. Operating cash before financial items and taxes rises from 147 to 206 million in Q2, but falls from 343 to 285 million over H1. [S13] The original 2025 release reported Q2 sales of EUR1,213 million. [S14]

**Interpretation.** Comparable sales growth is 6.13%; the incompatible older denominator would suggest 9.98%. The order-to-sales ratio is approximately 1.10, not utilization or a guaranteed revenue-growth rate. The quarter's 40.14% operating-cash increase and the half-year's 16.91% decline are simultaneously possible. Their periods differ.

**Proposed research output.** Preserve both original and re-presented historical observations, with the purpose of each clear. Use the matched business perimeter for a current comparison while retaining the earlier publication as historical evidence. Display booking, fulfillment and cash timing distinctly. An order-led thesis needs the scheduled conversion, execution costs, cancellation conditions and reinvestment burden, not just a ratio above one.

**Falsifier and remaining gap.** A strong booking period can be followed by delivery slippage, different margins, or working-capital absorption. Sequential forward guidance should not be compared with year-on-year history as though they were the same measure. This pass does not reconstruct every acquisition or the full orderbook's profitability.

### SE-C10 — Epiroc: equipment and service can be at different points in their cycles

**Evidence.** The visually checked Equipment & Service page of Epiroc's Q2 report shows organic equipment orders up 30% and service orders up 6%. Equipment & Service orders are down 9% sequentially on an organic basis after the strong prior quarter. Service is 54% of that business area's revenue, not of every Epiroc activity. [S15]

**Interpretation.** The comparisons describe different revenue mechanisms and different time bases. New machinery purchases can respond to project timing while service reflects a different installed-base and activity pattern. A year-on-year increase with a sequential decline is not inherently contradictory.

**Proposed research output.** Bind each percentage to business area, measure, comparison period and growth convention. Show the equipment-to-installed-base-to-service hypothesis as a dated relationship requiring evidence about commissioning, utilization and service capture. Do not book an entire machine order as current aftermarket revenue, or assume that a digital capability sold by the group is deployed across all customer equipment.

**Falsifier and remaining gap.** Mining projects may order equipment before sustained operation; maintenance may be deferred or handled by others. Service intensity can change with technology and operating conditions. Business-area aggregation is useful but does not establish mine-specific customer economics or a universal lag between orders and service demand.

### SE-C11 — Orica: a mining supplier's footprint can change while cash and adjusted earnings diverge

**Evidence.** Orica's May 7 report shows H1 EBIT of A$512 million and operating cash of A$230.6 million versus A$244.9 million. It describes alternative supply arrangements following an input-supplier outage and the acquired Danafloat product range's expansion into copper processing. The company's EBIT excludes individually significant items under its disclosed definition. [S16]

**Interpretation.** Operating cash falls 5.84% on the disclosed comparison; that is not the same as the stated direction of underlying EBIT. Product additions can broaden the role of a company historically associated with another part of mining. It would be misleading to ascribe all its future economics to one old sector label.

**Proposed research output.** Map the economic job and customer process for each material business, including purchased inputs and commercial pass-through. Track current versus acquired roles without backfilling the acquisition into older company exposure. A supplier's ability to maintain customer operations is relevant only when connected to supported delivery and economics; general reliability language is not an accepted causal performance claim.

**Falsifier and remaining gap.** Better price/mix or technology adoption could be offset by input costs, working capital, integration expense or capital needs. This pass does not assign the newly acquired range a revenue percentage or quantify outage-related benefit. It studies commercial economics only, with no instructions for manufacturing, handling or using hazardous products.

## 4. Five explicit mechanisms and their counterexamples

These are original, deliberately simplified research illustrations, not issuer forecasts, investment recommendations or production models.

### SE-H01 — Host activity can overwhelm a recovery improvement

Assume 100 units of suitable host feed contain one accessory unit each, with 50% recovery and 80% payable entitlement. Attributable payable output is 40 units. Host feed falls to 80 while recovery improves to 60%, with the same grade and entitlement. Output becomes 38.4 units, a 4% decline. The example does not stipulate a reason for the host decline or infer an elasticity from price.

The useful question is which variable actually responds to the accessory price. Host throughput may be controlled by another product's economics. Recovery may need investment, and recovered material may not all be payable. A modeled improvement must retain all those assumptions rather than simply increasing every company in a minor-metal basket.

### SE-H02 — A profitable selective-feed strategy can reduce headline tonnes

Assume two admissible feeds use the same constrained processing slot. Feed A yields 100 tonnes of the headline metal and total net contribution of $4,000. Feed B yields 70 tonnes of that metal and total net contribution of $4,500 after all stipulated purchase and processing costs. Choosing B reduces headline tonnes by 30% and increases contribution by 12.5%. Quantities, costs and process compatibility are invented.

The counterexample does not prove that any particular real residue is attractive. It demonstrates why the economic objective and the physical-output metric must be separate. If residue availability is limited, the current contribution cannot be extended indefinitely. If B displaces a valuable alternative use of equipment or creates additional liabilities, those omitted costs could reverse the conclusion.

### SE-H03 — A customer's efficiency gain can help or hurt the supplier

Assume a customer initially consumes 100 parts at $100 each; supplier variable cost is $70 per part. Buyer spending is $10,000 and supplier contribution is $3,000. A new part lasts 25% longer, so the same duty requires 80 parts, priced at $110. With unchanged unit cost, buyer spending falls to $8,800 and supplier contribution rises to $3,200. Both benefit under those assumptions.

If the new unit costs $75 to make, supplier contribution instead falls to $2,800. The customer's 12% spending saving remains, but supplier contribution is 6.67% below baseline. Longer life does not force a negative supplier outcome, and a higher price does not guarantee a positive one. Price, manufacturing cost, service capture, incremental share, and the customer's operating benefit all need evidence.

Avoid valuing downtime savings and additional production independently when the latter already monetizes the former. Actual customer trials need a specified baseline, duration and comparison; supplier claims should remain attributed until the relevant proof is obtained.

### SE-H04 — Metal-value inflation can absorb funding without improving conversion contribution

Assume a processor maintains an average inventory of 1,000 units and pays $100 of metal value per unit, with a fixed net industrial contribution elsewhere in its contracts. A metal-price rise to $150 increases the funding tied to that inventory from $100,000 to $150,000. No matching credit, hedge collateral change or payment-term adjustment is assumed.

The extra $50,000 is a working-capital scenario, not a proven expense or a universal change in cash flow. The relationship can differ under consignment, customer-owned metal, supplier credit, hedging and settlement arrangements. The model must first identify who owns and funds the material. This example does not calculate an issuer's actual financing need.

### SE-H05 — Accrued protection is not shipped product or collected cash

Assume a conditional annual minimum creates $12 of compensation accrued at an interim date. At that moment the illustration has no invoice, no collected cash and no additional delivered tonnes associated with the compensation. The reporting assertion may be supported while a cash or physical-flow assertion is not.

After the year-end volume reconciliation, the invoice could differ, and collection is a later event. The model should recognize valid earnings protection without converting it into volume growth or pre-funding a capital project. A missing settlement value remains unknown. This is a teaching example, not an implementation of a real take-or-pay contract or an assessment of enforceability.

## 5. Research taxonomy and candidate economic views

These categories are proposed facets under incumbent GMI ownership, not new canonical identifiers, live baskets, or a parallel database. They distinguish the investor's economic question rather than reproduce standard sector labels.

| Proposed view | What creates possible value | What would invalidate a simplistic thesis |
|---|---|---|
| Host-linked accessory recovery | More suitable feed, better recovery or improved payable rights | Host decline, unsuitable feed, absent commercial recovery, unrecovered or unpaid metal |
| Dedicated specialty production | Realization, scale, reliability and capital discipline at a focused operation | Ramp uncertainty, higher costs, noncash gains masking operations, financing claims |
| Selective-feed processing | Better net contribution per constrained processing resource | Short-lived feed, displaced valuable activity, unseen penalties or liabilities |
| Finite historical-material recovery | Economic retreatment of an existing stock | Inventory exhaustion, unproven recovery, restart costs or unavailable market access |
| Ongoing secondary-material processing | Collection, feed sourcing, metal recovery and service economics | Purchased-metal inflation, mixed-unit headlines, feed ownership ambiguity |
| New equipment and project systems | Orders converted into profitable deliveries | Cancellations, delayed installation, loss-making mix, cash absorbed in delivery |
| Activity-linked consumables | Productive duty, wear, price, customer share and fulfillment | Efficiency lowers units, deferred maintenance, changing material characteristics |
| Services and upgrades | Installed base, service attachment and customer value | Third-party servicing, unused equipment, unsupported renewal assumptions |
| Mine software and data products | Accepted user workflow, deployment, retention and monetization | Pilots called production, physical aftermarket called subscription, purchased growth |
| Production improvement | Verifiable customer benefit with a contract capturing part of it | Marketing metrics, unsuitable baselines, double-counted benefits, no supplier capture |

The same company can appear in several views. A useful mapping records the disclosed evidence and denominator, not a forced 100% allocation. Public company examples in the dossiers remain research examples, not an investable-universe certification. Listing aliases must resolve through existing security identity, and changing corporate ownership or acquisitions must remain dated.

The proposed moat is the accumulated explanation of how a company actually earns money from a particular constraint or improvement. A static company tag can be copied easily; a correction-aware history of assets, feed choices, rights, operational milestones, reporting bases and subsequent outcomes is a more useful research asset. That is a product-design thesis, not a claim that a defensible commercial advantage is already established.

## 6. Four investor workflows to test the eventual product

### SE-P01 — “This minor metal is scarce. Which company actually receives the benefit?”

Start with the material and select the relevant physical route. Show the host/feed association as context, then the specific asset, recovery evidence, output specification and economic rights. The answer must distinguish current supported revenue exposure from future recovery optionality. It must identify whether the binding uncertainty is host supply, process capability, product acceptance, payability or capital. Unknowns should appear beside the inference they limit.

A successful dossier could conclude that a company has a credible new recovery project but no supported current revenue estimate. That is still a useful answer when it names the next evidence: a process or contract update, qualified output, funded capacity, or realized sales. It is not a reason to stop at a generic warning or to invent an exposure weight.

### SE-P02 — “Why did output rise while sales or cash weakened?”

Separate material produced, physical shipments, payable quantity, invoicing, accruals and cash. Show the actual reporting basis and period before calculating a change. Identify possible explanations such as logistics, inventory, ownership, product mix or contract timing, while distinguishing issuer explanations from verified reconciliations.

The user should be able to compare a production-recovery thesis with an inventory-release thesis and see why they have different durability. An unreconciled difference stays visible; it cannot be silently allocated to the most convenient explanation. A derived comparison stops when its inputs are incompatible, but the underlying facts and follow-up research remain available.

### SE-P03 — “Can a supplier grow even if fewer replacement parts are needed?”

Start with the customer's duty and the supplier's product role. Show whether the proposed improvement changes life, uptime, throughput, energy, recovery, or labor, and whether the claimed benefits overlap. Then show the supplier's unit economics and contract mechanism. Separate better customer value from more supplier profit.

The output should distinguish increased market share, richer price/mix, recurring service, fewer physical replacements and lower manufacturing costs. A high aftermarket percentage is contextual, not the conclusion. Acceptance eventually requires a demonstrated user journey and supported trial/commercial evidence; this report supplies the discriminating examples, not that production proof.

### SE-P04 — “Is the reported growth an improving cycle or a changed reporting perimeter?”

Show current and prior periods on the matched basis, alongside the original publication and acquisition/restatement bridge. Separate organic activity from purchased businesses and currency. Explain order intake, recognized sales and cash independently. A producer's growth and a supplier's order acceleration may concern different stages and periods.

Then ask what changed relative to a dated expectation. Do not claim surprise, fair value or expected outperformance merely because a result improved. A modeled rerating mechanism remains a hypothesis until its expectation benchmark and validation exist. The next research action should be specific: for example, a compatible backlog-to-sales bridge or a customer-program revenue split, not an invitation to generate another sentiment score.

## 8. What remains to resolve before design and implementation

The report improves representative economic coverage; it does not finish the sector. Important remaining regimes include the bauxite–alumina–aluminium energy chain, nickel/cobalt and graphite/anode process economics, manganese and ferroalloys, additional specialty products and supplier service/software unit economics. Regional asset/issuer breadth and consistent cost curves remain incomplete.

The coverage/frontier companion distinguishes these breadth gaps from claim-specific nulls and genuine product gates. An unknown accessory payability does not prevent displaying an accurately scoped recovery project. Conversely, unavailable rights to retain/display a source cannot be solved by an attractive narrative. Current native identity, assertion/correction contracts, owner custody, data entitlement and publication boundaries must be reconciled before an implementation plan is accepted.

A mature Fable package should provide the selected investor jobs, a tested taxonomy, representative positive and negative examples, native integration boundaries, data-source/rights decisions, and acceptance criteria.
