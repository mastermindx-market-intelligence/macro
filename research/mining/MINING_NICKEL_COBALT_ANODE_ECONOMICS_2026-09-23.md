## 1. Research outcome

This pass tests the remaining battery-material business models against a precise investor question: **what must happen between growing battery demand and cash reaching the owner of a particular security?** The answer cannot be inferred from a material name. It depends on the demand denominator, product route, processing choice, commercial rights, customer requirements, and funding available over the required interval.

The proposed model now distinguishes an expanding end market from expanding demand for a specific input; an integrated process from an obligation to operate every stage; host-metal extraction from saleable by-product output; and technical progress from financed commercial readiness. These are original interpretations and proposed behaviors, not validated stock-return factors.

Ten dossiers below supply discriminating cases. Fourteen primary-source records support limited factual observations, with source-vintage and access limits retained. Investor-relations statements are evidence of what an issuer reported, not independent verification of operating performance. An accessible old publication does not establish that Mastermind retained it before the event. No company is recommended, ranked, assigned to a live basket, or granted trading significance by this document.

The conclusion for product planning is that broad regime exploration can now give way to native-interface and first-vertical design work. This is a research-sufficiency recommendation for a bounded dossier, not a claim of global sector completeness. The remaining claim-specific unknowns must travel into the design rather than be silently imputed or left for Fable to invent.

## 2. Ten focused dossiers

### NC-01 — Battery capacity, vehicle counts and material demand are different denominators

**Evidence.** IEA's Global EV Outlook 2026 reports that LFP exceeded 55% of EV battery deployment in 2025, against nearly 50% in 2024. The chemistry shares are weighted by deployed battery capacity, not vehicle counts. Battery deployment incorporates vehicle sales and average battery size; its discussion separately distinguishes nickel- and cobalt-containing chemistries. [S01]

**Interpretation.** An end-market forecast must be decomposed before it becomes a material forecast. A larger number of vehicles can be accompanied by smaller packs or a different chemistry mix. Even a correctly measured increase in battery capacity does not specify demand for each metal. The same chemistry label also must not substitute for a verified material intensity in a specific period and application.

**Proposed behavior.** Preserve vehicle category, geography, battery capacity, chemistry share, material-intensity basis and observation year. Show the contribution of each assumption to a modeled material-demand change. Do not multiply a vehicle-count share by a capacity series as though both describe the same distribution. A change on the cathode side must not automatically change every anode input by the same percentage.

A useful investor answer should identify which exposure is actually affected. It should be possible to say that battery deployment increased while a particular material-demand scenario weakened, without treating either fact as an error. The controlled example H01 makes this distinction testable without inventing a historical intensity series.

### NC-02 — A battery thesis does not describe the whole nickel market

**Evidence.** INSG's December 2025 market discussion identifies stainless steel as the dominant first-use market for primary nickel, at just under 70% in that publication. It separately discusses battery use and regional demand. Its forecasts are a dated vintage, not the latest verified 2026 balance. [S02]

**Interpretation.** Battery demand is one branch of the nickel thesis. A weaker battery-material outlook does not mechanically establish a weaker total nickel market, and a stronger industrial branch does not guarantee improved battery-chemical conversion margins. First-use demand and final end-use demand must also remain separate: adding both can count the same metal twice.

**Proposed behavior.** Connect an issuer to the product and customer pathway it actually serves. Proposed research facets include ore supply, ferroalloys for steelmaking, intermediates, refined metal, battery chemicals, recycling, and commercial procurement. They are not assertions that each route is freely interchangeable or equally profitable.

The dataset requirement is a time-stamped first-use allocation with consistent units and a separately modeled path to final demand. Where that allocation is missing, retain a bounded qualitative view rather than replacing it with an all-EV assumption. Do not infer that the December forecast remained correct merely because its webpage is still accessible.

### NC-03 — Weda Bay: scarcity, ownership and offtake do not create the same claim

**Evidence.** Eramet reports a 38.7% indirect interest in PT Weda Bay Nickel, arising from 43% of a holding company owning 90% of the operation. It separately reports a 43% NPI offtake interest. H1 external ore sales were 9.4 million wet tonnes on a 100% basis, versus 11.0 million; its share of EBITDA was EUR56 million versus EUR55 million. Higher realization was offset by volumes and costs. The mine entered care and maintenance after quota exhaustion while NPI operations continued. [S03]

**Interpretation.** A local ore constraint can raise prices without increasing the output a specific owner is able to sell. The economic effect can differ between the mine, a buyer of ore, and an offtake/trading role. Nor should mine status be copied mechanically onto a downstream facility that has a different supply position.

**Proposed behavior.** Keep wet ore, contained nickel and final products on different bases. Attach ownership and offtake to their respective claims. The 38.7% share must not be applied again to an already proportionate EBITDA figure; the 43% offtake must not be silently substituted for mine ownership. An offtake quantity requires its purchase and pricing obligations before any margin can be inferred.

This source supplies a dated company explanation, not an independent assessment of current quota compliance or a claim that the mine remained in the same state through the research cutoff. Later revisions must be applied to the affected asset and period, rather than overwritten into all historical observations.

### NC-04 — Terrafame: integration can be an option to sell before the final stage

**Evidence.** Terrafame reports temporarily curtailing battery-chemical conversion because intermediates had relatively better prices, then increasing production later as demand and customer agreements improved. H1 battery-chemical revenue was EUR127.8 million and intermediate revenue EUR138.3 million. EBITDA rose from EUR25.5 million to EUR51.5 million, while the operating result remained negative EUR8.5 million. These are unaudited IFRS figures and the issuer's causal explanations. [S04]

**Interpretation.** More downstream processing is not always more economic value. The relevant comparison is the net benefit of further conversion against the feasible sale of the same intermediate. A technically integrated company may have valuable flexibility if it can change its product destination lawfully and commercially. It may have little flexibility if its feed or customer commitments constrain the choice.

**Proposed behavior.** Model the opportunity cost of an intermediate, not just its historical manufacturing cost. Keep the sell-intermediate and convert-further cases separate and compare compatible quantities, delivery points and periods. Require evidence that the alternative is actually available; do not assume a liquid customer market for every unfinished product.

The earnings improvement does not prove that conversion optimization explains all of it or that earnings became cash. H02 isolates the decision with invented inputs. The company is used as an economic exemplar, not automatically a tradable basket constituent. A private operating entity can improve the ontology without creating an investable security.

### NC-05 — Glencore: host extraction can rise while marketable by-product output falls

**Evidence.** Glencore reports H1 own-sourced copper of 397.0 thousand tonnes versus 343.9 thousand, and cobalt of 10.2 thousand versus 18.9 thousand. Its explanation says that cobalt-export constraints led operations to prioritize copper and retain more cobalt in solution rather than process and dry it into saleable hydroxide. Future processing and sale are prospective. Its production basis is 100% for controlled assets and joint ventures except where specifically noted. [S05]

**Interpretation.** This is more specific than a generic host-metal dependency. The process stage at which material is held can change. A decline in reported saleable cobalt is not necessarily an equivalent decline in cobalt physically encountered in the ore, and a rise in host production does not establish proportionate by-product deliveries.

**Proposed behavior.** Distinguish material extracted, in-process material, finished stock, authorized delivery and cash collection. Keep material in solution separate from finished inventory that could be sold without further processing. Do not claim a future receivable or quantity of saleable stock from an unspecified in-process balance.

An investor assessing later supply needs to ask about recoverability, incremental completion costs, permitted shipment, customer specification and timing. A production headline is not enough to answer those questions. The correct output may be a clearly supported direction and mechanism with the releasable quantity still unknown.

### NC-06 — Sherritt: cash location and associated obligations constrain its use

**Evidence.** Sherritt reports June-end consolidated cash of C$203.8 million but available Canadian liquidity of C$80.1 million, with no availability under its credit facility. It also reports C$128.7 million of quarterly receipts recognized as payable to the Moa JV and used to meet Canadian operating costs on its behalf. Its quarter describes upstream disruption and subsequent refinery suspension when intermediate feed was exhausted. [S06]

**Interpretation.** A cash balance does not identify which project or claimant can use it. Receipts handled on behalf of another economic owner must not be interpreted as unrestricted profit. At the same time, automatically subtracting every disclosed receipt from the closing cash balance would be wrong: a period flow is not the outstanding closing obligation, and some may already have financed costs.

**Proposed behavior.** Bind cash to the legal entity, location, restriction or associated liability, reporting date and permitted purpose. Reconcile funds held, obligations outstanding and cash already spent before claiming funding headroom. Keep geographic components separate from JV balances excluded from consolidation.

The issuer's description of disruption is a dated commercial observation, not an independently determined legal status. This report does not advise on sanctions or assume a restart decision. The product should show the latest supported operating and funding evidence without turning it into a legal conclusion.

### NC-07 — NOVONIX: customer feedback reduces uncertainty without becoming a yield

**Evidence.** A June 11 announcement describes delivering a mass-production sample to Panasonic for validation. The September 10 release refers to a lead customer without naming it: internal tests met specifications, while customer testing passed twelve of fourteen tests; one miss was within roughly 1% and another was binary. Corrective work and the production target remained conditional. The September PDF's decisive page was visually checked. [S07–S08]

**Interpretation.** A programme can progress materially without completing qualification. The customer feedback narrows what needs to be resolved and therefore is not equivalent to having no information. But twelve divided by fourteen is not an 85.7% probability of commercial success, an output yield, or a justified revenue weighting.

**Proposed behavior.** Preserve sample identity, customer identity when disclosed, test setting and date, acceptance requirements, unresolved tests, and the scope of the eventual decision. Do not fill in an unnamed customer by proximity to an earlier announcement. Do not overwrite external feedback with the manufacturer's internal result or average the two into one quality score.

The user should see what improved and what remains necessary. A system designed only to reject claims would miss this positive information. Conversely, a material-sales forecast requires accepted volume, commercial terms, readiness and funding—not merely a favorable test count. H04 separates technical acceptance from commercially ready delivery.

### NC-08 — NOVONIX: the financing clock can shorten while qualification advances

**Evidence.** NOVONIX's September 10 financing notice states that a share-price-linked amortisation event required US$7 million, 20% of US$35 million outstanding, by September 21. It intended to pay from existing cash. The notice says the balance would fall to US$28 million upon payment and describes further conditional payments. The announced facility's maximum face value is US$100 million. The original SEC exhibit was read. [S09]

**Interpretation.** The agreement maximum is not undrawn cash available on demand. An announced required payment is neither a completed payment nor proof of default when the date passes. Future instalments require the precise original terms and event state; this research does not assume a geometric declining-balance schedule from a short announcement.

**Proposed behavior.** Link qualification timing to the funding horizon without conflating the two. Track available and committed funds, conditions, required uses and maturity/trigger dates. A forecast should distinguish an issuer able to wait for testing from one needing financing before commercial readiness. The cost of delay can arise from overhead, investment, interest or triggered repayment, not just a later revenue date.

No actual post-deadline settlement is established here. The useful investor conclusion is the disclosed obligation and its potential relevance, accompanied by the exact missing receipt. Do not manufacture a present solvency judgment from incomplete information. H05 illustrates a funding shortfall with invented inputs rather than predicting NOVONIX's runway.

### NC-09 — NMG: a funded mine does not automatically fund its downstream product

**Evidence.** NMG's August update describes the Matawinie mine's investment decision and construction, authorized annual graphite capacity of 106,000 tonnes, and a separate 13,000-tonne-per-year Bécancour anode project still targeting an investment decision. The September 14 release continues to target that decision and says Panasonic's possible investment remains an intention. It distinguishes the binding product offtake from prospective equity support. [S10–S11]

**Interpretation.** These are different projects, products and financing events. Dividing 13,000 by 106,000 would not establish conversion yield: the planned allocation of mine product, project timing and processing boundaries are not specified by that ratio. Similarly, a closed mine financing does not prove that every downstream use of its output has been funded.

**Proposed behavior.** Preserve project-specific capital, sanctioned status, schedule, proposed customer and material allocation. A binding offtake, an intent to invest, a completed investment and a customer acceptance decision are separate relationships. Do not add committed spending to incurred spending without determining whether their scopes overlap.

The September release is a useful continuity test: it does not authorize a false completed downstream FID, but it confirms a specific continued development path. The product should retain an actionable question—what financing or acceptance condition remains—rather than a generic company-wide development label.

### NC-10 — Graphite and silicon: compare claims at the same commercial scale

**Evidence.** Syrah's market overview describes graphite across several cathode chemistries and emphasizes difficulties of silicon substitution at scale. Group14's March 2026 release reports silicon-material production starting at a facility designed for 2,000 tonnes annually as it ramps. Sila's July release describes an operating silicon-carbon facility ramping after its 2025 start, new private financing, and prospective expansion beyond initial capacity. These are supplier claims, not independently verified utilization or total-market displacement. [S12–S14]

**Interpretation.** The evidence is not well represented by either “silicon does not work” or “graphite is obsolete.” A statement about global replacement differs from evidence of production for particular programmes. Commercial entry can be real while its market share, qualified capacity, delivered costs and competitive significance remain uncertain.

**Proposed behavior.** Separate natural graphite, synthetic graphite, silicon-carbon and other anode routes. Bind performance and commercial claims to the actual material, cell application, test conditions and customer programme. Do not apply a vendor's capacity-equivalence or displacement ratio across the global battery stock. Energy capacity and material mass are different denominators.

The proposed thesis is conditional: a substitute can reduce graphite intensity, but battery growth, product mix, natural-versus-synthetic choices and the cost of qualifying the substitute affect the total outcome. H01 and H03 explain the measurement logic without claiming a global forecast. Private suppliers belong in the competitive context even when they are not directly tradable constituents.

## 3. Proposed economic model: demand, route, acceptance and funding

The investment question should be decomposed into four connected views under the incumbent owners, not four new systems.

**Demand view.** Identify the final job, geographic market, product and observation horizon. Translate a supported demand observation through compatible capacity, mix and intensity assumptions. Retain uncertainty around each assumption. Do not confuse deployment, shipments, installed stock and replacement cycles. A scenario is not an observed demand fact.

**Route view.** Identify feasible processing and sale alternatives, each with a product specification, recovery basis, delivery point and economic rights. A route can share a constrained upstream asset or compete for the same intermediate. The relevant value is incremental cash after the opportunity cost of that intermediate and the expenditure required to finish the route. Internal transfer prices help explain segments but do not create consolidated profit.

**Acceptance view.** Identify what the buyer has actually accepted and for which programme. Distinguish samples, test results, operating qualification, contractual orders, delivered volume and cash collection. Unknown criteria are not failed criteria, but neither are they passed. Do not turn a checklist completion fraction into probability without a separately accepted empirical method.

**Funding view.** Identify what cash can legally and practically support the route until the relevant acceptance and production milestone. Bind proceeds to entity, currency, commitments and conditions. Show how a delay changes required funding under stated assumptions. A company may have valuable technical progress and worsening equity risk at the same time; an explanation should preserve both.

These views combine into a proposed bridge:

`End-market change → compatible material requirement → feasible route and rights → accepted saleable product → retained contribution → funding and reinvestment → cash and value per share → comparison with dated expectations.`

Each arrow is a research obligation. The model should not invent the intermediate quantity because the beginning and ending narrative sound plausible. Where only a qualitative relationship is supported, provide that explanation with a precise limitation. A blank calculation should not erase an informative source-grounded relationship.

### Investor implications, stated as hypotheses rather than factors

A chemistry-mix change could favor a different material, but only after compatible capacity and intensity are established. A constrained local ore market could help a supplier with saleable quota while hurting a converter that purchases feed; the affected issuer may hold both roles. Processing flexibility could improve retained margins while final-product output falls. Better customer feedback could narrow execution uncertainty without resolving financing. A new funding obligation could change the equity claim before commercial output changes.

None of those mechanisms establishes mispricing. The product must distinguish a change in management guidance, a reconstructed expectation comparison and a contemporaneously retained market benchmark. This pass does not acquire a sector-wide consensus series or make an expected-return estimate. It defines the economic questions a later valuation analysis must answer.

## 4. Six controlled examples

All inputs in H01–H06 are hypothetical unless explicitly identified as a limited arithmetic check of reported data. They are not issuer forecasts or production models. The companion checker uses standard-library Decimal arithmetic and deliberately incompatible inputs.

### H01 — More battery capacity, less nickel in the modeled branch

Assume 100 GWh of deployment, a 50% nickel-chemistry share measured on the same capacity basis, and 0.7 kg of nickel per kWh for that branch. Modeled nickel is 35.00 thousand tonnes. At 130 GWh but a 35% share with unchanged intensity, the result is 31.850 thousand tonnes: capacity grows 30% while this nickel requirement falls 9%.

This excludes other uses, recycling and changes within the nickel-containing branch. It is not a market balance. In a separate anode example, 100 GWh at 1 kg/kWh graphite intensity implies 100 thousand tonnes. Capacity growth to 130 GWh with intensity down to 0.8 kg/kWh implies 104 thousand tonnes. Substitution and absolute demand growth can coexist. Neither intensity is asserted as an actual cell recipe.

### H02 — Converting further can reduce contribution

For one stipulated, economically comparable contained-material unit, assume an intermediate can be sold for $17,000. Converting it yields one final saleable unit worth $18,500 and costs an additional $2,000. The incremental value of conversion is negative $500 relative to selling the intermediate. At a final realization of $20,000 with the same costs, it becomes positive $1,000.

The counterfactual matters: subtracting only a sunk historical feed cost would not answer whether conversion is the better current use. This illustration assumes both routes are commercially available and quantities compatible. Real feed losses, commitments, selling costs and capital can invalidate those assumptions; they must be added from evidence rather than hidden in a generic premium.

### H03 — Serial conversion does not create additive tonnes

Assume 100 tonnes of a specified starting material. Seventy percent passes a shaping stage, 95% of that mass passes a further stage, and 90% of the resulting lots meets a stipulated customer saleable-volume criterion. Saleable output is 59.850 tonnes, not 100 + 70 + 66.5 + 59.85.

At $600 per input tonne, the input-material cost is about $1,002.506 per accepted output tonne. With $15,000 of additional batch cost and $1,500 per saleable tonne, the simplified batch contribution is $14,775. No value is assigned to residual streams, and no taxes, financing, sustaining investment or disposal costs are included. A by-product credit can be added only if its realizable value is supported and has not already been credited.

The 90% here is an invented accepted-volume ratio. It is not the percentage of qualification tests passed. Mixing those concepts would turn a technical checklist into a fictional physical yield.

### H04 — Acceptance is not a mean score

Suppose a programme requires four criteria. Three pass and one is unknown. The acceptance state remains unknown, not 75% commercially ready. If one explicitly fails, the programme has not met its criteria even if the others pass. If all four pass, that establishes only the stipulated technical criterion set; delivery also needs the supported operating and commercial conditions.

A useful product recognizes progression from unknown to known results. It does not label every incomplete programme worthless, invent probabilities, or allow strong performance on one test to compensate automatically for failure on another. Only an actual acceptance rule can define permitted tradeoffs.

### H05 — A valuable product can still reach a financing deadline first

Assume $30 million of unrestricted cash, $8 million of binding near-term obligations and a required $7 million financing payment, leaving $15 million for an assumed programme. Four months at $4 million per month requires $16 million, leaving a $1 million shortfall. Delaying completion by another two months adds $8 million, creating a $9 million total shortfall under the same assumptions.

This is not NOVONIX's cash balance, burn rate or runway. Do not count a facility maximum as available cash, subtract a period receipt as though it were a closing payable, or assume contingent financing arrives. A fully specified scenario must also separate working capital, capital expenditure and repayment from recurring operating expenditure to avoid counting the same use twice.

### H06 — A by-product credit cannot be collected twice

Assume $100 of cash cost for a batch and $20 of realized by-product proceeds. If an advertised net cost is already $80, subtracting another $20 creates an incorrect $60 cost. Two equivalent presentations are possible: $100 gross cost with a separate $20 credit, or $80 net cost. They must reconcile without adding both credits.

The same principle applies when valuing in-process material: a later receipt cannot be counted both as current cash and as a full future sale. This simple example is not a complete allocation method for co-products, tax, working capital or joint ventures. It tests one double-counting failure only.

## 6. Four persona workflows for the existing Themes journey

### U01 — “EVs are growing. Which material and company actually benefit?”

Start with the relevant battery-demand observation. Display its capacity/count denominator and the material bridge. Show chemistry and anode assumptions separately. Then map to the supported product/issuer role rather than coloring every battery-material company alike. A useful answer identifies the assumptions most capable of reversing the interpretation.

The negative case is a rising capacity headline with falling modeled nickel demand. The positive case is a supported gain in the relevant product branch. The unknown case is missing material intensity: retain the end-market and chemistry observations, but do not output a false precise metal forecast.

### U02 — “Why is this integrated company selling more intermediate and less final product?”

Show feasible routes and the opportunity cost of the intermediate. Distinguish technical trouble from a reported commercial decision. Identify customer obligations or feed commitments that limit flexibility. Explain the expected economic mechanism, but do not attribute the entire issuer earnings change to it without a reconciled bridge.

A complete answer should include what would change the preference: final realization, input price, recovery, acceptance, or incremental cost. It should not conclude that less downstream production is inherently worse or that integration is automatically a moat.

### U03 — “Does this qualification announcement make commercial production investable?”

Show what changed between source vintages, who performed each test, and which programme is affected. Keep acceptance, orders, scale-up and financing adjacent but distinct. The user should recognize genuine progress without converting a test fraction into a probability or revenue forecast.

The next panel shows funding conditions through the relevant date. A disclosed repayment trigger is visible even when technical feedback improves. The output names the missing commercial or cash evidence and the counter-thesis, without asserting default, acceptance or a current solvency state that was not verified.

### U04 — “A by-product price rose; why did saleable output or cash fall?”

Show the host operation and the stage at which material is held. Separate in-process inventory from finished product and allowable shipment. Attach the source's explanation and alternative hypotheses. A later positive event should advance only the affected stage, not automatically all future quantities or all assets.

The user should be able to explain the gap between extraction, saleable output and retained cash. Source links support that task; they do not replace the synthesis. All four workflows require real-path browser proof at implementation, including changed-source and unknown-input cases.

## 8. Research sufficiency and next design boundary

P08 fills the absence of representative nickel/cobalt and anode economics. It does not close all chemistry routes or every supplier. Together with the preceding cases, it supports moving to a written design for a small economic dossier. The design must explicitly limit its initial coverage and preserve a path to the full Mining ambition.

The next principal action is current native-interface reconciliation: determine which incumbent identity, evidence/assertion, correction, membership and authenticated/public presentation interfaces the dossier can use. Consolidate the repeated requirements into a small coherent behavior set with many discriminating tests. Do not build another graph, contract service, source-retention layer, schedule, queue or ranking authority to enforce them.

Selected primary PGM, alloy-route and thermal-coal questions remain thinner than the core cases. They should be visibly out of the first dossier's acceptance scope until their own evidence is admitted, rather than blocking all design or being represented as complete. The first vertical should not promise global coverage, a universal fair-value model or automatic investment recommendations. That boundary does not cancel later research obligations.

Fable should receive the chosen user job, representative admitted facts, native contracts, data/rights requirements, phased implementation, failure cases and actual acceptance evidence. No final Fable packet is issued here. Current source access is not a licence grant; prior publication hashes are not live product proof; local research checks are not application or strategy validation.
