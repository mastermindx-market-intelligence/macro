## 1. What changed from R1

R1 established the Araguaia failure case, the Tocantinzinho financing case and a separate invented dilution worksheet. R2 moves the Tocantinzinho financial explanation from that generic example to its disclosed financing mechanics. It preserves R1 and the incumbent FCX/MP Mining work.

The new result is not merely “remember debt and dilution.” The source-bound reference distinguishes construction cash from net economic cost, a commitment from eligibility and an actual draw, face principal from cash proceeds and capitalized debt, mine output from delivered stream ounces, and commercial production from the separate loan-defined Completion condition. The reference also refuses to reconstruct an exact historical share count from a later annual total.

A full real diluted-equity range remains unqualified. In particular, the independent technical tables, complete executed agreements, tax/cash timing, historical capitalization and common valuation-date market inputs are not all bound. Reporting that missing result as unavailable is not a decision to cap the product at research briefings; it preserves the dependency that must be closed before investment estimates become credible.

## 2. Primary findings: what the sources actually support

### Construction cash has three different totals

The July financing release distinguishes USD427m before-tax initial capital, USD49m taxes paid and USD18m later recoverable taxes. Those inputs imply USD476m construction cash versus USD458m net economic cost. With USD21m already spent at 30 June 2022, the disclosed remaining cash requirement is USD455m. These are historical budget figures, not an updated cost estimate. [S1]

The model must therefore preserve both the cash peak and the recoverable asset. It cannot subtract a future tax recovery from today's funding gap. Recoverability, legal entity, eligibility and timing remain source-specific. The figures alone do not certify that construction cash is accessible or that the tax credit will arrive on schedule.

### Financing is conditional and sequential

Franco-Nevada's financing disclosure requires at least USD95m of qualifying project spend since 1 January 2022 before the stream deposit is drawn, with additional conditions; the loan follows full stream funding. The financier's USD352.5m package is a subset of the issuer's overall financing, not a second additive source of capital. [S2]

The constructive interpretation is that substantial financing commitments existed. The counter-thesis is that commitments can remain unusable at the moment cash is required. A model must represent both rather than treating either “fully financed” or “not drawn” as a complete investment verdict.

The standalone reference reports eligibility separately from disbursement. A passed predicate returns **ELIGIBLE_NOT_DRAWN**, never a cash receipt. A failed condition and an unknown condition can coexist; neither is erased to make a cleaner label. These are research-specimen states, not proposed replacements for the existing finance, source or execution owners.

### A commercial milestone creates another payment obligation

The original acquisition disclosure and the exchange's closing bulletin identify USD60m of deferred consideration. It is payable no later than the first commercial-production anniversary, subject to the reported optional deferral of half for one additional year at an extra USD5m. The alternative latest-anniversary schedules are therefore USD60m in year one, or USD30m in year one plus USD35m in year two. [S4, S5]

The one-year extension charges 5/30 = **16.67% on the deferred portion**, not 5/60. At an assumed 10% discount rate measured from the commercial-production trigger, the two cash paths have present costs of USD54.5455m and USD56.1983m. At an assumed 20%, the ordering reverses. These are arithmetic sensitivities, not a market borrowing-rate observation or advice to postpone payment.

The engineering milestone and the equity benefit are consequently different questions. A successful commercial start can make a payment clock relevant while also improving operating prospects. Failure to achieve the trigger does not prove the entire legal claim is extinguished: termination, restructuring and recovery treatment still require the executed agreement.

### Loan spread, issue discount and capitalized interest must remain separate

The announced loan is USD75m face capacity. Its pre-Completion margin is 5.75 percentage points **above** three-month Term SOFR; the post-Completion margin is 4.75 points. The disclosed 2% original-issue discount, standby fee and capitalization election are distinct financial terms. [S1, S2]

Under an explicitly assumed full USD75m draw at the start of one year, a 5% SOFR assumption, pre-Completion status and capitalized interest, the specimen produces USD73.50m cash proceeds and USD83.0625m closing principal. No real historical rate or draw is claimed. Paying the interest instead retains USD75m principal but consumes USD8.0625m of cash during that assumed year.

Unused face capacity must not be computed by subtracting previously capitalized interest from the commitment. Conversely, interest on prior capitalized debt must not disappear. The reference now handles face drawings and accumulated capitalized amounts separately. Exact legal treatment, day-count, rate floors, reset dates and elections remain agreement-qualification requirements.

### The lower stream rate is not reached by the disclosed base life plan

The issuer's February 2022 results release reports total life-plan recovered output of 1.838m ounces, including preproduction. The initial stream is 12.5%, reducing to 7.5% only after 300,000 **delivered stream** ounces. [S3, S2]

Assuming all of the forecast recovered gold is payable and delivered, with no delivery lag or additional output, the base plan supplies 229,750 stream ounces. A first step-down requires 2.4m produced ounces at the initial rate. The disclosed base plan falls 562,000 produced ounces short of that threshold. This does not predict that the actual property will never reach the threshold; future discoveries, plan changes and contract scope are not assumed away.

At a constant assumed USD1,600/oz and the stated 20% spot payment on delivered stream gold, the simplified **undiscounted, pre-tax net revenue transfer** is USD294.08m. It is not a profit margin, after-tax stream value, loan principal or a valid amount to subtract directly from the feasibility NPV. The same calculation gives USD257.32m at USD1,400 and USD367.60m at USD2,000. The price sensitivity changes the dollar transfer, not the number of stream ounces or the geological probability.

### Closed issuance is not a complete capitalization history

The September closing announcement confirms two equity closings: 160,062,500 shares on 22 July and 29,004,265 on 7 September, at CAD0.80. Their combined 189,066,765-share delta and CAD151,253,412 gross proceeds reconcile. That announcement also uses rounded shareholder percentages. [S6]

An exact earlier denominator is not obtained by dividing a holding by an approximate ownership percentage. Nor may a later year-end count be reversed through two observed issuances unless the entire intervening capital history is complete. The R2 research case retains the known issuance delta while its earlier exact opening count remains null.

### Later legal qualification remains later evidence

The 2022 AIF is dated 28 April 2023. Its corporate-financing sections describe a USD10m minimum reserve before certified Completion, the conditions for successive draws and the agreement/certificate references. Its capital section reports 447,517,060 basic shares at 31 December 2022. Its stock-option table has separate effective and vesting-date qualifications; the requested table screenshot did not succeed. [S7]

The interface exposes this as a later qualification view, not evidence silently added to July 2022. A term can be legally effective in July without our recovered corpus demonstrating it was publicly knowable then. The annual report is not evidence of historical system possession. A dated material-contract index is a navigation result, not proof the underlying signed document has been reviewed.

## 3. Financial contract decisions

### Choose one coherent valuation convention

A future implementation must use either a coherent enterprise-to-equity bridge or an explicit equity-cash-flow method, through the existing financial owner. It must not mix the two selectively.

For an enterprise approach, the asset value at the chosen horizon must already reflect the applicable stream/royalty obligations, remaining project costs, working capital, taxes and operating scenario. Add separately valued other assets and genuinely usable horizon cash; subtract the correctly scoped senior and contingent claims at that same horizon; divide by scenario-appropriate diluted shares.

For an equity-cash-flow approach, include operational cash, investment, borrowing receipts, interest, repayments and the applicable contingent payments on their correct dates. Track issuance and distributions to the actual share class. The exact treatment of a financing receipt must be consistent with the definition of the asset cash flows and the equity measurement date.

The forbidden shortcut is to start with a historical project NPV and casually add a stream deposit, subtract an undiscounted stream transfer, subtract loan face value and divide by a later basic share count. That mixes dates, taxes, real versus nominal values, financial claims and capitalization. R2 deliberately does not print a resulting “target price.”

### Required basis fields and falsifiers

Every economic cell needs a value or an explicit unavailable state, unit/currency, price year, nominal/real designation, legal entity/perimeter, interest fraction, effective period, cash/accrual basis, source/version and availability clock. Reported figures and derived values stay separate. An interest-bearing nominal financing path cannot be combined with a real-terms project schedule without a declared conversion convention.

The following counterexamples must fail the eventual owner-native implementation:

* A tax recovery after commissioning reduces the pre-commissioning cash requirement.
* A press-release commitment is counted again when its cash draw arrives.
* Capitalized interest vanishes from the repayment base, or consumes face capacity incorrectly.
* The same loan is repaid in projected cash flows and deducted a second time as an unrepaid horizon claim.
* A stream is deducted from the forecast metal receipts and again from an already post-stream asset value.
* A 300,000-delivered-ounce threshold is applied to 300,000 mine-produced ounces.
* A commercial-production headline activates a separately defined lender Completion test.
* A known future share issue enters the current count before its closing, or a missing opening count becomes an inferred exact number.

### Contingent claims and financing feedback

Resource cases require joint scenarios. Construction delay, cost overruns, metal prices, recoveries, funding terms and share issuance can share causes. A scenario that increases cash needs can also lower the issue price and increase dilution. These effects cannot be represented by independent success multipliers.

Where an issuance price is contractually fixed, use the qualified contractual terms. Where future funding terms are unknown, use explicit scenario rules and label their status. Do not create an unconstrained recursive loop in which an assumed higher valuation improves financing, which mechanically raises valuation again. Any later endogenous funding model needs an identified estimation target, falsifier and stability test within the existing model owner.

R1's shared decision-review question remains: a lower stock price may improve the entry reference while worsening future financing terms. Preserve geology and unchanged evidence; invalidate only the financing and equity scenarios that actually depend on the changed price. The #8061 owner retains adjudication of the shared contract.

## 4. Forecast-method implications

R2 strengthens the proposed history-aware, discrete-time competing-transition approach from R1. It does not fit a model or establish a mining prior.

The forecast target must specify the project, starting information set, current stage, event definition and window. “First output,” “issuer-declared commercial production,” “contract-defined Completion,” “sustained throughput/recovery,” and “positive shareholder return” are different outcomes. Their label dates need not coincide. A delayed project may eventually succeed while failing its original timing forecast.

Funding state should enter as a collection of observable/qualified attributes: timed usable cash, remaining costs, committed versus drawn facilities, unfulfilled conditions, reserve constraints, maturity and rate-reset exposure, cost variance, rights changes and financing dependence. These attributes do not manufacture a probability by being assigned a weighted score.

Compare three falsifiable candidates before a complex model is promoted: the issuer's announced window with an explicit overdue rule; cohort event-frequency/time baselines with censoring; and the proposed conditional transition model. The added model must improve time calibration and economically important errors without merely excluding difficult cases. Keep sponsor/project/time groups intact across validation splits and distinguish commodity, processing route, jurisdiction, stage and financing regime.

The two historical dossiers are purposively chosen design checks. They cannot establish predictive performance, sample adequacy or calibration. Retain entrants that fail to finance, delay, delist, restart or disappear from promotional coverage. Measure both qualified coverage and abstention. Evaluate false positives, missed winners, lead time and severe equity loss separately from engineering-label accuracy.

The existing evaluation owner must also distinguish public reconstruction from actual historical pipeline possession. Present-day LLM knowledge of project outcomes can contaminate retrospective narratives even when the visible evidence packet is dated. Use structured, auditable feature inputs for historical experiments and require prospective shadow forecasts before promotion. A current chat that has already seen the outcome is not an outcome-blind prediction.

Its eight public specimens cover cost basis, conditional draw eligibility, one-period loan cash/principal, equal-principal amortization, life-plan stream transfer, conditional acquisition payment paths, share-event roll-forward and missing-input explanation.

These are not production APIs or a second finance/admission plane. The input-readiness specimen never computes an equity value or grants rank/gate/size/origination/entry authority, even when every input flag is true.

Actual validation:

* R2 suite: **49 passed**, no failures.

* Seven deliberately mutated temporary candidates: **seven semantic failures detected**, each by a failing assertion rather than an incidental runtime error. They cover tax credit as cash, omitted SOFR, forgiven capitalized interest, incorrect stream threshold, future share leakage, wrong deferral denominator and capitalized debt incorrectly consuming face capacity.

The first unimplemented-stub run had 47 tests: 15 failures, 27 errors and five passing missing-value cases. It was not a clean functional RED proof. The later seven mutation runs supply measured counterexample evidence on the completed implementation. No old test count is relabeled as a new run.
