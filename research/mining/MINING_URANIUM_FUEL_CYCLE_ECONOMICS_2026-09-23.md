## 1. Research outcome and boundaries

This tranche answers a question that a generic uranium basket cannot: **which change in the fuel economy reaches which issuer, through which contract, in which period, and with what capital burden?** The proposed model now has representative tests spanning commodity procurement, mined production, financial inventory, commercial fuel services, development, customer commitments and common-equity financing.

The principal conclusion is that exposure should be represented as a relationship among **economic role, contract conditions and time horizon**, not merely a ticker-to-commodity association. Price improvement, earnings improvement, cash generation and a change in estimated value per share are separate hypotheses. A company can receive more revenue but less current cash from the same price change. A stronger order book can require investment before it contributes earnings. A physical holding can appreciate in dollars while its quoted shares lag because of currency and the discount to net asset value.

These are original research conclusions and design proposals, not an accepted predictive model, investment recommendation or production schema. All numerical issuer observations retain their dates and definitions. Illustrative calculations are labeled; they are not forecasts or complete accounting reconciliations. Commercial research here does not include operational nuclear-process instructions.

This work continues the accepted Mining mandate. Passes 01–03 and the separate, settled Healthcare scope correction are not reopened. No worker or additional research principal was dispatched. Fable remains the later implementation/orchestration recipient, not the originator of the foundational research.

## 2. The economic map: commercial roles, not one interchangeable chain

For later design review, use the existing graph to distinguish the following relationships. These are proposed analytical facets, not new canonical IDs or independent data stores.

| Commercial role | The research question | Wrong shortcut to reject |
|---|---|---|
| Resource owner and mine operator | What output and costs are attributable to the issuer, and what must be invested to maintain them? | Assigning all connected-mine output to the listed company |
| Procurement and portfolio supplier | How are customer deliveries sourced, priced and timed? | Treating delivered material as exclusively mined material |
| Physical inventory owner | What material is owned, where is title held, and what financing or delivery obligation attaches to it? | Treating gross storage balances as unrestricted exposure |
| Fuel-service provider | Which service is sold, with what purchased inputs, customer obligations and capital requirements? | Applying a uranium-price sensitivity to service revenue |
| Commercial capacity developer | Which conditions separate a customer commitment from a functioning, economic operation? | Equating a large conditional backlog with secured earnings |
| Project developer with other operations | Which project consumes capital, and which separate activities supply cash? | Calling an entire issuer pre-revenue because its flagship is not producing |
| Financial instrument or holding vehicle | How do assets, costs, liabilities, currency and the share denominator determine exposure? | Assuming a physical asset's return equals its security's return |
| Related service/investment interest | What is attributable income versus distributable cash from a joint venture or other investment? | Counting both underlying earnings and dividends as incremental profit |

An issuer may occupy several roles. The intended graph should preserve this overlap while preventing the same physical material, cash flow or earnings claim from being counted twice. A relationship to the nuclear-power theme can provide context without implying identity with a uranium-miner basket. No constituent or live weight is changed by this research.

## 3. Ten research dossiers

### UF-C01 — U.S. procurement data: market need, contracts and physical shortage are different

EIA's 2025 survey distinguishes price-covered deliveries from all deliveries. Table 7 totals 46.852 million pounds of uranium equivalent with reported prices; 40.854 million were under long-term contracts. The all-material spot average is $75.83/lb, whereas $76.01 applies to the concentrate-only row. Service charges are excluded from the reported uranium-component prices. [F01–F02]

The derived long-term share is **87.20% of price-covered quantities**, not a global market-share estimate. The row distinction explains why selecting a spot figure from surrounding prose without its material scope would produce an incompatible comparison. A rounded share does not justify filling suppressed cells or extending a U.S. survey to all world demand.

At end-2025, EIA's 2026–2035 contracted-delivery range was 132.508–174.095 million pounds equivalent. Its unfilled-requirements table provides two publication vintages with different endpoints: 184.211 million through 2034 in the older vintage and 186.278 million through 2035 in the newer one. [F03–F04]

**Original comparison:** the headline total rises 1.12%, but removing the old 2025 amount of 1.924 million and the new 2035 amount of 36.429 million gives a common 2026–2034 window of approximately **182.287 million versus 149.849 million**, down **17.80%**. Independent rounding can affect the last displayed thousand pounds. This is a comparison of reported requirements at two observation dates, not proof that the difference was entirely contracted or that reactor consumption fell.

The range between minimum and maximum contracted quantities is **41.587 million pounds**. It should not be treated as additional demand on top of maximum quantities, or as guaranteed deliveries at the minimum-plus-maximum sum. Unfilled procurement requirements are not a measured geological or processing deficit. A physical balance requires compatible production, inventory, demand and service-availability evidence.

**Product implication:** show the procurement calendar, the contract range and the observation vintage before drawing a market conclusion. A bullish contracting thesis should identify which delivery years remain exposed and which producers can deliver into them. The discriminating follow-up is a change in comparable-year coverage and achievable supply, not a larger rolling-ten-year headline alone.

### UF-C02 — Cameco: a higher spot price can reduce current-year cash flow

Cameco's June 30 portfolio sensitivity models a US$5/lb spot increase as **C$14 million more revenue, C$2 million less adjusted net earnings and C$12 million less cash flow**. The explanation includes sales ceilings and purchase exposure, with associated Inkai dividends crossing into 2027. The opposite spot shock is not symmetric. Its published constant-portfolio price table also shows materially different 2026 and 2030 responses. [F05, printed pp. 22–23]

This is not a bearish price forecast or a claim about total investment value. It is evidence that the direction of near-term cash exposure can differ from the direction of sales exposure. The issuer scenario conditions, portfolio date and reporting currency must remain attached.

Using four cells from the same published table, an assumed spot move from US$80 to US$100 corresponds to modeled realized prices of **US$66 to US$67 in 2026**, versus **US$76 to US$88 in 2030**. The illustrative finite response ratios are **0.05 and 0.60**. Because the published cells are rounded and the portfolio is held constant, these are not precise derivatives, estimated stock betas or forecasts of the contracts the company will actually hold in 2030.

**Proposed valuation treatment:** separate the current delivery book, the expected reset of contracted prices, uncovered future sales and any offsetting procurement obligation. A present negative cash effect should not automatically be applied to all later years; equally, a favorable distant sensitivity must not be used to fund current capital commitments.

Cameco also reports uranium, fuel services and a Westinghouse investment contribution separately. The Q2 comparison contains a prior-year contractual milestone in Westinghouse, reinforcing the need to distinguish a period comparison from a normalized earnings estimate. [F06]

**Discriminating questions:** has the portfolio changed, which purchases reprice first, what portion of a joint-venture benefit becomes distributable cash, and what capital remains outside consolidated capital expenditure? The investment hypothesis concerns the timing and durability of retained economics—not the direction of the uranium chart alone.

### UF-C03 — Kazatomprom: consolidated growth need not be attributable growth

The issuer's August 21 H1 release reports consolidated revenue of **KZT717,834 million versus KZT660,167 million**, while attributable EBITDA is **KZT264,840 million versus KZT302,408 million**. Operating cash flow, including income tax and interest paid, is **KZT239,590 million versus KZT532,870 million**. Its attributable measure has an explicit joint-venture, minority-interest and unrealized-gain adjustment definition. [F07]

Our calculations give revenue growth of **8.74%**, attributable EBITDA decline of **12.42%**, and operating-cash-flow decline of **55.04%**. These comparisons preserve the issuer's separate definitions; they do not provide a complete explanation of the differences. Multiplying consolidated revenue by one headline ownership fraction would not reconstruct that attributable measure.

**Proposed analytical use:** when a company looks like a direct way to express commodity exposure, examine the actual ownership and realization structure before assigning a weight. Attributable operating performance, parent-level cash and the shareholder distribution decision are related but not identical.

For a scenario, independently vary realized price, input cost, currency and the timing of partner cash. Do not infer that every unfavorable earnings change indicates weaker physical demand. The next source needed for a strong company thesis is the detailed operating and cash bridge, including working capital and the relevant joint ventures. That bridge is not completed by the percentage calculations here.

The same release discusses a future acid-plant schedule change and says its effect on mining operations must be evaluated against other supply sources. [F07] The proposed bottleneck model should therefore distinguish an identified project delay from an already-realized production curtailment. A specific input dependency can be important without justifying a blanket country or company risk label.

### UF-C04 — Centrus: headline backlog, definitive contracts and current revenue have different scopes

Centrus's Q2 release reports a **US$4.5 billion** backlog: **US$3.7 billion** in its LEU segment, including **US$3.0 billion** contingent on new capacity; **US$2.4 billion** of those contingent commitments are definitive agreements. The remaining **US$0.8 billion** Technical Solutions category includes unfunded or unexercised-option elements. In the quarter, LEU-segment revenue rose, but SWU-service revenue fell by US$25.7 million while uranium sales contributed US$53.4 million. [F08]

Two-thirds of the headline backlog is therefore in the specified contingent category. The **80% definitive share of that contingent category** does not make it unconditional. Subtracting the conditional component does not prove that every remaining dollar is guaranteed, profitable or collectible. These are nested classifications; US$2.4 billion must not be added again to US$3.0 billion or US$4.5 billion.

The revenue mix is equally important. A growth figure at the segment level should not be reused as growth of the enrichment-service business when an added material-sales component changes the denominator.

**Product implication:** describe the backlog as economic commitments with conditions, not a single confidence score. The research needs the commercial terms, capacity dependency, delivery windows, cancellation or option provisions where disclosed, payment schedule and investment burden. Unknown terms remain unknown; no arbitrary probability haircut is a substitute for recovering them.

The issuer's July 1 announcement describes a new US$900 million capacity-expansion award contract. [F09] That is a distinct contract from options associated with an older operating program. The proposed system should bind funding events to the exact contract, rather than treating the exercise or non-exercise of one instrument as a verdict on every program associated with the company.

### UF-C05 — New customer commitments and new capital must update the same dated model

On September 9 and 17, Centrus and counterparties announced definitive multi-year supply contracts with Radiant and Antares, respectively. Both include prepayments supporting capacity expansion and prospective deliveries before the end of the decade. Neither announcement supplies enough commercial detail to calculate the actual profit contribution or a revised dollar backlog. [F10–F11]

These are positive evidence updates, not reasons to keep repeating a June-only statement that demand is entirely hypothetical. They also do not prove completed capacity, collected full proceeds or an achieved operating margin. The proposed evidence history must advance the exact customer relationship while preserving the remaining conditions.

Centrus's September 9 financing announcement prices **500,000 common shares at US$199.64** with accompanying warrants, and **2,005,513 pre-funded warrants at US$199.54**, with a further **US$0.10** exercise price. It also includes common warrants covering up to **6,992,382 shares**. [F12] The September 11 Form 8-K describes issued/sold warrants and the warrant-agent agreement, including a possible cashless-exercise election. [F13]

The derived upfront gross consideration is **US$500,000,064.02**, before costs. Remaining pre-funded exercise payments total **US$200,551.30**. Treating the latter as the total economic consideration would be wrong. Conversely, ordinary-warrant exercise proceeds are not guaranteed additional funding; cashless exercise terms matter.

The common shares plus pre-funded instruments correspond to **2,505,513 potential/as-exercised share equivalents** for an explicit scenario. That is not a claim about actual legal shares outstanding, a fully diluted GAAP EPS denominator or the current common-warrant exercise state. Different questions require different denominators.

**Proposed valuation bridge:** show how commercial commitments reduce one uncertainty, how financing addresses part of the capital requirement, and how the instruments allocate resulting value. Do not credit the gross financing as operating profit or count it twice in an already-funded equity valuation. A company can improve its ability to execute while sharing more of the eventual upside with new capital providers.

### UF-C06 — Established service providers: delivery schedules and reinvestment still matter

Urenco's H1 release reports its order book increasing from **€21.3 billion to €27.3 billion**, while period revenue fell from **€830.4 million to €645.4 million**. EBITDA rose from **€171.0 million to €221.8 million**. Cash generated from operating activities was **€220.4 million**; the capital-expenditure definition includes **€259.2 million** of cash investment less a **€7.8 million** capital-accrual reduction. [F14]

The derived order-book increase of **28.17%** and revenue decline of **22.28%** are not contradictions: they concern different time structures. Nor does a larger order book establish a proportionate increase in present free cash. A clearly labeled operating-cash-less-cash-investment calculation is **negative €38.8 million**. Using the €251.4 million accrual-adjusted capital figure instead would mix bases; neither calculation is a normalized long-term valuation by itself.

Orano's H1 announcement similarly separates reported and adjusted earnings: reported parent-attributable net income increased from **€109 million to €173 million**, while adjusted income moved from **positive €25 million to negative €39 million**. The company attributes part of the reported improvement to returns on earmarked assets, and describes operating disruptions in other measures. [F15]

**Proposed synthesis:** a fuel-service comparison should track contract maturity, delivery realization, reinvestment and the availability of cash—not simply classify the company as a protected beneficiary of nuclear demand. Service and integrated-company examples are also useful economic comparators even when no suitable listed common-equity instrument has been resolved. No proxy ticker is invented for them here.

The positive research hypothesis is that durable customer relationships and favorable future realizations can support earnings as contracted work is delivered. Its counter-hypothesis is that investment, working capital, input procurement or non-operating contributions account for more of the apparent improvement than the headline suggests. The discriminating data is a compatible cash and margin bridge by activity and period.

### UF-C07 — Denison: investment inventory can fund development without becoming recurring mine cash

Denison reports selling **750,000 pounds** of physical investment uranium in Q2 at an average **C$122.16/lb**. Its original 2021 purchase basis was **C$36.67/lb**. It separately held **950,000 pounds** of investment uranium and **145,926 pounds** of produced uranium, with another **600,000 pounds** contracted for future deliveries. Phoenix was under construction, while the issuer also had other operating and service interests. [F16]

Calculated sale proceeds are **C$91.620 million** and historical acquisition cost for the sold quantity is **C$27.5025 million**, giving a historical-price difference of **C$64.1175 million**. That is not the quarter's IFRS profit: prior fair-value recognition and other accounting effects must not be ignored.

The inventory sale can have a real financing role. The analytical mistake would be to annualize its cash proceeds as a steady operating run rate, or to add the original inventory value again after the proceeds are used to develop the project. Future committed deliveries are not already shipped material. Investment stock and produced inventory remain separate categories even where a total-pound check is useful.

**Proposed developer assessment:** identify the sources of funds, the timing of remaining uses, any committed inventory disposition and the operation expected to replace finite funding sources with recurring cash. Evaluate project-specific milestones rather than assuming the whole issuer shares the maturity of its flagship project.

The investment hypothesis is execution-enabled future cash generation, not merely a larger mineral resource or a profitable sale of purchased inventory. It weakens if remaining capital requirements rise faster than realizable funding, if the schedule slips, or if expected future operating economics no longer support the investment. This pass does not produce a complete project valuation or funding adequacy verdict.

### UF-C08 — Yellow Cake: physical exposure still requires a per-share bridge

Yellow Cake's June update distinguishes **23,214,230 pounds held** from a **1,160,766-pound contracted purchase** expected later, which would bring holdings to **24,374,996 pounds**. Its estimated sterling NAV per share moved from **£6.33 to £6.34**, with uranium value, currency and buybacks contributing differently. The July pro-forma NAV includes the anticipated material and deducts the associated payable. [F17, pp. 1–2]

The sum of held and pending pounds is an arithmetic check, not permission to label all of them delivered at June 30. A pro-forma asset addition without the corresponding payable would overstate value. The fact that physical assets are stored at another company's facility does not transfer their economic ownership to that storage provider.

A deliberately hypothetical security example isolates another mechanism. If NAV grows 10%, but a share discount widens from 10% to 20%, the share-price change is **1.10 × 0.80 / 0.90 − 1 = negative 2.22%**, before distributions or fees. This is not Yellow Cake's actual return.

A separate hypothetical shows the capital-allocation effect: with net assets of 100 and ten shares, buying one share for eight leaves 92 assets and nine shares, or **10.222 per share** rather than ten. The calculation excludes costs and changes in asset values. Accretion does not imply unlimited buyback capacity or a guaranteed market-price response.

**Proposed product output:** separate commodity value, net cash/liabilities, unit or share count, currency, ongoing costs and discount/premium. A physical-holdings sleeve should not be evaluated using the same operating margin or capital-intensity lens as a mine. Whether it belongs in an investable basket is a separate accepted constituent-policy question.

### UF-C09 — Paladin: fulfilled sales can carry future material obligations

Paladin's June-quarter report describes delivery-support arrangements, including **400,000 pounds of outstanding product loans** at quarter-end with future repayment in kind. It also identifies **397,993 pounds** associated with a sale to be recognized in the following quarter for which an advance payment was already received. Its production-cost and capital-expenditure definitions exclude certain low-grade stockpiling and stripping expenditures. [F18]

These observations change the quality of an inventory or cash interpretation. Borrowed material is not cost-free incremental production; an advance can improve cash before revenue recognition while leaving a delivery obligation. Sales completion in one period can depend on logistics and repayment in another.

Paladin's annual summary reports an average realization of **US$70.0/lb**, production cost of **US$43.3/lb**, **4.35 million pounds sold**, and **US$52.2 million gross profit**. [F19] Multiplying the simple price-minus-production-cost spread by sales gives **US$116.145 million**, not reported gross profit. It is an incompatible-input demonstration, not an alternative earnings estimate.

The full cost bridge remains unverified. In particular, the short annual summary's reported revenue less cost of sales does not exactly reproduce its gross-profit line. We do not force that reconciliation or claim issuer error from a summary. The original annual PDF route failed; secondary extracted explanations were not promoted into admitted evidence.

**Proposed assessment:** evaluate operating progress alongside the obligation to deliver, repay borrowed product, fund capitalized mining work and replace inventory. A low reported production-cost number is not a complete measure of cash needed to sustain the business. The proposed model should allow a positive operating development without overstating immediate distributable cash.

### UF-C10 — Shared infrastructure and multiple claims: one physical system, several economic exposures

Cameco's disclosure identifies its purchase/production and investment relationships; Denison distinguishes produced inventory, development and service interests; Yellow Cake identifies third-party storage of its own material. These sources support different types of connection rather than one universal ownership relationship. [F06, F16–F17]

**Proposed graph interpretation:** connect an issuer to an asset or counterparty with a typed economic role. Keep the material's owner, the custodian, the service provider, the borrower and the future buyer distinct. A supply-chain visualization should reveal concentration or dependence without aggregating all connected material into each participant's asset base.

This matters for cross-theme analysis. A data-center power thesis and a nuclear theme can reference the same downstream demand evidence, but that does not create two independent units of upstream demand. A proposed nuclear project may support a long-horizon commercial scenario without becoming current mine demand or a booked service order.

The machine's job is to retain those distinctions; the intelligence job is to decide which connection materially affects a user's thesis. Provenance alone does not complete the task. A user should receive an explanation of the relevant exposure and the strongest contrary mechanism, not a list of every possible connected company.

At issuer level, display a coverage remainder. Do not force several overlapping lenses to add to 100%. Before using these views for investment analysis, evaluate whether two securities represent the same economic issuer and whether a diversified company's other operations dominate its valuation. ## 5. Valuation and rerating research: five mechanisms to distinguish

### 5.1 Contract reset versus spot appreciation

A favorable current price may have little near-term effect if selling terms are fixed or capped, while future contracts could have a different sensitivity. The proposed valuation asks when cash realization changes and what purchases or obligations offset it. The strongest counterargument is that the favorable reset is already reflected in expectations or is absorbed by costs and investment. Evidence required: dated contract/sensitivity changes, compatible forward assumptions and an acquired expectations benchmark.

The proposed output is a horizon-specific scenario, not a prediction of the next share-price move. Without a historical expected-value baseline, call it a revised economic scenario—not an identified mispricing. This preserves the distinction between sector enthusiasm and an investable surprise.

### 5.2 Capacity commercialisation versus announced demand

A customer commitment can improve financing feasibility, but the same future cash flow must still support construction, input procurement and shareholder returns. Separate the decision to sign, the cash received, the remaining conditions, the capital spent and the commercial service delivered. A prepayment can reduce external funding need while increasing the obligation to perform; it is not free capital without a liability or future cost.

The proposed research comparison should test whether the expected incremental margin and duration compensate for required investment. It should not presume a higher multiple merely because backlog increased. Reduced uncertainty can be economically valuable, but its value depends on the starting expectation and the amount of capital needed to resolve it.

### 5.3 Operational recovery versus sustainable cash conversion

Better output can lower unit costs or increase sales. It can also consume working capital, require greater mining preparation, or depend on temporary inventories and delivery arrangements. The proposed model separates the operating improvement from capitalized costs and timing effects rather than mechanically annualizing a strong quarter.

The research falsifier is evidence that the improvement disappears when replacement inputs, necessary sustaining work and ordinary delivery schedules are restored. Conversely, demonstrated sustained output with funded reinvestment would strengthen the operating thesis. Neither conclusion follows solely from a production beat.

### 5.4 Project de-risking versus common-equity value

A project can become more feasible while the ownership claims on its economics change. Debt, equity, pre-funded instruments, other warrants, minority interests and contingent cash obligations require explicit treatment. Do not assume that all potential exercise proceeds will be received or that an as-exercised share count is appropriate for every valuation purpose.

The proposed bridge compares like-for-like funded equity scenarios. Cash already included in the estimated equity value must not be added again. Funding that reduces failure risk can be valuable even when it is dilutive; the research question is the change in common-equity value after both effects, not whether dilution alone is good or bad.

### 5.5 Physical value versus security pricing

A physical holding offers a different path from operational leverage. Its per-share economic exposure can depend on currency, expenses, liabilities, pending purchases and capital allocation. The market price can also trade at a changing discount or premium. A commodity-price view therefore needs a security-level bridge before it becomes an expected investment outcome.

The proposed research should not rank a mine and a holding vehicle on one earnings multiple. It should explain the different sources of risk and return, then leave portfolio selection to its accepted owner and validation process. No strategy, entry, position size or trade authority is supplied by this report.

## 6. Four discriminating scenarios for the eventual dossier

These are original analytical scenarios to test a future product, not forecasts with assigned probabilities.

**Scenario A — Spot rises, near-term portfolio cash weakens.** Start with a dated issuer sensitivity. Separate the selling book from purchases and partner cash. Show the reported current-year sign and the later-year range without averaging them into a universal bullish score. The user must be able to tell whether a cash shortfall is a timing effect, a permanent margin loss or an unresolved mixture. Do not invent the following year's dividend amount.

**Scenario B — Contract coverage improves while a rolling headline rises.** Compare the same delivery years across observations. Explain the added terminal year, changes in anticipated requirements and minimum/maximum contract quantities. The user must see whether the evidence concerns procurement coverage, physical supply or both. Do not label unfilled requirements an observed deficit or suppressed supplier cells zero.

**Scenario C — A new customer contract and capital raise occur after the quarter.** Preserve the original quarterly snapshot and add each later event at its own date. Advance the customer relationship where supported. Do not fabricate a dollar backlog contribution when undisclosed. Update the security-instrument scenario with the financing terms without claiming that expected gross proceeds equal a reconciled bank balance or immediately available project funding.

**Scenario D — Producer cash improves through advances or inventory disposal.** Separate operating activity, investment-asset liquidation, financing and future performance obligations. Test whether the apparent cash improvement is repeatable. Show the remaining project or delivery obligation and the evidence required for it to become recurring economic value.

Each scenario should end with an actionable research answer: **what changed, who is exposed, when the economics may arrive, what remains unknown, and which next observation would change the interpretation**. This is a more useful output than a provenance panel or a list of potential beneficiaries.

## 7. Data and maintenance design: extend incumbent owners

The following are requirements for later architecture review, not an instruction to create a new contract database or queue.

**Observation scope.** A financial or operating assertion needs its subject, period, currency, scale, reporting basis and source date. A sensitivity also needs its base scenario, shock, horizon and fixed assumptions. A delivered quantity needs to identify whether it is mined, purchased, borrowed, swapped or drawn from investment inventory where the source permits that distinction. A null category must remain visible.

**Contract scope.** Where publicly supported, preserve product/service, counterparties, quantity range, delivery period, relevant price mechanism, option or condition, prepayment and outstanding obligations. Definitive execution and satisfaction of conditions are separate facts. Do not reverse-engineer confidential terms from aggregate revenue or publish restricted contracts into a public artifact.

**Capital scope.** Separate authorized financing, signed terms, reported issued instruments, gross versus net proceeds, residual exercise consideration, possible cashless exercise and legal versus illustrative share bases. Binding these to the existing issuer/security owner is essential; an analytical worksheet cannot become a second security master.

**Data cadence.** EIA annual releases and revision notices support structural U.S. procurement context. Issuer quarterly/annual filings support recurring operating and cash comparisons. Event disclosures update material contracts, financing and commercial milestones between reporting dates. Any automated update must use existing schedulers/evidence owners; no watcher or pipeline was armed here.

**Historical use.** A source bearing an old publication date and retrieved today is not proof that Mastermind held it then. Backtesting or historical expectation comparisons require the accepted contemporaneous retention contract. Keep source publication, observation period, effective event and native ingestion time distinct. A current reconstruction should say so.

**Rights and access.** This package contains original editorial synthesis and limited attributed facts, not third-party full reports, images, vendor price series or confidential agreements. Vendor values appearing in issuer tables remain source-scoped observations; their appearance does not grant bulk redistribution rights. Dataset-specific display and retention decisions are still owed before live ingestion.

**Conflict handling.** Resolve a difference at the smallest useful scope. EIA's material-specific versus aggregate spot rows are not interchangeable. A page link resolving to a different reporting period does not become the requested period because of its anchor label. Preserve an unclosed accounting bridge rather than force numbers to agree. If a later valid source changes one customer milestone, update that relationship without invalidating unrelated settled research.
