## 1. What this pass resolves

The preceding research separated physical supply, contractual entitlement, customer qualification and common-equity outcomes. This pass adds a necessary counterpart: **the customer's economic ability and reason to purchase the product**. The proposed intelligence model must connect buyer value to producer margin, investment requirements and the expectation being tested.

The evidence below tests eleven connected questions, spanning iron-ore benchmarks, delivered prices, product and capital mix, steelmaking inputs, fertilizer manufacturing, buyer credit, nutrient measures and expectation changes. It is not a global producer census or a recommendation to buy or sell any security. Corporate explanations remain attributed claims rather than independently audited causal conclusions.

The principal synthesis is a proposed analytical sequence:

**Demand purpose → usable product specification → buyer value and ability to pay → delivered price → producer's retained contribution → sustaining and growth capital → funded per-share outcome → change versus a dated expectation.**

This is not a second economic graph or a new scoring engine. It is a set of questions and evidence requirements to extend the existing Themes/GMI product. A system that correctly identifies a scarce input can still mislead an investor if the user of that input cannot pass through its cost, obtain credit, or operate profitably. Equally, a producer can capture more cash while a superficial benchmark-realization percentage deteriorates.

The prospective user should be able to determine which link improved, which link remains unproven, and which next observation could falsify the thesis. A price chart or a source-rich supply-chain visualization is insufficient without that explanation.

## 2. Eleven source-grounded dossiers

### D01 — An unchanged benchmark symbol need not measure unchanged material

**Evidence.** Platts changed IODEX's reference from 62% to 61% Fe effective January 2, 2026, also changing several impurity specifications while retaining its symbol. Its December pellet clarification retained a 62% contractual basis pending observed migration. The January pellet notice has a 61% headline but also retains that conditional 62% language. It separately introduces a 65%-basis premium and discontinues an outright calculation. [S01–S03]

**Interpretation.** The safe conclusion is not that every iron-ore assessment changed in the same way on January 2. The physical specification, assessment formula and contractual settlement basis must each be established. The pellet basis remains conditional in these sources; this research does not resolve a particular contract's migration date.

**Proposed product behavior.** Keep the publisher's original series and its effective specification versions. A change detector should distinguish a price movement from a reference-definition change. Where economic normalization requires an unavailable quality adjustment, show a break or an explicit incompatible comparison rather than creating an artificial smooth history.

Multiplying an old price by 61/62 is not an adequate default transformation: it assumes away changes in impurities, market valuation and contract basis. The presence of the same symbol, currency and tonne unit does not prove compatibility. Conversely, the right response is not to discard all history. Preserve both observations, their definitions and any later accepted bridge so a user can see what is known.

### D02 — Vale: a realized price and a cash break-even can have different boundaries

**Evidence.** Vale's Q2 bridge goes from a US$106.0/dry-tonne CFR reference to US$95.0/wet-tonne realization after a US$2.5 FOB-sales adjustment and US$8.5 moisture adjustment. Its separate landed-China break-even is US$61.6/dry tonne before sustaining investment and US$70.1 including it; quality and pellet contributions already reduce that cost measure. Prior benchmark comparisons are restated to 61% Fe. Decisive PDF pages 10–11 were visually checked. [S04]

**Interpretation.** The seemingly simple subtraction of 95.0 minus 61.6 crosses quantity and delivery boundaries. The resulting number should not be labeled cash margin. It also misses sustaining investment. Adding the quality benefit again to the already-adjusted break-even would count the same economic contribution twice.

**Proposed product behavior.** Support two reconciled views when the evidence permits: an actual-sales netback, and an issuer-defined benchmark-equivalent break-even. Explain the conversion between them instead of placing unrelated headline measures beside each other. A comparison needs the product mix, delivery convention, pricing period and moisture basis, not just currency and commodity name.

The buyer-facing interpretation should remain separate from the producer's netback. Freight may be economically important without being paid or recognized identically across all sales. The dossier should expose the actual incidence supported by the contract or report, rather than assigning every dollar of a route freight change to every tonne.

### D03 — Fortescue: volume, valuation and operating-cost bases must remain separate

**Evidence.** Fortescue's FY2026 release reports hematite realization of US$90.7/dry tonne and hematite C1 cost of US$18.74/wet tonne. It also identifies a US$525 million Iron Bridge impairment, while FY2027 shipment guidance includes 11–14 million tonnes from Iron Bridge on a 100% basis. The original issuer release was accessed through a filing mirror and its key financial page was visually checked. [S05]

**Interpretation.** A shipment outlook is not proof that prior invested capital earns the originally expected return. An impairment is not evidence that every future tonne is uneconomic either. Current operating contribution, remaining capital, carrying value and lifetime investment performance are separate questions.

**Proposed product behavior.** Keep hematite and magnetite/project observations distinct, including their ownership and measure bases. Do not apply a hematite realization to every group shipment. An unknown moisture factor must remain unknown; a benchmark's standard moisture is not automatically a mine's actual moisture.

The illustrative conversion later in this report deliberately assumes 8% moisture only to test arithmetic. Even a correct unit conversion does not transform C1 into a complete margin because other delivery, corporate, tax and investment terms may be absent. The useful research target is the full economic bridge, not an attractive-looking subtraction.

### D04 — BHP: replacement capacity is not incremental system growth

**Evidence.** BHP describes Ministers North's approximately 20 Mtpa as supporting sustained WAIO output above 305 Mtpa, not as an automatic addition to that system level. Its FY2026 WAIO unit cost is US$19.66/t, versus C1 of US$16.40/t; the latter excludes additional items. FY2027 unit-cost guidance also uses a different exchange-rate assumption. The relevant report page was visually checked. [S06]

**Interpretation.** The relevant counterfactual for a replacement mine can be falling output without the investment, rather than growth above today's production. This does not make replacement investment unimportant. It means the value lies in maintaining future output, avoiding deterioration, or improving economics while replacing depleted capacity.

**Proposed product behavior.** The project portfolio should identify whether a project adds capacity, replaces depletion, removes a system constraint, or maintains reliability. Its contribution to a consolidated supply forecast must be net of displaced or retiring sources and bounded by shared processing and logistics capacity.

A flat production forecast can therefore hide significant capital demand. The user should see both the physical counterfactual and the cash required to avoid it. A growth screen that rewards nominal new-mine capacity but ignores depletion is not an adequate investment screen.

### D05 — Jansen: high operating margin and attractive invested-capital return are different claims

**Evidence.** At its 2023 sanction, Jansen Stage 2 had US$4.9 billion of investment, a 15–18% modeled post-tax nominal return and FY2029 first-production expectations. The June 2026 update moves investment to US$6.9 billion and first output to late FY2031, followed by a two-year ramp; its modeled return is 11% while projected EBITDA margin remains above 65%. The price assumptions and their time/base conventions also change. Updated operating costs are stated in July 2026 real terms, with explicit exclusions. [S07–S08]

**Interpretation.** The capital estimate rises 40.82%, but it would be wrong to attribute the entire return change to that one variable. Schedule, ramp and price assumptions are also different. Nor should differently dated real-price assumptions be compared as if they were the same nominal series.

**Proposed product behavior.** Compare a project as a versioned bundle: scope, investment included, production/ramp profile, commodity assumptions, cost basis, financing and valuation conventions. Show the changes separately. A controlled counterfactual requires a model holding other inputs constant; these two issuer announcements do not supply one.

Distinguish the original investment decision from the decision to spend remaining capital. Sunk expenditure belongs in the historical investment-return assessment but is not paid again in a remaining-investment calculation. Both views matter to a shareholder, and neither should silently replace the other. An accounting write-down is not a fresh cash payment; a later accounting return can also change because its capital denominator changed.

### D06 — Rio Tinto: an economic improvement need not be an immediately distributable cash improvement

**Evidence.** Rio's H1 report distinguishes US$870 million of banked productivity benefits, US$1.3 billion annualized run-rate and a US$1.8 billion year-end target. It revises free-cash-flow definition and restates comparatives. The H1 group reconciliation is 9,173 − 5,037 − 302 = US$3,834 million. Capital contributions can be recognized when due, not only when received. Its market comparison still pairs 2026's 61%-Fe index with 2025's 62%-Fe index. [S09]

**Interpretation.** This supplies a contrast to Vale's restated benchmark history: the reader must inspect the comparator in each disclosure. Neither an issuer's percentage-change column nor a consistent series label guarantees a like-for-like physical comparison.

Banked benefits, run-rate and target describe different time concepts and must not be added. An economic-share cash measure may answer a useful ownership question while still differing from cash available on a bank statement. The proposed dossier should respect the disclosed definition and explain funding timing rather than alleging an accounting error because a simpler calculation differs.

**Proposed product behavior.** Separate realized productivity from forward annualization, gross investment from economic-share investment, and contributions due from cash received. Preserve the source's old and new definitions, with an explicit restatement flag. The PDF screenshot route failed, but the relevant figures and definitions were independently recovered in the issuer's SEC HTML exhibit; no unseen PDF table is the sole support for these claims.

### D07 — Warrior: a weaker benchmark-realization percentage can coexist with stronger unit cash contribution

**Evidence.** Warrior's Q2 release reports gross realization against its reference falling from approximately 80% to 66%, while net selling price rises from US$130.01 to US$137.82 per short ton and cash cost falls from US$101.17 to US$92.53. It attributes the mix/freight effect and the cost improvement to different drivers, including Blue Creek and a tax-credit benefit. Its cash table reconciles 132.277 − 28.923 = US$103.354 million free cash flow; the narrative US$18.3 million investing number is net investing cash, not gross capital purchases. [S10]

**Interpretation.** The reported unit cash contribution rises from 28.84 to 45.29, or 57.04%, even as the headline realization fraction declines. It is not sufficient to characterize this as deteriorating product competitiveness. Product mix, benchmark relevance, freight and operating costs need separate explanations.

The cash-flow table resolves another possible extraction mistake. Net investing cash includes other flows; treating it as gross capital spending overstates the simple operating-cash-minus-investment result. No issuer arithmetic error is inferred. The tax-related contribution should also not be silently relabeled engineering productivity or presumed permanent without its own evidence.

**Proposed product behavior.** Pair product-specific benchmark relevance with actual unit contribution, completed capital spending and future sustaining needs. Recognize a new asset's positive operating contribution when supported, but do not assume that all observed cash improvement is repeatable each quarter. A strong business result still requires a dated expectations comparison before it becomes a surprise or mispricing claim.

### D08 — Nutrien: fertilizer selling prices and fertilizer-manufacturer earnings can move in opposite directions

**Evidence.** Nutrien's Q2 phosphate net selling price rises from US$714 to US$781/t, while adjusted EBITDA falls from US$92 million to US$23 million. The company identifies higher sulfur costs as a major pressure. Potash has a different result: net price rises from US$248 to US$267/t and reported gross margin per tonne from US$138 to US$154. Its sub-US$60 controllable potash manufacturing cash-cost target is not reported cost of goods sold. [S11]

**Interpretation.** A sector-level fertilizer price signal can conflate businesses with different input exposures. The phosphate price increases 9.38% in this comparison, while EBITDA falls 75%; this is a useful falsifier of a universal positive-price-to-profit rule, not an estimate of permanent earnings sensitivity.

**Proposed product behavior.** Separate the mineral owner, chemical processor, compound-product manufacturer and distributor. Where ownership is integrated, reconcile the relevant internal transfers rather than adding independent segment margins. The model should ask whether a selling-price increase exceeds the combined movement in inputs, fixed-cost absorption, product mix and investment needs.

A sulfur-related thesis should identify the exact material form, location, delivery terms and counterparty. A positive sulfur-output example elsewhere in the Mining corpus does not establish that every phosphate producer buys that same product in that same market. Shared thematic exposure is an investigation lead, not a proven transmission coefficient.

### D09 — Mosaic: product scarcity, input availability and customer credit are distinct constraints

**Evidence.** Mosaic's Q2 North American consumed sulfur cost is US$522/long ton versus US$209, while ammonia is reported per metric tonne. Its phosphate gross margin becomes a US$4.5 million loss. In Brazil, Fertilizantes finished-product prices rise to US$585/t from US$474 while finished-product volumes fall 32%. The issuer cites both constrained raw-material supply and limited customer credit, alongside curtailments. [S12]

**Interpretation.** The observations do not identify one clean demand elasticity. A manufacturer may be unable to make product, a customer may lack credit, and another customer may rationally defer purchase. Those mechanisms can coexist and require different evidence.

**Proposed product behavior.** Maintain separate physical-input availability, producer contribution, customer affordability, credit availability and delivery-timing assessments. Do not infer a supply shortfall simply from lower sales, or excess supply from lower customer orders. A units check must prevent subtraction of a sulfur quote per long ton from another input quote per metric tonne without an explicit conversion and recipe basis.

The prospective research task is to establish which constraint is binding for a specified product/region/period. If raw-material supply improves but buyer credit remains limited, a volume recovery may lag. If credit recovers but the producer still lacks an input, stronger orders need not become shipments. This conditional explanation is more useful than a single bottleneck badge.

### D10 — K+S: guidance changes and customer-input relationships are not one-directional

**Evidence.** K+S raises its 2026 EBITDA range from EUR630–730 million to EUR680–760 million. Its report quotes July 16 full-year consensus of EUR718 million. The report's upper and lower scenarios distinguish manageable phosphate scarcity from scarcity severe enough to restrict compound-fertilizer production and potash demand. Agriculture volume and average-price definitions exclude trade goods in some views. The cover and scenario pages were visually checked. [S13–S14]

**Interpretation.** The guidance midpoint rises from 680 to 720, or 5.88%, but the new midpoint is only 0.28% above the quoted expectation. That is a reconstructed arithmetic comparison, not a claim about all market participants, a captured pre-event estimate, or the share reaction.

The compound-product discussion is also a useful condition test. The relation between inputs may change sign depending on which constraint binds. A formulation adjustment can be a possibility while a severe shortage of a complementary input prevents the final product from being made. This research does not prescribe substitutions or infer actual reformulation volumes.

**Proposed product behavior.** Retain the condition and scope of a dependency, including trade-goods exclusions, rather than assigning a permanent positive or negative edge. Preserve a visible source conflict: the scenario page's opening sentence says 2025, while its heading context, following assumptions, cover and separate release identify 2026. Use the corroborated 2026 guidance with that transcription discrepancy noted, not a silent corrected quote.

### D11 — Nutrient quantity and the buyer's calendar change the economic comparison

**Evidence.** University of Minnesota Extension distinguishes total, available and water-soluble phosphorus measurements, with different denominators, and discusses MAP/DAP product labels. The August 11 University of Illinois farmdoc analysis discusses fall 2026 purchasing/application decisions for the 2027 crop, including possible timing changes. These are scoped agronomic/economic sources, not a global forecast of realized purchasing. [S15–S16]

**Interpretation.** A tonne of product, a tonne of nutrient equivalent and a hectare's economically useful application are not interchangeable demand measures. Valuing the nitrogen and phosphate content independently without accounting for the buyer's actual needs can double-count usefulness. A cheaper cost per unit of one nutrient does not establish the best total application economics.

**Proposed product behavior.** Label product assay, available nutrient measure, co-nutrient assumptions, purchase date, application date, crop year and credit/payment terms. Regional evidence must remain regional. Do not assume a fall purchasing decline is permanent lost demand, or that a later application plan guarantees a later order.

The intended investor output is an explanation of purchase incentives and timing, not agronomic advice. Actual application decisions require field-specific evidence outside this research. The hypothetical examples below isolate measurement and funding effects without proposing a fertilizer program.

## 3. The proposed economic model: two-sided value and a capital hurdle

### Buyer value and seller contribution

For a specific product and buyer use, ask what changes relative to the feasible alternative. A proposed buyer-value bridge includes delivery cost, usable yield, processing expense, reliability, capacity use and any separately evidenced attributes the buyer actually values. This is a research checklist, not permission to sum every claimed benefit without testing overlap.

The producer's incremental bridge starts elsewhere: net realization minus the incremental mining, processing, transport, qualification and capital burdens of serving that demand. Price premium is revenue, not retained profit. The feasible premium range is bounded by both sides only after their quantities and baselines match.

**H01 — Hypothetical quality-premium feasibility.** Assume a specified premium product saves a buyer $12 per comparable delivered unit, while producing it costs the supplier $17 more than the feasible alternative. No premium can simultaneously leave both parties a non-negative incremental benefit under those assumptions. A $10 premium gives the buyer $2 but costs the supplier $7. If an accepted process improvement reduces incremental producer cost to $6, the same premium gives the supplier $4 and the buyer $2. These are invented values, not ironmaking estimates.

The moat is not merely knowing that a product is higher quality. It is knowing the demonstrated buyer use, the alternative it replaces, and how much value the producer retains after making it. The system should be able to expose why a technically appealing project lacks a feasible commercial spread, and recognize when a real cost or qualification change opens one.

### Delivered economics and unit compatibility

**H02 — Hypothetical moisture conversion using one reported input.** With explicitly assumed 8% moisture, a cost of $18.74 per wet metric tonne corresponds to $20.3696 per dry metric tonne: 18.74 / 0.92. The assumed moisture is not Fortescue's verified moisture. The result is not full delivered cost, cash margin or a valuation input. Without that assumption or an actual measurement, the conversion is unavailable.

A prospective transformation must preserve its input identity and basis. Do not apply dry-content conversion to a quote that is already dry, or change an entire company's sales mix because one product has a known assay. Freight, quality and tax adjustments are separate operations, each requiring its own supported meaning.

### Capital efficiency and the correct counterfactual

**H03 — Hypothetical operating margin versus investment value.** A ten-year project generates $100 of annual revenue and $70 of annual cash before any additional items. Assume for this teaching case that there are no taxes, later reinvestment, working-capital changes or terminal proceeds. It requires $500 upfront. A 70% operating cash margin and 7.1429-year simple payback coexist with a present value of only $430.1197 for those inflows at a 10% discount rate, giving NPV of negative $69.8803. The intentionally simplified assumptions isolate the distinction; this is not a mine valuation.

Existing production, incremental investment and original lifecycle returns need separate views. It can be rational to keep an already-built operation running with positive forward contribution even when the original investment earned an inadequate return. It does not follow that expanding the same operation is attractive. A low operating-cost position also does not reveal the price needed to justify a new entrant's capital investment.

The future cost-curve research should therefore distinguish operating cash cost, delivered contribution, sustaining break-even and a fully funded investment-hurdle price. These are proposed analytical views; a consistent global dataset has not been acquired. Avoid drawing a precise global curve from incompatible issuer definitions.

### Customer economics versus financing capacity

**H04 — Hypothetical affordability and credit.** An assumed crop output of 200 units at $4.50 generates $900 of gross receipts per area unit. A $210 nutrient bill represents 23.3333% of gross receipts. If output price falls 10% while the bill rises 15%, the bill is $241.50 against $810, or 29.8148%. This is not a complete farm-profit calculation or an estimate of demand elasticity. An upfront credit limit of $180 separately prevents the original $210 purchase even when an annual-profit model appears viable.

An investor needs to distinguish unfavorable expected economics from an inability to fund an otherwise desirable purchase. The timing of payment and crop realization belongs in the analysis. The same observed reduction in orders can lead to different later paths depending on which mechanism caused it.

### Product-tonne versus nutrient-value comparisons

**H05 — Hypothetical multi-nutrient comparison.** Define product A as 18% nitrogen and 46% available phosphate equivalent, priced at $600 per metric tonne; product B is 11% and 52%, priced at $650. On phosphate-only expenditure, A costs $1,304.3478 per tonne of phosphate equivalent and B $1,250. With an explicitly assumed usable nitrogen value of $0.50/kg, subtract $90 from A and $55 from B before dividing: A becomes $1,108.6957 and B $1,144.2308. The ordering reverses. These are stipulated compositions/prices and hypothetical credits, not observed quotes or recommendations.

A co-nutrient credit is valid in this illustration only because usefulness is stipulated. It cannot be inserted automatically when a farmer does not need the co-nutrient or when its benefit is already captured elsewhere. Nutrient equivalent does not mean elemental phosphorus, nor does it certify agronomic interchangeability.

## 4. Capital-cycle and rerating research views

The following are proposed ways to organize investigation, not validated predictive factors or new production baskets.

| Research view | What must be true for the thesis | Strong counterargument or falsifier |
|---|---|---|
| Quality-value capture | A verified buyer use supports a premium above incremental producer cost and investment | Premium reflects a benchmark change, product mix or a benefit the buyer cannot monetize |
| Delivered-cost resilience | A consistent netback remains resilient after actual freight, currency and operating costs | A mine-only cost excludes the growing burden or rests on stale delivery assumptions |
| Replacement investment | Spending avoids a supported depletion/reliability loss at an acceptable remaining cost | Claimed capacity duplicates existing output or depends on unavailable shared infrastructure |
| New-asset cash conversion | Saleable output and operating contribution emerge while development spending falls | Inventory release, credits or deferred sustaining work explain most of the improvement |
| Input-spread normalization | Output realization improves relative to the relevant consumed/replacement inputs | Input inflation, shortages or poor fixed-cost absorption continue to dominate |
| Buyer purchasing recovery | Customer economics, credit and timing all support realizable orders | Better crop prices do not resolve financing or product availability |
| Capital-supply response | Funded, scheduled, usable future output can enter the relevant market | Announcement, engineering completion or first production is mistaken for full sustained supply |
| Expectations revision | A result changes a matched, dated expectation and the future economic path | Guidance merely catches up with the benchmark, or the comparison changes definitions |

The machine should use these views to prioritize unanswered research questions. They must not automatically originate a position, set a weight or override accepted trading validation. A source-backed explanation of a mechanism is not evidence of statistically reliable excess returns.

For valuation planning, preserve at least the relevant period, price/cost conventions, capital scope, financing claims, share basis and required return. An issuer's IRR is a conditional model output; it should not be displayed as a certainty or mixed with a different nominal/real discount rate. A change in the investment case should be attributed to the changed assumption vector, not whichever headline was most visible.

### A candidate bulk/fertilizer classification map

These are research facets awaiting broader validation and native identity mapping. They are not new theme IDs or weighted constituent lists.

| Candidate facet | Physical/commercial distinctions to preserve | Financial mechanism to research |
|---|---|---|
| Direct-shipping iron ore | Assay, fines/lump, wet/dry, blending and delivery point | Benchmark-related realization less actual delivered cost and replacement spending |
| Concentrate and pellet products | Concentration/pelletizing role, usable specification, contract premium basis | Buyer processing value versus producer conversion and capital burden |

An integrated issuer may populate several facets, but the view must not sum the same underlying activity repeatedly. A selected facet should reveal the evidence and coverage of the mapping. A research tag does not prove material revenue exposure or identify the security through which that exposure should be priced.

## 5. Proposed user workflow: questions that must end in a useful answer

### P01 — “Is a higher iron-ore premium actually better economics?”

Start from the theme and select the product/asset dossier. Show the original benchmark observation, effective specification, issuer realization and any supported adjustments. The answer must state whether the change is a compatible premium movement, a product-mix change, a methodology effect, or unresolved. Where normalization is not supported, retain both facts and stop the derived claim, not the whole user workflow.

The next panel asks what the buyer gains and what the producer spends to deliver it. A positive premium with negative incremental contribution is different from a viable quality-upgrade thesis. Acceptance requires the user to find the missing evidence directly, not be confronted with a generic warning detached from the conclusion.

### P02 — “Does new capacity make this company a growth investment?”

Display the existing output baseline, depletion or displaced production, shared processing/logistics limits, first-output date, ramp and investment remaining. Present both the with-project and without-project explanation when supported. The output should distinguish maintained production, earlier cash, additional lifecycle volume and genuinely higher system capacity.

Then show the investment case's vintage and assumptions. A high modeled operating margin is not the answer to whether the project earns its capital hurdle. The scenario tool should keep original lifecycle and remaining-investment decisions separate, without manufacturing a numerical model from missing technical or financing inputs.

### P03 — “Fertilizer prices rose; why did this producer's earnings fall?”

Separate material ownership, manufacturing and distribution. Align output prices with consumed inputs and fixed-cost absorption, and distinguish price inflation from physical input unavailability. Show customer credit and seasonal purchasing as separate possible constraints. Explain which part of the chain retains value instead of coloring the entire sector positively.

A useful answer provides a falsifier: for example, stronger output prices alone would not close a thesis that requires an input spread to improve. The dossier should name the future observation needed—compatible consumed costs, production availability, funded orders or delivered volume—without pretending that correlation establishes causality.

### P04 — “Is the guidance increase new information for valuation?”

Show prior guidance, new guidance and an independently labeled dated expectation for the same period and measure. Do not average unrelated ranges or mix quarter and year. State whether the benchmark was captured before the event or reconstructed now. A small difference can still matter, but the system must not exaggerate it by comparing only with an older management range.

The second part asks whether the change improves future cash, reduces funding risk or merely changes timing. No stock-return inference is made until the user's accepted validation and valuation methods apply.

### P05 — “What could invalidate a demand-recovery thesis?”

Expose the buyer's product needs, feasible alternatives, economics, credit and purchase/application calendar. Distinguish orders from orders likely to become paid deliveries. Preserve the relevant geography and customer type. A regional research example must not become a forecast for a whole country or a worldwide demand balance.

Across all five workflows, a visible answer and its main counter-thesis come before the evidence detail. Provenance supports the reasoning; it does not replace it. The future UI must support the actual persona completing these questions on the real product path, including missing-data and incompatible-comparison states.

## 6. Proposed data and integration requirements

Use the existing native assertion, identity, correction and publication owners after their contracts are accepted. This report does not invent a production schema. The next written design should map each requirement into existing fields where possible and identify only genuinely missing domain-specific evidence.

A measurement needs its subject, product, period, unit, delivery point, ownership/reporting basis, source definition and correction vintage. Benchmark observations additionally need effective specification and transformation status. Project comparisons need linked assumption bundles, not free-floating IRRs. Product-use evidence needs a scoped buyer application and alternative, not a universal value-in-use claim.

For repeated acquisition, prioritize issuer operating reports and financial notes, publisher methodology notices, original commodity/customer research and accepted price/inventory sources. Assign refresh events to their natural changes: results releases, project updates, contract/methodology notices and relevant seasonal reports. This is a proposed refresh policy, not an armed watcher or a new ingestion service.

Keep raw editorial assertions and derived calculations distinguishable. Derived results need the exact input observations and formula, plus a clear reason when a calculation is withheld. Unknown basis should not be normalized away; newly compatible evidence should allow the useful calculation later without rewriting historical knowledge.

Public access to a PDF or methodology page does not grant permission to redistribute a full report, historical assessment series or licensed derived product. This package contains short attributed facts and original analysis only. Native retention, permitted display and source licensing remain separate acceptance work. A later schema or data purchase must reuse incumbent owners and receive its own current authorization.
