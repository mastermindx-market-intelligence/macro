## 1. The question this research now answers

The previous passes separated physical mining exposure, economic rights and value per share. This pass investigates a more specific question: **when does material become both usable by a customer and economically worthwhile to produce?**

The proposed answer is not a single capacity number. Research should distinguish material composition, process compatibility, output quality, customer-program acceptance, commercial orders, conversion economics and cash capture. The new cases show why each distinction can change an investor's interpretation. The model is a research proposal, not an accepted schema, quantitative investment strategy or instruction to trade.

Three conclusions guide the next product design. First, technical progress and commercial progress need separate evidence: a conforming product can still face weak conversion margins or insufficient orders. Second, actual progress must be recognized when evidence arrives; a cautious system must not freeze a company at an obsolete qualification stage. Third, a useful financial explanation must identify what a reported metric measures before comparing it across companies.

The strongest new tests are P-PLS's grade-conforming batch production; MP's precursor revenue and designation-based price protection; Lynas's element-specific output and contract scope; and Neo's September commercial launch alongside materially different cash-flow definitions. These are not relative stock rankings. They are discriminating cases for a system intended to understand what companies actually do and which developments change their economics.

## 3. Dossiers

### B01 — Albemarle: cash conversion, joint-venture income and available capacity

**Evidence.** Albemarle's Q2 release reports operating cash flow of $709.997 million, capital expenditure of $71.731 million and free cash flow of $638.266 million. It attributes cash conversion partly to increased Talison dividends and non-recurring working-capital benefits. [S3-01] Its filing describes a 49% equity-method interest in Windfield, intra-entity inventory profit deferrals, and Kemerton Train 1 entering care and maintenance in February 2026. Deferred inventory profit was $143.0 million in June versus $37.2 million in December. Reported Q2 Windfield net income was $461.155 million, while Albemarle's Windfield equity-income line was $165.835 million. [S3-02]

**Research interpretation.** The investor question is not simply whether lithium prices improved. It is which economic entity earned the profit, whether the issuer has recognized it, whether the joint venture distributed cash, and whether that cash can recur on the same timetable. An inventory-profit deferral and a dividend are different events. The product should not create extra earnings by adding a joint venture's gross profit to a segment measure that already includes the relevant contribution.

The mechanical 49% multiplication gives $225.96595 million, leaving a $60.13095 million difference from the reported equity-income line. That is **NOT_RECONCILED**, not proof of an error in the filing. The six-month change in deferred inventory profit is not a quarter-specific bridge and must not be forced into that difference. The useful output is an identified accounting question with exact periods, not a false reconciliation.

The proposed capacity view also needs a dated operating condition. An asset can remain geographically present and strategically relevant while not being available for current output. Do not delete it from the research graph, but do not count its historical nameplate as current production. A future restart thesis requires its own economics, spending, schedule and evidence.

**What would change the thesis.** A supported distribution policy and a complete equity-income bridge would improve confidence in recurring cash capture. A restart announcement without matched operating economics would not by itself do so. The research still lacks a complete contract-lag and normalized working-capital model; no normalized free-cash-flow number is invented.

### B02 — P-PLS: battery-grade conformity is not profitable utilization

**Evidence.** PLS's June-quarter narrative says P-PLS operated in batches: Train 1 produced 882 tonnes with 99% meeting battery grade, and Train 2 produced 2,679 tonnes with 98% meeting that grade. Production was adjusted to demand and conversion spreads; low-cost spodumene inventory acquired in 2025 was exhausted. [S3-03] POSCO's November 2024 plant announcement describes 43,000 tonnes of annual hydroxide capacity, different technologies for the two trains, and ownership then of 82% POSCO/18% Pilbara. Its SK On quantity was up to 15,000 tonnes **over three years**, not annually. [S3-04]

**Research interpretation.** The weighted conformity calculation is approximately 98.25%, based on the disclosed rounded percentages. It is neither lithium recovery nor a customer-specific approval rate. It says something about output already made, not how much input lithium became product or how much saleable material a customer will accept under a particular agreement.

This is a useful counterexample to the claim that technical qualification necessarily unlocks immediate production growth. The limiting factor can move to feedstock replacement cost, product realization or orders. The two trains should remain separate research subjects; their aggregate output does not establish equal reliability or economics.

For discipline, this pass does not label a ratio between current output and the older nameplate a verified utilization rate. That would require matched product-mass convention, calendar availability, train status and an appropriate annual-to-quarter conversion. Similarly, the historical ownership disclosure is not current confirmation that an option has or has not subsequently been exercised.

**Hypothetical merchant-conversion example.** Assume one tonne of conforming final product sells for $15,000, uses 7.5 tonnes of concentrate, and requires $4,000 of other variable costs. These are invented inputs, not P-PLS's reported costs. At $1,000 per tonne of feedstock, contribution is $3,500; at a $1,600 replacement price, it is minus $1,000. Technical yield is unchanged. A higher product price of $18,000 would restore contribution to $2,000, before fixed costs, capital and financing.

The case therefore suggests a **conversion-spread and inventory-vintage view** beside the physical flow. A positive margin on older feedstock does not establish the same margin on replacement inventory. Conversely, temporary negative spreads do not establish permanent technological failure. The falsifier is a matched feedstock, product-price, yield and cost bridge, rather than a general lithium-price chart.

### B03 — Direct extraction: test the complete process, not a technology label

**Evidence.** POSCO's June 15, 2026 release describes a cooperation agreement with Anson for a Utah direct-lithium-extraction demonstration. Its targets are operation in 2027 and technology verification using actual brine by 2028. POSCO describes commercial viability as something to be verified through the demonstration. [S3-05]

**Research interpretation.** A technology category should not become a blanket recovery, cost or production-speed assumption in the Mining model. A credible assessment would examine feed chemistry, variation, contaminants, selectivity, cycle life, reagent and water use, product conversion, waste handling and sustained operation. The important experiment is whether the full system produces the specified product repeatedly at a defensible cost under the relevant feed conditions.

These are proposed research questions, not measurements established by the announcement. A successful separation step should not be treated as proof of final battery-material economics, just as a demonstration schedule is not a commercial startup date. A future comparison must use a common boundary: excluding downstream purification in one case and including it in another creates an artificial technology advantage.

The long-run investor job is to distinguish proprietary process capability that can be replicated economically from a promising result dependent on a particular brine or test condition. That requires a separate evidence trail for each representative resource/process pairing, not a universal technology-success percentage.

### B04 — MP Materials: precursor sales, customer qualification and price protection

**Evidence.** MP's Q2 release identifies $16.524 million of Magnetics revenue as magnetic precursor products and separately discusses qualification/regulatory testing at Independence. [S3-06] Note 3 of its filing distinguishes nonduplicative price-protection designations for unsold produced stockpile, affiliate sales and external sales. The agreement uses NdPr-kilogram equivalents and a defined quarterly benchmark, with a $110 shortfall mechanism through December 2035. An upside-sharing provision applies after 10X reaches full production capacity. Price-protection income is a separate financial line. [S3-07]

**Research interpretation.** A company-level “magnet manufacturer” label is insufficient to identify the product behind current segment revenue. The model should retain material form, plant and customer-program stage. Metal, alloy, precursor and finished-magnet observations must not be relabeled merely because they share a segment name.

A price-protection mechanism also does not establish firm customer orders for all output. Its economic subject can include inventory or internal transfers. The designation rule therefore needs an anti-duplication boundary: material already designated at one stage cannot be counted again simply because it moves into another category.

**Hypothetical comparison.** On an assumed one million eligible NdPr-equivalent kilograms and an $80 benchmark, the illustrated shortfall is $30 million. This is not an estimate of MP's actual receivable: eligible designations, all contractual adjustments and the exact benchmark still need their native source values. At a $170 benchmark, the 30% upside illustration would be $18 million **only after** the specified capacity condition is satisfied; applying it earlier would be a different contract.

The proposed research output should show how protection changes price exposure while leaving costs, production, customer acceptance, funding and common-equity claims unresolved. It should not turn a contractual floor into a guaranteed gross margin, permanent valuation multiple or trade signal.

### B05 — Lynas: throughput must retain the element and feed specification

**Evidence.** Lynas's June-quarter table shows total ready-for-sale REO rising from 3,233 to 3,481 tonnes, while NdPr fell from 1,996 to 1,857 tonnes. The narrative links concentrate-quality variation to downstream cracking/leaching productivity. Its samarium disclosure distinguishes first production from customer qualification and expected orders. The HRE project estimate rose from approximately A$180 million to A$294 million, including scope for customer-specific characteristics. Its financing table separately records FY26 net share proceeds of A$914.3 million. [S3-08]

**Research interpretation.** The disclosed quarter-to-quarter changes calculate to plus 7.67% for aggregate REO and minus 6.96% for NdPr. A single aggregate-tonnage signal would miss the different direction of a commercially important product. Neither percentage by itself measures value creation: product prices, rights, costs and inventories are still needed.

The proposed supply graph should describe what the next process can accept, not merely connect two facilities with an arrow. More upstream material is not automatically useful throughput if its characteristics reduce downstream performance. A plant-wide average assay may also be insufficient for a customer whose specification concerns an impurity or physical characteristic.

The capital revision calculates to approximately 63.33%, but it should not be attributed wholly to execution failure. A changed scope, customer requirement and cost environment must be decomposed before judging like-for-like project performance. Improved addressable capability can come with more capital needs; both belong in the per-share analysis.

The funding observation is similarly separate from operations. A cash balance can support development without being generated by mature recurring cash flows. The proposed dossier should connect the funding source to the incremental capability and the common shareholder's claims, without assuming that equity financing is inherently good or bad.

### B06 — The same $110 headline can describe different risk exposures

**Evidence.** Lynas's March 10 JARE release specifies firm NdPr offtake of 5,000 tonnes annually within availability up to 7,200 tonnes; HRE commitments and availability are also distinct. It describes a $110/kg NdPr floor and payment of 30% of achieved price above $150/kg, capped at $10 million per calendar year. It includes annual review provisions. [S3-09] A separate March 16 U.S. announcement is explicitly a binding letter of intent to facilitate a definitive supply agreement; it must retain that historical status. [S3-10]

**Research interpretation.** The MP and Lynas announcements should not be normalized to one “government-backed price-floor company” factor. Their eligible subjects, purchasing obligations, reference prices, upside thresholds and conditions differ. This comparison concerns the disclosed commercial terms, not a political assessment or a legal opinion on enforceability.

For an illustrative JARE-like calculation, assume five million eligible kilograms are sold at $170/kg in one calendar year and no earlier sharing has used the cap. Thirty percent of the $20 excess is $30 million, reduced by the annual cap to $10 million. If $7 million has already consumed the cap, the remaining payment is $3 million. The model must not apply a fresh $10 million cap independently in every quarter.

Firm and maximum available quantities also must not be added: the 5,000 is within the 7,200 structure, not another 5,000 on top. A quarterly report's fiscal year and a contract's calendar-year cap require separate dates. The public notice is not a substitute for every detailed settlement term; these examples are conditional explanations, not a billing engine.

A sound assessment asks which part of the revenue distribution is narrowed and at what cost or retained upside. It also asks what remains contingent. The correct answer may be reduced price uncertainty with substantial production or capital uncertainty still present.

### B07 — Neo: a real commercial milestone must update the thesis narrowly

**Evidence.** Neo's August results described qualification shipments. Its September 14 release reports first commercial sintered-magnet volumes for an initial Tier 1 traction-motor customer program, with additional programs still prospective. The release separates approximately 2,000 tonnes of Phase 1A nameplate, a planned 5,000-tonne Phase 1B configuration and a longer-term roadmap. [S3-11, S3-14]

**Research interpretation.** This is a positive test for the system, not just another caution. It should recognize the reported transition from samples to commercial deliveries for the affected program. It should not retain a stale “only samples” conclusion because the financial quarter predates the operating update.

At the same time, one customer's commercial program does not qualify every product, platform or production line. No achieved annualized tonnage or plant-wide utilization can be inferred from the nameplate. “Commercial” is an economic stage, not a quantitative volume measurement. The customer's identity should remain at the disclosed level instead of being guessed from unrelated relationships.

For future research, distinguish three questions: has the initial program crossed its acceptance hurdle, are subsequent programs progressing, and can the facility make economic use of installed capacity? These can have different answers. A later order reduction would not necessarily invalidate the technical qualification, while a quality problem could have consequences beyond one quarter's shipments.

**Expectation discipline.** Neo raised guidance on July 8, before the August results. The published midpoint moved from $105 million to $145 million, approximately 38.10%. [S3-16] This is a change in management guidance, not a measured surprise versus market consensus. A chronology-aware dossier must not date that entire change to the subsequent earnings release.

### B08 — Neo: reported “Free Cash Flow” and cash after investment are not interchangeable

**Evidence.** Neo's H1 MD&A defines its displayed Free Cash Flow as adjusted EBITDA less capital expenditure net of recognized JTF grant: $93.264 million minus $5.154 million equals $88.110 million. [S3-12] Its H1 cash-flow statement reports operating cash of minus $49.645 million and cash spending on property, equipment and intangibles of $16.559 million. [S3-13] The Q2 release separately attributes substantial earnings to Rare Metals rather than solely to the new magnet facility. [S3-11]

**Research interpretation.** Subtracting the stated gross investing cash from operating cash gives minus $66.204 million. Label this exactly as **our operating cash less gross property/equipment/intangible spending calculation**. It is not a claim that the company's disclosed measure is arithmetically wrong, nor a universal substitute for every issuer's free-cash-flow definition.

The distinction can reverse the sign of a comparison while all underlying reported figures remain internally possible. A model that joins financial metrics by label alone could therefore infer cash availability that the metric does not measure. It needs the formula, period and adjustment scope, including whether a grant is recognized or actually received.

The complete cash statement reconciles a $57.864 million increase in cash using operating, investing, financing and exchange effects. This is not the same as self-funded growth. Equally, negative operating cash alone does not establish that the investment thesis failed: inventory and customer collections, growth preparation and other timing effects need investigation. The useful next evidence is conversion of those balances into cash and the cost of financing the interval.

The segment interpretation must also remain honest. A diversified advanced-materials company may have a valuable new magnet option without that option explaining the existing group's earnings. Current operating contribution, prospective revenue mix and investor expectations are separate analytical lenses.

### B09 — Recycling, tolling and the future business mix

**Evidence.** Neo's August 31 Carester notice describes a binding term sheet covering access to separated heavy oxides, recycling of magnet-manufacturing swarf, and a Silmet tolling arrangement that retains light output while returning heavy material. Caremag is described as under construction. [S3-15] Separately, S&P Global's September 10 publication reports Visible Alpha estimates of Magnetics contributing roughly 17% of MP revenue in 2026 and 71% in 2030. These are the provider's dated estimates, not achieved mix or our forecast. [S3-17]

**Research interpretation.** An announced recycling loop cannot be treated as an immediately available independent source of unlimited feedstock. Scrap availability depends on the activity producing it. Recovery, turnaround time, quality, ownership and the return obligation determine how much material becomes useful again. Customer qualification can apply to recycled material too; do not assume equivalence without evidence.

A tolling relationship can allocate different products to different parties without transferring ownership of an entire mine or plant. The proposed graph should preserve the service, material return and retained economic output, rather than flattening all arrows into “supplier to.” It must avoid counting the same element repeatedly as new supply when it passes through manufacturing, scrap and recovery.

**Hypothetical single-cycle example.** Begin with 1,000 kilograms of an already defined element-equivalent input. Assume 20% becomes recoverable scrap, 90% is recovered and 90% of that is acceptable for reuse. The return is 162 kilograms and the single-cycle external replacement need is 838 kilograms. This deliberately excludes additional cycles, timing, process losses elsewhere and new production. It is not Neo's reported yield.

The expectation example raises a related identity question. A company can be researched through a future business mix without rewriting its present exposure. Store the estimate horizon, provider, vintage and accounting boundary. A distant forecast cannot be used as a current revenue weight, and access to an article is not permission to distribute its underlying commercial consensus dataset. No expected stock return is inferred from that mix change.

## 4. Proposed commodity-specific analytical maps

These are research navigation patterns, not production graphs or assertions that every producer follows an identical chain. Each arrow is a question to answer from asset-specific evidence.

**Hard-rock lithium:** ore domain and mining plan → concentrate specification → feedstock purchase/transfer terms → conversion route → chemical product and impurity profile → customer/program acceptance → delivered pricing and inventory → issuer cash capture. Do not confuse gross tonnes of concentrate with tonnes of contained lithium or an LCE reporting convention.

**Brine and direct-extraction lithium:** reservoir/feed variability → extraction and selectivity → purification/concentration → chemical conversion → final specification → repeated operating economics → customer delivery. A test's boundary must include the steps needed for its claimed product. Water, reagent, energy and waste questions belong to that boundary rather than being invisible external costs.

**Rare-earth oxides to magnets:** feedstock/mineralogy → cracking and leaching → mixed intermediate → element-specific separation → oxide specification → metal/alloy → magnet process and finishing → customer program → commercial shipments. Recycling creates return paths, not independent mines. Mixed REO, NdPr oxide, contained NdPr metal, alloy and finished magnet mass must remain separately identified.

**Financial view across all maps:** ownership and retained product rights → external versus internal realization → reported profit and its adjustments → working capital/distributions → investment and financing → value attributable to the relevant equity. This extends the original Mining economic-rights framework rather than replacing it with a second sector-specific financial engine.

The maps should be capable of representing a supplier with several process roles, a joint venture with different economic and voting rights, an idled plant, a qualified but underutilized product, a contracted future facility and an existing business funding a new one. An unknown connection remains visibly unknown rather than being inferred from thematic similarity.

## 5. A proposed framework for productive, commercial and economic capacity

A serial chain cannot be understood by adding the annual tonnages of every stage. The units must first be normalized to a defined output, the relevant time interval and a compatible material basis. Inventory, alternative routes and recycling then need explicit treatment. The proposed model should reject a scalar “total supply capacity” when those conditions are not met.

For an intentionally simple steady-state illustration with no inventories, suppose compatible capacities are 100, 80 and 70 units of the same final-product equivalent. The physical upper bound is 70, not 250. If customer-qualified demand supports 60, that demand is the commercial bound. If only 45 units have economically acceptable conversion terms under stated assumptions, the economic case is narrower still. These are hypothetical bounds, not a multiplicative score or a claim that all constraints are independent.

The comparison should remain reversible. Improving a physical bottleneck might be valuable even when demand is temporarily lower, but that requires a horizon and a demand thesis. A qualified order might justify investment that is not yet physically available. An economic option should not be deleted simply because it is not current output; it should be shown with its remaining conditions and capital requirements.

Proposed observation dimensions are: subject plant/train and product; input and output basis; measured interval; capacity definition; process compatibility; operating availability; conformity criterion; customer-program scope; commercial stage; price/cost basis; economic-rights scope; and evidence date. These are requirements for extending native assertions, not a new set of global identity or state tables.

Do not introduce a single company-level “qualified=true” flag. A specific product and customer program can advance while other products remain in testing. A facility can produce samples, precursor products and commercial output concurrently. A downgrade should be equally specific, preserving what remains supported elsewhere.

## 6. Economic mechanisms worth tracking, without prematurely scoring stocks

The research suggests five useful mechanism families. They should be descriptive and independently evidenced before any later validated ranking use.

**Spread recovery.** Establish the matched output realization, input replacement cost, physical conversion factor and other variable costs. Then distinguish the short-run contribution decision from full-cycle returns after fixed costs and reinvestment. The strongest falsifier is a persistently adverse matched spread, not a decline in an unrelated benchmark.

**Qualification and program conversion.** Track what customer acceptance permits, what orders or deliveries follow, and whether repeat business is demonstrated. A program launch can reduce one uncertainty without establishing full utilization. The useful question is the incremental economic exposure unlocked by that program, not a generic positive-news count.

**Product-mix migration.** Compare like periods at element and product level, separating volume, realization and new-product effects. A higher aggregate selling price might come from mix rather than the same product earning a premium. The model should not credit the same change to both volume and pricing without a reconciliation.

**Risk-sharing contracts.** Identify the eligible volume, buyer obligation, benchmark, floor, upside participation, term, reviews and settlement dates. Compare residual exposure rather than scoring all floor agreements identically. A contract can alter downside price risk while leaving counterparty, production and financing risks material.

**Cash maturation.** Connect earnings, inventory, receivables, joint-venture distributions, grants, financing and investment. Distinguish recurring conversion from the release or absorption of working capital. A higher cash balance is informative only with its funding bridge; an adjusted performance measure is useful only with its definition.

For each mechanism, the proposed dossier should show the strongest counter-thesis, the next discriminating evidence, and the horizon over which that evidence matters. The purpose is not to eliminate judgment but to make the judgment inspectable and revisable. None of the five is presently a validated return predictor.

## 7. Six persona tasks for a useful first integration

**Investor examining a lithium rebound.** Move from the theme into the mine/converter exposure; see whether the thesis concerns price, replacement feedstock, qualification, inventory realization or a restart. The answer should explain which current measurements support the mechanism and which missing contract parameter limits it.

**Investor following rare-earth supply diversification.** Identify which elements and product forms a company supplies, the plants performing each step, and which customer programs are commercial. Do not substitute the jurisdiction of the listed parent for every physical stage or claim that geography alone establishes qualification.

**Analyst checking a new plant announcement.** Compare its present stage with the previous supported observation and preserve the announced future stages. The Neo launch is a required positive test: the initial program should advance, while planned expansion and other programs should not inherit that proof.

**Analyst comparing cash-generating businesses.** Display issuer-reported metrics alongside a clearly defined comparable calculation only when inputs and accounting boundaries match. The Neo/Albemarle contrast should expose the definition difference without alleging wrongdoing or replacing one company's measure invisibly.

**Portfolio user examining overlapping themes.** Show an asset or product appearing in Mining, electrification, robotics and other research contexts without multiplying its physical output or treating all future thematic exposure as current revenue. Price-basket membership and investment weighting remain under their existing acceptance policy.

**Researcher assessing a missing bottleneck.** Follow a stalled downstream node to feed specification, commercial demand, input economics or funding rather than assuming every delay is geological scarcity. Provide a source-scoped hypothesis and its falsifier instead of a confident causal arrow inferred from proximity.

## 9. Implementation implications, without starting implementation

The research favors an optional typed extension to existing source-scoped evidence, plus consumer composition through the shared Themes architecture, rather than a Mining-only database. Robotics/template work remains the shared interface precedent; it must be freshly reconciled when written design begins. This report does not claim the relevant assertion types, private storage or consumers already exist.

The first integration should be judged by completed user tasks, not by the size of the graph. A minimal useful dossier needs an intelligible conclusion, material inputs, an economic mechanism, its counter-thesis, and a bounded way to see what changed. A graph becomes valuable when selecting a node explains a specific dependency or retained right. It should not be required merely to understand a company's current business.

A historical view should select observations by the appropriate evidence/knowledge clock, preserve corrections and expose gaps. The model must not manufacture historical knowledge from an old publication retrieved today. Forward scenarios should retain their assumptions and horizons; a present-day forecast is neither an observed future result nor a historical backtest input.
