## 3. The central analytical correction: scarcity is not the endpoint

The proposed model has three linked views within the existing graph and evidence owners, not three new systems:

**Physical view:** demand, material specification, ore/process route, mine plan, production, inventories, processing and customer qualification.

**Economic-rights view:** asset owner, operator, joint-venture interest, product entitlement, payable fraction, royalty/stream, purchase obligations, price basis and settlement.

**Equity view:** attributable cash generation, sustaining and growth investment, funding, senior claims, dilution, distributions and the expectations embedded in valuation.

Every research thesis should complete the following chain or expose the missing link:

`Demand/commodity regime → stage-specific constraint → saleable output → economic entitlement → realized margin → cash available after capital needs → value per diluted share → comparison with expectations.`

A statement can be physically correct and economically incomplete. A company can improve economics without a share-price rerating. Conversely, an expectation can improve before production rises. These possibilities motivate the proposed tests below; they are not a validated return model.

### A proposed rerating decomposition

Research each case through seven questions rather than one composite bullishness score:

1. **Commodity realization:** Which price, grade, regional premium, contract mechanism or hedge actually changes receipts?
2. **Operating conversion:** Does throughput, grade, recovery or availability improve saleable output, rather than just nominal capacity?
3. **Margin retention:** Do input costs, treatment charges, royalties or other counterparties absorb the benefit?
4. **Reinvestment:** How much sustaining, closure, expansion and working capital is required to retain or grow those cash flows?
5. **Delivery risk:** Which remaining engineering, financing, qualification or project-specific permission condition changes the range of outcomes?
6. **Per-share capture:** After debt, minority interests, streams, preferred claims and dilution, what remains for the relevant common equity?
7. **Expectation gap:** Is the change incremental to an identified, dated benchmark expectation, or merely a repetition of an already-known thesis?

The last question cannot be answered by confident prose. Where a point-in-time consensus or market-implied benchmark has not been acquired and licensed, label the result an economic sensitivity or research hypothesis, not mispricing or expected excess return.

## 4. Ten source-grounded stress cases

Each case below contains a limited factual observation followed by a proposed modeling requirement. Sources are primary issuer, regulatory, intergovernmental or industry-publisher material. Corporate explanations remain corporate explanations, not independent operating audits.

### C01 — Freeport: copper price, output and value-chain role can diverge

In Q2 2026 Freeport reported copper production of 786 million recoverable pounds and sales excluding purchases of 710 million. Realized price was $6.17/lb versus $4.54 a year earlier; unit net cash cost was $1.97 versus $1.13. Its filing also explains that treatment charges are costs to mining operations but income to Atlantic Copper. PTFI is consolidated despite Freeport's 48.76% ownership. [S01]

Requirement: preserve production/sales and consolidated/attributable bases, and identify the affected value-chain role. Do not apply ownership twice to an already-attributable number. A concentrate shortage is not automatically the same earnings event for a miner and a treatment-service provider.

### C02 — Agnico Eagle: a cost label does not guarantee comparable definitions

Agnico revised certain non-GAAP cost measures from January 2026 for NTI lease payments at Meadowbank and restated comparative measures. Its disclosure identifies a difference from WGC AISC guidance in that treatment. [S02]

Requirement: attach definition version, inclusions, exclusions and restatement status to each cost observation. AISC is not a substitute for the complete cash-flow statement. A reduction created by reclassification must not be labeled operating productivity.

### C03 — Albemarle: distinguish physical growth, product mix and scenarios

In its Q2 2025 release, Albemarle reported Energy Storage volume growth of 15%, lower pricing of 28%, and a 13.5% segment revenue decline. [S03] In Q2 2026, the reported 65 kt LCE volume aggregates salts and spodumene. Approximately 40% of salts volume, or one-third of total volume, is under long-term agreements in its scenario assumptions. The $10/$20/$30-per-kg cases are conditional price scenarios. [S04]

Requirement: preserve the product mixture and contract denominator. Do not treat LCE as proof of equivalent product quality or value. Exact pricing-lag parameters were not verified in the admitted primary material and remain unknown. Do not multiply management's rounded price/volume bridge percentages as though they share a simple universal denominator.

### C04 — Cameco: purchased pounds and mine production are different exposures

Cameco's Q2 2026 disclosure reports 3.9 million pounds of uranium production on its-share basis, 2.8 million pounds purchased and 7.1 million pounds delivered. It separately reports uranium, fuel services and Westinghouse; financial amounts default to Canadian dollars unless specified otherwise. [S05]

Requirement: reconcile sourcing, inventory and delivery rather than substituting deliveries for mine output. A blended issuer result cannot be assigned wholly to uranium extraction. Currency and product units must survive aggregation.

### C05 — MP Materials: vertical integration can double-count apparent revenue

MP's Q2 2026 segment revenues of $95.629 million and $16.524 million reconcile to $108.490 million consolidated revenue after $3.663 million of intercompany eliminations. Price-protection-agreement income of $17.580 million is a separate line, not product revenue. Reported NdPr production and sales are also different measures. [S06]

Requirement: retain intercompany flags and distinguish ordinary external sales from other contractual income. Do not sum upstream and downstream segment revenues into a larger market exposure without eliminations. Qualification shipments or start-up activity must not silently become established commercial output.

### C06 — Antamina/Wheaton/BHP: a stream is a conditional right, not mine ownership

The BHP silver stream closed on April 1, 2026: a 33.75% share of payable silver, with a 90% payable factor, stepping down to 22.5% after 100 million delivered ounces. Wheaton paid $4.3 billion upfront and owes 20% of spot price on delivered ounces. [S07] BHP describes settlement through metal credits, without physical delivery to Wheaton. [S08] Wheaton's Q2 report separately describes funding with cash and credit facilities. [S09]

Requirement: represent the underlying mine, contractual counterparty, payability, cumulative-delivery threshold, ongoing purchase cost and financing. Neither a high cash margin nor a large attributable production figure proves a high return on invested capital. Do not duplicate physical mine output when recording multiple financial claims on it.

### C07 — Epiroc/Khoemacau: bookings, equipment readiness and delivered capability differ

Epiroc's September 1, 2026 release describes an approximately SEK 610 million equipment order for the Khoemacau expansion, booked in Q3. Delivery is scheduled from Q4 2026 through Q2 2028. The package includes equipment, a remote-control solution and support; Smart-series equipment is described as ready for automation. [S10]

Requirement: distinguish order intake, scheduled delivery, installation, customer acceptance and measured operating improvement. Do not assign the entire order to software or autonomous operations. Record the aftermarket connection separately without inventing a recurring-revenue percentage.

### C08 — Perpetua/Stibnite: project milestones must remain independently typed

Perpetua's August 17 report describes financing approval while definitive documentation and closing remain prospective, alongside early works and a targeted later final investment decision. [S11] Its August 31 update reports autoclave fabrication and workforce infrastructure progress; additional tungsten development is outside the current project scope and needs separate review/permitting. [S12]

Requirement: keep financing approval, signed closing, drawability, early works, full sanction, commissioning and commercial output separate. New mineralization is not an accepted addition to reserves or forecast revenue. These are dated issuer statements, not independently verified current legal or funding status.

### C09 — Gold supply: revisions and conflicting text must not contaminate a signal

The currently retrieved WGC/Metals Focus page labeled Q2 2025 reports 908.6 tonnes of mine production for that quarter. [S13] The Q2 2026 publication uses 947.7 tonnes for the same comparison period and describes historical revisions. Its recycling table implies about a 6% year-on-year decline, while a later paragraph says 13%. [S14]

Requirement: preserve the separately dated source versions without asserting an archived historical receipt. Flag the table/prose inconsistency instead of merging it into a stronger scarcity claim. These are limited extracts for research commentary, not a redistributed WGC or Metals Focus dataset.

### C10 — IEA copper: a long-horizon supply gap is not a current shortage reading

The IEA 2026 Outlook narrows its projected copper gap from roughly 30% to 25% in the base-case project pipeline relative to 2035 primary requirements under STEPS. Primary requirements net out secondary supply and account for refining losses. [S15]

Requirement: attach scenario, target year, pipeline assumptions and denominator. Do not display this as a 25% shortfall in today's refined inventories, a confidence probability, or a single-stock return forecast. Revisions to the pipeline are research events, not new observed production.

## 6. Required research observations and joins

This is a conceptual requirement list, not a new database schema or an instruction to mint identities. Bind to accepted native entities and assertion contracts only after owner review.

**Identity and subject:** existing issuer/security identifiers; operating entity; asset/project; operator; commodity/product specification; process stage; economic-rights counterparty. Symbols are display aliases, not permanent IDs. Headquarters, listing market, mine location and processing location remain separate.

**Physical measures:** ore treated, grade with units, recovery, product output, product quality, capacity basis, inventory boundary, purchased material and sales/deliveries. Every number identifies whether it is gross, attributable, payable, saleable or consolidated. Do not infer metal content from bulk tonnage without a valid conversion basis.

**Rights:** ownership percentage, effective interval, product-specific entitlement, payability, thresholds, stream/royalty formula, purchase obligations, guarantee scope where material, and whether settlement is physical or financial. An announced transaction does not alter current rights before the supported effective event.

**Economics:** realized price, contract basis, hedges, costs by reported definition, treatment/refining charges, freight, royalties, sustaining/growth capital, working capital, closure obligations, debt, senior claims and diluted share basis. Preserve gross versus net, cash versus accrual, and segment versus consolidated definitions.

**Evidence and time:** source URL/accession, precise section, issuer publication date, observation interval, effective date, correction vintage, scenario horizon, retrieval date, uncertainty and reuse restrictions. Retrieval time alone is not proof that a fact was available historically. Current discovery of an old page does not create an archived point-in-time receipt.

**State and confidence:** reported, calculated, estimated, forecast, scheduled, conditional or unknown. Confidence concerns evidence support, not a hidden investment recommendation. A null is not a zero, and narrative omission is not proof of no exposure.

## 7. Economic calculations: scope and four hypothetical checks

A possible physical bridge for a simple, single-product illustration is:

`contained metal = ore quantity × grade`

`recovered metal = contained metal × recovery`

`payable entitlement = recovered metal × contractual payable fraction × applicable economic interest`

Use this only where the definitions and contract support it. An already-attributable input must not be multiplied by ownership again. Production is not automatically period sales. A royalty on net proceeds is not an ownership percentage applied to physical tonnes.

A valuation worksheet should reconcile asset cash flows and any corporate assets to debt and other claims, using internally consistent enterprise/equity cash-flow and discount-rate conventions. Do not subtract capital twice when it is already inside asset cash flows, or add both the value of an asset and its future dividends. Report scenario ranges, not fabricated precision.

The following are deliberately invented teaching cases, unrelated to any issuer valuation:

- **H01 Physical denominator:** 1,000,000 tonnes of ore at 1% metal, 90% recovery and 96% payability gives 8,640 payable tonnes. A simple 60% economic interest gives 5,184 attributable payable tonnes. This is not a capacity or sales forecast.
- **H02 Dilution:** fully funded equity value of $1.0 billion across 100 million shares is $10/share. If the already-financed, net-of-claims equity value grows to $1.2 billion but shares rise to 130 million, value is about $9.23/share, down 7.69%. Financing cash is already reflected; do not add it again.
- **H03 Earnings versus rerating:** $2 EPS at 15 times earnings gives $30. $3 EPS at 10 times still gives $30: 50% earnings growth with no price gain. This excludes dividends and does not forecast an appropriate multiple.
- **H04 Revised comparator:** a synthetic series whose current observation is 110 and originally reported comparator is 100 shows 10% growth. Revising the comparator to 105 gives 4.76%. Both vintages must remain recoverable rather than rewriting what an earlier analyst knew.

These checks demonstrate arithmetic and specification pitfalls. They do not establish causal attribution, fair value, predictability, statistical significance or investability.
