## 2. Seven company dossiers

### 2.1 EOG — Separate production, realized prices, commercial elections and replacement capital

**Verified anchor.** EOG's Q2 gas-sales bridge moves from $600 million to $812 million through a $232 million positive volume contribution and a $20 million negative price contribution. Its issuer-defined Q2 free cash flow is $2.799 billion, using adjusted operating cash flow rather than raw operating cash flow. Its annual filing also supplies the acquisition perimeter and reserve-measure conventions needed to interpret growth. [S01, S02, S03]

The contract map is more informative than a generic U.S.-gas label. The amended Cheniere IPM announcement describes 420,000 MMBtu/day with staged, train-linked commencement. EOG's annual marketing description discusses a JKM/Henry Hub election. Separately, its quarterly filing describes a ten-year, 2027-start sale of 180,000 MMBtu/day: 140,000 linked to Brent and the balance linked to Brent or a Gulf Coast gas index at the counterparty's election. The exact active, selected and delivered volumes remain unresolved. [S02, S03, S04]

**Economic model.** Start with separate oil, NGL and gas revenue. Match each product's net sales volume to its realized price and location, then reconcile realized derivative settlements, transport, production taxes and operating costs. The commercial index can differ from the commodity physically produced. Who controls an index election is economically important: a buyer's option must not be valued as though the producer controls it.

The next bridge is from operating contribution to cash after replacing decline. A producer can report strong current cash by spending less while allowing future output or inventory quality to deteriorate. Conversely, investment can suppress current free cash flow while creating valuable future production. The research needs both current cash and the future spending needed to maintain a comparable asset base; reported capital expenditure alone cannot resolve that question.

**Potential revision and re-rating mechanisms to test.** Better realized basis, genuinely lower development costs, a longer economically comparable drilling inventory, or profitable contract diversification could improve a fixed-horizon cash estimate. A durable improvement in replacement economics could also change the value assigned to later years. Neither follows automatically from higher BOE production, larger reserves or a higher oil price.

**Strong countercase.** Acquired volume, a larger gas share, lower-quality replacement locations or deferred spending could produce apparent operational growth without better value per share. A favorable current commodity price may increase near-term cash while leaving normalized economics unchanged.

**Required observations and valuation approach.** Build an acquisition-adjusted basin/product bridge; cohort decline and sustaining-capital assumptions; product and location prices; hedge/contract elections; net diluted shares; and management versus analyst forecasts on a fixed horizon. Use scenario-based asset/cash valuation with explicit depletion and replacement assumptions. Do not treat the filing's standardized reserve cash-flow measure as current corporate fair value. Exact hedge coverage and contract weights remain gaps G02–G04.

### 2.2 Valero — Model conversion margins and operating perimeter, not crude-price direction

**Verified anchor.** The Q2 West Coast table reports throughput of 130 thousand barrels/day versus 262 thousand a year earlier and refining margin of $30.36 versus $18.02 per throughput barrel. Benicia had been idled by the end of April, and specified subsequent costs moved to Corporate and Other. The release identifies a temporary LIFO benefit separately. The annual filing distinguishes conventional refining, renewable diesel and ethanol, including the consolidated Diamond Green Diesel joint venture. [S11, S12]

**Economic model.** Conventional refining converts a particular crude slate into regional products. Revenue depends on yields and product prices; cost depends on feedstock, energy, purchased inputs, operating availability and applicable obligations. A benchmark crack spread is a useful reference, not the company's realized refinery profit. Keep the source definition of any compliance-adjusted spread so a cost is not deducted twice.

Changing the set of operating refineries changes the denominator and the mix. A higher reported margin per barrel can coexist with less throughput, a different regional portfolio and ongoing closure costs. The appropriate comparison is not simply current unit margin divided by last year's unit margin. It is a bridge through continuing assets, mix, temporary items, availability and the cash consequence of the idled operation.

Renewable diesel and ethanol need separate feedstock/product/co-product economics. Their investment, qualification and incentive exposure cannot be inferred from a conventional refining spread. Consolidated joint-venture results also require a separate parent-shareholder claim.

**Potential revision and re-rating mechanisms to test.** Sustained improvement in regional product spreads, advantageous feedstock sourcing, higher reliability or a demonstrable reduction in future maintenance burden could improve normalized distributable cash. Portfolio changes could remove persistent cash losses, but only after accounting for closure obligations, foregone production and any retained costs.

**Strong countercase.** A favorable inventory adjustment, an unusual regional shortage or the exit of a low-margin asset can lift reported unit measures without establishing a durable improvement at continuing facilities. A headline low earnings multiple during unusually strong margins is not evidence of inexpensive through-cycle value.

**Required observations and valuation approach.** Compare regional product/crude differentials, refinery availability and yields, turnarounds, working capital, continuing-asset margins, closure costs and joint-venture attribution. Value scenarios on normalized after-maintenance cash rather than extrapolating one quarter's crack spread. Retain issuer-published ratios: rounded aggregate inputs do not reproduce the reported $30.36 exactly and must not overwrite it.

### 2.3 Kinder Morgan — Distinguish capacity payments, throughput, renewal and contributed assets

**Verified anchor.** KMI's Q2 filing reports $1.365 billion of firm-service revenue and $732 million of fee-based-service revenue, totaling $2.097 billion. Those are revenue categories, not corporate EBITDA or cash-flow exposure weights. Its project-capital/first-full-year-EBITDA measure covers a defined project subset, not a traded enterprise-value multiple or a project IRR. [S08, S09]

Western Gateway's August announcement assigns KMI 35.1% ownership, involving approximately $1.5 billion of contributed existing assets and $250 million of cash. The project is primarily supported by ten-year take-or-pay commitments and targets 2029 completion. This is an announced investment decision and target, not current earnings. [S10]

**Economic model.** Separate a charge for reserving available capacity from a charge dependent on transported or handled volume. Identify service conditions, minimums, commodity-linked provisions, escalators, expiry and counterparties. A pipeline's physical utilization and its current cash sensitivity are not necessarily proportional. Fee-based revenue also need not be volume-independent.

The second dimension is renewal economics. A long contract can support visibility, yet a future renewal may reprice favorably or unfavorably depending on competing routes, customer economics and service value. Projected cash after expiry must not inherit the current contract indefinitely without an explicit scenario.

The Western Gateway case requires a no-deal counterfactual. Existing assets are an economic contribution even when they are not a new cash outlay. The post-deal distribution must be compared with cash that the contributed assets would otherwise have generated. A high apparent return calculated only on the $250 million cash contribution would answer the wrong question.

**Potential revision and re-rating mechanisms to test.** Commissioning a well-contracted project on budget, attractive renewal terms, incremental service demand on existing capacity or lower financing risk could improve cash durability and per-share outcomes. Value creation depends on the full incremental capital and risk, not the size of the announced backlog.

**Strong countercase.** Construction overruns, weaker counterparties, adverse renewals or surrendered asset earnings can absorb the apparent benefit. A service-revenue mix table cannot establish how much EBITDA is insulated from price or volume changes.

**Required observations and valuation approach.** Contracted capacity, actual throughput, fee structure, expiry ladder, customer concentration, sustaining versus expansion spending, financing and distributions must be matched. Use project and parent cash-flow scenarios; reconcile contributed-asset baseline earnings and total economic capital. Leave project return and precise corporate cash-exposure weights unknown until G06–G07 are resolved.

### 2.4 Cheniere — Separate infrastructure, LNG marketing, feedgas purchases and accounting

**Verified anchor.** The annual filing describes a general SPA family with fixed charges and a variable component linked to 115% of Henry Hub, including stated cancellation-payment terms. This is not a claim that every agreement is identical. Its Q2 adjusted-parent-net-income reconciliation moves from $3.068 billion GAAP income to $632 million after the stated fair-value, NPNS, tax and noncontrolling-interest adjustments. Adjusted net income is not cash flow. [S05, S06]

In June 2026, Cheniere designated an accounting scope exception for approximately 73% of fixed minimum contractual IPM volumes at that date. Designated contracts move away from recurring fair-value accounting, with deferred-basis amortization following deliveries; other contracts remain outside the designation. This is an accounting recognition change, not the disappearance of commercial exposure. [S07]

**Economic model.** Keep three cash engines distinct: contracted liquefaction/service economics; LNG marketing after gas purchases, liquefaction, shipping and delivery timing; and the parent company's share after debt and other owners' claims. Internationally indexed IPM gas purchases are a procurement exposure, not simply additional LNG revenue. Likewise, 115% of a gas index is a pricing formula, not a 15% corporate profit margin.

Contractual fixed fees also change marginal cargo decisions. A buyer may still benefit from lifting a cargo when a fee is payable whether it lifts or cancels, even if the cargo does not cover its full allocated cost. The analytical implication is conditional: a narrower international spread does not by itself establish that all contracted exports stop. Availability, credit and exact contract terms remain relevant.

**Potential revision and re-rating mechanisms to test.** Reliable commissioning, contracted cash conversion, reduced project risk and favorable renewal or marketing economics could improve attributable future cash. The higher-value research question is how much cash reaches the parent after expansion spending and other claims, not simply whether liquefaction capacity increases.

**Strong countercase.** A larger marketing margin can be transient; construction spending can consume new operating cash; and a new accounting policy can reduce visible earnings volatility without changing underlying contractual risk. A company-defined distributable-cash measure is not automatically the amount available after all expansion commitments.

**Required observations and valuation approach.** Maintain separate sales and purchase contracts, volume/start/expiry clocks, train milestones, service availability, capital to complete, debt/NCI treatment and accounting-regime markers. Compare like-for-like cash measures across the June accounting break. Do not apply a generic stable-infrastructure multiple to unseparated marketing exposure, and do not treat first LNG, substantial completion and full contractual cash generation as the same event.

### 2.5 SLB — Remove acquired growth and distinguish drilling, production, digital and capital-funded work

**Verified anchor.** Q2 revenue is $8.972 billion versus $8.546 billion a year earlier, including $870 million from ChampionX. Removing that current-period acquired contribution produces approximately a 5.2% decline rather than the approximately 5.0% reported increase; this is not a full constant-currency or pro-forma comparison. Its free-cash-flow calculation deducts capital expenditure, APS investment and capitalized exploration data from operating cash flow. [S13]

The source set also distinguishes Digital, Reservoir Performance, Well Construction, Production Systems and other activities. The annual indexed excerpt describes Asset Performance Solutions and data-center activities; full-file access was not established. Do not infer software economics from an AI association or treat the indexed excerpt as a complete segment audit. [S13, S14]

**Economic model.** Customer capital spending reaches different service lines at different stages. New-well construction, completion, ongoing production recovery and installed-base services need separate demand and operating-leverage assumptions. Digital delivery can include different revenue and cost models. Capital-funded performance arrangements require investment as part of the operating proposition, so ordinary capex alone does not represent their cash burden.

Acquisition normalization is equally important. Separate purchased revenue from growth in the comparable existing business, then inspect consideration, financing, share count and integration costs. Removing the acquired revenue is a useful first diagnostic, but it does not replace a complete pro-forma perimeter and currency bridge. Segment-level acquisition contributions must also reconcile through eliminations before being summed.

**Potential revision and re-rating mechanisms to test.** A better mix of resilient production-related work, profitable technology adoption, pricing discipline or increased service intensity could improve cash through a weak drilling period. Better cash conversion after all required investment could support a different economic interpretation from headline revenue alone.

**Strong countercase.** Purchased revenue can conceal organic weakness; software labels can conceal hardware or delivery costs; and a revenue rebound can coexist with capital-funded project exposure, slow collections or weaker margins. Acquisition synergies remain a hypothesis until observed after their costs.

**Required observations and valuation approach.** Customer region/program, service family, recurring versus project revenue, pricing, utilization, contract risk, investment and working capital should form the driver tree. Consider separately normalized business economics rather than assigning the entire issuer the multiple of its fastest-growing or most fashionable activity. G08 requires a fuller segment/perimeter reconciliation.

### 2.6 Halliburton — Completion intensity and turnkey economics cannot be read from one rig count

**Verified anchor.** Halliburton reports Q2 revenue across both North America and international operations, approximately $2.3 billion and $3.4 billion respectively. Its release separates Completion and Production from Drilling and Evaluation rather than treating them as one uniform activity. A July announcement covers a planned 285-well oil re-entry program under lump-sum-turnkey terms; disclosed well counts do not establish contract margin. [S15, S16]

**Economic model.** Distinguish drilling from completing a well, intervening in existing production and executing a turnkey program. The metric that leads demand for one service can be weak for another. Rig count may describe drilling activity but cannot supply undisclosed completion schedules, service intensity or contract prices.

A turnkey contract transfers a particular package of performance and cost risk. A larger award can increase future revenue while increasing exposure to execution, geology, logistics or cost overruns. The right bridge is contracted price minus cost to complete, adjusted for recoverable versus unrecoverable changes, rather than awards multiplied by a generic margin.

**Potential revision and re-rating mechanisms to test.** Better utilization, service intensity, technology-enabled cost efficiency and favorable project execution could improve earnings without identical growth in every activity count. Repeatable cash conversion and contractual risk management are possible quality differentiators, to be tested rather than assumed.

**Strong countercase.** Higher revenue may carry unfavorable mix or execution risk, and aggregate regional growth may conceal a weaker service line. A large multi-year well award is neither current revenue nor a guarantee of attractive return on invested capital.

**Required observations and valuation approach.** Separate North American and international demand, drilling/completion/intervention activity, segment margins, equipment utilization, cash collections, contract type and contingencies. Use normalized service-line earnings and cash scenarios, incorporating capex and project risk. Without pricing, escalation clauses and cost-to-complete evidence, G09 prevents a project-margin estimate.

### 2.7 EQT — Integrate gas, midstream claims and power-linked contracts without double counting

**Verified anchor.** EQT's July release reports a definitive ten-year agreement for 325,000 Dth/day of gas linked to PJM power prices. The detailed formula and commencement evidence required for a quantified current earnings claim remain absent. The same release separates consolidated Q2 free cash flow of $453.734 million from attributable free cash flow of $329.666 million. [S17]

The quarterly filing describes a Midstream JV distribution waterfall: available cash goes 60% to the BXCI affiliate and 40% to EQT until the specified Base Return. This is a conditional cash-distribution rule, not a generic issuer ownership percentage. The segment presentation also contains intersegment eliminations. [S18]

**Economic model.** Begin with upstream net gas realization, transport access, operating costs, compression and development capital. Then reconcile the midstream services provided within the group: an internal fee is not incremental external revenue merely because it appears in a segment. The final shareholder claim also depends on the distribution waterfall and capital contributions, not just consolidated EBITDA.

Power-linked gas introduces another commercial driver. The producer's cash may depend partly on a power index and its exact contractual formula, rather than solely on a local gas benchmark. However, a signed agreement is only one milestone. Project readiness, supply commencement, required infrastructure, counterparty protection and actual settlement all matter.

**Potential revision and re-rating mechanisms to test.** Better market access, lower bottleneck costs, profitable integrated operations or a genuinely attractive power-linked contract could improve realized and future cash. A changing distribution priority may alter attributable cash even when total JV cash changes little.

**Strong countercase.** New capital commitments, unfavorable contract terms, delayed project completion or another investor's priority claim can absorb an apparently strong operating result. A data-center association cannot be converted into an undisclosed high-margin revenue weight.

**Required observations and valuation approach.** Model production, price/basis, contracts, internal eliminations, JV distributions and attributable capital on consistent units. Value the actual parent cash claim, not consolidated cash multiplied by one guessed ownership factor. R3 should examine the CPV project and power-pricing mechanism before any quantified contribution. This is the first R2-to-R3 bridge, not a completed electricity dossier.

## 3. Economic bridges and worked examples

These are original, deliberately simplified research examples. They are not issuer forecasts, contract quotations, calibrated sensitivities or investment valuations. Each formula must be reconciled to the issuer and period before product use.

### 3.1 Producer cash and a partial hedge

For a defined product and period, begin with net sold quantity times realized cash price, then add or subtract separately reported settled hedges only if the realized-price measure excludes them. Deduct cash costs, taxes, required investment and changes in working capital on a declared cash-flow basis. Royalties already reflected in net production must not be deducted a second time. Mcf-to-MMBtu conversion needs heat content; the accounting BOE conversion does not provide it.

Assume 10 million units are produced and sold, six million are swapped at $4, and the same spot index rises from $3 to $5. Ignore basis, costs and tax. Physical revenue rises by $20 million, but swap settlements offset $12 million, leaving an $8 million cash-revenue increase. The physical volume is not the effective unhedged volume. A collar, basis hedge, contract election or different delivery period would require different arithmetic.

A current cash distribution also need not equal economic profit. Hypothetical end-year cash of 100 for five years, then zero, has present value 379.078676941 at a 10% discount rate. Capitalizing the same 100 as an indefinite perpetuity would give 1,000 and assume away depletion. Neither calculation estimates any company's reserves. The point is to make the replacement/terminal-value assumption visible.

### 3.2 Capacity revenue versus volume revenue

Assume one million units/day of reserved, available capacity at $0.40 per unit of daily reservation, paid for 365 days. The annual capacity charge is $146 million before costs. Add a separate term for actual transported volume times a volumetric fee. Availability, payment and minimum-volume provisions must be represented explicitly; this arithmetic does not prove that a real contract is unconditional or fully collectible.

For valuation, extend only the contracted terms through their actual life. Post-expiry cash needs a separate renewal scenario, not an invisible perpetual extension. Service obligations and sustaining capex also remain economic costs even when the customer pays for unused capacity.

### 3.3 LNG: lifting contribution differs from full-cost margin

Assume Henry Hub at $3/MMBtu, variable purchase cost of 1.15 times that index, $1/MMBtu of other marginal delivery cost, an unavoidable $2.50/MMBtu fixed fee and destination proceeds of $5.25/MMBtu. Lifting yields $0.80/MMBtu more contribution than cancellation under those assumptions, while the full-cost margin is negative $1.70/MMBtu. Cancellation terms, freight commitments, credit and operational restrictions could change the decision.

This makes two analytical mistakes visible: treating every fee as avoidable when testing a marginal cargo, or excluding the fixed fee when measuring total economics. It does not establish any current cargo arbitrage or destination price.

### 3.4 Turnkey profit is sensitive to unrecoverable costs

Assume fixed contract revenue of 100 and expected cost of 80. Initial profit is 20. A 10% cost increase with no contractual recovery raises cost to 88 and reduces profit to 12: a 40% profit decline despite unchanged contract revenue. Backlog growth therefore needs cost-to-complete and risk evidence before an earnings inference.

### 3.5 Attributable cash and the opportunity cost of a contribution

A useful parent-level project comparison is the present value of future cash received after the transaction, less future contributions and transaction costs, compared with the cash and asset position under a no-transaction case. This prevents contributed assets from being treated as free and prevents a JV ownership percentage from replacing its distribution waterfall.

For a full-enterprise valuation, use unlevered cash consistently with an appropriate enterprise discount rate and then reconcile debt and other claims. For an equity valuation, use the equity cash claim and a matching equity discount rate. Deducting interest inside unlevered cash and also charging it through financing claims can double count the same burden. Lease and minority-interest treatment must also be internally consistent.

## 4. What could cause an Energy re-rating?

The following is an analytical framework for testing hypotheses. It does not claim a validated causal relationship or a current mispricing.

### 4.1 More earnings is not the same as a higher multiple

For a positive, consistently defined forward earnings estimate, price equals earnings per share times the earnings multiple. Use the same forecast horizon and accounting basis at both dates. An increase in the selected earnings estimate and a change in its multiple should be measured separately. Negative earnings, changing share counts or mixed forecast horizons require another representation rather than a forced P/E decomposition.

Enterprise-value multiples require an equally explicit bridge. Changing debt, cash, minority interests or business perimeter can move the relation between operating earnings and the value of common stock. A buyback is not a separate free return added on top of a per-share model that already includes its share-count effect.

### 4.2 Six economically distinct hypotheses

**Quantity of distributable cash.** Higher realization, better utilization or lower costs could improve cash, but the model must include the incremental investment and working capital. A revenue forecast revision alone is insufficient.

**Duration of profitable cash.** A more durable asset base or a favorable contract extension can add valuable later years. It can also commit the business to unattractive terms. The analysis must compare the future margin and required capital, not count contract years mechanically.

**Returns on reinvestment.** A business can grow and destroy value if new capital earns an inadequate return. Research should identify incremental rather than average economics and distinguish cash spending from total economic capital contributed.

**Risk borne by the common shareholder.** Commissioning, debt repayment or clearer contract terms could reduce a specific risk. The costs of achieving that reduction, including dilution, must be included. Merely reporting smoother accounting earnings is different.

**Repeatability and comparability of earnings.** Cleaner working-capital conversion, less reliance on exceptional items and stable measurement can increase confidence in an estimate. This is a hypothesis to validate with records, not a license to discard unfavorable GAAP numbers.

**Difference from expectations embedded in price.** Even genuine improvement may already be anticipated. This requires point-in-time forecasts and matched valuation/price evidence, which R2 does not have. Management guidance is not analyst consensus, a new contract is not necessarily a positive surprise, and a strong theme is not automatically a good entry.

### 4.3 Model family by economic business, not ticker label

| Business | Proposed valuation lens | Most dangerous shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Depleting producer | Asset/commodity scenarios with explicit replacement capital and attributable shares | Peak cash yield or standardized reserve measure treated as enduring fair value |
| Refiner | Normalized regional conversion cash after turnarounds and obligations | One-quarter crack spread capitalized indefinitely |
| Contracted infrastructure | Contract and renewal cash, project capital and financing, actual parent claims | Fee revenue called risk-free cash or a project cost multiple called valuation |
| LNG platform | Contracted and marketing components separated, project milestones and parent distributions | Capacity times a generic margin, ignoring purchases and other owners |
| Oilfield services | Service-line/region cycle and utilization, cash conversion, required investment | Aggregate rig count or acquired revenue used as organic demand |
| Capital-funded or turnkey service project | Contract-level cost, risk allocation, investment and distributions | Backlog or well awards multiplied by an assumed margin |

An eventual sum-of-parts analysis must reconcile shared costs, intercompany transactions, financing and valuation dates. It must not manufacture a premium by allocating every cost to a different segment from its revenue.

The same shock can have different signs across the chain and over time. Research should therefore name the mechanism, horizon and condition rather than attach one universal positive/negative sector score.

## 6. Leading indicators and an evidence-to-earnings workflow

### 6.1 Acquisition priorities

**First: contracts and issuer accounting.** Use filings, results and original project announcements to establish pricing basis, option holder, capital obligations, NCI, delivery timing and comparable business perimeter. Contract economics cannot be recovered reliably from ticker tags alone.

**Second: physical and commercial series.** Acquire benchmark and local commodity prices, product spreads, storage, flows, production, availability and service activity only through approved existing owners and licenses. Match units, delivery periods and publication clocks before calculating changes.

**Third: expectations and valuation history.** Obtain licensed point-in-time forecast vintages, corporate actions, prices, debt/cash and diluted shares. This is required before claims about surprises, re-rating attribution or valuation attractiveness. R2 defines the inputs; it does not pretend they are present.

### 6.2 Indicator contracts

| Indicator family | Research use | Required limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Commodity curves and local basis | Separate immediate realization from later-year economics | Spot is not a forecast for every contract year; location and units must match. |
| EIA product supplied | Approximate product demand and conversion-market conditions | Primary disappearance is a consumption proxy, not direct final-user measurement. [S19] |
| EIA gas storage | Observe inventories, seasonal changes and supply/demand balance | Observation time, reporting time, non-flow adjustments and revisions are distinct. [S20, S21] |
| Four-week product averages | Reduce noise in a declared series | Overlapping windows are not four independent confirmations. Missing-value codes remain missing. [S24] |
| Baker Hughes rig counts | Track defined drilling activity | Drilling is not completion activity; geography-specific definitions differ, including Saudi Arabia. [S22] |
| Issuer orders and backlog | Test customer spending and future delivery | Binding orders, reservations, awards, cost-to-complete and cash receipts are separate. |
| Guidance and reported results | Track management's dated operational and financial outlook | Guidance is not consensus; changed business perimeter and accounting need explicit bridges. |

The Baker Hughes methodology-change attachment was unavailable on the attempted read. Its effective-date history remains a gap rather than being inferred. The FAQ discusses public use with attribution subject to terms; production rights and retained-data permissions still require review through the existing owner. [S22, S23]

### 6.3 Proposed sequence from an observation to a useful interpretation

Start with a dated change in an eligible source. Resolve the affected business and commercial exposure. Determine which forecast period could change and what costs or claims offset the benefit. Compare the resulting estimate with the same prior horizon. Only after that compare against separately acquired expectations and price. Then show the existing technical/entry context without letting research override its owner.

The product should distinguish an observation, a management expectation, a derived estimate and an untested hypothesis in the main reading experience. A large evidence drawer does not substitute for a plain-language explanation of the economics.

### 6.4 Evaluation design, not an executed backtest

Before predictive use, freeze the hypothesis, universe, signal availability, horizon and benchmark. Use actual publication/knowledge times and original versus corrected vintages. Include acquisitions, closures, delistings and changing memberships rather than testing today's winners against their full history. Separate exposure to commodity movements from any incremental information in the researched factor.

Compare against simple baselines such as the corresponding commodity, parent industry and fixed-horizon estimate changes. Preserve non-overlapping or appropriately treated overlapping observations, out-of-sample evaluation and uncertainty.
