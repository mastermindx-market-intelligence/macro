## 1. The job and the material correction

The job is not to reproduce a Robotics bill of materials with Energy labels. It is to let a user move from an Energy development to an evidence-backed explanation of who earns more, how durable those earnings may be, what must be financed, what belongs to the common shareholder, what expectations have changed, and what would invalidate the thesis.

The proposed organizing chain is:

**physical or commercial change → contract/market exposure → unit economics → attributable earnings and cash → reinvestment and financing → per-share value → expectations and valuation → observed market response.**

Supply bottlenecks remain relevant only where this chain explains their economic transmission. A scarce component is not automatically profitable for its supplier. A growing end market is not automatically valuable for an equity holder. A favorable business outcome is not proof of a favorable entry price.

The final research program remains principal-led here across substantive tranches. Fable later receives the evidence, domain decisions, remaining bounded gaps and implementation/acceptance plan. No Fable or subagent research commission has been issued.

## 3. Separate the quantities the market narrative often collapses

### 3.1 Activity is not value

For every proposed beneficiary, distinguish physical output, invoiced revenue, gross contribution, operating profit, cash collected, maintenance capital, growth capital, financing, common-equity ownership and share count. A volume forecast does not determine any of the later quantities without more assumptions.

A conceptual operating model is:

`operating contribution = sum(quantity × realized net unit margin) − fixed operating costs`

This is a decomposition, not a universal accounting definition. Quantity and margin units differ by business: barrels, MMBtu, delivered MWh, accredited capacity, contracted pipeline capacity, equipment deliveries, module watts or service revenue. Do not add incompatible units or equate boe conversion with equal economic value.

### 3.2 Business growth and per-share growth are different

For a positive, consistently defined forward earnings measure:

`share price = forward EPS × forward P/E`

Holding the earnings horizon and accounting definition fixed makes the change decomposable. Moving from a nearer to a farther forecast year is not itself an analyst revision. A multiple change is descriptive; it is not proof that a narrative caused the change.

Illustrative arithmetic, not a forecast: 10% EPS growth with a 20% multiple contraction gives `1.10 × 0.80 − 1 = −12%` price change, before dividends. Likewise, a 10% increase in total attributable earnings with 20% more shares gives `1.10 / 1.20 − 1 = −8.33%` EPS change.

For enterprise valuation, first specify the convention and consolidation perimeter. Bridge operating enterprise value to common equity by reconciling net debt, leases where relevant, preferred claims, minority interests and non-operating assets. Do not subtract a liability twice or count an equity-accounted investee both in operating earnings and as an extra asset. Dividing by diluted shares is essential. Buybacks change cash and share count; they are not an additional free return on top of an already adjusted per-share valuation.

Use a discounted cash-flow or asset-level model when profit is negative, volatile, temporarily distorted or unrepresentative. A low trailing P/E on temporarily elevated commodity earnings is not sufficient evidence of cheapness. Present a range of normalized commodity/spread assumptions instead of selecting the convenient cycle point.

### 3.3 What re-rating means in this program

Reserve re-rating for a change in valuation attached to a comparable economic quantity. Investigate whether the change reflects cash-flow duration, cyclicality, financing risk, incremental returns, contract quality, competitive position or option value. Keep earnings revisions, a change in the valuation multiple, cash distributions and dilution as separate explanations.

This tranche does not establish current fair values, price targets, expected stock returns or a consensus gap for any issuer. No point-in-time analyst panel, matched valuation series or event-return study has been acquired here.

## 5. Primary-source cases that already change the design

### 5.1 EOG: compare volume bases and cash definitions

EOG's February capital plan described 2026 oil output as flat relative to Q4 2025 while still implying year-over-year growth. Its August release reported $2.8 billion of company-defined free cash flow for Q2 and separated oil, NGL and gas volumes. [E03,E04]

**Research consequence:** retain sequential run-rate, annual comparison and acquisition perimeter separately. Build realized-price and cost bridges by commodity. Keep the company's non-GAAP FCF definition, reconciliation and excluded items visible rather than treating all issuers' FCF labels as identical. Next work: asset/hedge/inventory reconciliation, not a claim of superior valuation.

### 5.2 Valero: a spread is not net income

Valero's Q2 operating table reported a $23.62 refining margin per throughput barrel and $16.56 adjusted refining operating income per barrel after the stated operating-cost and depreciation deductions. [E05]

**Research consequence:** distinguish benchmark spreads, realized refining margin, operating income and cash. Build regional crude/product exposure and planned/unplanned outage context. A falling feedstock price can help only through the actual product-cost relationship; a rising crude price is not enough to sign the equity effect. Renewable diesel and ethanol require separate feedstock, product and credit economics.

### 5.3 Cheniere: contractual infrastructure and commodity accounting coexist

The 2025 10-K generally describes LNG SPAs as a fixed fee plus a variable component of 115% of Henry Hub; the fixed component remains payable on customer cargo cancellation/suspension under the described terms. Q2 2026 results separately identify large derivative fair-value movements affecting reported net income. [E06,E07]

**Research consequence:** contract formulas, counterparties, physical delivery, marketing exposure and hedge-accounting timing need distinct fields. Neither revenue growth nor the net-income swing alone measures the change in recurring economic value. Preserve parent/subsidiary and non-controlling-interest attribution.

### 5.4 Kinder Morgan: project returns are not trading multiples

Kinder Morgan described a fee-based business with long-term contracts and disclosed a 5.6x first-full-year Project EBITDA multiple for a defined $8.5 billion subset of its project backlog, excluding specified businesses. [E08]

**Research consequence:** that project capital/EBITDA measure is not the company's traded EV/EBITDA and not a complete project IRR. Record its included assets, cost scope, start year, maintenance/tax/financing assumptions and commissioning dates. Do not code all fees as capacity reservations; volume-linked and take-or-pay obligations must be classified from contracts.

### 5.5 Duke: approved investment recovery and financing determine the equity result

Duke's Q2 earnings release attributed improvement to infrastructure investment recovery, with depreciation and interest offsetting part of the increase. Its August 11 financing announcement described $1.75 billion of equity units combining notes with future common-stock purchase contracts. [E09,E11]

**Research consequence:** track approved rate base, allowed/earned returns, revenue recovery, debt and future dilution together. The announcement does not by itself prove the offering closed. Historical Duke Indiana material separately demonstrates that a requested return and the approved return can differ. [E24]

Illustrative regulatory arithmetic: $1 billion of qualifying rate base × 53% regulatory equity × 10% allowed ROE implies $53 million of annual allowed equity return in this simplified model. It is neither total allowed revenue nor guaranteed common-shareholder cash, and no actual Duke forecast is inferred. A 100-basis-point ROE change changes that simplified allowance by $5.3 million, not $10 million. Do not subtract debt interest from this already equity-only return twice.

### 5.6 Vistra: the repricing clock matters

As of August 3, Vistra reported hedging approximately 100%, 94% and 72% of expected generation volumes for 2026, 2027 and 2028 respectively. [E12]

**Research consequence:** today's wholesale price cannot be mechanically applied to every future MWh. Preserve hedge horizons, contract prices, location/basis, output uncertainty and retail obligations. Hedged generation volume is not a statement that all corporate margins or risks are fixed. Decompose power-price, capacity, acquisition and share-count effects before calling a change a re-rating.

### 5.7 GE Vernova: useful bottleneck work must reach margin and cash

GE Vernova reported Q2 organic order growth of 88%, adjusted EBITDA margin of 11.3%, and 116 GW in a combined gas-equipment backlog/slot-reservation measure. The release distinguishes remaining performance obligations from the combined capacity measure. [E13]

**Research consequence:** separate binding orders, reservations, customer funding, cancellation rights and delivery slots. Research Power, Wind and Electrification separately. Require margin, working-capital, warranty and service economics alongside order growth. Do not annualize one quarter's reported FCF without checking cash timing; a detailed cash/deposit bridge is still owed.

### 5.8 Cameco: mining, procurement, delivery and reactor services are different

Cameco reported Q2 uranium deliveries of 7.1 million pounds, production of 3.9 million pounds attributable to it, and purchases of 2.8 million pounds. It also describes an uneven future delivery schedule under its contract book. [E14]

**Research consequence:** prices, volumes, inventory cost and third-party procurement must be modeled together, with Westinghouse treated at the correct ownership/accounting perimeter. Production is not the same as sales. The release's Fuel Services bullet uses a first-quarter label inside the Q2 announcement; those numerical details are excluded pending MD&A reconciliation. Primary-source provenance does not eliminate source-level errors.

### 5.9 First Solar: distinguish recurring margins, credits and exceptional items

First Solar's Q2 filing attributes gross-margin improvement to multiple factors, including a net expected tariff-refund benefit, more qualifying domestic production credits and logistics costs. Its release separately reports contracted backlog. [E15,E16]

**Research consequence:** create a unit-margin bridge with credits, ordinary costs, exceptional items and cash-collection timing separate. Do not capitalize a one-time refund as perpetual earnings or assume backlog is cancellation-proof. A manufacturer's technology/geography/contract exposure is not equivalent to a solar-project owner's exposure. This is financial interpretation of disclosed facts, not a political evaluation of any policy.

### 5.10 Enphase: shipment timing is not final demand

Enphase reported $291.9 million of Q2 revenue including $84.3 million of safe-harbor revenue, and identified a tariff-refund effect in GAAP gross profit. [E17]

**Research consequence:** distinguish company sell-in, installer/channel inventory and end-customer installation demand. Safe-harbor revenue is not automatically incremental recurring demand, and a non-GAAP adjustment is not automatically the correct normalized result. Establish a transparent recurring/non-recurring reconciliation and separate microinverter, battery and emerging-product economics.

### 5.11 Fluence: preserve negative evidence when the theme is strong

The August 5 Q3 release disclosed record $6.4 billion backlog and reduced the FY2026 revenue midpoint to $3.0 billion. On September 16 the company revised revenue to approximately $2.4 billion and adjusted EBITDA to approximately a $200 million loss, citing continued U.S. production difficulties. [E18,E19]

**Research consequence:** the September outlook supersedes the August forecast for current guidance, while both survive as point-in-time vintages. Order demand, shipment feasibility, cost-to-complete, cash conversion and liquidity belong on separate dimensions. A growing backlog can increase funding needs. The record does not establish an actual stock-return attribution.

### 5.12 SLB: end-market activity and profit conversion can diverge

SLB's Q2 release reported revenue up 5% year over year and adjusted EBITDA down 7%, with substantial geographic differences. [E20]

**Research consequence:** service-company exposure requires geography, product/service mix, customer budgets, utilization and margin conversion. Do not use one global oil price or aggregate spending number as sufficient evidence of profit acceleration. Acquisition, integration and comparison-base effects need a separate bridge.

## 6. Energy does not have one cycle

EIA's September outlook, finalized September 3 and released September 9, simultaneously projected tight U.S. distillate inventories, end-October natural-gas inventories 5% above the five-year average, and rising electricity sales. These are dated forecasts, not observed October outcomes and not a universal Energy stance. [E01]

IEA's Electricity 2026 demand work also separates regions and demand sources; electricity growth is not solely an AI/data-center phenomenon. [E02]

The proposed regime view therefore retains multiple axes: oil supply/demand and inventories; gas storage/basin balances; refining spreads; power demand and available capacity; capex/project delivery; financing; contract repricing; and sector-specific rules. These are not forced into one bullish/bearish label.

Conditional scenario research should ask the following rather than assign universal signs:

| Shock | Potential channel | Required qualifications |
|---|---|---|
| Higher oil benchmark | Higher upstream realizations | Local basis, hedges, royalties/taxes, depletion replacement, cost inflation and existing expectations |
| Higher natural-gas prices | Producer benefit; fuel-cost pressure elsewhere | Local gas basis, gas-indexed contracts, power-price pass-through, hedge book and retail exposure |
| Lower module/battery prices | Lower new-project cost; possible manufacturer pressure | Existing inventory cost, fixed-price procurement, customer repricing, credits, project finance and differentiation |
| More power demand | Generation, networks and supplier investment | Location, timing, funded/binding customer commitments, capacity versus energy need, interconnection and recovery terms |
| Lower discount rates | Higher present value of some long-duration cash flows | Fixed versus floating debt, refinancing dates, growth expectations, credit spreads and allowed-return resets |
| More infrastructure capacity | More delivered volume and potentially less scarcity | Who owns existing scarcity rents, customer volumes, new-entry margins and timing of contract repricing |
| Higher policy-related credits or compliance costs | Changes in project/manufacturer economics | Exact jurisdiction, eligibility, effective date, phaseout, cash realizability and contract allocation; no policy endorsements |

These are hypotheses to parameterize and test, not forecasts or causal relations admitted from thematic similarity.

## 7. Why solar/storage needs realized value, not a perfect model

Berkeley Lab's 2026 work examined the 2024 U.S. solar-plus-storage fleet and compared optimized dispatch with empirical operations. Its 51-project observed sample captured 62% of the modeled optimized value applicable to that sample. [E21]

The research lesson is not that every storage investment realizes 62% of a forecast. It is that perfect foresight, market access, dispatch incentives and contract restrictions materially affect what a project can actually capture. Do not compare the sample's observed value with a national model using a different population.

The GMI model should separate: resource potential; technical output; dispatch feasibility; market/system value; contracted payment; costs; debt/tax-equity distributions; and cash to common equity. The 2025 solar database is a candidate project-level reference, not proof of current 2026 asset economics or immediate production-ingestion rights. [E22]

## 8. Re-rating hypotheses and the evidence each needs

The following taxonomy is proposed analysis. It does not assert that any named stock will re-rate.

**Earnings-level revision.** Higher realized price, throughput or margins changes a matched-year earnings/cash forecast. Required evidence is a fixed-horizon forecast bridge, not a comparison of this quarter with a seasonally weak quarter.

**Cash-flow duration extension.** Contract renewal, reserve-life extension or repeat service revenue could increase the duration of realizable cash. Establish the contract/resource evidence, costs and counterparty quality before interpreting duration as value.

**Cyclicality reduction.** A changed contracted/merchant mix may alter earnings variability. Measure actual terms and downside cases; do not equate a long contract with an unconditional guarantee.

**Incremental-return improvement.** Better pricing, execution, unit costs or asset utilization may increase returns on new capital. Show the incremental investment, working capital, maintenance burden and cash taxes, not merely EBITDA growth.

**Funding and balance-sheet de-risking.** Commissioning, debt reduction or funded expansion can reduce financing risk. Include the cost of issuing debt/equity, maturity schedule, covenants, project recourse and dilution. Debt reduction funded by new shares is not free de-risking.

**Accounting or cash-quality normalization.** Recurring operating economics can differ from GAAP earnings or management-adjusted figures. Explicitly reconcile, retain both, and explain exclusions. The objective is comparability, not selecting whichever number supports the thesis.

**Development-option maturation.** Design, permitting, binding offtake, financing and operational milestones can change the range of plausible project outcomes before meaningful revenue. Do not invent success probabilities. Identify the exact risk retired and which risks remain.

**Market expectations or required-return change.** Even unchanged business forecasts can carry a different market valuation. Establish comparable-date prices, financial metrics, balance sheet and share count. No claim that the market has missed an opportunity is allowed without actual expectations evidence.

**Unwinding of an excessive valuation.** Strong operational growth can coincide with multiple contraction. Preserve this possibility for every positive thesis. Business quality and entry attractiveness remain separate native dimensions.

## 9. Three clocks and a commercial milestone ladder

For every material claim preserve at least an observation/period date, first public availability date and research retrieval date. Add effective/settlement or cash-delivery dates when different. A September document about June operations is not a September observation. Corrections and supersession append history rather than rewriting what a model could have known earlier.

The commercial ladder is not one universal linear pipeline. Still, distinguish inquiries, MoUs, reservations, binding contracts, financing, final investment decision, construction, commissioning, commercial operation, revenue recognition and cash collection. Some orders precede financing and some projects never reach operation. Backlog is not a probability-one cash forecast.

Each economic archetype requires its own catalyst clock: commodity repricing can differ from hedge roll; rate recovery can differ from construction spending; delivery can differ from backlog booking; uranium contracting can differ from fuel delivery. Equity prices can respond before, at or after those milestones. This tranche does not estimate that response timing from historical returns.

## 10. Research record and data acquisition design

### Proposed record dimensions

A future owner-compatible economic exposure record should connect existing issuer/security IDs to a sourced business/asset/contract scope. Include economic archetype; primary/secondary role; demand unit; price benchmark and location; realized-price formula; revenue and earnings exposure where actually disclosed; contract term and counterparty; costs and pass-through terms; operating volume/capacity basis; hedge horizon; maintenance versus growth capital; debt/lease/ownership perimeter; dilution; cash-recognition timing; catalysts; falsifiers; and provenance.

Unknown exposure percentages remain null. A qualitative role is not a fabricated 20% revenue weight. Geographic exposure can refer to production, manufacturing, customers or cash; label which. Do not infer a supplier/customer edge from companies appearing in the same basket.

Confidence must be split into source authenticity, measurement/comparability, causal interpretation and forecast uncertainty. A clearly authentic company forecast remains a forecast. Multiple syndicated copies are one underlying claim, not independent confirmation.

### Source acquisition sequence

Start with issuer filings and releases, regulator orders/tariffs, EIA physical series, grid-operator market/queue data and technical/project databases. Record access, revision behavior and redistribution rights before adopting an ingestion source. A public webpage does not establish permission to republish full text or commercial datasets.

Then acquire point-in-time expectations and security/valuation history through existing licensed/native owners. Essential fields are estimate as-of time, fixed forecast horizon, contributor count/coverage, dispersion, revisions, split/currency/accounting normalization and source rights. Management guidance is not consensus. No data acquired in this tranche establishes that this capability is currently available.

Private/full-fidelity research must use the existing authenticated/private owner path. Only authored summaries, source locators and research proposals are placed in this public branch. Do not put copied reports, paid data, raw corpora or private provider artifacts into Git-tracked public research/evidence files.

## 11. User workflow and information design

Within the existing Theme Tracker/basket/issuer journey, propose an **economics explanation** rather than another independent dashboard:

A user first selects the subtheme and sees what customers pay for, the main economic profiles, the current dated evidence and missing coverage. They can compare companies by the exposure actually documented. A company view explains a price/volume/cost bridge, contract and funding calendar, per-share reconciliation and counterevidence. A scenario comparison shows why the same shock can have different effects without assigning an unvalidated trading score.

Useful visual specifications are: a realized-economics bridge; contract/hedge maturity timeline; an exposure matrix with honest nulls; a project milestone view; matched-vintage forecast changes; and a risk/coverage panel. A supply network appears where it answers who captures or loses economics. Do not use Sankey widths unless a valid additive quantity is known.

The graph explains relationships; the economic bridge explains money; the expectation comparison explains what changed relative to prior beliefs; native price/entry context explains the separate market state. Provenance supports these jobs but does not replace synthesis.

## 12. Evaluation and acceptance requirements for the eventual build

These are proposed acceptance cases, not tests executed against the application in this tranche.

1. The user can distinguish a producer, refiner, contracted terminal, regulated utility and merchant generator exposed to the same commodity or demand shock.
2. Fixed-horizon EPS/multiple decomposition does not treat rolling a forecast year as an estimate revision.
3. Enterprise-to-equity and diluted-share reconciliation cannot double-count debt, leases, investees, minority interests or buyback effects.
4. Unit checks distinguish barrels/day from total barrels, power from energy, AC from DC and MW from MWh; commodity-equivalent units are not revenue weights.
5. A requested regulatory return cannot populate the approved-return field. Approved return cannot populate realized return without evidence.
6. Hedged expected volume does not imply all margins/risks are fixed, and a hedge observation is not applied before its availability date.
7. Backlog, reservations, binding orders, commissioned capacity and recognized cash cannot silently substitute for each other.
8. Refining margin is not presented as net income per barrel.
9. Parent/subsidiary and joint-venture attribution remain correct.
10. September Fluence guidance supersedes August guidance in a current view but both remain available to point-in-time replay.
11. First Solar/Enphase exceptional accounting items cannot become permanent margin assumptions without explicit analyst treatment.
12. Cameco's internally inconsistent quarter label is flagged rather than repaired by guessing.
13. Missing exposure, consensus, weights or causal strength remains unknown, not zero or fabricated confidence.
14. Observed and optimized project datasets cannot be combined across unmatched populations.
15. Theme membership alone grants no supplier relation, bottleneck claim, prediction or trade authority.
16. Authenticated publication, degraded/missing/stale states and source-rights restrictions are proven on the real production path when implemented.
17. Real user/browser proof shows a complete evidence-to-economic-explanation workflow, not merely a green build or static screenshot of synthetic data.
18. No ranking, entry, sizing or trading effect is introduced by this research vertical without separately accepted validation and authority.

For predictive evaluation later, predefine fixed event windows and economic/market baselines, preserve delisted and failed projects, avoid survivor selection, control for market/sector/commodity moves, use information available at the time, and distinguish association from causation.
