## 1. The question R3 answers

An investor reading that electricity demand is rising still needs to know which business can monetize the change, through which assets and contracts, after what investment, and over which years. A power producer, a utility, an equipment manufacturer and a construction company can participate in the same development without receiving equivalent economic benefits. They can also have opposite sensitivities to a particular cost or timing shock.

R3 therefore uses a common **physical scenario**, not a common stock conclusion. It examines merchant generation and retail through Vistra, regulated investment through Duke, contracted generation through Clearway, equipment and service through GE Vernova, and construction through Quanta. Eaton, Talen and AEP Ohio add three focused comparators: measurement horizons, capacity-payment arithmetic and large-customer tariff design. These examples establish a reusable research method; they are not a complete power-company census or a preferred-securities list.

The work makes three separate judgments possible:

1. **Demand credibility:** what has actually been observed or commercially committed, and what is only a forecast or proposal?
2. **Business capture:** what price, quantity, performance and financing conditions govern the cash received by this business?
3. **Security expectations:** how does the new evidence compare with a matched, historically available valuation and forecast baseline?

A positive answer to the first question does not settle the second, and neither establishes the third. That separation is the organizing principle. Supply shortages matter when they change price, delivery, cost, competitive position or the timing of cash. They are not an alternative to doing the economics.

### What is already resolved, and what remains open

The R1/R2 shareholder-cash framework remains in force. There is no need to recreate the Robotics template, another theme graph, or the hydrocarbon dossiers. R3 adds power-specific mechanisms and concrete negative examples. It does not acquire a point-in-time analyst panel, matching market prices, validated exposure weights, full company-level discounted cash-flow models or private contract schedules. Those absences constrain valuation conclusions, not the usefulness of the research.

## 2. Normalize the demand before attributing the benefit

### 2.1 One gigawatt is not a complete economic input

For an illustrative facility with 1 GW of maximum facility demand and a 90% load factor, a non-leap year implies 7.884 TWh of energy. If 1 GW instead refers to IT equipment and the assumed power usage effectiveness is 1.2, the same calculation gives 9.4608 TWh of facility energy. Those are different assumptions, not two additive sources of demand. A 2028 calendar has 8,784 hours rather than 8,760.

These are hypothetical calculations, not descriptions of any issuer's project. They establish the minimum input discipline: meter boundary, facility versus IT power, load factor, calendar, ramp, location and interruptibility. A peak load estimate should never silently turn into full-time energy consumption. Nor should a procurement commitment automatically become evidence that a building is operating at that level.

The same physical scenario must then be placed in a **specific market and service arrangement**. AEP Ohio's tariff cannot be applied to a Duke asset simply because both are utilities. A PJM capacity price cannot become universal North American power revenue. A renewable plant's annual production does not automatically deliver firm power at the hours a customer needs it. Each mapping needs its own accepted source and contract description.

### 2.2 Credibility is an evidence problem, not an invented score

A development can have an application, a completed engineering study, executed service terms, posted security, construction progress and eventually metered load. Those observations answer different questions. A large application may identify an opportunity but not establish financing, siting or energization. A security deposit may reduce one counterparty risk without proving the full construction schedule. Actual energization still does not establish that the mature ramp has been achieved.

The proposed product should expose these evidence facets under existing owners. It should not create another project lifecycle authority, assign a guessed probability to every request, or sum duplicate applications across utilities as independent final demand. The investor needs to see the known commitment and its limitations, not a synthetic certainty percentage.

PJM's January 2026 explanation illustrates why this matters. Its summer-2027 forecast was 160,451 MW, 3,735 MW below the preceding forecast vintage, while the operator continued to describe substantial longer-run growth. The explanation includes tighter treatment of large-load projects. This is a revision to a forecast, not a measured fall in electricity use. [P01]

### 2.3 A newer event cannot be inserted into an older model retrospectively

EIA's September outlook, released September 9 with a September 3 model cutoff, forecast electricity sales of 4,135 billion kWh in 2026 and 4,211 billion kWh in 2027. These are sales forecasts, not actual consumption or the separately scoped generation series. [P02]

On September 21, the Texas governor's office announced a directive to TCEQ to halt data-center permits while the ERCOT audit proceeds and requested an October 19 compliance update. The update date is not an automatic reopening date. The notice alone does not establish existing-site shutdowns, the cancellation of a named generation contract, or an equivalent restriction outside Texas. [P03]

The research implication is conditional and limited: a later, geographically scoped permitting development may require a new scenario for affected projects. It cannot already be contained in a model completed eighteen days earlier. It also cannot be used, without project evidence, to erase a national load forecast or declare that every proposed project is cancelled. Policy chronology belongs in the scenario assumptions; political approval or disapproval does not.

## 3. Separate the payment systems before comparing companies

A power investment can receive payments for energy delivered, capacity availability, ancillary services, a long-term commercial contract, a regulated revenue requirement, equipment delivery or construction progress. An integrated business can contain several of these at once. Our proposed analysis must identify the payer, recipient, performance obligation, unit and period before translating an observation into earnings.

| Commercial form to identify | Economic question | Research error to prevent |
|---|---|---|
| Merchant energy | What hourly price is captured after fuel and variable costs? | Annual hub price treated as realized margin in every hour. |
| Generation hedge | Which price and quantity are fixed, at which index and dates? | Expected-volume hedge coverage treated as an outage guarantee. |
| Capacity payment | How much accredited capacity cleared, at what rate, for which delivery year? | MW-day revenue confused with MWh revenue or net profit. |
| As-generated contract | Who retains production and curtailment risk? | Fixed price interpreted as fixed annual cash. |
| Fixed-shape supply | Who replaces power when actual generation is short? | Contract volume presumed to match actual generation. |
| Tolling or availability service | Who buys fuel and bears dispatch/performance obligations? | A generic merchant spark-spread model imposed on all contracts. |
| Regulated recovery | Which costs and capital can be recovered, when and under what order? | Announced spending immediately treated as approved rate base. |
| Equipment/service contract | When are cash, delivery, revenue and later service earned? | Customer deposits or production reservations treated as profit. |
| Construction arrangement | Who bears labor, material, productivity and change-order risk? | Awards or backlog multiplied by an assumed uniform margin. |

This is a research comparison grammar, not a replacement for owner-native contract definitions. Undisclosed commercial terms remain unknown. Legal terms, economic rights and accounting recognition should not be blended into one field.

### 3.1 An illustrative power-and-fuel bridge

Assume a gas plant has a heat rate of 7 MMBtu/MWh, an electricity price of $50/MWh and fuel priced at $3/MMBtu. Its contribution before all other costs is $29/MWh. If electricity rises to $70 while gas rises to $4, that contribution becomes $42: the improvement is $13, not $20. A different plant with a fixed electricity-sale price but unhedged fuel would have a different result.

Nothing in this illustration establishes a company's heat rate, contract formula or profit. It shows the importance of a matched fuel and power scenario. R2 already established the analogous issue for power-linked gas: the supplier's extra receipt can be part of the generator's extra cost. The same surplus cannot be counted twice as though both parties retain it.

### 3.2 An illustrative hedge-and-outage bridge

Suppose expected generation is 10 TWh and 8 TWh is fixed by a simple financial hedge at the same price point. Under deliberately simplified assumptions, a $20/MWh spot increase changes net energy receipts by $40 million on the remaining 2 TWh, not $200 million on all expected output. This excludes fuel changes, retail obligations, basis, collateral and hedge-option effects.

If actual output falls below the hedged quantity, the interpretation changes. In a separate illustration, a 1,000 MW outage lasting four hours requires 4,000 MWh of replacement energy. Buying that energy at $1,000 rather than a $50 reference costs an additional $3.8 million. That is only the replacement-price difference, not a complete outage loss estimate.

This is why the proposed dossier must distinguish expected-generation coverage, actual operational availability and the economics of the full retail/generation portfolio.

## 4. Merchant generation and retail — Vistra

### Verified source anchors

Vistra's August 7 release reports expected-generation hedges as of August 3 of approximately 100% for 2026, 94% for 2027 and 72% for 2028. Its 2027 midpoint opportunity is explicitly not guidance and uses October 31, 2025 market curves. The relevant forward measures exclude specified Cogentrix and Meta effects. [P10]

The January Meta announcement separates 2,609 MW of agreements into 2,176 MW of existing generation and 433 MW of uprates. Purchases begin in phases; the annual-filing excerpt further distinguishes the existing-output ramp from uprate deliveries beginning later and reaching their full level in 2034. The exact pricing and incremental shareholder cash are not established by these descriptions. [P11, P12]

### The economic model

Vistra should be researched as a collection of generation, hedging, retail and contract positions, not as one unhedged electricity-price exposure. The core analysis needs actual asset geography, generation shape, fuel, operational availability, expected and realized output, hedge books and retail obligations. A power-price increase can affect merchant receipts, hedge settlements, customer procurement costs and collateral at different times.

The investment question changes with the delivery year. In a heavily hedged near-term year, operating execution and acquisition integration may matter more to the current earnings revision than a new spot-price move. A later, less-hedged year can retain more repricing exposure. But a coverage percentage alone cannot establish its dollar value: hedge prices, basis, volume assumptions and the relevant operating portfolio are still needed.

The Meta agreements add a different question. A long contract can replace uncertain future market receipts with a specified commercial arrangement. That may improve duration or financing visibility, but it can also surrender some future merchant upside. The correct comparison is the contracted cash after required investment against a defensible no-contract scenario, not contracted revenue against zero.

Existing plants, asset preservation, uprates and new construction must remain separate. Paying an operating plant under a new contract is not the same as adding all of the contracted MW to the grid. Uprates require their own spending, milestones and operational evidence. A signed future contract can have present valuation relevance without being current revenue; the model should permit conditional future-value analysis while refusing premature operational claims.

### Re-rating mechanisms to investigate

Three mechanisms deserve separate tests. First, revisions to a fixed-year energy-margin estimate can change expected cash. Second, a demonstrably more durable contract mix can change the risk and useful life assigned to those cash flows. Third, proven output additions or operating improvements can change the profitable quantity available to sell. None is interchangeable with a higher broad electricity-demand forecast.

The potential strategic advantage to investigate is the ability to supply reliable output from already established assets and market access. The research still needs to establish the relevant site, commercial terms, available output and investment burden. An existing connection is not a license to assume unlimited incremental power or an automatically premium valuation.

### Strong countercase and monitoring design

The strongest countercase combines optimistic later power prices with underestimated obligations: fuel repricing, plant outages, replacement-energy purchases, basis risk, capital required for uprates and terms that shift benefits to customers. A hedged portfolio can also have cash collateral movements that differ sharply from its economic earnings.

The watch set should therefore include year-specific power/fuel curves, the dated hedge book, plant availability, actual versus expected generation, retail-load obligations, PPA effective dates, investment and commissioning evidence. The product must attach the old curve cutoff to a repeated 2027 opportunity rather than suggesting that an August release recalculated it using August prices. Acquisition status and portfolio changes need their own dates.

**Unresolved:** current regional hedge prices and shapes, later transactions, contract-level Meta economics, the full acquisition-perimeter bridge and matched expectations. No valuation target or present mispricing claim follows from this dossier.

## 5. Regulated investment and large-customer protection — Duke and AEP Ohio

### Verified Duke anchors and a critical scope correction

Duke's quarter discusses infrastructure recovery alongside depreciation and interest offsets. The relevant project excerpts distinguish Anderson's proposed 1,365 MW combined-cycle development from the separate Buck combustion-turbine project. Anderson's possible 1,170/100/95 MW ownership allocation is subject to final negotiation. Buck's construction discussion says CWIP would not enter rate base during construction and AFUDC would accrue instead. That treatment must not be transferred to Anderson simply because the sections appear in one filing. [P13–P15]

The full June filing exceeded the read tool's size limit; this dossier uses identified primary indexed excerpts and does not claim complete legal or regulatory review. The March approval announcement, April written-order date described later, and changing service targets remain distinct source events. [P14, P15]

### The regulated economic bridge

The analytical sequence is proposed spending, approved project and recovery treatment, actual construction cost, eligible capital, in-service timing, authorized financing structure, earned outcome and per-share funding. Corporate capex is only one input. A utility can spend more without immediately earning an approved return on every dollar, and accounting earnings during construction need not represent cash already collected from customers.

A deliberately hypothetical example makes the distinction concrete. Begin with a $1 billion asset, subtract $200 million of customer-funded construction under an assumed treatment that leaves $800 million eligible for return, apply a 53% equity share and a 10% allowed equity return. The model yields $42.4 million of annual allowed equity earnings, or $21.2 million for a simple half-year exposure. These are invented assumptions, not Duke or AEP allowed returns. The illustration is not a forecast of net cash or a full revenue requirement.

Customer-funded construction, refundable security and a minimum demand charge are different instruments. A deposit can secure a customer obligation without permanently financing the asset. A contribution can reduce capital funded by shareholders, but its precise rate-base and tax treatment requires the governing rules. The model must not automatically earn a shareholder return on amounts funded by someone else.

Financing determines whether operating growth reaches each continuing share. New debt affects interest and refinancing risk. New common shares change the earnings denominator. A sale of minority interests can fund investment while transferring part of the future cash claim. None of these is inherently good or bad without terms and a counterfactual. The useful question is whether the new investment creates sufficient attributable earnings and cash for its cost of capital and dilution.

### Construction accounting is not construction cash

Where AFUDC is accrued rather than collected through current rates, an accounting return during construction can coexist with an ongoing cash-funding requirement. Where a construction cost recovery mechanism is approved, timing can differ. The research must identify the exact project and jurisdiction before using either model.

The later valuation must also be internally consistent. A cash-flow forecast that includes financed construction costs should not mechanically capitalize an allowance and then count it again as an independent benefit. Likewise, an unlevered valuation cannot deduct interest in its cash flow and then discount as though financing had not been deducted. These are proposed modeling controls, not statements that a particular issuer has made an accounting error.

### AEP Ohio: a demand floor is not an energy-consumption promise

AEP Ohio's primary explanation describes service terms effective July 23, 2025, a ramp lasting up to four years, and an agreement clock tied to actual energization. The minimum billing demand uses a historical ratchet and a size-dependent contract formula, with an 85% cap on that size branch. The page also describes collateral and cancellation/buildout obligations. It is a summary, not the complete tariff schedule or a customer-specific bill. [P16]

For a hypothetical 100 MW contract, the size branch is 57.5 MW plus all capacity above 75 MW: 82.5 MW. It is not automatically 85 MW. A separate prior-demand ratchet or other applicable billing terms can matter. For 150 MW, the uncapped branch would be 132.5 MW, so the size-branch cap reduces it to 127.5 MW. These calculations exclude ramp and customer-specific historical observations and are not complete billing determinations.

Most importantly, a minimum **billing demand** does not establish that the customer must physically consume that fraction of annual electricity. Treating it that way could simultaneously overstate energy demand and misunderstand the utility's protection. The proposed GMI view should show the commercial payment safeguard separately from actual metered load.

### Re-rating questions and countercase

Potential improvements to examine include clearer approved recovery, shorter cash-recovery lag, credible customer commitments, better sharing of buildout costs and profitable incremental investment. A larger project pipeline alone does not establish any of these.

The countercase is a mismatch between promised demand and the capital committed to serve it, compounded by delay, cost escalation, disallowance, funding needs or unfavorable financing. The research should also examine whether a tariff shifts an identifiable risk, rather than assuming that every long agreement fully insulates shareholders.

**Unresolved:** complete current orders and tariff schedules, asset-specific actual costs, final ownership, earned-versus-allowed returns, customer histories and the funding/share-count path. Full project-specific financial and legal interpretation remains a separate evidence gate.

## 6. Contracted generation — Clearway

### Verified anchors

Clearway lowered its 2026 CAFD range from $470–510 million to $430–470 million. It reported Q2 adjusted EBITDA of $409 million, operating cash of $214 million and CAFD of $167 million. Its reported renewable generation/sales increased year on year, principally with growth investments, which is a different comparison from performance against the year's resource assumptions. [P21, P24]

The July 16 operating preview preceded the August 5 guidance revision. Its performance measures exclude equity-method investments, use different weightings and can include compensated but ungenerated power in compensable generation. The investor presentation also distinguishes the sponsor pipeline from assets owned by the listed company and contains section-specific historical dates in its appendices. [P22, P23]

### Why fixed-price contracting is not fixed cash

A contract can stabilize a unit selling price while leaving production, availability, curtailment or replacement obligations exposed. The plant's resource and the contract's delivery shape both matter. An as-generated agreement differs from a commitment to deliver a fixed quantity or profile. A capacity or tolling arrangement can allocate a different collection of risks again.

For a synthetic 100 MW solar project at 25% capacity factor and $50/MWh, annual energy revenue is $10.95 million. A 5% output shortfall reduces it to $10.4025 million. If fixed operating cash costs and principal/financing obligations together are held at $7 million solely for this illustration, residual cash falls from $3.95 million to $3.4025 million—approximately 13.86%. This is not a Clearway asset forecast. It shows why a modest revenue shortfall can be amplified in a leveraged residual-cash measure.

Consequently, the research should track volume quality even when price is contracted. High availability does not prove normal wind or sunlight, and physical generation differs from a measure including paid curtailment. Different population exclusions can explain apparently contradictory metrics without making either wrong.

### Cash available for distribution needs its own bridge

Clearway's company-defined CAFD reconciliation includes adjustments for working capital, investments, other owners' distributions, notes, maintenance spending and principal amortization. It should not be substituted for another issuer's free cash flow before growth, or for a generic operating-cash-minus-capex measure. Parent cash depends on financing and project restrictions as well as operating performance. [P21]

For valuation design, keep consolidated operations, equity-method interests, ownership-weighted capacity, tax-equity or minority claims, restricted cash and parent distributions on a consistent basis. The presentation's Mesquite Sky example is 340 MW gross and 170 MW at a stated 50% ownership weighting. That capacity arithmetic does not independently establish a 50% share of every cash distribution at every point in a financing waterfall. [P23]

Sponsor pipeline opportunities require another boundary. A sponsor development can provide a future acquisition opportunity, but the listed company may still need an offer, negotiation, approval, capital and actual closing. Adding the entire sponsor pipeline to the public company's operating portfolio would fabricate exposure before the transaction occurs.

### Contract improvement can leave legacy obligations

The company describes Texas contract restructurings alongside in-substance financing used to settle existing derivative liabilities over time. The research should preserve both the improved future commercial terms and the retained obligations. A new PPA is not evidence that the old economic burden disappeared without cost. [P21, P24]

This is relevant to re-rating hypotheses. A contract may improve future revenue quality, but incremental value still depends on the price, the cost of restructuring, required capital, output and funding. Longer duration can add value or lock in unattractive economics; the answer is conditional, not inherent in the label.

### A source-first leading-indicator case, not a proven trading signal

July 16 to August 5 is a twenty-day disclosure interval. The earlier operating preview is an observable research input before the later formal guidance change. That gives Mastermind a concrete workflow to build: identify new operating evidence, match its scope to the existing financial assumptions, flag the exposed forecast and preserve the subsequent revision.

It does not establish a twenty-day information advantage over the market, a surprise, or a profitable strategy. The preview was public. Prices and analyst forecasts may have adjusted immediately. Any future test needs first-known timestamps, estimates available at those times, market returns and controls for other events.

### Countercase and monitoring design

A growing asset base can raise year-on-year output even while comparable resource disappoints. A lower CAFD midpoint can coexist with a credible long-term pipeline. The system should show this difference in horizon and population rather than choose one headline as the overall truth.

Monitor resource versus the relevant baseline, availability, uncompensated curtailment, contract shape, debt service, distributions, repowering commitments and funding. Preserve the specific date of any appendix assumptions. An August presentation containing an October 2025 sensitivity cannot make that sensitivity an August calibration merely by republishing it.

**Unresolved:** site-level PPA and curtailment terms, complete cash waterfalls, resource normalization, acquisition economics and a matched expectation history.

## 7. Equipment and service — GE Vernova, with Eaton as a comparator

### Verified GE Vernova anchors

GE Vernova reported $24.2 billion of Q2 orders and 116 GW of combined gas-equipment backlog and slot reservations. Its filing separates equipment and service RPO: $87.821 billion and $88.463 billion. Customer obligations, reservations and manufacturing run-rate targets therefore require different measures from installed grid capacity. [P17, P18]

The February acquisition of Prolec's remaining stake involved $5.254 billion of cash and a $3.992 billion noncash pretax remeasurement gain. June contract-liability/deferred-income balances were $40.037 billion versus $25.950 billion at year end. Those rounded balances and the source's reported change have a small rounding difference; acquisition effects also matter. None of these measures is automatically recurring operating profit. [P18]

### The order-to-cash model

Equipment economics need separate order, reservation, backlog, production, delivery, revenue, collection and service observations. A customer may reserve a production slot without all terms matching a firm order. A firm order may require capacity expansion and working capital. A delivery can seed later service revenue, but warranty, utilization and service attachment determine the value of that installed base.

The useful bottleneck question is therefore not simply whether turbines or grid equipment are scarce. It is whether the supplier can price new work attractively, manufacture it at the assumed cost, fulfill contracts on time and convert the installed base into profitable service. Manufacturing expansion can increase future throughput while producing near-term spending, ramp inefficiency or later excess capacity. Those are scenarios to test, not predictions of the current company outcome.

An output run-rate expressed in GW per year is also not the same object as a stock of operating generation measured in GW. The system needs a dimensional guard. If it adds a factory's projected annual output to an existing plant fleet, its supply map becomes physically misleading before valuation even begins.

### Customer-funded working capital: useful financing, not free profit

A deposit can improve funding before the associated work is complete. In a synthetic contract priced at 100 with total cost 80, a deposit of 30 and early spending of 20 create an initial positive cash balance of 10. With the remaining receipt of 70 and later cost of 60, total life-of-contract cash is 20. The early cash is not additional profit on top of the final margin, and the example does not prescribe revenue recognition under an actual accounting contract.

The economic advantage to investigate is whether customer advances reduce external funding needs on profitable work. The counterquestion is what delivery, refund or performance obligations remain. A liability balance is a stock; its change is not identical to a cash-flow-statement contribution, particularly when acquisitions and noncash effects are present. H1 cash and Q2 cash must also not be mixed. The H1 cash-flow statement reports a $13.695 billion contribution from contract liabilities/current deferred income alongside $10.680 billion of operating cash. This source-defined contribution is not the balance-sheet change and is not independent profit. [P18]

Likewise, a large service RPO may indicate long-duration obligations and potential revenue, but contract profitability and timing still matter. Cumulative expected recognition percentages must not be added as though each were a separate annual rate. RPO is neither the entire future sales forecast nor a guarantee of earned margin.

### Eaton exposes a different measurement trap

Eaton's quarter reports 21% sales growth, including 14% organic growth and a 7% acquisition contribution. Electrical Americas' 41% organic order increase is a trailing-twelve-month measure, not a quarterly rate. Total segment margin fell year on year while Electrical Americas margin improved sequentially. Electrical Global's reported growth also includes the acquired Boyd Thermal contribution. [P25]

These distinctions make Eaton a useful comparator rather than a duplicate equipment case. The proposed engine must recognize whether a number describes a flow, a stock, a rolling window, a segment or the consolidated company. Improving sequential margins can be entirely compatible with a lower margin than a year earlier. Calling both “margin expansion” without the comparator would lose the most important information.

### Re-rating questions and countercase

Potentially material changes include better pricing on genuinely new work, more durable profitable service, improved manufacturing execution and financing provided by customers rather than shareholders. Their valuation relevance depends on capital requirements, contract obligations and what was previously expected.

The countercase includes low-margin legacy orders, warranty or ramp costs, customer delays, a cash-flow tailwind that reverses, acquisition-driven apparent growth and capacity additions that eventually erode scarcity pricing. Track these separately instead of treating all equipment exposure as a permanent high-multiple service business.

**Unresolved:** reservation-to-order conversion, contract-level margins, cancellation rights, service attachment, normalized advances, acquired versus organic backlog and the matched valuation baseline.

## 8. Engineering and construction — Quanta

### Verified anchors

Quanta's Q2 backlog was $53.440496 billion versus $33.554155 billion of RPO. The broader backlog includes estimated MSA work and renewals; it is not simply another name for RPO. Fixed-price revenue was $6.083463 billion of $9.556997 billion total quarterly revenue. The filing also reported $411.1 million of unapproved change orders and claims already recognized in revenue. [P19, P20]

These are different scopes: backlog, a contractual revenue measure, revenue mix and recognized claims. They must not be added or assigned one shared certainty label.

### Demand becomes shareholder value through execution

Construction receives the demand signal through specific work packages and schedules. Labor availability, productivity, material procurement, subcontractor performance, permitting and changes in scope determine the cost to complete. A fixed-price contract, a unit-price arrangement and cost-plus work allocate those risks differently. Higher demand can improve utilization while also tightening labor and material markets.

In a hypothetical fixed-price job with price 100 and expected cost 90, expected profit is 10. A 5% increase in cost takes cost to 94.5 and profit to 5.5, a 45% decline, assuming no recovery from the customer. That is not Quanta's forecast margin or evidence that its actual contracts lack protections. It demonstrates why cost inflation cannot be evaluated solely through revenue growth.

The strategic advantage to investigate is reliable execution at scale: whether workforce, customer relationships and operating processes permit profitable delivery when projects become more demanding. The research must distinguish an evidenced capability from an assumed moat. A large workforce alone does not establish pricing power, and a record backlog does not prove cost control.

### Billing and revenue are separate evidence

A contractor can receive advances before performing work, recognize revenue over time before final billing, and have claims awaiting approval or collection. These differences can be economically normal, but they are essential to cash analysis. The product should not equate revenue recognition with collected cash; nor should it automatically label every unapproved claim a loss or wrongdoing.

Quanta's source-specific DSO measure nets certain contract liabilities against receivables and contract assets. Comparing it with a simple receivables-only DSO for another company would manufacture a false quality comparison. Acquisitions also affect the balances and operating perimeter. [P20]

The correct research bridge follows revenue, cost-to-complete estimates, contract assets and liabilities, agreed change orders, collections, and any reversals or revisions. A decline in a claims balance requires its own explanation: cash collection, settlement, reclassification and estimate changes are not interchangeable.

### Re-rating questions and countercase

Potential improvements to examine include profitable conversion of firm work, a better risk-sharing mix, demonstrated labor productivity, repeatable customer demand and cash conversion after acquisitions. A stronger order book can reduce uncertainty about activity without reducing every execution risk.

The countercase combines optimistic backlog conversion with underestimated fixed-price cost exposure, delayed customer approvals, acquired growth and a cash-flow benefit from advances that does not repeat. The system should identify which part of the thesis depends on an estimated MSA renewal and which part rests on an existing performance obligation.

**Unresolved:** full contractual escalation rights, cost-to-complete revisions, the collection history behind claims, acquisition-adjusted productivity and profitability of the different work families.

## 9. Capacity-revenue decomposition — Talen's two delivery years

Talen reported 8,745 MW clearing at $333.44/MW-day for June 1, 2027 through May 31, 2028, and later 10,180 MW at $325/MW-day for June 1, 2028 through May 31, 2029. The latter is the July 2026 auction result, not a forecast copied from the older auction. The company also reported a changed portfolio, including the June Cornerstone closing. [P07–P09]

The arithmetic is:

`gross capacity revenue = cleared capacity × price per MW-day × delivery days`

The first period has 366 days; the second has 365. The disclosed inputs imply approximately $1.067231 billion and $1.207603 billion respectively, consistent with the issuer's rounded $1.067 billion and $1.208 billion. That is approximately 13.15% more gross capacity revenue despite a 2.53% lower price. Cleared MW increased approximately 16.41%; the calendar also changed.

This does not prove organic growth, incremental net income or a current cash receipt. Performance requirements, hedges, costs, ownership, acquisition consideration and the portfolio counterfactual remain separate. The value of the example is its ability to reject a plausible but false explanation: rising corporate capacity revenue does not necessarily imply a higher market capacity price.

PJM's contemporaneous rule explanation also matters. The observed 2028/2029 clearing price sits within a defined cap/floor framework. It should not be read as an unrestricted measure of scarcity, and a hypothetical uncapped outcome is not the settlement received by the company. [P04–P06]

The unit guard is equally important. MW-day capacity revenue is not energy revenue per MWh. The product should show the delivery-year ladder and the applicable accreditation rather than apply the auction price to nameplate capacity or an annual energy-volume forecast.

## 10. Compare the same demand scenario without creating a universal winner

The following table is an analytical experiment, not a ranking or forecast. It holds the physical demand premise broadly constant while changing commercial rights and constraints.

| Conditional scenario | Generator/retailer question | Utility question | Equipment/construction question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Credible demand ramps on schedule | Which years and locations retain net unhedged margin after fuel and retail? | Which approved assets and recoveries serve the new load? | Which binding work converts profitably and with what cash timing? |
| Project is delayed after orders/security | Which contracts defer delivery, preserve fees or require replacement? | Who pays for stranded buildout and what returns are delayed? | Can production/work be rescheduled, and who absorbs inventory and idle capacity? |
| Demand rises but fuel/labor costs also rise | What happens to net margin and procurement exposure? | Which costs pass through, with what lag? | Which contracts allow escalation or unit-price recovery? |
| New supply expands faster than expected | Which later merchant years lose scarcity value? | Does new investment still earn its approved return? | Does a stronger installed base offset less favorable new-order pricing? |
| Resource disappoints with fixed-price PPAs | Is delivery as-generated or fixed-shape, and who bears the shortfall? | What procurement and reliability costs are recoverable? | Are warranty, maintenance or construction obligations implicated? |
| Financing becomes more expensive | What debt/refinancing claims sit ahead of equity? | How much new equity or debt is required per increment of earnings? | Do customer advances offset funding, and does customer financing affect orders? |

A disciplined model may find a favorable near-term outcome for a supplier alongside a delayed cash outcome for the asset owner. That is not a contradiction. The supplier might be collecting against manufacture while the owner still funds construction. It also should not produce double-counted aggregate value: one party's receipt may be another's investment, operating cost or liability.

Nor should the model require current operations before allowing meaningful research. New information can change the value of conditional future cash today. The requirement is to state the conditions and the comparison baseline, not pretend that no value exists until the first invoice is paid.

## 11. What can change earnings, value duration and the multiple?

### 11.1 Earnings and cash revisions

For merchant assets, a revised same-year margin assumption can arise from power and fuel prices, hedges, availability or contract mix. For utilities, a new approved recovery or revised financing path can change attributable earnings. For equipment, price/cost execution and conversion can move profit; advances can move cash timing separately. For construction, scope and cost-to-complete revisions can dominate activity. For contracted generation, output and debt service can change residual cash even with a stable contract price.

Those mechanisms should be modeled separately before combining them. Management guidance, analyst consensus and an internally authored scenario are three different inputs. A repeated management opportunity is not a new independent forecast print. A forecast horizon that rolls from one year to another should not be interpreted as a same-year upward revision.

### 11.2 Duration and quality

The relevant quality question is not whether the business sounds defensive. It is how much uncertainty remains in the attributable cash stream: commercial rights, customer credit, performance, replacement spending, refinancing and the terminal period after a contract expires. A longer contract may reduce one uncertainty while increasing opportunity cost or future investment obligations.

The research should test whether newly evidenced durability remains valuable after its price and financing cost. For merchant-to-contracted transitions, compare with a no-contract merchant scenario. For utilities, compare earned returns with the incremental funding burden. For suppliers, separate recurring service from one-time equipment. For contractors, distinguish repeatable profitable work from a temporarily favorable order environment.

### 11.3 Multiple re-rating is an empirical question, not a narrative label

An improving business can already be priced for even stronger performance. A share-price rise can reflect higher earnings expectations, a higher valuation multiple, lower debt, repurchases or factors outside the theme. A higher multiple during depressed earnings can also be a denominator effect rather than evidence that the market has assigned a superior franchise value.

The later study needs matched prices, diluted shares, net debt and other claims, the same earnings/cash definition and horizon, and historically available expectations. It should also use relevant regional power/fuel and broader market comparisons. The purpose is not to label all residual price movement as thematic alpha, but to test competing explanations.

R3 does not provide that panel. It provides the driver hypotheses, the accounting and contract controls and the countercases needed to build a valid study. No calibrated probabilities, current undervaluation verdicts, price targets or trading authority are claimed.

## 12. Leading indicators and product workflow

The proposed monitoring layer should be organized by the decision it can update, not by whichever data is easiest to collect.

| Research question | Candidate leading evidence | Necessary limitations |
|---|---|---|
| Is demand becoming more credible? | Signed service terms, deposits, studied delivery capacity, construction and metered ramp | Prevent duplicate project counts; separate forecasts from observations. |
| Is merchant margin changing? | Matched power/fuel curves, hedge vintages, hourly generation and availability | Preserve location, shape, tenor, collateral and retail offsets. |
| Is utility investment earning? | Asset-specific orders, authorized treatment, in-service dates and financing | Requested, approved, collected and earned are not synonyms. |
| Are contracted assets meeting the cash plan? | Resource previews, availability, compensated curtailment, debt schedules | Match portfolio scope and distributions; do not annualize one quarter. |
| Are equipment orders valuable? | Firm order terms, reservation conversion, costs, advances and deliveries | Backlog and RPO do not establish margin; acquisition effects matter. |
| Is construction converting? | Contract mix, work milestones, change approvals, claims and collections | Distinguish estimated MSA work from obligations and recognized revenue from cash. |
| Is new information already reflected in forecasts? | Licensed historical estimates and exact publication timestamps | No hindsight backfill or management-guidance substitution. |

Candidate data feeds must use the existing data/evidence owners and their rights, revision and clock contracts. This dossier does not create new collectors, queues or storage. It also does not establish permission to retain full issuer reports or redistribute paid data. Only authored synthesis and source locators belong in this research package.

### A concrete user journey

The eventual investor should be able to start in Theme Tracker, open a power-demand subtheme, select a business model, and see a source-bound explanation of what changed, how the company can monetize it, when cash could arrive, what must be invested and what would weaken the interpretation.

That sentence is illustrative product copy, not an accepted description of an entire current portfolio.

A research-defined economic profile is not automatically a new canonical theme or tradable basket. A company can participate in multiple profiles without invented exposure weights.

Keep thesis health, fundamental evidence, market expectations and entry context as named dimensions. ## 13. Research limitations and the next substantive work

This tranche is broad enough to reject many misleading power-beneficiary narratives, but it is not the end of the Energy program. Full current tariff schedules and project orders, private PPA economics, matched regional hedge prices, comparable organic series, complete ownership waterfalls and company-specific replacement capital remain gaps. The Duke filing limitation is explicit. A section-specific source correction is preferable to a fabricated universal fact.

Several regions and business forms also require later coverage. R3's U.S.-weighted examples cannot stand in for European power-market arrangements, Canadian regulated or contracted assets, emerging-market distribution risks, hydro resource economics or every independent producer. Global expansion should reuse the commercial questions while respecting local market structures rather than export the U.S. examples as law.

The next main research tranche is R4: nuclear fuel and services, renewable manufacturing versus development versus ownership, storage dispatch and integration, and transition technologies. It should retain the same distinction between physical demand, commercial capture, capital and attributable cash. R5 must acquire or explicitly resolve the availability and licensing of point-in-time expectations and valuation history. R6 then freezes the owner-compatible product design, implementation sequence and real-path acceptance cases.

Before any publication of this portable R3 candidate, reconcile #7791's current head and cumulative checkpoint. Preserve newer authored work, merge only genuinely additive findings, and do not create a competing R3 package. Fable remains the later build orchestrator; the Energy research/design mission is incomplete and the Fable handoff remains unissued.
