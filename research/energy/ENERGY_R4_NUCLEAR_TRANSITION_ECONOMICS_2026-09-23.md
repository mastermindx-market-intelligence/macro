## 1. The principal conclusion

Energy demand is not the same thing as the profit pool, and the profit pool is not the same thing as the existing shareholder's claim. R4 tests that distinction in businesses where the transformation is particularly easy to misread: future nuclear capacity, customer-funded equipment, policy-sensitive manufacturing, capital-recycling developers, storage and hydrogen.

The useful sequence is:

**Demand or technology change → commercially addressable activity → contractual right to payment → achievable unit contribution → capital and funding → attributable future cash → current security expectations.**

The central research question is not whether an industry will be larger. It is whether a particular business can increase cash per continuing share at acceptable incremental investment and risk, and whether that improvement is different from what the security already discounts.

This produces three separate outputs. First, describe the economic mechanism. Second, identify evidence supporting or weakening it. Third, test expectations and valuation using matched historical information. R4 materially develops the first two. The third still requires R5 data; no current cheap/expensive, surprise, price-target or alpha conclusion is established here.

### Proposed economic lenses, not a new taxonomy authority

| Research lens | What must be valued | What must not substitute for it |
|---|---|---|
| Resource producer and procurement book | Realized contract margins, resource replacement and purchased supply | Spot commodity price times all deliveries |
| Fuel-service provider and capacity expansion | Current contracted spread plus conditional future commissioned economics | All backlog treated as funded current revenue |
| Nuclear technology or engineering vendor | Licensing/engineering/product rights and development obligations | Plant electricity cash belonging to another entity |
| Nuclear components and services | Program mix, contract performance, installed base and incremental capital | One multiple for every nuclear-related business |
| Renewable manufacturer | Product contribution, credits, utilization, warranty and cash conversion | Installation growth or accounting margin alone |
| Developer/constructor/asset owner | Development proceeds, construction risk and retained operating cash | Cash from a sale plus all of the sold asset's future cash |
| Storage integrator | Delivery economics and continuing service obligations | Automatic ownership of the battery's trading income |
| Storage owner | Feasible dispatch, contract payments, degradation and competition | Perfect-foresight spreads or capacity alone |
| Geothermal operator/supplier | Site resource and sustainable net output versus equipment margin | A pilot or surface product as proof of a commercial reservoir |
| Fuel-cell and hydrogen businesses | Fuel-specific delivered economics and financing | A common technology label or gross break-even |

These lenses can coexist inside one issuer. They extend the research description consumed by existing GMI owners; they do not create a second graph or force economic relationships into canonical themes without the accepted crosswalk.

## 2. Nuclear: contracts, capital and the correct asset

### 2.1 Cameco — higher realized prices can still mean weaker unit economics

**Resolved evidence.** The Q2 website's Fuel Services highlight describes a first-quarter comparison, and its embedded link opens the Q1 report. The actual Q2 MD&A, printed page 28, reports Q2 realized price of C$41.67/kgU versus C$36.79 and unit cost including depreciation of C$33.06 versus C$27.32. Q2 Fuel Services earnings before tax were C$30 million versus C$44 million; adjusted EBITDA was C$42 million versus C$57 million. The correct report's cover and relevant tables were visually checked. This resolves the R1 source-period question, not every underlying valuation question. [S01, S02]

**Economic interpretation.** The reported price-cost spread is C$8.61 rather than C$9.47, approximately 9.08% lower. It is a blended segment measure, not EBITDA per unit and not a pure conversion-market quote. The correct research response is to investigate cost and product mix, utilization, delivered volume and contract vintage—not to infer that every fuel-service franchise deteriorated by exactly that percentage.

Mining, purchased uranium, conversion/fabrication and the Westinghouse investment must also remain separate. The Q2 revenue note distinguishes fixed and market-related sales, but the resulting revenue proportions are not volume proportions or forward commodity sensitivities. The segment presentation eliminates displayed Westinghouse sales in the equity-method adjustment column. Adding those sales to consolidated revenue would count the investment incorrectly. [S02]

**What could improve earnings or valuation?** A favorable replacement-contract cycle could improve later realized prices before it appears fully in a current-quarter average. More reliable production could reduce the need for expensive outside purchases. Better utilization could spread fixed costs over more deliverable output. Repeatable service or engineering income could change the duration and volatility of attributable cash. Each hypothesis needs its own contract and cost evidence.

**What would weaken the thesis?** Lower-value product mix, replacement purchases, higher costs to sustain output, or early cash from a nonrecurring project could absorb the apparent benefit. A rising spot price is not sufficient evidence of a proportional earnings increase; a large opportunity pipeline is not booked business.

**Required model.** Retain separate production, purchased supply, inventory and deliveries; sales-contract vintages and formulas; segment-specific units/currency; replacement capital; investee earnings and distributions; and the common-shareholder cash bridge. Compare like-for-like measures before asking whether a different multiple is justified. Do not subtract a US-dollar prior-period project contribution from a Canadian-dollar earnings comparison without a justified currency bridge.

### 2.2 Centrus — funding progress creates value only through the rights it buys

**Backlog evidence.** The June backlog was approximately $4.5 billion: $3.7 billion in LEU and $0.8 billion in Technical Solutions. Within the LEU amount, $3.0 billion was contingent, including $2.4 billion of definitive agreements tied to conditions for new capacity. Technical Solutions includes funded, unfunded and option-dependent work. The new $900 million enrichment award and options under the earlier operating contract are different contract objects. [S03]

A definitive signature can strengthen customer commitment while leaving construction, financing or delivery conditions unresolved. Conversely, contingent business is not automatically worthless. The research should represent the obligations, dependencies, earliest payment, and what changed in the probability or timing of future cash without inventing a calibrated probability.

**New financing evidence.** September's offering combined 500,000 common shares, pre-funded warrants for 2,005,513 shares and common warrants for up to 6,992,382 shares. The pricing release expected a September 11 close; transaction counsel subsequently confirmed that date. The 8-K separately describes issuer cashless-exercise election rights and other warrant terms. Closing therefore is established by a later participant source, but a post-close cash balance and subsequent warrant exercises are not established here. [S04–S06]

**Financing interpretation.** The upfront raise can reduce a financing obstacle, while the securities alter the existing shareholder's claim. The correct comparison is not 'new money good' versus 'dilution bad.' It is value created or risk reduced by the financing, less fees and the value of rights issued, compared with the credible no-financing alternative. Future warrant exercise proceeds cannot be booked as present funds; cashless provisions also mean exercise is not a universal promise of equivalent new cash.

**Commercial update.** September supply announcements with Radiant and Antares include prepayment provisions and deliveries expected before decade-end. They improve the specificity of the customer pipeline, but this source set does not disclose the full volumes, prices, protections or actual prepayment receipts. The Radiant webpage and syndicated dateline differ by one date; intraday first-known time remains unresolved. [S07, S26]

**Re-rating hypotheses.** Construction milestones, funded customer commitments, lower capital required to complete, and a demonstrated operating margin could progressively turn conditional capacity into a more observable cash-producing business. The countercase is that delays, supplier costs, funding gaps, dilution, customer timing or less attractive contracts consume that progress. Current fuel-supply economics must not be replaced with a valuation of a fully built expansion.

**Required model.** Use separate current and future business cases; a conditional-contract schedule; funds received versus committed; cost to complete; commissioning dates; restricted cash; and instrument-specific share rights. Preserve the distinction between a valuable financing event and proof that the whole project is financed or economically attractive.

### 2.3 BWXT — nuclear demand does not remove segment and acquisition effects

**Evidence.** Q2 government revenue was $601.3 million versus $589.0 million, while government operating income was $105.7 million versus $109.4 million. Commercial revenue included acquired activity. The PCG acquisition closed July 1, after Q2, and was included in the revised forward outlook. The medical-business disposal was announced; the cited release does not by itself prove closing. [S08]

**Economic interpretation.** There are different earnings engines inside the issuer: manufacturing programs, commercial components, services and the changing business perimeter. Government program visibility is not the same thing as price immunity. Contract adjustments, utilization, mix, productivity and capital expansion can move earnings differently from sales. An acquisition can broaden addressable activity without improving comparable-business growth.

**Re-rating hypotheses.** Evidence of repeatable program execution, profitable commercial expansion and a more recurring service mix could change expectations about through-cycle cash. Greater visibility is valuable only after the cost of providing it. A company with contracted work may still require substantial spending to increase capacity.

**Counterevidence.** Weaker margins, unfavorable estimates at completion, labor constraints, acquisition consideration or integration costs can offset stronger demand. Selling a business can simplify the portfolio but also remove cash; the sale price and retained earnings must be reconciled.

**Required model.** Separate organic versus acquired growth; pre- and post-acquisition periods; contract-risk types; government/commercial/medical scope; invested capital; disposals; financing and share count. Do not assign an entire issuer the multiple of its fastest-growing nuclear activity.

### 2.4 NuScale — licensing and engineering are not plant ownership

**Evidence.** The Q2 release describes ENTRA1/TVA discussions toward a definitive PPA and outstanding project conditions, rather than establishing a fully executed operating project. It attributes part of the revenue comparison to earlier FEED work completing in 2025. The NRC's US460 standard design approval allows the design to be referenced in licensing applications; it is not a site operating license. The product description uses conventional light-water-reactor fuel, so 'all SMRs require HALEU' is an invalid dependency. [S09–S11]

**Economic interpretation.** A technology provider's income may arise from engineering, licensing, module supply or other commercial rights. The value of an eventual electricity plant may belong to a different owner. Research must follow the signed economic rights rather than multiply potential megawatts by a merchant-power margin and allocate it all to the vendor.

Milestones can still have value before plant revenue begins. A design approval may remove one uncertainty; a site authorization, customer contract, funding package and delivery milestone may remove different ones. Their valuation effects are not identical and cannot be expressed through a generic percent-complete score without evidence.

**What could improve the economic case?** A repeatable module margin, credible customer financing, binding commercial terms and evidence that subsequent projects require less incremental engineering could reduce uncertainty around a replicable business. More projects are not automatically better when every project requires large vendor-funded support or resets the design.

**Countercase.** Nonbinding demand, customer schedule changes, unrecovered engineering, supplier commitments made ahead of firm orders and future capital raises can dilute the apparent value of the opportunity set. Lower current engineering revenue is not automatically evidence that a future plant was cancelled; the original work scope may simply have ended.

**Required model.** Bind every opportunity to technology vendor, developer, owner, customer and economic agreement; retain separate permits and financing; distinguish intellectual-property optionality from delivered product economics. No invented project-success probability, market share, target multiple or current PPA revenue.

### 2.5 Oklo — a real milestone must stay attached to the right reactor

**Evidence.** The August announcement reports first criticality for the Groves isotope test reactor. It is not an announcement that an Aurora electricity project entered commercial grid operation. [S12]

The research should credit the demonstrated milestone without transferring its proof to another asset or product. A test result can improve knowledge, supplier readiness or confidence in a relevant development path; it does not establish an unrelated asset's output, customer billing or operating costs.

The valuation work consequently needs an explicit evidence-to-asset map: what has been demonstrated, where, for which purpose, under which approval, and what economic uncertainty it resolves. The most important next evidence may be a customer or funding obligation rather than another headline technological event. A broad advanced-nuclear basket should not erase those differences.

This is a focused identity-and-proof case, not a full Oklo financial model. Additional cash commitments, financing instruments and the exact commercial deployment portfolio remain an R5 company-expansion obligation.

## 3. Renewables: manufacturers, service providers and owners face different economics

### 3.1 First Solar — the same credit has an earnings clock and a cash clock

**Evidence.** The filing records production credits in cost-of-sales/receivable accounting and separately describes historical monetization. One 2025 transaction transferred $701.9 million of credits for $668.1 million. Q2 2026 gross profit of $605.004 million included an $88.6 million net expected tariff-refund benefit. These are different accounting and timing items; the 2025 discount is not a current transfer-market quote. [S13]

**Economic interpretation.** Start with product economics, then add only applicable and recognized credit economics under the source's accounting policy, and finally reconcile when a receivable becomes usable cash. If the credit has already benefited reported profit, its later cash realization is not a second profit. Transfer discounts, transaction expenses, payment timing and recourse matter to the cash value.

A sensitivity that removes one credit or refund can be informative, but it is not automatically the company's 'true earnings.' Removing the incentive can also change pricing, sourcing, capacity decisions and customer demand. A full counterfactual must allow behavior to change rather than subtract a benefit while freezing every other favorable assumption.

**Re-rating hypotheses.** Better conversion of backlog into cash at repeatable contribution margins could improve earnings visibility. Lower production cost, stronger customer protections and less capital tied up in receivables could improve quality. Factory expansion may be valuable when the incremental return survives the complete investment and policy-sensitive assumptions.

**Counterevidence.** Order termination payments, refunds, credit recognition timing or favorable product mix can lift a reported quarter without a comparable improvement in repeatable per-share cash. Strong domestic economics can attract new capacity and competition; the durability of the return needs testing rather than being presumed from scarcity.

**Required model.** Track plant/product/location, sale and production qualification, price-cost bridge, source-specific credit treatment, receivable aging, discount and cash receipt, warranty, capacity ramp and total economic investment. This is issuer accounting analysis, not a tax-law eligibility opinion or a policy judgment.

### 3.2 Enphase — distinguish shipment timing from end-customer demand

**Evidence.** Q2 revenue was approximately $291.9 million versus $282.9 million in Q1, including safe-harbor revenue of $84.3 million versus $34.5 million. Refunds of $52 million were allocated to gross profit, interest income and inventory; $41 million was received in Q2 and $11 million later. The cited data-center opportunities included RFI/RFP stages. [S14]

Subtracting the disclosed safe-harbor amounts gives $207.6 million versus $248.4 million, approximately 16.43% lower. This is a diagnostic constructed from rounded disclosures, not official organic revenue and not proof that consumer installations fell by that percentage.

**Economic interpretation.** The supplier's revenue depends on sell-in, channel inventory, installer activity, product mix and commercial timing. Future revenue can be pulled into a period without changing the total customer need. Conversely, destocking can weaken current sell-in even if end demand stabilizes. The research needs separate channel and consumer evidence.

For prospective new markets, a demonstration, request for information, request for proposal and firm sales contract are different facts. Product capability can broaden future opportunity without creating current revenue. That distinction is especially important when a stock's narrative changes faster than its reported business mix.

**Re-rating hypotheses.** A cleaner inventory/channel position, sustained end demand, profitable new products and stronger cash conversion could support better forward earnings. The strongest evidence would connect observed installations or orders to comparable product economics, rather than rely only on management's potential market size.

**Counterevidence.** Timing-driven orders, incentives that change purchase dates, refund effects or distributor inventory can inflate an apparent recovery. Pricing pressure, warranty or support costs can absorb volume growth. No undisclosed theme-exposure percentage should be manufactured to make a new market look material.

### 3.3 Vestas — an installed base is an asset and a continuing obligation

**Evidence.** The Q2 report distinguishes €36.0 billion turbine backlog and €40.9 billion expected service revenue. Service revenue was €896 million versus €948 million and EBIT before special items €149 million versus €163 million. The warranty liability reconciles €1,929 million opening plus €259 million provided minus €367 million consumed to €1,821 million closing. Its EPS row is explicitly twelve-month even within a quarterly key-figure table. [S15]

**Economic interpretation.** A service franchise may be more durable than new equipment orders, but it is not a software business by default. It has labor, parts, vessel, availability and contractual performance obligations. A larger base can increase recurring income and the burden of maintaining promised performance simultaneously.

Backlog duration is also economically important. A long-lived expected revenue total does not equal one-year revenue or the present value of a high-margin annuity. Indexation, foreign exchange, acquisitions, new contracts, cancellations and completed work can change the total without identical changes in profitability.

A falling warranty provision is not automatically a release into earnings. In the disclosed rollforward, consumption is a major reason the closing balance is lower. The cash schedule and reimbursement rights still require separate analysis. The metric must retain its source meaning rather than being labeled a cash payment merely because it reduces a liability.

**Re-rating hypotheses.** More reliable equipment, better risk-adjusted pricing, improved cost recovery and repeatable service economics could increase the quality of future cash. New equipment delivery may seed future service, but the attach rate, economics and capital requirements need evidence. A decline in loss-making repair activity can have a different meaning from lost market share.

**Counterevidence.** Warranty experience, costly service obligations, ramp-up losses or working-capital needs can offset a stronger order book. The required operating model is by equipment vintage and contract type, not only total GW under service.

### 3.4 Orsted — capital recycling is not the same as recurring free cash

**Evidence.** H1 2026 operating cash of DKK9,124 million, gross investment of DKK18,261 million and divestments of DKK9,501 million reconcile to DKK364 million of reported free cash flow. Defined EBITDA excluding partnerships/cancellation fees increased, while statutory EBITDA and net profit declined on different comparison bases. The company distinguishes construction-agreement earnings from operating generation. [S16]

**Economic interpretation.** The business can create value by originating projects, building them, selling part of the interest and operating the remainder. Those are several profit sources, with different capital demands and repeatability. A sale can recycle capital at an attractive price while reducing future ownership. The model cannot count the sale proceeds and then retain all future cash from the sold stake.

The no-divestment arithmetic is DKK9,124 million less DKK18,261 million, or negative DKK9,137 million. That is a diagnostic, not an alternative company-defined FCF or a judgment that divestments are economically undesirable. The right question is whether the development-and-sale model creates value after development expense, construction risk, retained obligations and transaction costs.

**Re-rating hypotheses.** Commissioning a difficult project, reducing contingent commitments, realizing a credible farm-down or lowering financing risk could change the distribution of future outcomes before recurring cash rises. Attractive new awards may create growth only if contracted prices and capital costs support adequate returns.

**Counterevidence.** Lower bid prices, construction inflation, resource or capture-price disappointment, more expensive financing or a poor sale price can weaken the economic benefit. A lower accounting impairment does not itself produce cash, and restoring a proposed future dividend is not evidence of a payment already made.

**Required model.** Stage each project, identify legal and economic ownership before/after a transaction, preserve development cost and remaining construction obligations, and calculate cash under the contract's price and production shape. Separate pre-financing asset returns from residual shareholder cash. R5 should extend regional contract structures rather than reuse one US-style model globally.

## 4. Storage: equipment demand and asset rents can move in opposite directions

### 4.1 Fluence — a growing end market does not repair a delivery problem

**Evidence.** September guidance moved FY2026 revenue from an earlier $3.0 billion midpoint to approximately $2.4 billion and adjusted EBITDA from approximately negative $10 million to negative $200 million. Management attributed the revision to continued production difficulties. The later cash-generation objective is not an achieved cash result. [S17]

R1 already preserved that negative case. R4's addition is the economic boundary: an integrator normally needs delivery margin, acceptance, warranty/service economics and working-capital funding. The battery owner's arbitrage or capacity payments are not the integrator's income unless a specific agreement allocates those rights.

**Re-rating hypotheses.** Reliable production, fewer expensive delays, a higher-quality booked margin and release of working-capital pressure could improve future cash. Mere backlog growth does not establish those changes. A valuable repair must improve deliverability or expected contribution, not just move a target date.

**Counterevidence.** Liquidated damages, cost-to-complete revisions, supplier performance, customer acceptance delays or cash tied up before delivery can overwhelm end-market demand. Signed volume can even increase the funding requirement before it improves liquidity. Keep revised estimates as estimates; do not present the fiscal year as completed before the company reports it.

### 4.2 Ormat — one issuer can own scarcity rents and sell equipment with different margins

**Evidence.** Q2 storage revenue/cost were $42.772 million/$18.728 million, versus $14.494 million/$12.769 million a year earlier. The same quarter's product revenue/cost were $46.739 million/$42.217 million. Management describes storage growth from a mixture of market conditions, capacity and availability. Its electricity prose uses a sequential label while the dated table compares Q2 2026 with Q2 2025; the latter is the supported numerical comparator. [S18]

**Economic interpretation.** Storage ownership provides exposure to asset operation and payment rights; product supply provides a delivery margin. A profitable storage period cannot be multiplied across an equipment backlog or applied to every new battery without considering market competition and contract form.

The energy-arbitrage component is conditional on the hourly price pattern, losses, usable capacity, cycling restrictions and market participation. Adding storage capacity can change the price pattern it is intended to exploit. Falling equipment costs can help a new project while simultaneously allowing more competitors to enter. The research must test both effects rather than extrapolate a favorable historical spread indefinitely.

There may also be contracted availability or capacity income. These cannot all be stacked at maximum value in hours where their operating obligations conflict. Market rules, location, availability and contractual rights determine the feasible bundle. A perfect-foresight optimization is an upper-bound scenario, not a realized cash forecast.

### 4.3 Transparent battery example

Assume a hypothetical 100 MW / 400 MWh usable battery, 90% round-trip efficiency and 300 full cycles annually. At $30/MWh charging and $90/MWh selling prices, one cycle requires 444.444 MWh of purchased energy. Gross energy contribution is approximately $22,666.67 per cycle or $6.8 million annually, before fees, degradation, fixed costs, taxes and financing.

If charging rises to $35 and selling falls to $60, the same hardware produces approximately $2.533 million annual energy contribution under those assumptions. That is about 62.75% lower despite unchanged physical capacity. This is a conditional arithmetic example, not an assertion that these prices or cycles are achievable in any named market.

The published 2025 ATB uses a different 85% efficiency assumption and includes augmentation within its fixed O&M scenario. Importing that cost model and adding a second complete augmentation allowance would double count costs. Its technology-cost projections are not market-revenue forecasts. [S22]

**Required model.** Distinguish nameplate and usable energy, power, efficiency, operating state, dispatch rights, contract payments, degradation, warranty coverage and replacement spending. Preserve both new-project returns and the possible erosion of incumbent asset rents. Storage installation growth alone cannot determine which issuer benefits most.

## 5. Geothermal, fuel cells and hydrogen: value the actual operating proposition

### 5.1 Ormat geothermal — proving surface equipment does not prove the resource

Ormat's business includes owned geothermal generation, products and storage; its new geothermal pilots and product development are distinct from currently operating cash generation. DOE describes geothermal development as heavily exposed to early exploration and development costs, with operating choices also depending on maintenance and power prices. [S18, S23]

**Research interpretation.** The model needs sustainable net output after auxiliary load, site-specific resource evidence, available generation, contract prices and the cost of maintaining the field and plant. Equipment performance can reduce one engineering risk without proving commercially durable underground output.

The potential re-rating mechanism is not simply 'geothermal is firm power.' It is stronger evidence of repeatable project economics: less capital or time to obtain an economic resource, better net output, an attractive contract, and financing that leaves value for the owner. A commercial contract with a long lead time can matter before reported electricity earnings, but only as a conditional future cash claim.

The countercase is that the resource, drilling program, site constraints or replacement spending are worse than assumed. A cheap source of heat is not automatically a cheap delivered electricity project. R5 should add differentiated project/geographic cases before describing a global cost curve; no single pilot is treated as universal proof.

### 5.2 Bloom — stationary fuel-cell growth is not necessarily hydrogen demand

**Evidence.** Bloom reported Q2 revenue of approximately $1.065 billion versus $401.242 million. Its product description permits natural gas, biogas and hydrogen/blends. Product fuel flexibility is not a measurement of customers' actual fuel consumption. [S19, S20]

**Research interpretation.** A customer may value speed to power, reliability, a constrained grid connection, operating cost, emissions characteristics or heat recovery. The economically relevant comparison is the delivered service under a specified fuel and contract, not a generic label such as clean-energy equipment.

For shareholder analysis, separate hardware contribution, installation costs, service or replacement obligations, financing arrangements and collections. A combined heat-and-power efficiency cannot replace electrical efficiency in an electricity-only cost model. Nor can a technologically possible fuel be treated as observed demand for the corresponding producer basket.

**Re-rating hypotheses.** Repeatable unit economics, lower service cost and a credible delivery advantage could support a stronger business model. But the durability of a speed-to-power premium needs testing against alternatives and eventual grid availability. Large revenue growth is not enough to establish a persistent margin, an organic growth rate or an attractive current valuation.

This is a focused business-model distinction. A complete contract, customer concentration, financing and diluted-share bridge remains a further company-model requirement.

### 5.3 Plug — recurring revenue can carry recurring losses

**Evidence.** Q2 fuel revenue was approximately $39 million at approximately negative 48% gross margin, while service revenue was approximately $30 million at positive 27%. Total gross margin was approximately break-even with operating expenses of approximately $62 million. The stated future objective was positive EBITDAS, not free cash flow. [S21]

The rounded disclosures imply about $18.72 million gross loss in fuel and $8.1 million gross profit in service. These are illustrative products of rounded inputs, not substitutes for an exact financial reconciliation. They demonstrate why 'more recurring revenue' is not automatically evidence of a moat.

**Economic interpretation.** An expanding installed base may improve service utilization and customer retention while increasing the obligation to supply uneconomic fuel. The relevant unit model includes production or purchased hydrogen, conditioning, delivery, losses, customer price, reliability and capital. Equipment sales, service, fuel and electrolyzer supply are distinct businesses, even when sold to related customer groups.

A favorable hypothesis is that scale and reliability improve delivered unit cost faster than prices fall. An unfavorable one is that increased volumes expand an unrecovered cost obligation or require fresh capital before the economics become positive. The observed improvement should therefore be measured per delivered economic unit and after required investment, not only by total deployments.

**Hypothetical electricity sensitivity.** At an assumed 55 kWh per kilogram, electricity at $40/MWh costs $2.20/kg; at $80/MWh it costs $4.40/kg. These exclude plant capital, utilization, water, maintenance, compression, transport and every credit. They are not Plug's plant efficiency or cost estimate. Low hourly electricity prices do not automatically give low delivered cost if they leave expensive equipment underutilized.

**Required model.** Preserve definition-specific gross profit, EBITDA/EBITDAS, working capital, capital spending and liquidity. Do not estimate runway by dividing a total cash figure by one selected quarterly cash-usage label, ignoring restricted balances, commitments and subsequent events.

## 6. Re-rating mechanisms to investigate, not preassigned winners

The next valuation study should distinguish the following hypotheses. Each requires an unchanged forecast horizon, metric definition, business perimeter and market-data cutoff before it can be tested against price.

| Mechanism | Evidence that would improve the case | Counterevidence or offset |
|---|---|---|
| Contract replacement | Better realized terms on comparable delivery volumes | Higher procurement/operating cost or weaker mix |
| Conditional capacity becomes fundable | Actual funding and customer obligations cover a more defined build | More expensive capital, customer conditions or overruns |
| Development becomes repeatable | Subsequent projects need less unrecovered engineering/capital | Every project resets design or vendor support obligations |
| Service economics mature | Reliable installed base produces contribution after performance costs | Warranty, labor, vessel and replacement burden |
| Credit cash conversion improves | Realized proceeds and timing match recognized value | Discounts, delays, changing eligibility or behavior |
| Capital is recycled productively | Sale proceeds exceed the value surrendered plus obligations | Proceeds counted without foregone future cash |
| Storage captures durable payment rights | Feasible contracted/market cash after degradation and competition | New capacity compresses the revenue pool |
| Resource development becomes economic | Repeatable site output relative to complete investment | Resource or sustaining-cost disappointment |
| Distributed power advantage persists | Customer economics survive realistic alternatives | Premium disappears when alternative supply arrives |
| Liquidity risk declines | Net available funds cover the actual pre-revenue program | Restricted cash, options, commitments or dilution omitted |

A larger multiple can reflect lower risk or a longer cash-flow duration without current earnings growth. It can also reflect exuberant expectations that later prove unjustified. The research must not decide between these explanations from price momentum or management narrative alone.

### Financing is not mechanically good or bad

Assume, purely hypothetically, a business worth 200 with 100 shares raises 50 at the fair pre-money price of 2. It issues 25 shares; post-money value is 250 across 125 shares, still 2 per share before costs. The old holders' ownership falls to 80%, but the financing itself has not mechanically destroyed 20% of their value. The outcome changes with issue price, fees, warrants, investment returns and the no-financing alternative.

This is why Centrus-style instrument details matter. A straight common-stock illustration cannot value its warrants. A shareholder-value model needs the economic value and possible cash consequences of every claim, not simply a fully diluted share denominator attached to an unchanged numerator.

### Cheaper technology does not guarantee a better equity return

A hypothetical project costing 100 and producing annual cash of 10 has a 10% simple cash-on-cost ratio. At cost 85 and unchanged cash, the ratio becomes 11.76%. If competing bids reduce annual cash to 8, it becomes 9.41%. These are not IRRs and have no assumed terminal value. They isolate how capital savings can be competed away.

The same logic connects renewable manufacturers, developers and asset owners. Cheaper equipment can help a prospective buyer while pressuring the seller. More low-cost generation can alter capture prices. A supplier to the expansion can grow while an incumbent asset's scarcity income falls. Cross-theme analysis should retain these conditional opposing effects rather than label the whole chain a beneficiary.

### Timing belongs in valuation before it belongs in reported revenue

A hypothetical ten-year stream of 100 annually discounted at 10% has present value about 614.46. Delaying every receipt by two years, with no other change, produces about 507.82: approximately 17.36% less. Real delays can also change costs, contractual payments and financing. This is a timing illustration, not a project valuation or estimated failure probability.

The system should allow analysis of future value without claiming current delivery. A financing or regulatory milestone may be economically important before the first commercial output. Requiring operational proof for a current-operation claim should never be confused with refusing to study a conditional future opportunity.

## 7. The machine and user jobs this research enables

The investor should be able to start with a development—fuel contract, equipment order, plant approval, credit payment, asset sale or resource update—and follow the actual beneficiary through a sourced economic explanation.

The machine should preserve five separate statements: what was observed; what management expects; what is conditional; what our model derives; and what is still unknown. A source-referenced number is not useful if it belongs to the wrong period, asset, cash-flow definition or shareholder claim.

**Cameco:** Q2 source conflict → correct segment table → price/cost/volume distinction → no false market-price or investee-sales inference. This extends an existing R1 gap into a resolved, concrete evidence-quality case.
