## Targa Resources — contract duration can improve while funding burden rises

Targa's August 6 Q2 release retained 2026 net growth-capital guidance of approximately **$4.5 billion** and described record Permian/NGL volumes, while noting temporary producer curtailments in response to negative Waha gas prices. On August 17, Targa announced new **20-year fee-based integrated midstream agreements** with ExxonMobil, three new Permian Delaware processing plants, and Bull Run II. The later release updated 2026 net growth-capital guidance to approximately **$5.0 billion**. It states that Bull Run II is supported by take-or-pay commitments and is expected to begin operations in the first half of 2028.

Primary sources:
- https://www.targaresources.com/node/29286
- https://www.targaresources.com/node/29316

**Research interpretation.** Stronger future commercial coverage and a larger near-term funding requirement are simultaneously true. Preserve both. A fee-based arrangement, acreage dedication, fee floor, and explicit take-or-pay commitment are not interchangeable protections; Bull Run II's disclosed take-or-pay support must not be propagated to every related asset. Separate gathering/processing, residue-gas takeaway, NGL transportation, fractionation, export services, optimization/marketing, maintenance capital and growth capital. Planned H1-2028 operations remain future until evidenced as operating.

**Re-rating questions.** Does longer commercial duration improve future utilization/cash confidence enough to exceed the full capital and financing burden? Are returns attractive after cost to complete, start dates, utilization, contract-specific floors and per-share funding? Point-in-time expectations and matched valuation data are still required before calling the event under- or over-priced.

## Baker Hughes — order strength is not yet profitable cash

Baker Hughes reported Q2 2026 Industrial & Energy Technology orders of **$7.088 billion**, revenue of **$3.291 billion**, and segment EBITDA of **$678 million**. Corporate operating cash flow was $1.345 billion and company-defined free cash flow was $1.109 billion; the release defines FCF as operating cash flow less capital expenditures net of asset-disposal proceeds. On July 27, Baker Hughes announced a major CP2 LNG technology order and explicitly stated that the order was **booked in the second quarter**.

Primary sources:
- https://investors.bakerhughes.com/news/press-releases/news-details/2026/Baker-Hughes-Announces-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx
- https://investors.bakerhughes.com/news/press-releases/news-details/2026/Baker-Hughes-Secures-Major-LNG-Technology-Order-for-Venture-Globals-CP2-LNG-Expansion/default.aspx

**Research interpretation.** Preserve the chronology:

`commercial award -> booking -> executable backlog/RPO -> production -> delivery -> revenue recognition -> cash collection -> installed-base service`

The July announcement must not create a duplicate Q3 booking. Orders, backlog/RPO, revenue, EBITDA, customer advances and cash collection are different observations. Order value cannot become forecast profit without pricing and cost-to-complete evidence. A stronger installed base may improve future service durability, but service attach, warranty, delivery, working-capital and collection economics remain separate questions.
