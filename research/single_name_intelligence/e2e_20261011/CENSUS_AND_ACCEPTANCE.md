## 5. Assessment: the highest-risk failure modes

**False integration completeness.** A populated JSON object or a green UI can hide fixture data, stale sources or missing issuer support. Require source-generation receipts and useful per-issuer coverage, not a screen count.

**Identity laundering.** A source-backed counter/CIK relationship gets turned into an invented canonical issuer/security ID. Test actual known missing RMB counters and unresolved HK issuer states, not only easy US examples.

**Point-in-time laundering.** A current fundamentals panel, hindsight-corrected filing or retrieved document is presented as knowledge available before its real observation. Separate reconstructed history from retained then-known publications.

**Scoring by narrative.** An LLM's prose implies probabilities, causal impact or “already priced in” without an evaluated estimator. Display the independent facts, assumptions and research status; do not let language silently promote authority.

**Scientific overfitting.** Small issuer event counts, linked securities, repeated reports and overlapping windows create an illusion of sample size. Freeze splits, clusters, trial budgets and target-specific estimability; simple baselines can win.

**Orchestration that costs more than it delivers.** A native fleet doing repetitive labor loads the principal with context and creates integration contention. Use domain coordinators, bounded external task operators, progressive disclosure and a real review budget. The proposed hierarchy requires measurement; no token-saving percentage is asserted.

**Scope capture by dependencies.** A missing issuer field turns into completing FIF, Options, TOI or GMI end to end. Freeze the minimal consumer contract, ask the incumbent for one bounded extension, and progress independent lanes.

**Premature finalization.** Reference release, a merged research PR, a pending 21-session forecast and SNI-8 scale readiness are different outcomes. Record each honestly and preserve natural-time obligations.
