## 4. Narrow amendment requests to the one shared contract

### AM1 — Exact signed measures without weakening physical-quantity rules

R9 already proposes one optional, closed `measurement` branch inside `theme_graph.curation_assertion.v1`. Retain its exact decimal text, metric label, unit, currency, scale, quantity basis, period, consolidation/valuation basis, inclusions/exclusions and source precision. The shared owner may accept that additive branch or an equivalent versioned representation that demonstrably preserves the same semantics. Materials does not mint a second assertion schema.

The current `_check_observation` rejects `-31` even with predicate `REPORTED_FINANCIAL_MEASURE`; it also rejects a string such as `781.000000000000000001`. Both are reproduced in the bounded R11 function probe. Moving a minus sign into prose, taking an absolute value, setting the numeric field to zero or coercing exact text through a float would corrupt the economic explanation.

Keep `physical_count` nonnegative and integral, but **do not classify all physical quantities as counts**. A proposed `nonnegative_quantity` domain permits fractional mass, volume or area. Signed financial values, signed changes and signed spreads retain their explicit domain. R11 narrowly adds this continuous-quantity requirement to R9's domain list; it does not infer the sign domain from a label such as cost or price.

Use the existing generic observation bases (`absolute`, `per_period`, `per_unit`) where correct, with an explicit denominator/physical unit in the reviewed measurement. Do not add a new global quantity-basis enum for every material merely to spell tonne, troy ounce or board foot. Different meanings must remain explicit: product tonne versus contained nutrient/metal, gross versus payable/attributable output, revenue period versus stock date, and reporting currency versus quotation currency. ## 5. Rights qualification: a useful improvement with a strict proof boundary

The candidate implements `load_registry_snapshot(path=None)` and `assert_current_emission_allowed(families, snapshot=...)`. The first computes its revision from the bytes it actually decodes; the second evaluates those returned families. A selected-function probe using a temporary registry observed that a fresh same-process read after a restriction blocks emission without a cache-clear workaround.

An old snapshot still allows under its old contents. That is expected behavior for a snapshot, not a revocation service. The authorized request path must obtain a current snapshot and recheck the applicable current version at its final response-release boundary. A hash assembled from new bytes cannot attest to decisions made from old parsed objects. A change during loading must invalidate the affected composition; old content cannot fall back through a public or formerly permitted copy.

This **supersedes R9 T2's proposed requirement to repair the legacy `emission_allowed` call itself**. Keep the old R9 experiment as historical evidence; Materials consumes the new snapshot API and requests end-to-end behavior through it. No consumer calls private cache-clearing functions. Production policy refresh, source-family completeness, entitlement revocation and API wiring remain unproved by the excerpt exercise.

Current content permission, paid entitlement and the actual storage/publication access boundary remain different checks. R4's bucket-access and entitlement-grace questions are not waived here. No promise is made to recall copies already lawfully delivered to a user.
