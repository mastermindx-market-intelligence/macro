## 1. Product thesis: explain who captures the economics

The proposed organizing question is:

> What changed in a material market; which operating businesses capture or lose economic value; how does that change cash available to equity holders; and what evidence says the stock has or has not already reflected it?

A useful Materials system must connect five separate layers:

`material conditions -> business economics -> company cash flow -> per-share valuation -> market/entry context`

The arrows are hypotheses or accounting relationships with explicit conditions, not automatic signals. Economic improvement does not establish undervaluation, and undervaluation does not establish an actionable entry. The eventual user outcome is a proactive, source-backed explanation of where attention is warranted, with relevant companies and change conditions. Admission to ranking or trades remains separately governed.

The durable differentiation should be the ability to trace a changing profit pool through a real business and its financial claims. More commodity charts or a larger ticker list alone do not accomplish that job.

### Alternatives evaluated

**Copy Robotics BOM as the primary model:** useful for selected processing and qualification chains, but insufficient for pricing, contracts, depletion, capital structure and local-market economics. Retain value-chain detail only where it explains an economically material mechanism.

**Use a sector-wide commodity/momentum composite:** simple to display, but conceals opposite signs, timing lags and non-comparable business models. Reject as the foundational explanation model.

**Use source-bound operating models plus existing-owner projections:** selected research direction. It can express heterogeneous economics without inventing a competing graph or claiming one scalar captures everything.

The strongest objection is complexity and sparse public data. The response is not invented precision: allow semantic-only coverage, publish the missing denominator, and begin with a small set of reconciled business cases before scaling.

## 2. Primary-source findings that discriminate between models

These are dated analytical cases, not assertions about today's investment attractiveness. An issuer explanation is management-attributed evidence, not independently identified causation. Sources and exact locators are in section 12.

### R1-F01 — Demand growth is not the same as scarcity

The IEA's 2025 outlook reports nearly 30% lithium demand growth in 2024 while describing a price decline of more than 80% since 2023 as supply expanded rapidly. This is a counterexample to using end-market growth alone as a bullish commodity conclusion. It is a statement from the 2025 report's information set, not a September 2026 price calculation. [S04]

**Design implication:** model demand, deliverable supply, inventory and product suitability separately. A positive electrification narrative cannot bypass a surplus or a financing problem.

### R1-F02 — A processor's margin has several components

Aurubis's May 8, 2026 preliminary H1 release describes higher metal results, recycling-processing earnings, sulfuric-acid revenues and copper-product revenues offsetting substantially lower treatment/refining charges. Its Q2 operating EBT was EUR121 million versus EUR105 million in Q1. [S07]

**Design implication:** lower treatment charges are not a complete company earnings model. Preserve feedstock type, fee terms, by-product ownership, recovery, maintenance and integration; do not label all copper-related businesses as one directional exposure.

### R1-F03 — Realized output/input spreads matter

CF's February 18, 2026 FY2025 release reports adjusted EBITDA of USD2.89 billion versus USD2.28 billion, with sales volumes approximately unchanged and higher selling prices. Its realized natural-gas cost, including settled derivatives, increased to USD3.31/MMBtu from USD2.40. [S05]

LyondellBasell's January 30, 2026 release attributes Q4 margin pressure to higher feedstock/energy costs, maintenance and seasonal demand limiting product prices. [S06]

**Design implication:** evaluate the realized margin bridge, not simply whether an input price rose. A producer's own energy cost, competitors' costs, output-price response and contract lags need separate observations.

### R1-F04 — Reported revenue changes can have little profit meaning

Linde's FY2025 10-K describes long-term contracts with energy/feedstock pass-through. Its Americas discussion reports a 1% sales increase from cost pass-through with minimal operating-profit impact. [S08]

**Design implication:** distinguish price attainment, cost reimbursement, volume, acquisitions and FX. Contract-backed service economics should not be scored like an unhedged spot-price commodity producer.

### R1-F05 — Price, mix and acquisitions must not be conflated

Vulcan's February 17, 2026 FY2025 release reports aggregate shipments up 3%, principally from acquisitions, with same-store shipments slightly lower. Mix-adjusted pricing rose 6% versus 4% reported pricing; cash gross profit per ton rose to USD11.33. [S09]

Sherwin-Williams' January 29, 2026 release separately reports same-store growth and acquisition contributions, including Suvinil's contribution to Q4 sales. [S13]

**Design implication:** distinguish organic demand, acquisition growth, mix, delivered pricing and unit profitability before interpreting demand acceleration or pricing power.

### R1-F06 — Cost rankings can change with accounting basis

Freeport's June 30, 2026 10-Q presents the same U.S. copper operations' Q2 copper unit net cash cost as USD2.94/lb under the by-product method and USD3.29/lb under the co-product method. These are different allocations, not two different mines. [S10]

The World Gold Council identifies AISC and AIC as non-GAAP measures, calls for numerical reconciliation and highlights comparability issues in by-product/co-product treatment. [S12]

**Design implication:** no cross-company cost ranking without compatible scope, period, denominator and treatment. A negative by-product-net cost must not be described as zero economic resource consumption.

### R1-F07 — Precious-metal demand requires a different lens

The World Gold Council's January 29, 2026 full-year report records 2025 ETF holdings growth of 801 tonnes and central-bank purchases of 863 tonnes, alongside weaker jewellery volumes. These are the report's estimates, not a new observation of September positioning. [S11]

**Design implication:** monetary/investment demand is a material-level overlay, while a gold miner still needs its own production, cost, capital and ownership model. Physical gold, a miner and a royalty claim are not interchangeable expressions.

### R1-F08 — A new web date is not new economic data

USGS's data-release landing page shows September 18, 2026. The associated Science Data Catalog describes February 6, 2026 publication and data spanning 2021–2025. The MCS report page identifies revisions through May 2026. [S01–S03]

**Design implication:** preserve publication, revision, reference period and first observation separately. The page-date/catalog discrepancy is recorded, not resolved by assuming September observations or assuming the newly listed page proves when every underlying file first became available.

### Orthogonal descriptors

**Value-chain role:** resource owner, developer, mine/forest operator, concentrator, refiner/smelter, chemical converter, fabricator/formulator, recycler, distributor, service provider or royalty/streaming holder.

**Material specification:** product form, grade, purity, chemical basis, relevant process route, accepted market benchmark and unit. No automatic substitution between different usable forms.

**Demand connection:** construction, grids/electrification, manufacturing, transport, agriculture, electronics, packaging, investment or another source-supported end market. These are exposure assertions, not sector-membership replacements.

**Geography:** asset, processing location, sale/delivery market, cost currency, functional/reporting currency and legal jurisdiction. A company's headquarters cannot stand in for all six.

**Asset/project state:** evidenced milestones and current operating state. Do not force permitting, financing and engineering into a fictional universal order.

**Financial claim:** operating equity, minority interest, project interest, royalty, stream, debt or physical-market exposure; preserve the existing identity owner's object types.

Uranium/energy, metallurgical coal, timber REITs, mining equipment and semiconductor-related inputs require explicit cross-sector ownership mapping. Economic relevance to Materials does not authorize changing the security's accepted sector classification.

## 4. Five initial operating models, with overlays rather than more silos

The following are proposed analytical templates, not fitted models. They are deliberately not mutually exclusive at company level. Model a segment or asset first, then reconcile to the issuer.

### A. Resource production economics

Start with saleable/payable output, realized net price, cash production/delivery cost, coproduct economics and ownership. Then add maintenance/sustaining investment, development capital, working capital and taxes under a stated accounting basis.

For a mining example, the research must distinguish material moved, processed ore, contained metal, recovered metal, payable metal and sold metal. A change in any conversion stage may alter economics even without a change in the headline commodity price. Use the issuer's actual process and disclosures; do not apply a universal recovery coefficient.

Required questions: What is genuinely incremental production? Is cost improvement physical efficiency or a larger by-product credit? Is higher revenue due to price, grade, recovery, sold inventory or capacity? What investment is needed to sustain the asset? Which owner receives the cash?

R1 support: S10 and S12. Forestry's biological inventory needs a later distinct validation; it is not declared solved by this mining model.

### B. Conversion and co-product spreads

For a fixed process boundary, consider proceeds from all material outputs plus relevant service/processing fees, less purchased inputs, conversion costs and other non-overlapping charges. Separate tolling from ownership of metal or feedstock. For integrated businesses, eliminate internal transfers before aggregating economic exposure.

Required questions: Is the reported benchmark relevant to contracted realization? Who bears raw-material inventory risk? Are co-product economics offsetting the main product? Does changing one output require unwanted production of another? Is working-capital funding absorbing apparent operating gains?

R1 support: S05–S07. This accommodates nitrogen and commodity chemicals without pretending they have the same feedstock or demand process.

### C. Local delivered-material economics

Model realized net selling price, market/product mix, freight boundary, cash cost per comparable unit and available capacity in the relevant served market. Separate volume from acquisitions and product/customer/geographic mix before estimating operating leverage.

Required questions: Are order trends comparable across locations? Is a price increase realized on the same product and customer mix? Does customer-delivered pricing conceal higher transport cost? Is the margin change repeatable after maintenance and acquisition effects?

R1 support: S09. The hypothesis that local market structure matters must be tested with geographically specific sources rather than a national commodity proxy alone.

### D. Differentiated formulation and qualification economics

Investigate customer qualification, value-in-use, channels, price realization, mix, raw-material lag, retention and product-development conversion. A new product's announcement, qualification, order and commercial revenue are different evidence states.

Required questions: Is improvement recurring organic demand or acquired sales? Is margin growth mix, procurement, price or restructuring? Does customer qualification create durable economics, and what actual revenue establishes the exposure?

R1 support is limited: S13 provides a segment/organic-versus-acquired-sales discriminator. Qualification-based moat claims for individual companies remain unproven pending dedicated issuer and customer evidence.

### E. Contracted production/service economics

Separate base/service charges, reimbursed inputs, volume obligations, utilization, start-up economics and returns on project capital. Classify contract descriptions at their actual scope; do not infer universal take-or-pay protection or riskless cash flow from the phrase long-term contract.

Required questions: What varies with utilization? Which costs pass through and with what lag? Are projects earning revenue or merely in a backlog? How much capital is committed before cash generation? What customer/asset risk remains?

R1 support: S08. Hydrogen or other projects cannot inherit the economics of an unrelated established contract portfolio.

### Cross-cutting overlays

Material demand regime, monetary/investment behavior, asset development/depletion, integration, financing and shareholder claims remain overlays. A developer is not simply a smaller operating producer. A recycler is not automatically an unhedged commodity-price bet. A royalty is not the same cost structure as the underlying operator. These are questions for source-specific modeling, not blanket directional conclusions.

## 5. Earnings growth, rerating and stock returns must remain different

### 5.1 Define the bridge before estimating sensitivity

A schematic operating-cash bridge is:

`cash operating result = external realized revenue + non-overlapping operating receipts - cash operating costs`

`unlevered free cash flow = operating cash result - relevant cash taxes - capital expenditure - change in operating working capital - other non-overlapping operating cash obligations`

This is an analytical bridge, not a universal GAAP definition. Match the cash-flow and valuation basis, classify interest/leases consistently, and avoid subtracting a cost already included upstream. Reported EBITDA, adjusted EBITDA, operating profit, AISC and issuer-defined free cash flow cannot be silently interchanged.

A fully consolidated operating valuation can be bridged conceptually to common equity as:

`common equity value = operating enterprise value + separately valued non-operating assets - net debt - preferred/minority/other claims not already accounted for`

`value per share = common equity value / consistent diluted share count`

If the operating valuation is already proportionate or equity-based, do not subtract minority interests or debt a second time. Cash already included in net debt must not also appear as a separate asset addition. All forecasts require explicit commodity, production, cost, capital and financing assumptions.

### 5.2 Simple arithmetic exposes false shortcuts

Illustrative units only, not a company forecast: output price 100 and unit operating cost 70 produce a contribution of 30. A price rise to 110 with unchanged cost increases contribution to 40, or 33.3%. If cost instead rises to 75, contribution is 35, or 16.7% higher. Neither calculation includes tax, capital investment, debt, dilution or valuation changes.

For positive, comparable earnings, the identity `P1/P0 = (EPS1/EPS0) * ((P/E)1/(P/E)0)` separates earnings growth from multiple change. It is not meaningful for negative/near-zero or non-comparable earnings, and excludes dividends. A stock can rise without multiple expansion; a multiple can fall while the stock rises.

Proposed analysis should separately report cash-flow revisions, multiple/discount-rate changes, financial-claim/share-count changes and market positioning. Do not label every price rally a fundamental rerating.

### 5.3 Industry-aware valuation research

| Business/claim | Proposed primary valuation lens | Required caution |
|---|---|---|
| Operating mine | Asset cash flows/NAV with scenario assumptions; normalized cash generation | Reserves/resources, mine plan, ownership, sustaining investment and terminal treatment |
| Development project | Scenario project value and financing bridge | Resource size alone is not a funded, permitted or economic asset; dilution must be explicit |
| Cyclical converter | Through-cycle earnings/FCF and replacement economics where justified | Spot peak margins can make low multiples misleading; integrated and merchant economics differ |
| Local materials operator | Normalized cash generation and returns on reinvestment | Regional mix, acquisitions, freight and maintenance cannot be ignored |
| Specialty/contracted operator | Cash-flow durability, reinvestment return and growth conversion | A narrative moat or backlog is not proof of future margins or timely cash flow |
| Royalty/stream/financial exposure | Contract-specific cash flows and claim structure | Do not import the operator's ownership, cost base or attributable production wholesale |

These lenses are proposed starting points. No fair values, price targets, probability estimates or stock rankings have been calculated in R1.

## 6. Company exposure must be a set of measurements, not one weight

The research proposal is a source-bound exposure record that can later map into existing native ownership. It does not create a new global asset/product master or another financial-data warehouse.

Minimum conceptual fields:

`issuer/security reference; source-scoped business or asset description; material/form/grade; operating role; ownership basis; reporting perimeter; measurement type; value/unit/denominator; reference period; publication/observation/revision clocks; source locator; method; uncertainty; rights; limitations`

Keep these measurement types separate:

- reported segment revenue/EBITDA exposure;
- saleable/payable production or sales exposure;
- asset/resource/reserve exposure under its reporting standard;
- scenario-based attributable cash-flow/NAV exposure;
- estimated marginal earnings sensitivity;
- statistical price sensitivity.

They answer different questions. They must not be averaged into an invented universal copper exposure percentage. Statistical beta is not reported business exposure, and a company mentioning a material is not evidence that the material is economically important to it.

The next research should reconcile gross, consolidated and attributable quantities, ownership changes, minority claims, intersegment transfers, hedges, by-products, currencies and timing. Report unknowns as unknowns. A missing revenue percentage is not zero and not an invitation for an LLM to invent a plausible weight.

### Basket implications

Initially propose descriptive cohorts by operating role, material form, geography and financial claim. Preserve the difference between a research cohort and an accepted price basket. Equal-weight, market-cap, production and economic-exposure weighting are separate experiments, not interchangeable defaults.

An issuer with multiple listings should not become several independent economic observations. Historical analysis must use eligible point-in-time membership or be labelled current-membership/non-PIT. Overlap between copper, electrification and critical-materials cohorts cannot be counted as three independent confirmations.

## 7. Supply, inventories, capacity and cost curves

### Stage- and unit-correct physical balances

Every balance must bind to a material form, grade, geography, interval and definition. At a defined refined-material boundary, a possible reconciliation is primary refined output plus secondary refined output plus net imports, less use, with the remainder allocated to inventory change and an explicitly identified residual. It is a proposed reconciliation structure, not a replacement for the source's published definition.

Never add mined and refined quantities as if they were independent supplies of the same metal. Do not combine gross material tonnage and contained-metal tonnage without an evidenced conversion. Apparent consumption must not silently become measured end-user demand.

Track visible inventories and their coverage separately from estimates of total stocks. A stock movement between regions or warehouses may change one visible series without establishing a corresponding change in global availability. Validate such explanations; do not assert them merely because they fit the thesis.

### Capacity and project evidence

Maintain source-supported milestones for announcement, studies, permitting, financing, investment decision, construction, commissioning, ramp-up and commercial/qualified delivery. These milestones can overlap and need not follow one universal order.

Nameplate capacity is not output; output is not necessarily saleable grade; saleable production is not always customer-qualified delivery. For each project, retain schedule vintage, stage, technical constraints, funding, dependency and source uncertainty.

Do not assign fixed success probabilities by project stage without calibration. Begin with explicitly named scenarios and date ranges. A future pipeline gap is neither a measured current deficit nor a calibrated forecast probability. Conversely, a near-term surplus does not resolve long-horizon project risk. S04 supports the need to preserve these horizon distinctions, not a new current deficit claim.

### Cost-curve discipline

A credible curve requires comparable asset scope, cost basis, currency, period and quantity denominator, plus explicit coverage. Public issuer costs may support a reported-cost comparison, not a complete world cost curve.

Retain pre-credit costs, by-product/co-product basis, sustaining/growth capital treatment, recovery, transport, royalties and unusual exclusions when available. If the data cannot be reconciled, do not force an ordinal ranking. S10 and S12 supply concrete comparability tests.

Distinguish operating cash-cost support from full-cycle investment economics. Do not assume either is a hard commodity-price floor. Estimate uncertainty and missing supply coverage rather than manufacturing precise marginal-cost thresholds.

### Scarcity is a vector before it is a score

Proposed dimensions are physical balance, inventory coverage, usable-grade availability, delivery/concentration risk, project-arrival uncertainty and substitution flexibility. No arbitrary weighted scarcity score is proposed for deployment. A future model must demonstrate incremental value over simpler measures and remain separate from equity-entry authority.

## 8. Data acquisition and evidence requirements

### Public foundation versus unverified coverage

USGS provides a useful public structural starting point: its data release describes CSV tables and associated metadata and marks the release CC0. This does not establish real-time coverage. [S02–S03]

Company filings/releases provide asset, segment, operating and accounting detail; they do not by themselves establish a complete market balance, independent causal identification or a reusable commercial dataset. IEA and WGC provide complementary industry frameworks, with publication-specific rights and underlying-source limitations. Do not assume an article's public availability licenses bulk raw-series redistribution.

Commercial benchmark histories, treatment-charge series, consensus vintages, comprehensive asset costs and some customer/qualification data remain coverage/rights questions. No new paid provider is approved. First reconcile existing Mastermind entitlements and adapters during the relevant source-specific research unit.

### Mandatory clocks and measurement context

Preserve economic reference period; event/effective time where applicable; actual source publication time; first observed/ingested time; revision time; forecast horizon; and source/model vintage. An unavailable date stays unavailable. Source-page edits, retrieval time and Git commit time never fill missing upstream publication history.

Every derived observation should retain its source inputs, formula version, accounting boundary, units, coverage, assumptions and supersession relation through existing owners. Confidence should reflect source quality, completeness and dependence, not the number of URLs repeating one announcement.

### Source independence and statement mode

Source records must distinguish reported actual, preliminary result, management guidance, project target, analyst estimate, derived calculation and model hypothesis. The May 8 Aurubis release is preliminary. A later final document should be reconciled rather than counted as an independent confirmation of the same event.

USGS landing/catalog/report entries are related metadata, not three independent data sources. Syndicated versions of an issuer press release are one source family. A model-generated interpretation cannot overwrite the reported fact.

The Robotics handoff identifies a public Git-tracked Theme Graph evidence parquet and requires a proven approved private owner/publication binding before full-fidelity paid evidence admission. Preserve that constraint. This research file contains original design analysis and short summaries of public sources, not raw licensed datasets or a current paid-product evidence payload.

## 9. Product implications for the existing shared template

The primary page should answer: what changed; which material/business role is affected; why that matters for cash generation; what evidence is missing; and what would change the read. Complexity should open on demand.

Proposed additions to the shared dossier's existing dimensions, not a new page framework:

1. **Economic read:** material conditions, demand/supply horizon and operating-model context.
2. **Profit-capture view:** relevant business roles, conditional directional effects and margin-bridge evidence.
3. **Company exposure:** actual economic role and measurement basis, not a bare ticker list.
4. **Valuation context:** stated scenario/normalized basis, financial-claim caveats and missing inputs.
5. **Market confirmation:** existing owner observations for leadership, participation and entry, displayed separately.
6. **Evidence/change conditions:** source clocks, contradictions, missingness and explicit watch conditions.

A copper dossier might explain that a concentrate-market observation and a refined-metal inventory observation concern different stages, then show why a miner, smelter and fabricator need different analysis. It must not infer a current stock recommendation from that explanatory example.

Useful visualization candidates are a compact value-chain/earnings bridge, stage-specific balances, source-vintage comparison, project milestone timeline, comparable reported-cost table, and company-role exposure matrix. A complex supply-chain diagram is justified only when it helps the user complete the economic task. Detailed visual design must consume the neighboring template and binding design system; R1 does not redesign them.

Prophet integration should initially be read-only evidence/context attached through accepted consumer contracts. Future candidate generation, ranking or entry changes require explicit owner admission and evaluation; no model summary or sentiment observation originates or sizes a trade.

## 10. Evaluation hypotheses and proposed acceptance cases

### Research hypotheses, not demonstrated predictive edges

**H1:** role-aware economics explains issuer earnings changes better than commodity direction alone. Test against a commodity-only baseline using point-in-time reports and subsequent realized company metrics. Failure to add robust out-of-sample explanation weakens the proposed complexity.

**H2:** realized spread/contract information adds value over spot output prices. Test actual realized margins with lagged and scenario-aware inputs. If timing/measurement error overwhelms the gain, simplify or keep the field descriptive.

**H3:** source-clock and unit controls reduce false opportunities. Measure corrections to deliberately misleading cases, abstention behavior and missingness, not only coverage growth.

**H4:** economic improvements plus existing market-confirmation context improve useful early discovery without raising false-positive burden. This requires later point-in-time out-of-sample and prospective shadow evaluation. R1 makes no such performance claim.

**H5:** financial-claim/financing modeling changes conclusions for developers and leveraged issuers. Test per-share outcomes and financing events, not merely commodity beta or asset-level project appreciation.

### Proposed semantic and product tests

These are acceptance specifications only; no application test has been executed.

- **BM-R1-01:** rising end-market demand with faster usable supply cannot automatically yield tightening.
- **BM-R1-02:** a mine-stage balance and refined-metal balance remain separate.
- **BM-R1-03:** treatment-charge changes do not bypass the processor's other margin components.
- **BM-R1-04:** output price, own input price and competitor-cost context retain distinct clocks and signs.
- **BM-R1-05:** cost pass-through revenue is not treated as equal incremental operating profit.
- **BM-R1-06:** acquired sales and organic/same-store activity remain distinct.
- **BM-R1-07:** by-product and co-product costs cannot share an unlabeled ranking.
- **BM-R1-08:** a negative net cash-cost metric retains its credits and exclusions.
- **BM-R1-09:** product tons, nutrient tons, contained metal and payable metal cannot silently mix.
- **BM-R1-10:** internally upgraded/intersegment output is not double-counted as independent external production or revenue.
- **BM-R1-11:** gross/consolidated/attributable quantities and minority claims reconcile without duplicate subtraction.
- **BM-R1-12:** project announcement or nameplate capacity is not current commercial delivery.
- **BM-R1-13:** a resource estimate is not silently promoted into a reserve, financed project or proven cash flow.
- **BM-R1-14:** a page refresh cannot become an economic-data refresh or point-in-time proof.
- **BM-R1-15:** preliminary, final and corrected versions preserve supersession rather than becoming independent events.
- **BM-R1-16:** multiple issuer listings and overlapping thematic cohorts do not manufacture independent confirmation.
- **BM-R1-17:** absent data stays unavailable, not zero, false or an invented exposure weight.
- **BM-R1-18:** research context cannot modify existing recommendation/rank/entry/size outputs without separate admission.
- **BM-R1-19:** private/full-fidelity evidence is inaccessible through anonymous/public mirrors on the eventual production path.
- **BM-R1-20:** a real source observation can eventually be traced from the existing Theme Tracker through its dossier and company to the source, with correct clocks, units, limitations and desktop/mobile display.

Predictive evaluation must preserve the historical information set, revisions, eligible issuer universe, delistings, currency and liquidity. Compare against simple sector/commodity/momentum baselines; separate earnings-explanation quality, discovery timing, false positives and investable returns. Do not treat narrative plausibility, schema tests, retrospective examples or UI screenshots as alpha evidence.
