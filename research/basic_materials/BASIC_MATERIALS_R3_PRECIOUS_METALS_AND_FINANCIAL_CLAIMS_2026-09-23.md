## 1. Research outcome and architecture boundary

The central R3 result is a proposed separation between **physical metal, operating activity, contractual economic rights, financing and the value attributable to a share**. R2 showed why different operations capture material economics differently. R3 shows why two investors exposed to the same mine can receive very different cash flows, why apparently capital-light businesses still require capital, and why an improving asset can produce a disappointing per-share outcome.

The intended user job remains proactive opportunity discovery: identify an economically important change, explain the businesses and financial claims affected, establish what was already expected, and connect qualified research to the existing company/watchlist/entry workflow. Accounting precision is necessary but not the product itself. The product must tell the investor what the distinction changes and what to investigate next.

## 2. Material demand is not one precious-metals regime

### 2.1 Gold: separate investment flows, physical use and inventories

The World Gold Council's July 30 Q2 2026 report records total demand including OTC of approximately 1,269 tonnes, while ETF holdings contracted by 45 tonnes and central-bank demand was 289 tonnes. The supply page separately reports 965.6 tonnes of mine production, -22.8 tonnes of net producer hedging and 326.1 tonnes of recycling. These are the report's dated estimates, not September positioning observations. [P01, P14]

**Research consequence:** a single gold-demand arrow loses the mechanisms. The proposed dossier should preserve monetary/investment flows, fabrication, recycling, mine output and producer hedging at their actual grain. A rise in one category cannot become evidence that every category is improving. An estimated OTC component should retain the source's methodology and uncertainty rather than become a precisely observed end-user purchase series.

**Hypothesis to evaluate:** a gold-equity opportunity becomes more interesting when a material-level change coincides with an issuer-specific improvement in retained cash, investment conversion or financial risk. The metal narrative alone is insufficient. The research should distinguish changes in the commodity valuation environment from changes in mining output, local-currency costs, hedges, royalties, remaining investment and share count. No permanent coefficient linking gold to real yields or the dollar is assumed here; that relationship requires later regime- and horizon-specific evaluation.

### 2.2 Silver: growth in applications can coexist with lower metal demand

The Silver Institute's World Silver Survey 2026 release, prepared with Metals Focus, describes 2025 industrial demand of 657.4 million ounces, down 3%. It attributes an important offset to photovoltaic thrifting and substitution despite support from other industrial applications. Its mine-production discussion also identifies growth in silver obtained as a by-product of copper operations. The release date was not unambiguously captured in the inspected page text; its report year and 2025 reference period are known. [P02]

**Proposed decomposition:** physical application demand should be modeled as units of activity, material loading per unit, technology mix, manufacturing yield and inventory movement. These variables must not be compressed into the end-market growth rate.

**H3, illustrative only:** 20% growth in application units with a 15% fall in metal loading gives `1.20 × 0.85 = 1.02`, or 2% higher material demand before other effects. This is not a photovoltaic or silver-market forecast. It demonstrates the missing term in a simple adoption-to-demand mapping.

On supply, research should distinguish primary silver economics from silver whose production is governed partly by a copper, lead/zinc or gold operation. Do not assume a higher silver price creates an equally elastic response from every mine. The existing issuer/asset evidence must establish the actual production incentives and bottlenecks before making that claim about a company.

### 2.3 Platinum: preserve forecast revisions and the horizon split

The September 9 WPIC/Metals Focus Platinum Quarterly forecasts a **265 thousand ounce full-year 2026 surplus**, comprising an estimated 548 thousand ounce first-half surplus and a projected 283 thousand ounce second-half deficit. Its revised outlook is not a completed full-year observation. The glossary excludes ETF, exchange and working inventories from its defined above-ground-stock estimate. [P03]

Our arithmetic preserves the source's displayed precision: `7,353 - 7,089 = 264` thousand ounces versus its published annual balance of 265; `1,906 - 1,663 = 243` versus the Q2 balance of 244. Both one-thousand-ounce residuals remain explicit. No source-error claim or invented unrounded correction follows.

**Design consequence:** the current view should not repeat an older deficit narrative after the relevant source revises it, nor average old and new forecasts as independent confirmations. At the same time, a full-year surplus does not erase a second-half deficit forecast. The existing conflict vocabulary should express vintage and horizon differences before attempting an economic synthesis.

The proposed state is therefore a set of dimensions: defined physical-use balance, investment/stock flows, available inventory at a stated boundary, regional delivery conditions and forecast horizon. An inventory-to-use ratio is not an exhaustion countdown, especially when the numerator deliberately excludes other categories of metal.

### 2.4 Palladium and the smaller PGMs need separate hypotheses

Johnson Matthey's May 2026 report release anticipated that palladium could move into a small surplus, with lower automotive and investment demand and a recovery in recycling among its explanations. It also distinguished rhodium and ruthenium balances rather than assigning all PGMs the same outcome. These are the author's May forecasts, not a September forecast update or observed annual result. [P04]

**Research direction:** separately investigate platinum/palladium substitution, powertrain mix, catalyst loading, scrappage and recycling lags, industrial process demand and the contribution of smaller metals to producer revenue. A proposed platinum, palladium, rhodium, ruthenium or iridium subtheme should identify the particular mechanism and evidence coverage. A broad PGM label may remain useful navigation, but it should not manufacture a common directional signal.

We have not completed independent long-history models for individual small PGMs, nor should low-coverage series receive confident numerical forecasts merely to fill the template.

## 3. Worked operating-company case: Agnico Eagle

Agnico's July 29 Q2 release reports 855,816 payable ounces produced versus 835,505 ounces sold. Its realized price uses sold ounces; its AISC uses produced ounces. The release also restates the prior-period cost composition following the treatment of NTI payments and identifies in-kind royalties. Its cash-flow reconciliation reports USD2,144.120 million of operating cash, USD809.255 million of property/plant/mine-development additions and USD1,334.865 million of free cash flow. [P05]

The source-reconciled calculation is:

`2,144.120 - 809.255 = 1,334.865 million USD`.

The reported adjustments for non-cash working-capital components sum to negative USD31.916 million; applying them gives free cash flow before these changes of USD1,302.949 million. These are two measures on their stated bases, not competing versions of one supposedly incorrect result. [P05; arithmetic AEM-01–04]

**Rejected shortcut:** `(realized gold price - AISC) × production` is not an admitted free-cash-flow calculation. The price and cost denominators differ, the cost definition is not a complete shareholder-cash definition, and production less sales is not automatically an inventory increase. Our 20,311-ounce arithmetic difference is only a difference between the reported quantities, not an inferred stock ledger. The source's in-kind royalty treatment makes that caution concrete.

**Proposed operating view:** show price realization, quantity realization, comparable cost basis, sustaining/development spending, tax/interest and cash conversion as named dimensions. Preserve the issuer's original and restated comparative versions; a definition change must not create a false operating inflection.

The resulting user explanation should go beyond an accounting warning. It should identify whether an apparent opportunity is driven by physical efficiency, exposure to higher prices, credit allocation, capital-spending timing or a favorable comparison. Each explanation should identify the next observable confirmation. A project approval is not equivalent to the project's cash generation, and a capex increase cannot be evaluated without its scope and expected output.

Agnico serves as a proof case for these dimensions, not a recommendation or a declaration that this single producer represents all gold-mining economics.

## 4. Stream-holder case: Wheaton's economics and the Antamina rights

### 4.1 Ongoing purchase cost is not the investment's full cost

Wheaton's August 6 Q2 release distinguishes sales from attributable production and separates cost of sales excluding depletion from depletion. Selected USD-million figures reconcile as `929.201 - 118.843 - 122.502 = 687.856` gross margin. After the disclosed operating, other, finance and tax items, net earnings reconcile to USD543.236 million. The report also describes substantial upfront stream payments and financing during the period. [P06]

**Research consequence:** a stream portfolio should not be compared with an operator by subtracting one reported ongoing purchase-cost figure from the metal price and calling the result investment return. Upfront investment, depletion, finance costs, future commitments, taxes and delivery timing belong in the relevant analytical bridge. Depletion is not itself a current cash payment, but excluding it does not make the original investment free.

Reported produced-but-not-delivered estimates, delivered metal and sold metal must also remain separate. Our difference between reported GEO sales and GEO production is not admitted as the portfolio's produced-but-not-delivered rollforward. Timing, source estimates and conversion methods must be reconciled first.

### 4.2 An exact stream interest is not equity ownership

The April 1 Antamina closing release describes USD4.3 billion of upfront consideration, 33.75% of payable silver using a 90% payable factor until 100 million ounces have been delivered, then 22.5%, and ongoing payments of 20% of spot. BHP's counterparty disclosure explains that the mine company is not party to the arrangement, governance is unchanged and settlement uses metal credits rather than physical shipments. [P07, P08]

**H1, illustrative scaling exercise:** assume total-mine contained silver output of 10 million ounces for a period and a USD30/oz realization, solely to demonstrate the published coefficients. Before the delivery threshold:

`entitled ounces = 10,000,000 × 0.90 × 0.3375 = 3,037,500`.

`contribution before other costs and upfront-capital recovery = 3,037,500 × 30 × (1 - 0.20) = USD72.9m`.

After the threshold, the same hypothetical physical output and price produce USD48.6m on the reduced percentage. This is neither an Antamina production forecast nor a valuation of the stream.

The 33.75% input in this calculation is already expressed relative to total mine production under the described arrangement. Multiplying by the BHP interest a second time would understate it. Conversely, recording the streamer as another physical producer would overstate global metal supply. A metal-credit right does not establish ownership of a mine, operational control, a shipment route or an actual supplier relationship between every named party.

**Proposed research opportunities:** approaching a contractual delivery step-down; new mine output inside or outside a stream's coverage; a funding completion that changes delivery risk; changes in realization and delivery timing; or returns on deploying cash into additional rights. These are testable questions, not reasons to automatically favor streaming companies over operators.

## 5. Franco-Nevada: economic equivalents and available capital

Franco-Nevada's August 11 release explicitly changes 2026 GEO conversion to fixed guidance-based ratios from its prior prevailing-market-price method. Its Q2 Net GEO calculation is `132,405 - USD45.9m / USD4,500 = 122,205`. The same release decomposes USD4,305.4m of available capital into USD1,014.2m cash, USD1,041.2m investments and USD2,250m available credit. These are not interchangeable cash categories. [P09]

**H4, synthetic example:** 1,000 gold ounces and 60,000 silver ounces equal 2,000 gold-equivalent ounces at 60 silver ounces per gold ounce, but 1,750 at 80. The physical quantities did not decline; the equivalence method changed. A 12.5% decrease in this illustrative aggregate is not a production contraction.

Our proposed measurement layer should preserve original quantities and each method's ratio basis, version and period. A constant-method comparison may be derived only when adequate components exist. Otherwise show the published metrics with a comparability limitation, rather than fabricate a harmonized growth series.

A Net GEO is a useful issuer-defined economic measure, not a new amount of mined metal. Similarly, available credit is not cash, and investments may not be realizable at a displayed carrying/market value without cost or market impact. A funding judgment requires eligibility, conditions, maturities and committed uses, not only a large headline capital number.

**Royalty-family model proposal:** do not group every claim as a fixed percentage of mine revenue. In a deliberately simplified scenario, a claim paying 2% of a defined net-revenue base reacts differently to a claim paying 10% of a defined profit base. With revenue 100 and eligible cost rising from 80 to 90, those toy receipts are 2 versus 2 for the revenue-based claim and 2 versus 1 for the profit-based claim. The calculation does not define the legal terms of any actual royalty. Real deductibles, loss carry-forwards, payout status, geographical coverage and buy-back terms must come from the applicable source.

Even a revenue-based claim with no direct operating-cost deduction retains exposure to whether the operator can continue funding and producing. Cost insulation is not absence of counterparty or shutdown risk. The proposed framework should express direct cash sensitivity and indirect operating vulnerability separately.

## 6. Co-produced PGM basket case: Valterra

Valterra's July 29 interim release reports distinct own-mined, refined and sold quantities, while its AISC is denominated per 3E ounce. It attributes lower AISC partly to higher sales, by-product revenues and lower sustaining capital. Its April operating report explicitly labels total metal-in-concentrate production as 5E+Au. [P10]

**Held calculation:** do not subtract a 3E cost from a total-PGM basket price or apply that cost to 5E+Au production until the producer's exact component, ownership and sold/produced conventions have been reconciled. R3 establishes that the published denominators differ; it does not admit a guessed conversion or a complete producer-specific margin model.

This case also motivates a proposed distinction among mining output, purchased concentrate, refining throughput and metal sales. A refining recovery after earlier disruption need not imply an equal increase in newly mined metal. A purchased-concentrate activity can have different economics from producing the same metal at an owned mine.

For company exposure, the proposed PGM view should connect individual metal revenue, by-product contributions, local costs, process stage and the source-defined basket. Material substitutions in one end market may have a different effect on a diversified co-producer from their effect on one metal price. This is a research hypothesis requiring the actual payable mix and realization terms, not permission to assign arbitrary weights.

## 7. Resources, reserves and mine life: use the stated evidence boundary

The CIM 2014 definition document distinguishes resources with reasonable prospects of eventual extraction from economically mineable reserves supported by modifying factors and at least pre-feasibility work. It requires a reserve reference point and clarity on whether reported resources include or exclude reserves. It does not equate reserve classification with a fully constructed operation or all approvals already obtained. We inspected the relevant definition and reporting pages. This is the specific definition reference, not a claim that all current global reporting regimes are identical. [P11]

**Proposed data requirements:** retain source standard/version, author/qualified-person attribution, effective date, publication date, asset/ownership basis, classification, commodity/grade, quantity, cut-off assumptions, commodity-price assumptions, recovery/reference point, resource-reserve inclusivity, study type and relevant technical limitations.

A proposed resource/reserve-change bridge should separate depletion, exploration/conversion, acquisition/disposal, ownership, methodology and economic assumptions. Without that decomposition, rising reported ounces should not become proof of successful exploration. A lower cut-off can alter reported tonnage and grade without a new discovery; the source must support the actual explanation.

Research coverage should distinguish at least these questions: Is the geological estimate credible at its stated confidence? Can the material be mined and processed economically under the study assumptions? Is the project financed? Are the required permissions and infrastructure in place? Has the plant achieved its completion tests? These are concurrent dimensions, not a new universal linear lifecycle engine.

For valuation, do not divide an unqualified aggregate ounce count by one year's output and market the result as a fully evidenced mine-life forecast. A schedule must handle grade, recovery, throughput, sequence and capital requirements. An issuer's long-horizon exploration opportunity should remain distinct from reserves supporting its current plan.

## 8. Koné: real financing evidence with milestone and contract conditions

### 8.1 Liquidity is not an unrestricted construction budget

Montage's August 11 Q2 activity release reports June 30 liquidity of USD309.0m, comprising cash of USD154.4m, other liquid assets of USD29.6m and undrawn facilities of USD125.0m. Against a USD885m headline construction estimate and USD623.8m disbursed, remaining headline investment is USD261.2m. The source separately identifies the oxide and hard-rock completion milestones. [P12]

Our mechanical residual `309.0 - 261.2 = USD47.8m` is not a complete financing surplus. Our cash-only comparison `154.4 - 261.2 = -USD106.8m` is not proof of an unfinanceable shortfall. They are deliberately different perimeters. Eligibility and timing of facilities, other commitments, exploration, financing costs and contingency must be reconciled before a conclusion.

The proposed product should distinguish cash, securities/other liquid assets, committed undrawn facilities and merely proposed financing. It should also distinguish disbursed, committed and forecast investment, without adding committed amounts to disbursed amounts when the former already includes the latter. A quarter-end figure and a later press-release update must not share one unlabeled as-of date.

Dry commissioning, an oxide first pour, sustained hard-rock processing and commercial production should remain different observations or targets. Evidence for one must not silently trigger the financial or product consequences of another. A loan-price step-down tied to a completion condition is a condition, not an automatic calendar forecast.

### 8.2 The original stream terms are not a flat perpetual percentage

Wheaton's October 23, 2024 Koné announcement describes USD625m of staged upfront consideration, a 19.5% payable-gold interest stepping to 10.8% and then 5.4% after delivery thresholds, a 99.9% payable factor, and a time-limited price adjustment to the headline 20%-of-spot ongoing payment. Its example pays USD675 at USD3,200 gold, rather than the naïve USD640. It also describes schedule and area-of-interest conditions. These are dated announcement terms, not a complete current contract audit. [P13]

**H2:** where a simplified flat-20% assumption is expressly valid, the operator retains `1 - 0.195 × (1 - 0.20) = 84.4%` of the defined payable-metal value before other costs and taxes. That is not a universal Koné realization formula. The original price adjustment, payable basis, delivery milestones and later amendments must be applied as relevant; the general adjustment curve cannot be reconstructed from one example alone.

The announcement's key terms and cautionary prose contain different deposit amounts (625 versus 600 million). R3 uses the clearly labeled key-terms figure for describing that publication but flags the inconsistency; no blind whole-page extractor or guessed legal resolution is accepted. A future production contract needs the authoritative agreement/current filing and the actual payment history.

**Economic conclusion to test:** funding can make an asset feasible while transferring part of its future economics. A transaction can be useful without being free. The same headline metal-price gain may accrue differently before and after thresholds, expiry dates or repayment conditions.

## 9. H5 — a worked financing and dilution scenario

This is an intentionally simplified one-date thought experiment, not a discounted-cash-flow valuation of Montage, Wheaton or another issuer. All amounts are USD millions except per-share values. The aim is to expose accounting boundaries and the difference between share-count dilution and transferred asset economics.

### Assumptions

The present value of future after-tax, unlevered operating cash is 1,200, **before** remaining construction spending. Existing cash is 50, remaining construction is 400, existing shares are 100 million and there is no existing debt. New debt contributes 200 and has fair value 200; its future obligations are represented by that fair value, with no additional assumed financing subsidy or separate tax shield. All funding and construction spending occur at the modeled valuation date, leaving no unused construction cash. No fees, options, warrants, minority interests or other assets are assumed. A real model must include them where applicable.

The remaining funding requirement is `400 - 50 - 200 = 150`.

### Equity funding at different issue prices

After funding and construction, modeled equity value is `1,200 - 200 = 1,000`. The share count depends on the price at which the required 150 is raised.

| Scenario | New shares, millions | Total shares, millions | Modeled value/share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Raise 150 at $3/share | 50 | 150 | $6.67 |
| Raise 150 at $6/share | 25 | 125 | $8.00 |
| Raise at the model's pre-financing $8.50 value | 17.6471 | 117.6471 | $8.50 |

The last case is an internal consistency check. The unfinanced residual value is `1,200 + 50 - 400 = 850`, or $8.50 per existing share. Issuing equity at that modeled fair value does not itself destroy per-share value in this frictionless setup. The lower issue-price cases transfer more of the modeled residual to new shareholders. They do not establish that an actual market price is wrong or that the assumed asset value is achievable.

### Stream funding instead

Suppose a stream supplies the same 150 without new shares, but the present value of the future **net** operating cash surrendered is 220 on the same after-tax basis. Modeled equity becomes `1,200 - 220 - 200 = 780`, or $7.80 per share.

This is better than the $3 equity-issue case and worse than the $6 equity-issue case under the stated assumptions. That is not a universal ranking of financing methods. It demonstrates why the issue price and the value of cash rights surrendered both matter. The stream's upfront 150 funded construction; it must not remain as extra cash in the completed-project value. Likewise, do not subtract the stream burden again if the operating cash-flow valuation already includes it.

### Overrun and delay

With a 100 construction overrun funded by more equity at $3, unchanged operating value and unchanged debt, total shares become 183.3333 million and modeled value falls to $5.45 per share.

If instead only the operating cash-flow schedule is delayed one year at a 10% discount rate, its present value falls to `1,200 / 1.10`. With the original 150 million shares and debt fair value 200, modeled value is $5.94 per share. This isolates timing. It deliberately assumes no extra construction spending, financing fee or debt-obligation change; an actual delay scenario must reconcile those rather than automatically count every possible cost twice.

**Product consequence:** the project page should expose asset quality, deliverability, financing, retained economics and per-share value separately. A financing announcement is not just an event badge. It can change the share count, contractual commodity sensitivity, repayment schedule and completion conditions.

## 10. Proposed granular precious-metals research cohorts

These are proposed research groups, not approved canonical themes, resolved securities or new price baskets. One company can participate in several views with explicit source bases.

| Research cohort | Main question | Earliest useful evidence to seek | Evidence that weakens the interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monetary/investment gold | Which demand and valuation inputs changed? | Dated flows and relevant macro/market observations | Opposing flows, stale observations, unsuitable historical mapping |

The minimum company dossier should describe the business/asset and its economic role before listing associated themes. It should also keep securities/listings distinct from the issuer and distinguish source-supported company activity from a proposed thematic association.

A basket's historical return should not silently use today's contract perimeter, today's corporate portfolio or today's equivalent-ounce ratio. Historical membership and exposures require point-in-time support; otherwise the display must be explicitly descriptive/non-PIT. The same mine appearing through an operator, a royalty and a stream is overlapping underlying risk, not three independent confirmations.

## 11. Candidate rerating mechanisms and evaluation questions

R3 adds the following focused hypotheses to R2's economic framework. They are research candidates, not weights, probabilities or stock-selection rules.

**Contract-right transition:** a delivery threshold, payment expiry, buy-back or coverage change alters retained cash at unchanged physical output. Test the incremental value of contract terms against a metal-price-only model. Separate known scheduled events from new information.

**Funded development transition:** financing and verified milestones reduce a specific execution constraint, while dilution and surrendered cash rights remain acceptable under explicit scenarios. Test subsequent operating and per-share outcomes rather than classifying every financing announcement as favorable.

**Cash conversion after investment:** operating capacity begins producing saleable/payable output and cash after a capital-intensive phase. Distinguish a commissioning press release from sustained operating proof and actual cash conversion.

**Exposure clarification:** a market narrative overstates or understates the metal sensitivity of the actual operating or contractual right. Test whether clarified exposure explains company responses out of sample; a plausible mechanism is not enough.

**Intensity/substitution inflection:** demand per end-market unit changes. Test against an activity-only baseline with correct technology shares, data lags and forecast vintages.

**Co-product profit migration:** a changing payable metal mix alters the company's marginal economics. Avoid treating the corresponding by-product credit as both higher revenue and a second independent cost-efficiency improvement.

**Forecast-vintage correction:** an apparently attractive setup rests on superseded balances or mismatched periods. Evaluate false-positive prevention and useful abstention, not only the number of opportunities produced.

For each candidate, retain the information set available at the decision time, announcement versus actual dates, comparable financial definitions, corporate actions, survival/delisting, eligible liquidity and currency. Compare against simple metal, sector and momentum baselines. Distinguish explanation accuracy, earnings/cash forecasting, discovery lead time, false-positive burden and investable returns. A short-horizon stock move cannot validate a multi-year project-value thesis by itself.

The model should be allowed to abstain when entitlement, measurement basis, funding availability or expectation history is missing. Missingness is not a low score or evidence that the economics are adverse. It is a limit on the conclusion that can be supported.

## 12. Product and integration implications

### Preserve the shared journey

Use the existing Theme Tracker/sector entry, the accepted material/subtheme dossier, the existing company workflow and the lawful source view. No new main dashboard or rival template is proposed.

The primary view should answer: **what changed; which business or cash right is affected; why it matters; what is already known; and what observation would change the read**. The contractual and accounting detail opens on demand.

A proposed economic-rights section would show source-bound asset/business identity, rights holder, legal/operating counterparty as reported, affected metal, ownership versus payment right, payable basis, ongoing payment formula, thresholds/expiry, source clock and coverage limitations. Until an existing canonical owner supports durable asset identity, retain source-scoped descriptions rather than inventing a global asset master.

A proposed funding section would distinguish cash, liquid investments, eligible undrawn facilities, remaining committed/forecast investment and other cash obligations at one date. The user should be able to inspect how an illustrative change in equity issue price, stream burden, overrun or timing affects retained value. Scenario inputs must be conspicuously labeled, editable only within the eventual governed product design, and never presented as reported facts.

### Useful proposed visualizations

The preferred research composition is a compact material-to-cash explanation with drill-downs: a physical-output/entitlement comparison; a cash-flow bridge; a dated contract-threshold timeline; a funding-sources/uses view; and a scenario comparison. A multi-node graph is justified only where it helps the user understand overlapping mine risk or rights coverage. The neighboring template and binding design system retain layout/style ownership.

Material state, company economics, valuation and entry must remain separate. A dossier should be able to explain that an industry's outlook changed while a particular claimant's delivery percentage declines, or that a promising project has significant remaining financing exposure. These are not necessarily contradictory signals.

### Prophet boundary

Initial integration should provide read-only, source-backed context through accepted consumer contracts. The future user should receive a useful company shortlist with the reason each company expresses the researched mechanism, but research membership alone is not an instruction to rank, buy or size it. The existing candidacy, technical entry and authority owners retain those decisions until an explicitly admitted and evaluated extension is accepted.

Fable's eventual packet must include exact source-owner mappings, scope and failure behavior, private-publication gates, prototype fixtures, test requirements and real-path proof. R3 informs that packet; it does not pre-approve a native schema or assign implementation custody.
