## 2. Evidence that distinguishes the operating models

These are dated issuer/industry observations. Management explanations remain attributed; they do not independently establish causation. Calculations below use stated boundaries. Source locators are in section 13.

### 2.1 Albemarle: benchmark recovery does not make every processing route viable

Albemarle's August 5 Q2 release reports Energy Storage average realized prices of $19.53/kg LCE versus $12.17. Its measure combines salts and spodumene on an equivalent basis. The issuer reports Q2 operating cash of $709.997m, capex of $71.731m and FCF of $638.266m. Its H1 cash statement removes $247.857m of equity-method income and adds $131.744m of received dividends. These are not the same cash measure. [B02]

The February 11 Kemerton announcement said recent lithium-price improvement was insufficient for that hard-rock hydroxide conversion operation. Management expected idling to improve EBITDA while meeting demand through other channels without reducing projected 2026 sales volumes. Those were announcement-date expectations, not a verified permanent plant outcome. [B03]

**Design consequence:** maintain a distinction between extractive economics, merchant conversion, captive conversion and portfolio routing. A materials price rise can improve one segment while leaving another uneconomic. Investigate the route's delivered input cost, conversion cost, yield, specification, logistics and contract realization rather than assigning the issuer one undifferentiated lithium sensitivity.

A company may improve retained economics by changing the production route, not only by producing more. Conversely, an apparently strong affiliate contribution may not yet have become distributable parent cash. The proposed synthesis should explain both mechanisms without calling route idling inherently positive or negative for the stock.

### 2.2 PLS: matched price/cost bases, quality and commercial economics

PLS's July 30 June-quarter report gives an estimated US$2,107/t CIF realized price at approximately 5.2% shipped grade, US$2,415/t SC6 equivalent, US$437/t FOB unit cost and US$600/t CIF unit cost. Cost footnotes specify exclusions and credits. Its downstream JV produced 882t and 2,679t of hydroxide in the two trains; reported battery-grade shares were 99% and 98%. Operations used flexible batches according to conversion spreads and customer demand. The reported ownership was 18%, with an option to increase to 30%, not current 30% ownership. [B04, pp1,4,9]

An indicative same-basis subtraction is $2,107-$600=$1,507 per shipped tonne. Mixing the SC6-equivalent price with FOB cost produces $1,978, a $471 larger number. Neither is company FCF. The latter is deliberately an ineligible comparison, not an alternate margin estimate.

**Design consequence:** a high product-conformance rate does not establish economic utilization, customer approval for every application, or attractive conversion returns. Preserve the reason for batch operation. Do not interpret idle capacity as automatically broken, nor full nameplate as automatic earnings growth. The JV ownership option belongs in a separate contingent exposure, not the current attributable production calculation.

### 2.3 SQM: equivalent volume, attribution and flexible output

The official SEC release reports Q2 lithium sales of 84.1kt LCE, comprising approximately 75.8kt from Novandino and 8.3kt from international operations. The international table gives 48.0kt SC6 concentrate and 1.2kt LCE hydroxide, explicitly on SQM's attributable basis, with a 6.74x concentrate conversion factor. Management describes a dual-purpose carbonate/hydroxide conversion project expected by mid-2027 and a separate mine/concentrator expansion with expected first output in 2030. These are different projects and horizons. [B05]

The source-specific calculation 48/6.74+1.2=8.3216617kt explains the approximately 8.3kt total within displayed precision. It does not measure actual refinery recovery or prove physical product interchangeability. Do not apply ownership again to a figure already attributable.

**Design consequence:** keep material equivalence separate from process yield, qualification and value. Product-switching capability could be economically useful, but needs the relevant route, costs, timing, approved customers and maximum switching limits. Do not add carbonate and hydroxide nameplates when they share equipment. A multi-year expansion is not current deliverable supply.

The initial issuer PDF returned 403. A different original SEC HTML source was recovered; the source gap is resolved for these selected observations, not for a full legal review of every joint-venture distribution right.

### 2.4 NOVONIX: internal tests, customer qualification and full ramp are distinct

The June sample announcement reports internal conformity and shipment for customer validation. On September 9, the company reported that its lead customer's testing met 12 of 14 parameters; one remaining parameter was near its limit and the other used pass/fail criteria. Internal testing had met all parameters. Mass-production sales were still expected in H2 2027, conditional on successful qualification. [B07-B08]

The August 31-authorized H1 financial report separately extends the full-capacity planning assumption from December 2027 to September 2031. It reports US$23.055782m operating cash outflow and US$59.534916m closing cash, and describes further expansion funding needs. [B09, pp20-22,36]

**Design consequence:** 12/14 is not an 86% qualification probability, 86% sellable nameplate, or final acceptance. A binary requirement can be decisive. The observation must bind to the customer, sample/product, production process and test conditions. A later customer result should update the qualification interpretation without deleting the earlier internally observed result.

First commercial sales and full plant ramp must remain separate forecasts. A useful research signal might be confirmed repeatable qualification plus a credible funding path and orders, not the sample announcement alone. This is a proposed mechanism; no probability or investable advantage has been fitted.

### 2.5 NMG: mine construction does not prove the downstream plant is ready

NMG's August 13 update records a mine FID and construction launch, with C$33m of expenditures and C$167m of commitments at June 30. The separate 13ktpa battery-material plant remained targeted for an H2 2026 FID, with a C$374m estimate. Its C$427m equity package comprised C$295m and C$132m components. Potential Eni offtake discussions and potential Panasonic investment were described separately from executed financing. [B10]

**Design consequence:** represent evidence by asset and obligation. A mine's financing or permission cannot automatically satisfy the converter's funding, construction or product-qualification requirements. The contracted product can be concentrate rather than active anode material; do not relabel it because both are graphite. Preserve committed versus incurred costs and distinguish released escrow proceeds from additional new financing. Never add the components and their total as three separate sources of liquidity.

NMG provides a natural-graphite development comparison; it is not evidence of current commercial battery-grade production. Syrah remains relevant to future coverage, but its latest report body was not recovered in this unit and its operating numbers were not imported from generated summaries.

### 2.6 MP Materials: intermediate revenue, internal transfers and separate support income

MP's August 6 release reports $108.490m consolidated revenue and $17.580m separately reported price-protection income. Segment revenues of $95.629m and $16.524m require a $3.663m intercompany elimination. Consolidated adjusted EBITDA is $28.493m after corporate and intercompany adjustments. Magnetics revenue is labeled magnetic precursor products. NdPr sales of 1,006t include intercompany activity and differ from 840t of NdPr production. [B11]

**Design consequence:** a segment name is not proof that revenue comes from a finished qualified magnet. Track the reported commercial product, not the aspirational endpoint. Do not treat internal material transfers as external demand or another independent addition to output. Market receipts and contractual support can both matter to value, but need separate accounting, eligibility, duration and cash-realization views.

Subtracting $17.580m from adjusted EBITDA leaves $10.913m as a one-factor arithmetic residual. It is not a modeled no-support counterfactual: removing an agreement could alter production, investment, financing and behavior. The research should explain the dependency, not use a subtraction to imply a policy verdict or autonomous trading conclusion.

### 2.7 Lynas: product mix, contract scope and funding composition

The FY2026 issuer annual report shows A$977.945m revenue, A$392.452m gross profit and A$222.352m profit. A$80.7/kg is the average across all sold rare-earth products, not a USD NdPr quote. The balance sheet separates A$504.128m cash from A$704.971m short-term deposits. The reported JARE arrangement describes a 5ktpa firm NdPr offtake with a US$110/kg floor and distinct availability/upside-sharing terms. A floor is therefore not automatically the realization for every product and tonne. [B12, printed pp17,86-87; screenshots inspected]

**Design consequence:** separate element, chemical form, reporting currency and contract coverage. Changes in a mixed-product average may reflect mix or contracts rather than a comparable market-price move. A term deposit is not the same cash category as immediately reported cash equivalents. Revenue/cost and balance-sheet figures above were visually verified; the report's cash-flow-page image failed retrieval and that statement is not admitted to R4 arithmetic.

## 3. Proposed material and process ontology

The ontology below is a research description to be mapped into existing owners. It is not a new global product master or an automatic set of GMI canonical themes.

### Lithium

Distinguish the resource/process description; concentrate, chloride/sulfate intermediate and finished carbonate/hydroxide; technical or customer-specific grade; original units; LCE conversion method; shipped versus saleable quantities; tolling versus owned conversion; and economic ownership. Keep customer contracts, price windows and logistics attached to the relevant sale rather than a universal spot ticker.

Do not assign every brine project a common cost or every hard-rock route a common yield. Study project-specific energy, reagents, water, recovery, impurities, waste handling, transport and capital needs before estimating a cost curve. Direct extraction or another process innovation is a technical route hypothesis until commercial scope, inputs, output and economics are evidenced.

### Graphite and alternative anodes

Separate natural concentrate, purified/spheronized material, coated active material and customer-specific formulations; synthetic feedstocks, processing/graphitization and finished formulations; and silicon-containing or other alternatives as explicitly described by their source. Where purification, shaping and coating differ by process, do not force a universal stage order or yield.

A purity specification is one attribute, not the full qualification contract. The relevant observations may include particle distribution, morphology, density, first-cycle efficiency, cycling, swelling and application-specific performance, but do not manufacture those fields or values where the issuer/customer does not disclose them. Argonne's cited research illustrates that electrode loading and paired chemistry affect performance; a laboratory result is not proof of commercial qualification. [B13]

### Rare-earth and magnet materials

Separate concentrate/mixed products, individual oxides, metals/alloys, precursor products and finished magnet descriptions. Preserve the source's elemental composition and subset relationship: NdPr is not additional to a total in which it is already included. Do not equate total REO tonnage to magnet-relevant tonnes, or an oxide's kilogram to a finished magnet's kilogram without a source-supported mass boundary.

Map the evidenced role of Nd/Pr and specific heavy-rare-earth or other materials at actual product/configuration scope. A supply-chain position is not proof of qualification, pricing power or confirmed sales. Product performance, motor/magnet configuration and customer application should remain source-specific. Full magnet design and Chinese producer coverage are unfinished; this unit does not assert one universal magnet BOM.

## 4. Demand: units, energy, chemistry, loading and inventory

IEA's May 2026 outlook estimates 2025 EV battery deployment at 1.2TWh and defines the measure from vehicle sales and volume-weighted battery sizes. Its cell-manufacturing capacity measure exceeds 4TWh and has a different, broader use/perimeter. The ratio is not accepted here as industry utilization. Its sodium-ion discussion identifies hard-carbon supply as a distinct constraint, rather than simply assuming graphite demand follows all battery growth. [B01]

The proposed demand decomposition is:

`application units -> usable energy or relevant device specification -> technology/chemistry shares -> material intensity -> manufacturing requirements -> inventory-adjusted purchasing`

These are connected quantities, not measurements that should be added. Battery deployment may lag material purchasing. Manufacturing scrap, recoverable material and recycling may change primary demand. Distinguish installed capacity, actual production, shipped cells, installed packs and end-use deployment. Require units, populations and clocks at every step.

### H1: illustrative demand divergence

Assume one million vehicles with 60kWh packs: 60GWh. Next assume 1.2 million vehicles with 55kWh packs: 66GWh. Vehicle growth is 20%, but energy growth is 10%.

For a hypothetical nickel-bearing share of 60% falling to 45%, and an assumed 0.6kg nickel/kWh within that share, demand falls from 21,600t to 17,820t, or 17.5%. These are transparent scenario inputs, not measured chemistry shares or industry intensity coefficients.

The point is not to forecast nickel from this example. It is to make the source of a material-demand change inspectable. Storage, vehicles and other applications need separate populations, then an explicit aggregation. A positive EV headline must not be applied mechanically to lithium, nickel, cobalt, graphite and rare-earth magnets together.

### Alternative chemistries and circular supply

Maintain technology announcements, demonstrated cells, customer products and actual deployment as different evidence. Forecast substitution with scenarios until an accepted model is calibrated. Do not extrapolate a laboratory performance claim into universal displacement of an incumbent material.

Likewise, an installed recycling plant needs suitable accessible feedstock, recovery and output-quality evidence. Future retired batteries cannot be counted as current feed. Material recovered from manufacturing scrap cannot be counted as both additional primary production and a reduction in primary demand without a reconciled flow boundary. End-market growth alone does not establish the availability or profitability of recycled supply.

## 5. Capacity: customer eligibility is a constraint, not a probability multiplier

A proposed capacity observation should answer: capacity of what product, at what process stage and time, measured on what basis, operating under what conditions, and accepted for which application?

Keep engineering nameplate, achieved throughput, recovered product, conformance, qualification, customer orders, delivered sales and cash collection separate. Do not multiply a sequence of arbitrary percentages to manufacture a precise accepted output. Several stages may share equipment, so capacities are constraints on a common flow rather than independent supplies.

### H3: explicit output allocation example

Assume a 10,000t/year finished-product rating, 60% actual utilization and a 75% first-pass saleable fraction. That gives 4,500t/year at this chosen boundary. Suppose 60% of this output is allocated to recipes with established customer eligibility: 2,700t/year. If actual orders cover only 2,000t, the scenario's ordered output is at most 2,000t before shipment/inventory timing.

The 60% allocation is an assumed share of recipes, not a 60% probability of passing tests. Rejected material could be reworked, downgraded, stored or lost; this scenario does not silently price it at zero. The full cash model must reflect that disposition and its cost.

NOVONIX's 12/14 example is an adversarial test: counting passed parameters cannot bypass an unmet acceptance condition. PLS's reported grade conformance is another: quality does not establish favorable conversion economics. NMG's asset-specific FID is a third: financed extraction does not imply a financed downstream plant.

## 6. Margins, realization and financing

### H2: the converter can lose much of a price recovery to feedstock

Assume one tonne of a specified finished product requires eight tonnes of a specified dry input. This is a hypothetical process coefficient, not the SQM LCE equivalence or a universal chemistry yield. Assume conversion cost of $4,000 per finished tonne.

At $20,000 output and $2,200 input per tonne, contribution is -$1,600. At $25,000 output with input unchanged it is $3,400. If input reprices to $2,600, the contribution falls to $200. These figures precede tax, overhead, working capital, financing and capital expenditure.

The proposed intelligence should show the realized purchase and selling windows, usable input grade, process route and inventory cost layers. Spot spreads are at most contextual estimates until matched to those actual economics. An integrated operator and a merchant converter should not inherit the same response simply because they sell the same final material.

### Cash reconciliation before valuation

Albemarle's H1 CFO less capex is our $885.834m residual; the Q2 issuer FCF reconciliation is separately $638.266m. Keep quarter and half-year separate. NOVONIX's H1 operating, investing and financing flows reconcile to a $20.811337m reduction before FX, then to $59.534916m closing cash. This does not establish a future runway: the company explicitly anticipates additional expansion funding. [B02, B09]

PLS's selected summary amounts do not fully reconstruct the cash movement: 579-162+443=860 versus the displayed balance increase of 835, leaving A$25m unexplained by that selected bridge. It is retained as incomplete, not assigned to a guessed tax, FX or working-capital item. Cash-balance growth must not be attributed solely to operations when financing is material. [B04]

### H4: a qualified ramp must still cover fixed cash costs

Assume quarterly shipments of 1,000t at $7,000/t, variable cash cost $4,000/t, fixed cash cost $3m and testing/rework cash $1m. Contribution is -$1m before working capital and capex. At 2,000t with other assumptions unchanged it is +$2m.

This is not a NOVONIX forecast. It illustrates why an accepted product and a positive unit spread are not enough. Volume, repeatability, fixed costs, remaining investment and liquidity determine the transition to cash generation. Model a qualification delay through its actual consequences rather than merely shifting an arbitrary valuation multiple.

### Contract-supported economics without confusing market evidence

The proposed contract view should identify eligible product, quantity, term, calculation reference, caps, sharing, performance conditions, counterparty and recognition/collection timing. A company-reported support arrangement is not proof that every product realizes the headline price, and eligibility may require additional original agreement evidence.

H5 assumes 1,000t sold at $80/kg with an independent floor payment to $110/kg covering only 400t. Market receipts plus the assumed scoped payment total $92m; wrongly applying $110 to all output gives $110m, an $18m overstatement. This is a synthetic agreement, not a replication or legal interpretation of MP or Lynas contracts. Preserve support income separately from customer revenue and do not infer current legislative eligibility from an issuer headline.

## 7. Rerating mechanisms worth testing

The mechanisms below are research hypotheses. They are not validated entry signals, probabilities, weights or an automatic priority ranking.

| Proposed mechanism | Discriminating evidence | What would weaken the interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Form-specific demand shift | Dated chemistry/application mix and purchasing evidence | Loading reductions, inventory release or substitution offset unit growth |
| Viable conversion-spread improvement | Matched realized prices, input grades/costs and relevant timing | Input repricing, poor recovery, royalty escalation or working-capital absorption |
| Qualification-to-commercial transition | Customer-bound acceptance and subsequent repeatable shipments | Internal-only tests, unresolved criteria, limited approved scope or no orders |
| Ramp-to-cash transition | Throughput, saleable output, cash costs and remaining investment | Yield instability, unfinanced expansion, weak orders or delayed collection |
| Portfolio route optimization | Documented output/routing changes and reconciled margins | Claimed savings absorbed elsewhere or lost contracted sales |
| Specific contractual de-risking | Executed obligations, scope, timing and payment evidence | Unmet conditions, expiry, counterparty concentration or double-counted support |
| Product-mix/value migration | Material/form quantities and non-overlapping revenue | Averages change without comparable economics or costs rise equally |
| Financing/completion de-risking | Funded asset milestones and per-share obligations | Downstream financing missing, dilution, contingent facilities or scope expansion |

For each hypothesis, identify what was known before the new observation. A revised capacity target is not a realized volume change. A customer result is not independent confirmation of every issuer claim. A rerating story must retain valuation and market context: improving economics can already be reflected in a security's price.

The strongest counterargument is that sparse, delayed public data may make these distinctions expensive without adding predictive value. Test that objection. Simpler commodity, sector and momentum baselines must remain in the evaluation. Retain semantic descriptions when there is insufficient coverage for a measured or predictive claim.
