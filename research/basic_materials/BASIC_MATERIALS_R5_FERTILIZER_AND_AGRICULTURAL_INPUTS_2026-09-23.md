## 1. Outcome and principal conclusion

The useful fertilizer question is not simply whether food demand, crop acreage or nutrient prices are rising. It is **which nutrient and product is needed, when it will be purchased and applied, which business can supply it at a retained margin, and when those economics become cash attributable to shareholders**.

R5 develops that question through seven issuer cases, agricultural research, original statistical definitions, explicit arithmetic and a consumer-oriented research casebook. It connects three levels that should not be conflated: the field's nutrient requirement; the producer/distributor's commercial cycle; and the equity's earnings, financing and expectations. The result extends the previous Materials framework instead of inventing another fertilizer score, graph or operating system.

The selected design is an owner-preserving dossier with separate dimensions for application demand, procurement, realized product/input margins, operating reliability, working capital and expectation changes. Its competitive value should be an explanation of who captures an economic change before that change becomes obvious in reported earnings. Whether it achieves that timing advantage remains an empirical question, not a claim made by this research.

Two simpler alternatives remain useful baselines. A nutrient-price screen is cheap and transparent, but cannot explain an input-driven margin squeeze. A crop-acreage screen supplies demand context, but cannot distinguish soil nutrient substitution, deferred purchases or channel inventory. The proposed richer model is justified only where it adds a checkable explanation or demonstrably better discovery. It must be able to abstain rather than hide poor coverage behind model confidence.

Phosphate industrial/feed uses and non-agricultural minerals remain explicit cross-links, not agricultural tonnage by assumption. Low-carbon ammonia projects may be economically relevant but require separate commercial-use and funding evidence; announced capacity is not automatically fertilizer supply. Product descriptors remain source-scoped until existing owners admit stronger identity.

For geographical context, distinguish production location, delivery destination, grower crop system, currency and season. North American spring application, Brazilian crop procurement and European off-season inventory do not occupy the same calendar position. The research architecture should accept those differences rather than force them into a single month-over-month comparison. This is a proposed modeling requirement, not a claim to have completed every regional calendar.

### Nutrient accounting is a vector, not just a normalized tonne

FAOSTAT's July 2025 methodology describes N, P2O5 and K2O nutrient equivalents, default product factors, country substitutions and imputation flags. It separates intermediate/secondary production to reduce double counting, acknowledges missing stock information and notes exceptions to calendar-year reporting. Its public annual series is not a current inventory feed. [F08]

The proposed observation therefore preserves original product quantity, assay, nutrient vector, mass basis, delivery terms, geography, economic period and method. A default factor is labeled default, not assayed. Nitrogen in DAP remains nitrogen while the same product also carries phosphate; the physical tonne is not counted twice as two independent shipments.

A grade such as 18-46-0 is a product composition convention, not evidence that every field can replace another product at equal nutrient cost. Mosaic's product catalog itself lists several MAP grades as well as multiple potash specifications. [F13] The engine should prefer the specific documented product over a generic acronym.

Ton, short ton, long ton, metric tonne, nutrient tonne, dry basis and wet basis must be explicit before arithmetic. A known conversion is not permission to invent a missing assay or process yield. Leave a source's ambiguous unit unresolved when it would change the comparison.

## 4. The grower's economic demand is not the producer's shipment series

I propose an explanatory demand model with separate components:

`crop area and rotation -> economically chosen nutrient application -> purchased product requirement -> procurement timing -> channel orders -> producer shipments`

This is not a fixed waterfall in which every observation must be available. It describes the questions a source-supported hypothesis needs to answer.

For a selected crop/region, application demand can be represented conceptually as area multiplied by a rate conditioned on soil tests, previous crops, manure/residual contribution, expected crop economics, weather, access and risk. A change in crop area should not mechanically receive the same percentage change in each purchased nutrient.

A stylized profit expression makes the missing link clear:

`expected crop receipts at application rate n - nutrient cost(n) - other relevant costs`

If a smooth response and interior solution were actually justified, the marginal condition would compare expected additional crop revenue with the marginal nutrient cost. That is economic reasoning, not a field recommendation or an estimated yield response curve. Constraints, uncertainty and application practicality may make the simple condition unsuitable.

Iowa State's March 2026 discussion distinguishes P/K response according to soil-test categories and describes when maintenance applications can be deferred, alongside longer-run consequences of nutrient removal. Its findings are specific to the studied crops, soils and methods; they are not a universal country-level elasticity coefficient. [F10] The proposed model should retain that heterogeneity instead of assuming all omitted applications destroy an equal amount of yield or must all be purchased next quarter.

### Hypothetical area/rate example

Assume area rises 5% while the average application rate for the relevant nutrient falls 8%. Then:

`1.05 * 0.92 - 1 = -3.4%`

The numerator is nutrient application under those assumptions, not necessarily current producer sales. This is a deliberately simple scenario illustrating why acreage growth alone cannot settle demand. It makes no claim about a particular 2026 crop or fertilizer rate.

### Soil nutrient availability is not a warehouse balance

For P/K, the research should distinguish nutrient removal, available soil tests and long-run replacement requirements. It must not convert a one-year crop removal estimate into a precise immediately purchasable deficit. Soil measurements are diagnostic and method-dependent; they do not measure an interchangeable bag of inventory. [F10/F12]

Nitrogen deserves its own state. Crop timing, soil contribution and loss risk need evidence rather than a blanket rainfall multiplier. Iowa State's 2026 nitrogen work reinforces the value of location-specific testing; it does not establish a national reduction in fertilizer demand. [F11] A model may use a scenario when local data are absent, but cannot hide the absence by borrowing another region's coefficient.

### Affordability and credit are related but different

I propose three separate economic views: marginal crop benefit relative to input cost; cash budget at the time of purchase; and ability to obtain appropriate financing. A favorable expected return can coexist with a financing constraint. Conversely, having credit does not make an input application economically desirable.

USDA ERS's September 3 forecast expects 2026 fertilizer/lime/soil-conditioner expenditure to rise 15.3%, while pesticide expenditure falls 6.6%. These are annual U.S. spending projections, not physical-volume observations or a description of every grower's balance sheet. [F09]

The system should not use total farm income as a sufficient proxy for a specific crop's purchasing power. Separate crop receipts, sold quantities, input costs, existing commitments and timing. Financial support, where relevant, must use the appropriate cash/eligibility evidence and current governing sources, not be inferred from a political headline.

## 5. Seven worked company interpretations

### 5.1 CF — distinguish operating reliability, reported cash and ownership

The source supplies a useful TTM reconciliation:

`2,977 CFO - 1,067 capital expenditure - 376 distributions to noncontrolling interests + 289 contributions from noncontrolling interests = 1,823 free cash flow`, in USD millions. [F01]

A simple CFO-minus-capex result is 1,910, a different measure. Neither is a universal normalized earnings measure. The ownership-sensitive cash definition is not a reason to remove financing flows arbitrarily; it is a reason to state what the metric answers.

CF's H1 capital components sum to 494. An illustrative application of its 40% Blue Point ownership to the disclosed JV construction component gives a different allocation of 408.2. This is not reconstructed actual parent funding: contributions, restricted cash and payment timing still matter. Reported utilization also excludes the unavailable facility, so the denominator must remain attached. [F01]

**Original design consequence:** the company panel should show whether a better utilization figure is physical improvement, a denominator change or a plant selection. The capital view should distinguish gross consolidated construction from parent economic funding. An analyst can then investigate whether a genuine cost advantage survives required reinvestment without inventing a comparable global cash-cost quartile.

The source's adjusted earnings include an approximately 170m settlement. The optional 2,005m residual removes that single approximate item from H1 adjusted EBITDA; it is not a comprehensive recurring estimate or a causal counterfactual. [F01] The research casebook should label it accordingly rather than overwrite management's definition.

### 5.2 Nutrien — the same issuer can have different nutrient cycles

For its reported phosphate product basis, the cost increase is 166 per tonne and the price increase is 67, producing a 99 decline in gross margin per tonne. The comparison is useful because the price/cost boundary is stated. It must not be applied to all phosphate products or all producers. [F02]

The whole issuer remains a portfolio of businesses. Segment sales require a 478m elimination to reach 10,812m consolidated sales. A dollar of upstream sales into its own distribution channel is not a second dollar of external demand. Its 55-per-tonne controllable potash cost excludes specific items and uses production, not sales, as denominator. [F02]

**Original design consequence:** display nutrient and business-role reads alongside one issuer, rather than force an averaged fertilizer label. A potash advantage should not erase phosphate pressure; a retail profit improvement should not be mistaken for a production-price advantage.

For cash seasonality, Q2 CFO less reported capital is 1,993m while the H1 equivalent is 817m. Subtracting Q2 CFO from H1 implies negative 851m Q1 CFO. These are simple period-bound calculations, not annual run-rate forecasts. [F02] The proposed cash panel should default to seasonal comparisons and disclose working-capital timing before extrapolating.

### 5.3 Mosaic — contracts, consumption and earnings run on different clocks

The important observation is not simply a higher DAP price. Mosaic's release combines higher DAP realization with weaker total phosphate margins and a stated delay from new sulfur contracts to their main earnings effect. DAP price is a selected product measure; phosphate segment margin is broader, so their differences are not an exact single-product cost decomposition. [F03]

The Q2 cash reconciliation is direct:

`167.4 operating cash - 320.3 capital expenditure = -152.9 free cash flow`, USD millions. Segment adjusted EBITDA sums to 407m. [F03]

**Original design consequence:** create a read-only explanation of input cost exposure by vintage: contract date, delivery/receipt, inventory, consumption and COGS. This consumes existing accounting/evidence owners; it is not a new event lifecycle. An announced decline in a benchmark cannot immediately become improved reported margin when expensive input inventory remains.

The next research question is whether the future realized product/input spread improves enough, at viable output and demand, to restore cash generation. It is not enough to see a production cut or assume the resulting supply reduction guarantees producer gains. Required counterevidence includes persistent input pressure, output volumes below economic scale, weak customer orders and a price rise that does not catch up with consumed costs.

### 5.4 Yara — strong margins and cash absorption can coexist

The original Q2 cash statement reconciles to 682m operating cash. Its displayed working-capital block is negative 43m, and 255m cash PP&E spending leaves a 427m limited-perimeter residual. CFO plus net investing is 582m, which is not an interchangeable recurring-free-cash number because investing includes other receipts. The investing components differ from the displayed total by 1m. [F04]

**Original design consequence:** a cash-conversion explanation should distinguish receipts for sold fertilizer, inventory preparation, payments for feedstocks, project capital and other asset receipts. When earnings improve before the sales season, the working-capital need may rise; whether it later reverses requires subsequent evidence.

Deferred procurement is a hypothesis to track through the relevant season, not an excuse to relabel every missing sale as future demand. A useful watch condition is a source-supported move from delayed ordering to customer sell-through with acceptable credit and cash collection. Failure to see that transition by the appropriate application window weakens the deferral thesis.

The announced acquisition context also remains distinct from the current operating footprint. A pro-forma claim about a more balanced input exposure must not rewrite the current company until closing and attributable operation are established. No new acquisition status is inferred in this research.

### 5.5 ICL — mixed-use businesses can raise sales without increasing economics proportionately

ICL's Q2 Phosphate Solutions sales increase of 85m accompanies only a 2m EBITDA increase. Its broader segment mix and planned 2027 reorganization are relevant to historical comparability. The 196m difference between reported CFO and FCF is an arithmetic difference; without the complete reconciliation it is not labeled as a verified capex category. [F05]

**Original design consequence:** retain both operating business and end use. Food/industrial phosphate, agricultural phosphate and specialty formulations should not automatically inherit the same demand explanation. A nutrient theme may be relevant to the issuer while remaining financially immaterial to a particular segment.

For the later historical system, proposed organizational changes should be recorded as future classification changes with a source vintage. Earlier periods may be restated if the issuer supplies a valid bridge. Until then, do not manufacture like-for-like growth or silently move old revenue into newly named businesses.

This also provides an entry point to R6's specialty-chemicals work. A successful pass-through price increase may defend a margin rather than expand it. To identify a genuine rerating mechanism, the analyst needs evidence of retained product mix, volume, efficiency, customer retention or return on invested capital, not only a higher sales line.

### 5.6 FMC — commercial innovation, licensing and recurring operations are different

Removing only the identified 200m licensing receipt leaves 163m from rounded Q2 operating cash and 157m from rounded FCF. Applying the same isolated subtraction to the 75–225m annual guided FCF range produces negative 125m to positive 25m. These are our one-factor views, not alternative company guidance or a forecast of operations without the agreement. [F06]

**Original design consequence:** a licensing receipt can legitimately improve liquidity while changing future economics. The user should see both. The capital-rights analysis from R3 therefore applies to agricultural technology as well as mining: cash received now does not determine the retained royalty, commercialization or future product value without contract evidence.

For crop-protection research, distinguish a new active ingredient, a formulation extension, geographic launch, registration milestone, partner order and grower repeat purchase. A company can sell more differentiated products while an older product faces a difficult commercial transition. The dossier needs enough decomposition to explain the net effect without promoting a marketing claim into a durable moat.

A postponed distributor order is not proof of unchanged farm application. The model should monitor whether orders subsequently arrive and whether the financing/receivable terms remain acceptable. For now, recurring performance and cash actions remain separate observations.

### 5.7 K+S — improving guidance does not settle the expectations question

The original report's cover identifies the cited consensus vintage: July 16. The new annual EBITDA midpoint of 720m is only 2m above its cited 718m estimate despite being 40m above the prior company midpoint. This does not establish the latest market information set at the August release or a forecast of the stock's response. [F07b]

The report also reconciles H1 FCF of negative 132.9m to adjusted FCF of positive 101.2m through 234.1m of financial-investment adjustments. The primary cash statement supports a selected non-securities investment bridge to the same 101.2m. However, statement cash purchases of PP&E plus intangibles exceed the narrative capital figure by 19.6m; that difference remains unresolved, not silently repaired. [F07b]

**Original design consequence:** score neither the sign of headline FCF nor the word 'raised' in isolation. Use the specified definition and vintage. A cash investment in securities is different from an operating loss; an expected earnings improvement is different from an unanticipated revision.

Its Industry+ exposure further cautions against assuming all consolidated improvement is agricultural demand. The case motivates a source-supported look-through to business roles and investment obligations rather than simply assigning all revenue to a potash basket.

## 6. Original scenarios for nutrient and company economics

All prices, coefficients and balances in this section are explicit hypothetical assumptions unless labeled otherwise. They are not current market quotations, production recipes, actual issuer forecasts or fertilizer application recommendations. Exact calculations are in the accompanying arithmetic file.

### H-A: compare nutrient cost without declaring agronomic substitutes

At assumed metric-tonne prices of 600 for urea, 400 for UAN32 and 900 for ammonia, source-convention grades produce costs of approximately 1.3043, 1.2500 and 1.0976 dollars per kg N. The arithmetic standardizes nutrient content only. Application logistics, timing, losses, equipment, safety and crop suitability are outside that comparison.

For a trading/research product, the legitimate use is to investigate whether a documented commercial product switch changes the supplier's realizations or volumes. The comparison must not become advice that a grower should buy the lowest ratio. Delivered cost and usable value require their own source-specific model.

### H-B: the second nutrient can reverse a price comparison

Assume DAP18-46 costs 800 per metric tonne and MAP11-52 costs 850. If the nitrogen genuinely replaces otherwise required nitrogen worth 1.20/kg, residual cost per kg P2O5 is:

- DAP: `(800 - 180*1.20)/460 = 1.2696`.
- MAP: `(850 - 110*1.20)/520 = 1.3808`.

Without an eligible nitrogen credit, the respective ratios are 1.7391 and 1.6346. The mathematical ordering reverses. In this synthetic example the crossover nitrogen value is approximately 0.5814/kg.

The original design lesson is conditional economic value: a nutrient credit is only meaningful when its amount, availability, timing and displaced purchase are valid for the scenario. Do not give the entire product's nitrogen a universal positive credit. Nor should the nitrogen credit disappear in accounting simply because the product is displayed under a phosphate theme.

### H-C: higher phosphate prices can coexist with a lower conversion contribution

Assume a finished product price of 800, input costs represented by 0.4 long tons of sulfur at 500 and 0.22 metric tonnes of ammonia at 600, plus 250 of other cost. The contribution is 218 per finished tonne.

Increasing sulfur to 700 and ammonia to 800 reduces contribution to 94. Increasing the finished price to 900 restores it only to 194, still below the initial 218. Coefficients are financial scenario assumptions, not claimed Mosaic or other plant yields.

The useful question becomes how quickly sales realization can adjust relative to contracted and consumed inputs. A correct calculation must also identify freight, product mix, credits and overhead exclusions. It cannot be turned into a full company EBITDA forecast simply by multiplying by a capacity figure.

### H-D: channel replenishment is not additional field application

With 100 product tonnes of beginning distributor inventory, 120 received and 90 sold, ending inventory is 130, assuming no other movements. Producer shipments exceed distributor sell-through by 30. Those extra tonnes cannot also be counted as farm application.

A complete observation would distinguish dealer inventory, farmer-owned/prepaid inventory, transit and other ownership categories. The quantity bridge requires ownership rules so one shipment moving from a port to a warehouse does not appear as new supply in both locations.

The economic implication is conditional. Replenishment may precede genuine demand, or may represent inventory accumulation under weak demand. The next observation is subsequent sell-through and cash collection at the relevant season, not another repetition of the shipment statistic.

### H-E: currency has more than one side

Assume local-currency crop receipts of 15,000, imported nutrient costs of 10,000 and other fixed local costs of 4,000. The initial partial contribution is 1,000. If currency changes lift both the USD-linked receipts and imported costs by 10%, the partial contribution is 1,500. If receipts remain fixed while inputs reprice, it is zero.

Neither outcome is a Brazil forecast or a full farm-income estimate. It demonstrates why currency depreciation is not a universal fertilizer-demand coefficient. Crop sales, existing purchases, financing and local costs can have different currency and timing exposure.

### H-F: collection timing can dominate near-term cash

At assumed credit sales of 10m per day, thirty additional collection days tie up 300m, before changes in payables, inventory, deposits or financing. The calculation is a sensitivity, not evidence of a bad debt or a company forecast.

A stronger revenue line accompanied by longer collection may therefore need a different research interpretation from cash sales at unchanged terms. Separate collection delay, default risk and seasonal credit expansion. Do not label every receivable increase deterioration, or every eventual payment a new sale.

## 7. Four clocks for the existing dossier

The proposal organizes existing owner observations into four time views. It does not introduce another timestamp service, scheduler, lifecycle or event store.

**Agronomic clock:** the crop, relevant growth stage, soil/test context and feasible application window. The comparable horizon may be a crop or fertilizer year rather than a calendar quarter.

**Procurement clock:** prebuy, contract/pricing date, shipment, physical arrival, ownership, distributor inventory and farmer purchase. A contract can govern later output without producing current recognized revenue.

**Accounting clock:** inventory cost recognition, COGS, revenue, receivables, tax, capital and cash settlement. Replacement margin and reported accounting margin answer different questions.

**Information/valuation clock:** first public availability, subsequent correction, issuer guidance vintage, relevant consensus vintage and market observation. Previously disclosed news is not a fresh event merely because another report repeats it.

A natural user question is: 'Input prices have fallen; why have profits not recovered?' The answer may be an old-cost inventory layer, a pre-priced sales book, weak utilization, customer deferral or a genuinely incorrect thesis. The dossier should show which explanation is supported and which is merely possible.

It must also explain apparent cross-page conflicts without averaging them. A current price observation, an older reported margin and a forecast application recovery can all be valid at their respective horizons. The page should render a timeframe split or missing evidence rather than assign a synthetic consensus probability.

## 9. Proposed early-rerating mechanisms and their counterarguments

These hypotheses identify research priorities, not trades or newly admitted scores. Each must preserve the prior information set and be evaluated against simple baselines.

| Hypothesis | Potential early observation | Discriminating confirmation | Strong counterargument |
|---|---|---|---|
| Producer margin repairs before headline earnings | Newly contracted input/output spread improves | Subsequent same-basis realization, cost and cash | Expensive inventory, fixed-price sales or lower utilization delays/erases the gain |
| Delayed application returns as purchasing | Better affordability and feasible seasonal window | Dealer sell-through, application evidence and receipts | Credit constraint or lost window turns deferral into reduced use |
| Replenishment replaces destocking | Channel stock reaches a supported operating need | Orders followed by farmer demand, not inventory buildup | Producer shipments are merely moving inventory downstream |
| Operating reliability unlocks profitable output | Independently evidenced facility availability | Saleable production and volumes at retained margin | Excluded capacity or deferred maintenance flatters reported utilization |
| Product mix earns a durable premium | Repeat adoption of differentiated products | Like-for-like gross profit and retention | Cost pass-through, acquisitions or price inflation explains revenue |
| Regional netback improves | Freight, routing or customer mix changes | Comparable delivered realization less all relevant costs | New costs, longer transit or credit terms absorb apparent arbitrage |
| Financial risk falls without destroying retained economics | Funding/asset/license action with clear rights | Cash received, obligations reduced and future economics retained | Upfront cash surrenders excessive future returns or is not yet closed |
| A genuine expectation change is under-recognized | New evidence differs from a valid prior forecast | Matched-vintage revision and later economic result | Improvement was already guided or consensus already anticipated it |

The first useful investor action is often to investigate or monitor a company whose economic mechanism is changing. Entry eligibility and portfolio action remain with existing decision owners. An LLM-generated explanation cannot supply a missing validated forecasting model or originate or size trades.

## 10. Valuation: match the duration and owner of cash

For resource and commodity producers, the proposed analysis should distinguish temporary favorable product/input spreads from sustained advantages. A low multiple on peak earnings is not automatically cheap. A higher valuation based on recovered mid-cycle cash requires explicit production, costs, tax, capital and financing assumptions.

For distributors, normalize by season and explain cash requirements rather than capitalizing one strong seasonal quarter. A higher gross-margin dollar on expensive inventory can coexist with heavier credit financing and future mark-down risk. Conversely, a temporary working-capital outflow need not destroy business value if collection and unit economics are sound.

For differentiated crop inputs, assess the cash rights and commercial duration associated with intellectual property, registration, distribution and customer evidence. A patent transition, licensing agreement or new product may change both receipts and future margin ownership. Do not substitute R&D expense, launch counts or a large addressable market for commercial proof.

For new projects, preserve the financing method, remaining capital, ownership and end-use commitments from R3/R4. A project described as ammonia capacity may serve a different end use from the fertilizer cohort. Planned availability and economically viable supply are different. A model should not award both a full growth benefit and a cash saving from the same cancelled expenditure.

## 12. Evaluation design that can reject unnecessary complexity

The descriptive evaluation should begin with falsifiable tasks, not a backtest of an invented fertilizer score. Can the system identify the direction of a comparable realized margin? Can it explain why a price change did not reach cash? Does it preserve the issuer's business mix and time horizon? Does it abstain on the mismatched AMS body rather than produce a confident current quote?

For forecasting research, compare a role-aware model with simple sector, product-price and reported-volume baselines. Define the target before fitting: next reported segment margin, realized price, operating cash, or another accepted outcome. They are not interchangeable targets. The evaluation must record whether better accounting explanation improves any investor-facing decision.

Use expanding information sets with genuine publication/observation cutoffs. Retain revisions, delayed filing availability, regional seasons, units, restructuring and delisted securities. The model may know an August cost forecast in August; it may not retroactively know the October invoice. If consensus is unavailable, evaluate relative to the explicit company guidance or a declared model baseline, not an invented consensus.

Hold out issuers, input-price regimes and regions where possible. An input shock shared by every firm is not many independent confirmations. Management attribution is a hypothesis feature, not a causal label that proves the model. Test whether the source-specific mechanism still works when volumes, inventories or financing move adversely.

For user evaluation, ask whether the dossier reduces mistaken conclusions and changes useful research behavior. Suitable measures include correctly distinguishing product-price strength from company economics; locating source scope; interpreting an unavailable field; and choosing the next evidence to watch. Avoid rewarding more cards, more citations or more aggressive language.

Only a later accepted prospective shadow program can support candidate-ranking changes. Preserve fees, liquidity, timing and policy restrictions in any investment-outcome evaluation. No historical explanatory fit establishes trade authority.
