## 1. Outcome and the central research decision

R1 established economic roles rather than a universal Materials score. R2 studied industrial-metal cash and ownership; R3 financial rights and funding; R4 commercially usable output; R5 nutrient demand, distribution and timing. R6 now asks a different question: **when an improvement occurs, what evidence suggests that a business can retain it and reinvest at attractive incremental returns?**

The selected research direction is to distinguish four possible sources of improving reported economics: a temporary market spread; contractually reimbursed costs; a defensible customer benefit that the supplier can partly capture; and the productive deployment of additional capital. These can coexist inside one company. None should be inferred merely from the words commodity, specialty, critical, advanced or recurring.

The desired user capability remains practical: discover a material economic change, identify the relevant company or business role, understand why cash and value could improve, inspect the evidence and know what would change the interpretation. The machine should preserve product, customer, financial and time boundaries while producing an intelligible explanation. It must not convert an association or persuasive narrative into a trade instruction.

The principal recommendation is therefore **business-role-specific earnings durability, followed by an explicit capital and per-share bridge**. A supply bottleneck is one possible input, not the organizing answer to every industry. A high current margin is evidence about current economics; it is not by itself evidence that the margin will persist or that growth will create value.

## 3. Eight source-grounded discriminators

These are dated report cases, not declarations that every item is the latest market state. Issuer causal explanations remain attributed to management. Related releases and filings are not independent confirmations. Source locations are in section 15; arithmetic IDs refer to the accompanying verification file.

### 3.1 Dow — a company-wide rebound does not prove every business improved

Dow's Q2 release reports company-wide local pricing up 20% while volume fell 1%. Performance Materials and Coatings sales rose from $2,129m to $2,361m, but operating EBIT fell from $152m to $133m. Its reported Q2 FCF reconciles as $1,324m operating cash less $632m capital expenditure = $692m. The release also identifies suspended recognition of Sadara equity losses as an earnings factor elsewhere. [C01; DOW01–04]

**Research consequence:** decompose the economic change by business, and separately identify changes in accounting recognition. A rebound in commodity pricing does not establish better physical demand in every segment, while a specialty label does not establish margin resilience. A later candidate needs evidence of comparable realization, costs, utilization and continuing customer demand. A suspended loss is not automatically a recovered asset or newly available parent cash. R6 does not quantify the Sadara counterfactual or audit that accounting policy.

### 3.2 LyondellBasell — portfolio restructuring can improve the future while consuming cash now

The Q2 release supplies CFO of $752m and capex of $270m: a $482m limited-perimeter residual. The H1 primary statement gives $483m CFO and $539m capex, plus a separately classified $310m cash contribution **to** disposed businesses. The H1 CFO-minus-capex residual is therefore -$56m; including that additional payment gives -$366m. The investing statement reconciles -539 -310 +55 = -794. [C02–C03; LYB01–05]

**Research consequence:** disposing of a difficult operation is not necessarily an immediate cash inflow. Evaluate restructuring as a dated investment decision: current cash obligations, liabilities retained, business perimeter removed, future cost exposure and transition arrangements. The original cash-flow statement resolves the sign and category; the $310m is not removed from CFO again. Neither residual is a no-restructuring forecast or a complete estimate of sustainable FCF. Reduced current investment also requires distinguishing genuine efficiency from deferred maintenance and forgone growth.

### 3.3 Olin — joint-product economics and asset reliability can pull in opposite directions

Olin's Q2 chlor-alkali/vinyls segment earnings bridge is $64.9m prior earnings, minus an attributed $40.1m operating-issue impact, plus $28.6m of remaining improvement, yielding $53.4m. Epoxy earnings improved by $39.7m. Its revenue reconciliation also includes a non-chemical segment, which must not be counted as chemical demand. [C04; OLN01–03]

**Research consequence:** separate the joint-product price/cost opportunity from the asset's ability to realize it. An outage addback can describe management's explanation without becoming a guaranteed run rate or an extra future sale. Lost output, lost profit, repair expense and subsequent inventory sales must not be counted multiple times. The product should ask whether the repair was completed and whether comparable production, realization and cash confirm the recovery.

### 3.4 Linde — pass-through margins and adjusted capital returns require different interpretations

In Q2 EMEA, Linde reports margin down 40 basis points, but up 10 excluding cost pass-through. Company-wide Q2 FCF is $833m versus $954m a year earlier: CFO rose $60m while capex rose $181m. The appendix separately reports 12.2% after-tax ROC and 23.5% adjusted ROC; the adjusted capital calculation excludes specified Linde AG goodwill and indefinite-lived intangibles. [C05; LIN01–07]

The 2025 filing defines sale-of-gas backlog as estimated capital cost of large plants under construction. It distinguishes on-site minimum-purchase arrangements from merchant and packaged contracts that generally lack such commitments. [C06]

**Research consequence:** price reimbursement, volume commitments, project capital and investment returns are separate questions. Do not call reimbursed energy inflation superior pricing power, classify all contracts as protected volume, or compare an adjusted return with another issuer's differently defined ratio. Maintain both reported and adjusted views with their purposes visible; do not automatically reject or privilege either.

### 3.5 Air Liquide — operating progress, factoring and investment cash can diverge

Air Liquide's H1 cash statement reconciles 3,371.9 -1,393.7 -23.9 = €1,954.3m net operating cash. Management attributes €1,042m of the receivables increase to ending factoring programs. Subtracting €1,828.4m industrial capex leaves €125.9m, before separately financing-classified net interest and lease payments. Including those selected obligations gives -€119.7m, still not every cash use. Its €6.0bn investment backlog is distinct from investment decisions and opportunities; planned project start-ups can reduce that stock. [C07; AIR01–06]

**Research consequence:** a cash conversion change may reflect the timing of monetizing receivables, not just sales quality. Adding back one reported factoring effect is an isolated calculation, not normalized or recurring CFO. Likewise, falling backlog may reflect successful conversion rather than weaker demand. Future explanations need source-defined startups, new decisions, cancellations, perimeter changes and remaining investment—not a generic backlog-growth signal.

### 3.6 Sherwin-Williams — an announced price increase is not an achieved revenue increase

The Q2 release reports Paint Stores same-store sales growth of 4.2%; its segment profit rose $41.1m while reported margin slipped from 24.8% to 24.6%. It announced an 8% list-price increase effective September 1. Q2 FCF reconciles to $1,239.1m. Comparing the July annual adjusted-EPS range with the April range gives a $0.30 midpoint increase, not a consensus-surprise calculation. [C08–C09; SHW01–06]

**Research consequence:** same-store sales are not pure physical volume. A list-price action needs customer/product coverage, timing, discounts, retention and actual realization. A valuable coatings business might improve through application performance, service or channels, but the reporting alone does not prove each mechanism. Later evidence should separate comparable price from mix, acquisitions, currency and the cost of supporting growth. A July announcement is not proof that September implementation succeeded.

### 3.7 Eastman — the strongest short-term improvement may be the least durable component

Eastman's tables reconcile adjusted EBIT of $320m versus $275m. Chemical Intermediates improves by $88m, while Advanced Materials declines $12m, Additives/Functional Products $2m and Fibers $45m; Other improves $16m. Management attributes part of the intermediates strength to disrupted supply and expects some normalization. Reported circular-revenue growth lacks the base and standalone profitability needed for a complete investment claim. Q2 CFO less PP&E is $124m; H1 is -$116m. [C10–C11; EMN01–05]

**Research consequence:** treat a temporary spread benefit differently from durable customer adoption, without denying its real near-term cash potential. Demand, margin durability and entry horizon may differ. New-product revenue growth needs retained margin, invested capital and repeat purchasing before it can establish an economically successful ramp. A small undisclosed base must not acquire significance merely because its percentage growth is large.

### 3.8 Entegris — qualification supports a hypothesis, not guaranteed pricing power

The 2025 filing describes predominantly consumed/unit-driven revenue and customer-specific qualification; it also reports 50% of sales to the top ten customers and warns of their bargaining power. Q2 2026 sales reconcile 371.3 +514.6 -2.7 = $883.2m. CFO less gross PP&E is $116.9m, or $120.3m when the separately received incentive is included. Cash nevertheless fell $89.1m after investing and financing. [C12–C13; ENT01–05]

**Research consequence:** a qualification barrier may help preserve business while also making customer replacement difficult. Test both sides. Separate wafer activity, material content per process, share of qualified supply and equipment-related demand. A technical association with advanced chips does not reveal revenue materiality or a specific customer relationship. The company remains under existing classification ownership; relevance to Materials cannot silently change its accepted sector identity.

## 5. Joint production and utilization: hypothetical mechanisms H1–H3

The examples in sections 5–7 use explicitly assumed inputs. They are not issuer forecasts, chemical process specifications, a global cost curve or security valuations. They test whether the proposed explanation retains its economic boundary.

### H1 — exact reimbursement can lower margin percentage without reducing contribution

Assume revenue 100, cash cost 65 and contribution 35. A reimbursed input rises by 20, with matching revenue collected in the same period. Revenue becomes 120, cost 85 and contribution stays 35. Margin falls from 35% to 29.17%, a 5.83-percentage-point decline.

This arithmetic does not prove any particular contract has complete reimbursement. Real questions include eligible costs, index basis, caps, timing, credit exposure, minimum charges and unbilled consumption. A lag can create a cash-funding requirement even when the eventual reimbursement preserves the contribution. Fixed fees and volumetric earnings may also respond differently.

**Proposed interpretation:** separately report absolute retained profit, reimbursement, percentage margin and working capital. Do not call every denominator-driven margin decline deterioration, and do not call every pass-through sale a pricing win. The same rule works in reverse: falling reimbursed input cost can improve percentage margin without an equivalent increase in underlying profit.

### H2 — price direction of one co-product can mislead

Assume a modeled joint-output batch produces one unit of A and 1.1 units of B, with all units usable and sold, and total relevant variable cost 360. This coefficient is an assumption for the scenario, not a verified chemical yield or plant constant.

At prices A=100 and B=300, contribution is 70. At A=70 and B=350, it is 95. One headline price falls while the combined contribution improves. If A instead has an adverse delivered netback of -20, contribution falls to 5 despite the stronger B price.

The proposed company model must therefore preserve joint output and the disposition of unwanted material. It cannot maximize each co-product independently when the process does not permit independent production. A captive downstream plant introduces an alternative-use question: which external opportunity cost is relevant, and are internal transfer prices being counted as another group profit?

The important data requirement is source-specific: sales proceeds, usable output ratio, relevant variable cost and constrained outlets at an explicit boundary. Where these inputs are missing, keep a qualitative conditional explanation rather than calculate a spurious exact joint margin. R6 admits no commercial chlor-alkali ECU price series or plant-level process balance.

### H3 — utilization only has meaning with its capacity and cost assumptions

Assume 100,000 units of available annual capacity, contribution of $300 per sold unit and fixed cash cost of $18m. At 80,000 units, the operating contribution after fixed cost is $6m; at 70,000 it is $3m. The model's fixed-cost break-even is 60% utilization.

This is not an industry threshold. Saleable yield, maintenance, input availability, customer acceptance, freight, tax, capital spending and working capital remain outside the simple example. A plant may also be unable to sell every technically producible unit.

The proposed analysis should distinguish an improving physical output ratio from a changing denominator. The Federal Reserve defines capacity around sustainable output under realistic operating conditions; company utilization measures may use their own perimeter. Neither automatically represents customer-qualified material capacity. [C16]

This distinction should support investigation of an opportunity, not create a universal rule that a particular utilization level predicts a stock return.

## 6. Pricing power and customer value: hypothetical mechanisms H4–H5

### H4 — list price, realized price and value capture

Assume an initial price of $200 and variable cost of $120. A company announces an 8% increase, but it applies to only half the relevant volume and 90% of the announced amount is realized. With unchanged mix and volume, average price becomes $207.20, not $216. If variable cost rises to $126, contribution becomes $81.20 versus $80 initially.

Applying the full list increase to all volume instead creates an unsupported $90 contribution. The correct conclusion is not that list-price announcements have no information; it is that the magnitude and timing of realization need evidence.

The proposed explanation should ask whether higher price reflects a genuinely better product, preserved supply during a temporary disruption, a changed mix, contractual cost recovery or a general price increase. Each can improve receipts, but has different implications for durability and competitive response. Support costs, lost customers, smaller order sizes and longer credit terms can offset an apparent price win.

For a future measurement, use comparable cohorts where possible. Preserve customer/product substitutions rather than pretending unchanged names imply unchanged scope. A consolidated price/mix bridge cannot establish product-level pricing power without additional inputs.

### H5 — supplier economics can be small relative to customer value, but must still be demonstrated

Assume a customer attempts 100,000 units and a material change raises good output from 90% to 92%, with no change in attempted units. Additional good output is 2,000 units. At an assumed $50 contribution per additional good unit, the gross benefit is $100,000.

If the material premium is $30,000 and validation/transition costs $20,000, the customer's modeled net value is $50,000. If the supplier incurs $10,000 of incremental support/production cost, its incremental contribution is $20,000 before other obligations. These values are not Entegris, a coatings customer or any observed factory result.

The proposed research should ask who captures the benefit, under what contract and against which alternative. A technically better input does not guarantee that the supplier receives a higher price. A powerful customer may bargain away much of the benefit; the supplier may have to finance qualification, offer guarantees or share the improvement with competitors. Qualification can also make the supplier dependent on a small set of customers.

**Evidence needed for a durable thesis:** application-specific performance; actual cost of the relevant failure or inefficiency; control for other process changes; accepted product use; repeat purchases; net realized price after support; customer concentration; and plausible substitution. A catalog capability, patent, management statement or laboratory result alone does not establish these properties.

Customer-specific information may be confidential or absent. That should limit the claim. The product can still say what is documented and what needs verification without fabricating a secret customer relationship or revenue percentage.

## 7. Reinvestment, backlog and valuation: hypothetical mechanisms H6–H8

### H6 — identical growth can create different value depending on new-capital returns

Assume next-period after-tax operating profit of 100, perpetual growth of 4%, a 10% required operating return and a steady-state identity in which net reinvestment equals growth divided by the return on new invested capital, multiplied by operating profit. The resulting schematic model is:

`FCFF_1 = NOPAT_1 × (1 − g/RONIC)`

`Operating EV = FCFF_1 / (r − g)`

At a 12% return on new capital, the modeled enterprise value is 1,111.11, or 11.11 times next-period operating profit. At 20%, it is 1,333.33, or 13.33 times. At a return equal to the 10% required return, the value is 1,000; at 8%, it falls to 833.33 under the assumed growth plan.

These are not estimated company multiples or recommended price targets. They illustrate why growth alone is insufficient. The model assumes positive stable cash flows, r greater than g, a consistent net-reinvestment definition, constant returns and a perpetual regime. It omits finite patent life, price cycles, changing capital needs, shutdown obligations and financial claims. Actual valuation requires explicit finite scenarios and a consistent shareholder bridge.

The key research question is the return on **additional** capital, not just a company's historical adjusted accounting ratio. Separate maintenance, replacement, expansion, purchased businesses, working capital and internally expensed development where possible. Do not add back all R&D as though it had no economic cost, or capitalize every expense without a defined life and method. A gross-capex division by a one-quarter profit change does not identify marginal project returns.

For specialty materials, a premium multiple hypothesis should be tied to demonstrable cash durability, retained customer value and reinvestment opportunity. For cyclical chemicals, model a spread path rather than extrapolate a temporarily elevated quarter. These are hypotheses that may be rejected by later evidence.

### H7 — a smaller capital-project backlog can indicate productive conversion

Assume a source-defined investment backlog starts at 6,000, adds 1,000 of new qualifying projects, removes 1,500 through start-up and 100 through cancellation, and ends at 5,400. The decline has two very different causes. A product should identify both rather than interpret the sign alone.

This is a hypothetical capital-stock roll-forward, not a sales-order formula or a reconstruction of either gas company's exact backlog. Backlog, a period's investment decisions and opportunities can overlap or have incompatible scopes; they are not automatically additive. A commissioned project should lead to a separate question about actual revenue, cash, utilization and return. It does not prove full ramp or contract profitability merely by exiting the backlog.

The proposed data model should retain definition, currency, accounting perimeter, entry/exit criteria, observation date and relevant asset references. If a source publishes only the stock, show the unknown bridge. No invented cancellations or orders are needed to force equality.

### H8 — an accounting capital reduction can raise a return ratio without creating cash

Hold the numerator at 100. If the accounting capital denominator falls from 1,000 to 800, the ratio rises from 10% to 12.5%. No operating improvement has been assumed.

This does not mean every adjustment is inappropriate. It means a comparison must state the analytical question. Asset productivity excluding a historical purchase premium, total acquisition economics and shareholder returns are different questions. Use both reported and adjusted measures when helpful, but never let a label silently choose the comparison basis. Do not turn a write-down itself into evidence of successful reinvestment.

## 8. How the research should detect an early rerating candidate

The proposed detector is initially an evidence workflow, not a numerical score. Its output should be a research question with a company-specific mechanism and observable conditions.

| Candidate mechanism | Evidence worth investigating before the next earnings report | Main counterargument or falsifier |
|---|---|---|
| Realized spread inflection | Same-basis input/output contracts, inventories and price resets | Feedstock catches up, old inventory is costly, sales mix changes or buyers defer |
| Reliability restoration | Documented restart, stable usable output and delivered sales | Repeated problems, unrecovered customers, extra maintenance or adverse product economics |
| Durable price capture | Comparable realized prices, retention and customer value | Reimbursement only, temporary scarcity, lost volumes or rising service cost |
| Qualified content growth | Customer/application approval followed by repeat use | Qualification fails, architecture changes, dual sourcing or customer bargaining removes margin |
| Capital conversion | A source-defined startup followed by utilization and receipts | More capex, delayed ramp, weak offtake or unrecovered working capital |
| Productive portfolio change | Future obligations removed, proceeds/payments and retained liabilities reconciled | Earnings optics improve while economic costs or stranded liabilities persist |
| Comparable operating recovery | Volume and margin improve after removing scope, FX and reporting changes | Restocking, acquired sales or denominator revisions explain the apparent turn |
| Better retained capital allocation | Returns on new investment and per-share cash rights supported | Growth below cost of capital, dilution, expensive acquisitions or undermaintenance |

A plausible mechanism is not an unexpected mechanism. Each candidate must also carry the source publication time, event/economic period, first-observed time when known, and a clearly identified expectation baseline. Prior company guidance, a dated analyst consensus and an internal scenario must not be merged.

The initial product should admit a descriptive statement such as: 'The next reported margin could improve if the documented input reset reaches consumed costs without a corresponding output-price decline.' It should show why that possibility matters and what would disprove it. It must not automatically turn the condition into a probability, stock rank or entry signal.

Research horizons should follow the actual mechanism: a price reset, a maintenance return, a customer qualification, a plant ramp or a multi-year reinvestment decision. Do not force every observation into one generic emerging/accelerating/mature label. A near-term windfall can coexist with poor long-term economics, and durable business value can coexist with a poor current share-price entry.

## 9. Measurement, public sources and vintage safety

A useful first data expansion would combine existing authorized price sources, issuer product/segment disclosures and public manufacturing indicators. Do not approve a new paid provider or create a chemical data warehouse merely because a desired series is missing.

Federal Reserve capacity data is a candidate for broad utilization context, not proof of a specific product's shortage. Census M3 inventories are value-based accounting observations with definitions for materials, work in process and finished goods. Its new-order/unfilled-order identities belong only to eligible series; they must not be copied onto industrial-gas capital backlogs. [C16–C17]

Euro Chlor supplies a concrete revision case. Its May 2025 release reports March 2025 output of 655,288 tonnes and utilization of 65.3%. Its February 2026 release retains that output but gives 67.0% utilization for the same month. This 1.7-percentage-point change is a different reported vintage, not another month of physical improvement. R6 does not diagnose a specific capacity change behind the revision. [C14–C15; VINT01]

A future feature should preserve both source vintages, use the information available at the relevant historical cutoff, and expose revisions as revisions. Output divided by an undocumented or revised denominator should not become a new independent confirmation. Where a stable historical denominator cannot be established, keep the observation descriptive.

### Proposed minimal evidence requirements

Each observation should retain its existing-owner subject identity; source-scoped business/product/customer description; region and delivery basis; raw value and unit; measured numerator and denominator when available; exclusions and accounting basis; event/reference period; actual source publication time; retrieval/first-observation time where available; revision relation; source locator and rights; and the limited claim the evidence supports.

Derived calculations additionally need the ordered input references, formula version, output unit and scope. Missing values stay unavailable rather than being filled by a model. A source percentage or average does not establish its full distribution. Related report tables and an issuer press release remain one evidence family rather than independent confirmation.

No new identity, correction or freshness service is proposed. This is the content that existing owner contracts must preserve or explicitly mark unavailable. Full company asset mapping, benchmark entitlements and forward contract series remain incomplete. Public readability is not permission to redistribute raw commercial data.

## 12. Evaluation: make added complexity earn its place

The primary research hypothesis is that economic-role and definition-aware explanations reduce consequential false interpretations relative to commodity price and broad-sector summaries. That can be evaluated before claiming a return advantage.

Start with a blinded semantic task set. Give the same dated evidence to a baseline and the proposed composition. Ask whether higher revenue establishes pricing power, whether a backlog decline establishes weaker demand, whether adjusted returns are comparable, whether cash movement establishes operating deterioration and whether qualification proves durable supplier margin. Score against explicit source definitions and the possibility of abstention, not persuasive writing or the number of citations.

Next, define numerical outcomes separately: subsequent segment margin, realized output price, qualified sales, cash conversion or capital-project receipts. The target, reporting window, information cutoff and correction policy should be frozen before analysis. A model that explains cash statements well has not thereby forecast share returns.

For economic prediction, compare simple baselines with progressively added information: spot output/input prices; broad sector and utilization context; company realization/contract information; product/qualification evidence; and cash/capital-rights effects. Ablate one layer at a time. Evaluate calibration, signed error, coverage and abstention. Sparse customer data may mean that a descriptive view is more defensible than a fitted score.

For equity discovery, preserve eligible securities, point-in-time membership, disclosure lag, delistings, currencies and the then-known share/ownership structure. Compare attention timing and false-positive burden as well as eventual returns. Deduplicate the same source across several themes. Cluster observations from the same event or issuer so dependent evidence cannot masquerade as a large sample.

A credible contrary result is valuable. If commodity-only measures explain outcomes just as well, or the customer-specific data is too sparse to add stable signal, simplify the model and retain only the explanation value that survives. If observed improvement comes from later-known source revisions, reject it. No arbitrary likelihood, scarcity or moat weights are proposed for deployment.

The production learning owner should retain forecasts, outcomes, misses and data failures separately. Do not infer profitable trading permission from good schema tests, a correct source reconciliation or a polished browser demonstration.
