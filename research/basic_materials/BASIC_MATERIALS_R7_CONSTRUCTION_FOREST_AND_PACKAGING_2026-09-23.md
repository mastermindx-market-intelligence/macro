## 1. Outcome and principal conclusion

This unit asks how local delivered markets, biological assets and manufacturing/conversion chains change the path from a material thesis to shareholder value. The organizing proposal is **economically usable local supply, correctly measured demand and retained cash after renewing the assets that generate it**.

The preceding units established distinctions worth preserving: R1 economic roles and measurement; R2 operating quantities, cash and ownership; R3 financial rights and financing; R4 commercially accepted output; R5 purchasing and accounting time; R6 customer value and returns on new capital. R7 extends them rather than creating a separate Materials ontology or score.

The investor job is to identify the part of a construction, forest or packaging chain whose economics are changing, understand which companies can capture the change, and distinguish genuine business improvement from mix, consolidation, valuation marks or presentation adjustments. The machine job is to preserve market geography, material/process boundaries, external versus internal sales, capital renewal, source vintage and the prior information set while composing a useful explanation.

Nine developed issuer cases are used: Vulcan, Martin Marietta, Heidelberg Materials, Weyerhaeuser, West Fraser, Suzano, Mondi, Packaging Corporation of America (PCA) and Amcor. A source-authored Anhui Conch report provides a **limited China textual supplement**, not an admitted numerical comparison: the needed report images were not recovered. Selected public statistical and academic definitions establish measurement requirements. Related releases, filings and report pages remain one source family, not independent corroboration.

These are dated cases selected to discriminate mechanisms, not a list of attractive stocks today. Management explanations are attributed observations, not independently identified causal estimates. Subsequent changes should be reconciled when a decision actually requires a current observation; this annex is not a live market feed.

### Alternatives and selected direction

A broad construction/housing dashboard would be fast to build but could misinterpret nominal expenditure as material demand and national growth as local pricing power. A universal supply-chain diagram would add detail but could double-count internal flows, miss land economics and ignore contracts. A financial-ratio dashboard would be simple to compare but could confuse cash-generation definitions, asset sales and biological marks.

The selected direction is a set of **owner-preserving economic explanations**: local delivered competition for construction materials; biological and alternative-use value for timber; process/grade and yield for mills; external margin transmission and material intensity for packaging. Shared identity, evidence, correction, publication, ThemeState, F04, Prophet and Evaluation owners remain authoritative. The extra detail must earn its place by changing an interpretation or improving a user's decision, not merely by populating another chart.

## 3. Construction materials: price is local, delivered and mix-sensitive

### 3.1 Vulcan — realized price improvement must survive operating and transport costs

Vulcan's Q2 release reports freight-adjusted aggregate price of $22.97 per shipped ton and cash cost of $10.95, versus $22.11 and $10.23 respectively a year earlier. The comparable cash gross profit per unit moves from $11.88 to $12.02: the $0.86 price increase exceeds the $0.72 cost increase by $0.14. Gross profit of $567.3 million plus specified depreciation, depletion and amortization of $152.8 million reconciles to $720.1 million of cash gross profit. The release distinguishes reported pricing from mix-adjusted pricing and attributes part of cost pressure to diesel. [B01; VUL01–07]

**Proposed interpretation.** A strong price series is not enough. The dossier should explain which costs moved with it, on the same quantity and freight boundary. Cash gross profit is not free cash flow: interest, tax, sustaining investment, acquisitions and working capital remain outside this unit measure.

For local analysis, preserve product specification, customer type, source region, delivery term and actual commercial route. A freight-adjusted selling measure does not automatically tell us who bore every transport cost or whether a price increase is durable. Conversely, higher external freight in a pass-through sale may inflate revenue and expense without changing the underlying quarry economics.

The useful research transition is from a comparable delivered-price change to retained unit profitability, then from unit profitability to company cash. A request for a global aggregate-price score would discard exactly the geography and operating detail needed to explain that transition.

### 3.2 Martin Marietta — lower reported price need not mean weaker underlying pricing

Martin Marietta reports Q2 aggregate average selling price of $22.74, down 2% on a reported basis but up 2.1% organically and 3.7% organically after mix adjustment. Total shipments rose 17%, versus 2.3% organically. Its $418 million aggregate gross profit included $52 million of acquired-inventory purchase-accounting expense. The release also attributes part of organic unit-cost inflation to external freight passed through to customers. [B02]

The single adjustment $418 million + $52 million = $470 million is useful reconciliation arithmetic, **not** organic profit, cash flow or an independently normalized result. Its H1 operating cash decrease from $605 million to $339 million also has a stated tax-payment context connected with a divestiture; it is not automatically an ongoing cash-earnings deterioration. [B02; MLM01–03]

**Proposed interpretation.** The system should preserve a price/mix/ownership bridge before treating a declining blended price as lost pricing power. Acquired operations, geographic mix, product grade, freight terms and customer mix can all change the aggregate comparison. An issuer's organic and mix-adjusted methods should remain named; their existence is not permission to invent the equivalent series for another company.

A positive thesis could be local same-market pricing that outpaces comparable costs while acquired assets are integrated. The counterargument is that the apparently stronger cohort may be selected, its actual volume may be weak, or required investment may exceed the assumed benefit. Verify the population, costs, operating performance and cash after acquisition rather than annualizing a single adjusted quarter.

### 3.3 Worked mix scenario

Assume two markets and identical total sales of 1,000 tons. Initially, 600 tons sell at $30 and 400 at $20; average price is $26. Later, prices rise 5% in both markets, but the mix shifts to 300 tons at $31.50 and 700 at $21. Average price becomes $24.15, down about 7.12%. [H01–03]

Nothing in this example proves volume or profit improved. It demonstrates that the sign of blended price growth can differ from the sign of every underlying price change. To evaluate a real company, the proposed system should display what is measured, which population is comparable, and which drivers cannot be decomposed from the available disclosure.

### 3.4 Delivered-market advantage is a route hypothesis, not a fixed-radius moat

In an assumed example, a nearby quarry quotes $22 per ton and requires 30 distance units of trucking at $0.15 per ton-distance unit: delivered cost is $26.50. A more distant quarry quotes $18 but requires 80 units of the same trucking: $30 delivered. If the latter gains a feasible route with a $2 terminal charge and $0.04 per ton-distance unit line-haul, its delivered total becomes $23.20. [H04–06]

This is not a freight quote or universal transport coefficient. It demonstrates why a map should investigate available modes, terminals, handling, reliability and product conformity rather than draw an arbitrary radius around each quarry and call everything inside protected.

The proposed analytical object is an existing-owner, source-bound **served-market view**: eligible material, available origin, feasible route, relevant customer/project, delivery cost assumptions and alternatives. Do not create a canonical transport network, infer confidential customer lanes, or claim route capacity from proximity alone.

Local scarcity matters only if the source can produce the needed specification, deliver reliably and retain the benefit. Alternative supply, rail/barge access, changing customer geography, substitution and capacity additions are material falsifiers. Geographic concentration is not itself an estimated margin or validated moat.

## 4. Cement and construction demand: count the correct stage

### 4.1 Heidelberg Materials — operating improvement does not eliminate seasonal capital needs

Heidelberg's H1 cash statement shows continuing operating cash of €50.5 million after large working-capital and provision movements. Adding discontinued operating cash of negative €14.9 million yields total operating cash of €35.6 million. Selected continuing cash less €534.3 million of property investment and €12.3 million of intangible investment leaves negative €496.1 million; adding €56.5 million of capital grants gives negative €439.6 million. These are explicitly defined residuals, not issuer-defined free cash flow. The displayed continuing-CFO bridge retains a €0.1 million difference. [B03; HEI01–04]

The same report describes a new Airvault kiln replacing older semi-dry units and expected electricity and emissions improvements. These are management's project expectations, not independently proven realized savings. [B03]

**Proposed interpretation.** A positive operating or project thesis must still pass through the cash and capital timetable. H1 cash is not an annual run rate, and capital grants cannot be counted once as a funding source and again as an unreduced benefit to enterprise value.

A plant replacement should be modeled against the old equipment removed, the available grinding and blending stages, customer-accepted output and remaining investment. Replacement is not automatically additive capacity. An efficiency claim needs comparable output, actual input consumption and stable operating evidence after commissioning.

### 4.2 Cement, clinker and supplementary materials are not interchangeable volumes

USGS describes cement manufacture and the role of clinker, gypsum and supplementary materials. The research implication is to bind balance and capacity measures to the relevant process stage. [D03]

Consider an assumed market with 100 million tonnes of cement and an 80% clinker share: 80 million tonnes of clinker are required. Cement output rises to 110 million tonnes while the share falls to 70%; clinker requirement becomes 77 million tonnes, a 3.75% decline. [H07–09]

For a separate assumed plant constrained to 8 million tonnes of clinker, the mathematical cement capacity rises from 10 million to approximately 11.43 million tonnes when the clinker share moves from 80% to 70%. This conclusion requires grinding, additions, logistics, applicable product performance and customer acceptance to be unconstrained. It is not a general process yield or an announcement of new capacity. [H10–11]

The proposed system should investigate where the actual constraint resides: kiln, grinding, eligible supplementary material, delivery, working capital or customer specification. A lower clinker factor can be important without establishing that every producer benefits or that the same mix is appropriate for every application.

### 4.3 A construction dollar is not a material tonne

Census construction-spending definitions include installed materials and other project costs, including labor and specified overhead, while excluding land acquisition. Its construction-start definition concerns the beginning of foundation work; authorizations and completions are separate. [D01–02]

The proposed demand view should map a project type and phase to a material-use hypothesis rather than multiply a national spending total by a universal coefficient. Highway work, foundations, structures, finishing and maintenance have different material mixes and purchase schedules. These are distinctions to research, not invented project bill-of-materials constants.

If nominal construction spending increases from index 100 to 110 while the relevant price index rises 10%, the deflated index remains 100. That assumed arithmetic does not prove physical construction was unchanged in any real dataset; it shows why nominal growth alone cannot establish tonnage growth. [H28]

For a usable leading indicator, identify the project, geography, financing/approval stage, physical work and purchase timing. A planned project is not an order; an order is not installed material; a start is not completion. Missing local coverage should remain explicit. National macro series may contextualize the thesis but cannot substitute for source-supported regional exposure.

### 4.4 Limited China supplement: do not force global uniformity

An Anhui Conch original-author interim report was recovered through a public filing mirror. Its text distinguishes self-produced cement/clinker from trading and describes different regional operating conditions. However, relevant report-page screenshots failed, so its detailed tables, accounting-basis comparisons and derived numerical scenario remain **unadmitted** in this unit. The mirrored posting date is not claimed to be the first time all facts became public. [B10]

The useful research consequence is a coverage requirement: self-produced and traded quantities, regional price/cost movements and the local capacity response must remain distinct. One Chinese issuer would not establish all-China conditions even after complete source recovery. R8 should close consequential geographic gaps with original issuer and statistical evidence rather than import the U.S. aggregate-pricing case into China by analogy.

## 5. Timber: biological assets, land rights and mills need separate valuations

### 5.1 Weyerhaeuser — a land sale, an earnings gain and recurring operating cash are different

Weyerhaeuser's Q2 net earnings of $162 million include a $71 million timberland-sale special item; earnings excluding it are $91 million. The Oregon transaction involved 29,000 acres and $114 million of proceeds. Proceeds are not the same number as the earnings gain. Its segment adjusted EBITDA reconciles to $310 million, only $2 million above Q1, despite a $58 million wood-products improvement and a $64 million decline in Strategic Land Solutions. [B04; WY01–03]

Operating cash of $399 million less all reported capex of $139 million leaves $260 million. The company's adjusted funds available for distribution of $323 million adds back $63 million of specified growth-facility spending. This adjustment does not mean that the $63 million was not spent. [B04; WY04–06]

**Proposed interpretation.** Separate recurring harvest and manufacturing economics from land monetization, easements or other rights, valuation changes and the cost of renewing or expanding productive assets. A valuable land transaction may improve liquidity and crystallize value; it need not be a repeatable operating margin. Conversely, growth spending may be a sound investment despite reducing current cash. Show both rather than deciding that every adjustment is good or bad.

A land or timber valuation must specify whether standing timber, future rotations and alternative uses are already in the estimated asset value. Selling the land can eliminate future cash streams. Do not add sale proceeds to a perpetually unchanged harvest forecast, or add a timber valuation to a land valuation that already includes it.

### 5.2 Harvest timing is an economic option, not free storage

The University of Florida framework distinguishes the existing stand's harvest economics from future land use and discounted future rotations. [D04] The proposed company analysis should investigate age/species/grade distribution, access, harvest costs, expected growth, damage risk, financing needs and future-use opportunity at the actual asset scope. No universal harvest age or prescription is proposed.

In a one-period illustration, an asset can be harvested for 100 now. Waiting produces 110 next year but requires 2 of holding expense. Discounting at 8% gives a present value of 100. If waiting also imposes an explicitly assumed next-use opportunity cost of 4 at that future date, the value falls to about 96.30. [H12–13]

This is not a fitted forestry model. A full optimal-rotation analysis needs the complete future sequence, relevant risks and alternative uses; the simple debit isolates a point that a price-times-volume model misses. More biological volume does not automatically imply better discounted value or more immediately available cash.

The proposed dossier should distinguish biological growth, commercial-grade progression, actual harvested/sold output, fair-value accounting and cash received. A noncash loss might signal real economic deterioration; removing it from a ratio does not prove it can be ignored in valuation. Equally, an unrealized valuation gain cannot automatically fund distributions.

### 5.3 West Fraser — replacing an old mill is not adding the new mill's full output

West Fraser's Q2 release reports $1.434 billion of sales, a $61 million loss and $59 million of adjusted EBITDA. It describes the new Henderson mill more than doubling output sequentially and reaching output equivalent to the mill it replaced. Other curtailments and restarts concern separate assets. Operating cash of $192 million is reported, but a complete comparable cash-capital bridge was not established from the selected evidence; no issuer free-cash-flow estimate is admitted. [B05]

**Proposed interpretation.** Establish gross new output, retired output, actual net saleable output and the remaining ramp. A replaced mill reaching its predecessor's output can still represent progress in reliability, quality or costs. It is not evidence of the new mill's entire capacity being incremental to market supply.

The company and industry perspectives differ. A competitor's closure may improve a surviving mill's market conditions, while a company's own closure may consume cash and reduce its sales. The model should distinguish announced closure, actual downtime, permanent retirement and restartability. Higher utilization after removing capacity is not necessarily higher physical demand.

The proposed lumber/OSB/engineered-wood views should preserve product specification and customer applications rather than treat each cubic unit as interchangeable. Lumber-price strength, log costs, resin, energy, transport and usable recovery can affect different products and sites differently. Source evidence must determine the actual exposure and timeframe.

## 6. Pulp and paper: distinguish cash definitions, fiber yield and currency

### 6.1 Suzano — the label cash generation does not establish a cash-statement starting point

Suzano defines Q2 operating cash generation as adjusted EBITDA less sustaining capex: R$4.705 billion minus R$1.820 billion equals R$2.885 billion. Its stated free cash flow is R$2.296 billion; adding back R$882 million of nonmaintenance capex and R$139 million of distributions produces adjusted free cash flow of R$3.317 billion. Its reporting notes also change sustaining-capital allocation by segment. These measures have different boundaries. [B06; SUZ01–06]

Selected original SEC cash-statement text separately reconciles operating cash of R$4.368576 billion. Subtracting cash additions to property, intangibles and biological assets leaves R$1.864754 billion; including financing-classified lease payments gives R$1.520011 billion. Those selected residuals do not reproduce or replace the issuer's FCF. The source is original SEC HTML text; underlying slide/image retrieval failed, so no full visual source audit is claimed. [B06; SUZ07–09]

**Proposed interpretation.** Before comparing any cash number, record its starting measure, cash versus accrual capital basis, lease/interest classification, biological reinvestment and distributions. A concept named operating cash generation may begin at EBITDA rather than reported operating cash flow. A changed allocation method can affect a segment comparison without altering the group asset's physical performance.

The unit-cost model should also separate normal operations from maintenance downtime and use matched currency and quantity definitions. Pulp price, exchange rate, cash input cost, downtime, sales inventory and capital intensity may move differently. An average exchange rate does not automatically translate a realized contract price correctly, and derivative receipts are not physical market prices.

The research should test whether an export-price improvement reaches local-currency receipts and sustainable after-renewal cash. Higher foreign-currency selling prices can coexist with a smaller local-currency benefit, but the actual effect requires matched realization, hedging and timing evidence. No simplified dollar-price-times-average-FX product is admitted as a verified company realization here.

### 6.2 Mondi — removing a biological mark still leaves a different operating outcome

Mondi's H1 underlying EBITDA of €379 million includes a €35 million forestry fair-value loss; the prior €564 million includes an €18 million gain. Removing only those marks gives €414 million versus €546 million, a €132 million decline rather than the reported €185 million decline. This is a one-factor earnings comparison, not normalized cash flow. [B07; MON01–05]

Its visually checked cash statement reports operating cash of €319 million. Subtracting property, intangible and forestry investment gives a €78 million residual. Including the selected interest received, interest paid and lease cash categories changes that residual to negative €20 million. This is not a harmonized issuer FCF definition. The detailed forestry-asset rollforward image was unavailable and is not admitted into the numerical case. [B07; MON06–10]

**Proposed interpretation.** The system should show both the business result and the valuation mark. Biological accounting is not operating cash, but it can contain information about future asset economics. A mechanical addback should neither manufacture cash nor suppress a valid change in expected value.

For integrated paper and packaging, separate the forest, pulp/paper process, energy and downstream conversion stages before aggregating. Internal transfers should be eliminated; external sales and purchases remain economically exposed. An integrated business is not automatically insulated from its input market when it sells part of its production externally, buys a different grade, or has a timing mismatch.

### 6.3 Useful fiber matters more than purchased waste tonnage

Assume a mill purchases 1,000 tonnes of recovered material at $100 per input tonne and obtains 800 usable tonnes. Input acquisition cost is $125 per usable tonne, before sorting, processing, energy and disposal.

A later feedstock costs $90 per input tonne but yields only 70% usable material. Obtaining the same 800 tonnes now requires about 1,142.86 input tonnes, and acquisition cost rises to about $128.57 per usable tonne. [H17–20]

The example is not a real recovered-paper quote or universal process yield. It demonstrates why a cheaper feedstock may not improve conversion economics. Contamination, moisture, relevant fiber properties, strength requirements, rejects, transport and processing costs belong in the source-specific model. Do not infer usable fiber from gross recovered tonnage or assume every recycled grade replaces the same amount of virgin material.

A linked research hypothesis is that changes in sawmill output could affect residual-chip availability and therefore another mill's costs. That hypothesis requires actual regional supply and buying evidence; a relationship diagram alone does not prove its sign, size or investability.

## 7. Packaging: distinguish more packages, more material and more company revenue

### 7.1 PCA — acquisition growth can conceal different legacy economics

PCA reports total corrugated shipments up 24.3% while legacy shipments rose 4.1%, with the same number of shipping days. Its adjusted EPS bridge is $2.48 less $0.27 from legacy changes plus $0.14 from acquired business, producing $2.35. That is $0.02 above the company's cited $2.33 guidance, not an independently verified consensus surprise. [B08; PKG01–04]

**Proposed interpretation.** Preserve acquired and legacy populations, area versus weight, shipping-day conventions, product mix and internal board transfers. Strong consolidated growth need not mean equal organic improvement. Acquisitions may create value, but the share of reported sales obtained by purchasing another business does not measure the return on the price paid for it.

The useful early opportunity could be legacy order and volume improvement combined with actual price retention and more productive integration. The counterargument is that volume growth is low-margin, mix deteriorates, working capital rises, or integration requires more cash than expected. Follow source-bound margin and cash evidence rather than applying a fixed acquisition discount or automatically celebrating scale.

The selected PCA release does not supply a complete cash-flow bridge sufficient for this unit's derived FCF calculation. No estimate is created merely because capex and earnings are visible.

### 7.2 Amcor — reimbursement and acquired revenue are not organic volume

Amcor's Q4 sales increase from $5.082 billion to $6.398 billion includes approximately $962 million of acquisitions net of divestitures and $280 million of raw-material-cost pass-through. The remaining $74 million is not a pure volume measure; other price, mix and currency effects remain. [B09; AMC07–08]

FY operating cash of $2.151 billion less $922 million of cash capital/intangible purchases gives $1.229 billion. Adding $73 million of proceeds gives $1.302 billion, versus named FCF of $1.303 billion; the $1 million difference remains unassigned. Adjusted FCF of $1.593 billion less $290 million of integration/transaction cash costs reconciles to $1.303 billion. Q4 named FCF of $1.396 billion exceeds the full year, implying negative $93 million for the first nine months on that stated basis. Do not annualize Q4. [B09; AMC01–06]

**Proposed interpretation.** Show reported sales growth, comparable volume, achieved pricing and reimbursed input changes separately. Preserve the management method if a combined-company growth estimate is used; it is not automatically a point-in-time organic series. Integration expenses can have a legitimate investment purpose while still using cash. Their removal does not prove that the remaining cash measure describes cash after all ongoing investment needs.

The company is a useful counterexample for the shared Materials grammar: increased input prices can raise a converter's reported sales without producing equal profit. Material-lightweighting or a change in package design can affect tonnes without the same effect on the number of products sold to customers.

### 7.3 More packages can mean less fiber

In an assumed case, one million packages each require 0.6 square metres of material at 500 grams per square metre. Total requirement is 300 tonnes.

A later design sells 1.1 million packages, but each requires 0.55 square metres at 470 grams per square metre. Material requirement is 284.35 tonnes, down about 5.22%, despite 10% more packages. Functional performance and yield are held constant for this illustration; they are not proven. [H14–16]

The proposed demand model is therefore units times material area/volume times specification, adjusted for usable manufacturing yield and inventory. It should not multiply every e-commerce or consumer-unit forecast by an unchanged packaging weight. Equally, lower material use does not establish lower converter value if the customer obtains better function; performance, achieved pricing and supplier costs need their own evidence.

Rigid/flexible packaging, paper, polymer, glass and metal can share a customer job but not a universal cost model. This unit develops selected paper/consumer-packaging examples; it does not establish a complete census of container formats or substitute one category's unit economics for another's.

### 7.4 Higher board prices can help a mill and hurt an independent converter

Assume board sells for 100 and costs 70 to make. A converter sells the final package for 150, buys board for 100 and spends 20 converting it. Both retain 30 before other costs.

If external board rises to 120 while end-package price and costs are unchanged, upstream contribution becomes 50 while the independent converter's contribution falls to 10. For a fully captive integrated chain, consolidated contribution remains 150 minus 70 minus 20 = 60; the transfer price cancels. If end-package price later reaches 165, the independent converter earns 25 and the integrated chain 75 on the stated assumptions. [H21–27]

This is not a universal natural hedge. Actual external exposure, mismatched grades, inventories, contract reset lags, transport and joint ownership matter. The proposed model should ask who faces the external market and when—not assign the same directional score to every company mentioning containerboard.

## 8. Industry measurements: capacity withdrawal, inventories and timing

AF&PA's Q2 public summary reports containerboard production down 2% year over year, domestic demand up 1%, exports down 19%, and an operating rate around 95% versus 93% in Q1. Its Q1 release attributes weaker production alongside capacity reductions. The publication uses different comparison horizons; do not turn all those numbers into a single unqualified acceleration signal. [D05–06]

Higher utilization can result from a smaller capacity denominator. It may still support local or product-level pricing, but it is not proof of increasing physical demand. The proposed industry view needs actual capacity removed, announced versus executed closure, output, inventories, trade and the relevant specification. Restartability and alternate capacity are falsifiers for a permanent-scarcity narrative.

AF&PA's September printing/writing release reports August shipments and inventories but discusses July purchase and trade observations. Those clocks are different. August shipments cannot be combined with July imports to produce an August balance. The public summary also does not license reproduction of unavailable subscription tables. [D07]

The future system should expose original periods and source availability, not launder a mixed-frequency read into one page date. Inventory changes must specify physical quantity versus value, company versus industry, mill versus merchant versus converter, and any acquired stock. Production minus shipments is not automatically a verified inventory change when scope and other movements are incomplete.

## 10. Eight conditional rerating mechanisms and falsifiers

**R7-H1 — Local comparable price retention.** Research whether same-market, comparable-specification realization outpaces relevant cash costs while volumes and delivery reliability remain adequate. Early evidence: orders, accepted price resets and matched company disclosures. Falsifiers: mix explains the change, a cheaper feasible route opens, or lost volume/capex absorbs the gain. Test next-period retained unit economics before stock-return claims.

**R7-H2 — Productive replacement or blending.** Research whether replacing old assets, reducing inputs or shifting accepted mix produces more useful output/cash from the same constrained capital. Falsifiers: retired output is double-counted, downstream capacity or customer acceptance binds, or commissioning costs persist. Plant announcements alone do not satisfy the hypothesis.

**R7-H3 — A local project cohort reaches material purchasing.** Research whether funded, geographically relevant construction moves into a stage requiring the material. Falsifiers: authorization never converts to work, nominal cost inflation accounts for growth, projects move elsewhere, or the company lacks documented exposure. A national spending correlation is only a baseline.

**R7-H4 — Timber cash improves without sacrificing more future value.** Research realizations, harvest mix, biological growth, land decisions and renewal together. Falsifiers: cash is mostly a nonrecurring land sale, harvesting depletes future economics faster, rights obligations are omitted or a mark is mistaken for cash. Valuation needs remaining asset value, not only current yield.

**R7-H5 — Mill-to-converter spread transfer.** Research matched external prices, input purchase vintages, end-product reset terms and inventory. Falsifiers: internal prices are double-counted, grades differ, customers receive the whole benefit, or working capital offsets the gain. The same commodity move can support different signs for different financial claims.

**R7-H6 — Genuine legacy demand and mix improvement.** Research comparable shipments, shipping days, package area/weight and customer end use. Falsifiers: acquisitions supply the growth, lightweighting changes the measurement, or low-margin products dominate. Preserve both volume and value creation; do not force one to proxy the other.

**R7-H7 — Capacity rationalization improves remaining economics.** Research executed closures and their net effect on product/geographic supply, demand and costs. Falsifiers: supply restarts, closures are offset elsewhere, demand falls faster, or the issuer bears greater exit liabilities than the remaining benefit. Utilization alone is insufficient.

**R7-H8 — Cash conversion and investment returns become visible.** Research whether prior capital projects, integration spending and seasonal working capital produce actual receipts and productive output. Falsifiers: adjusted cash relies on excluding repeated necessary investment, funding expands or the asset is overpaid for. Compare new evidence with a dated prior expectation rather than equating retrieval novelty with market surprise.

These mechanisms can be relevant over different horizons. A shorter-lived spread opportunity is not automatically invalid, but its expected duration and exit conditions must be explicit. No fixed weights, probabilities or automatic ranking rules are proposed here. Each hypothesis needs point-in-time tests and prospective shadow evidence before influencing the existing decision owners.

## 11. Valuation: valuable assets can still be unattractive purchases

A proposed local-materials valuation should explain comparable delivered cash, feasible competition, reserve/access duration, sustaining and closure costs, future expansion and ownership. A forest valuation should explain standing timber, future rotations, alternative land uses and contractual rights without double counting. A mill/converter valuation should explain through-cycle spread, reliability, maintenance, net external exposure and customer value. Acquisition accounting is not a substitute for the economics of the purchase price.

Consider a purely hypothetical ten-year asset producing 8 annual cash units, with 20 of terminal value in year ten, discounted at 10%. Deduct 3 of immediate integration cost and 2 of separately measured obligation present value. The result is approximately **51.87 value units**. Paying 60 is not justified by this particular scenario merely because the asset is physically scarce. [H29]

All cash-flow inputs, terminal scope and obligations must be internally consistent. Do not deduct a liability a second time if it is already in operating cash, or include terminal land/resource value already reflected in the cash stream. The example is not a fair-value estimate for any company, land parcel, quarry or security.

For the final masterplan, scenario outputs should separate changes in operating assumptions, financing/claims, share count and valuation assumptions. A multiple re-rating can coexist with earnings decline; earnings can grow without multiple expansion. Attribution is useful only with comparable measures. Negative or cyclical earnings should not be forced into a simple P/E identity.

The long-term moat hypothesis is not possession of more source data. It is the ability to show how an observed change travels through a specified material, market, business and financial claim, preserve the conditions, and learn which interpretations improved useful discovery. That requires synthesis and evaluation, not only provenance.

## 13. Evaluation and research-exit criteria

First test **semantic usefulness**. Can the user distinguish improving underlying local prices from a lower blended average, replacement from expansion, cash from valuation marks, and producer margins from converter margins? Does the explanation make the next evidence concrete? An honest unavailable result is useful when it prevents a false conclusion, but missingness alone is not the whole product.

Then test forecast targets separately. Next-quarter realized price, unit contribution, production, operating cash and forward equity returns are different outcomes. A strong explanation of a past quarter is not a calibrated forecast. Use original publication/availability times, unrevised historical data where available, historical membership/ownership, delistings, liquidity and currency. Compare role-aware models with simple commodity, sector, company-trend and momentum baselines. Do not fit a complex model to nine illustrative cases and claim alpha.

Prospective shadow evaluation should track whether early research conditions occur, how often they fail, lead time, abstentions, false-positive burden and incremental discovery value. Treat restatements, same-event reprints and related sources as corrections or dependencies, not independent confirming events. Preserve no-trade authority until the designated owners accept prediction or decision-bearing behavior.
