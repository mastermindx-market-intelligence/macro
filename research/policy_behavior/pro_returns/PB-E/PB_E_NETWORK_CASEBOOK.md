# PB-E — Circular financing and selective protection: network casebook

**Status:** complete research return; not production integration. **Cutoff:** October 6, 2026, America/Chicago; sources retrieved October 7 UTC. Every later filing supplies a dated observation, not a complete current-state certification.

## Research finding

The selected networks contain real funding, commercial contracts and operating activity alongside concentrated financial relationships and conditional support. A relationship can be economically productive while creating correlated risk. There is no defensible universal circularity score or industry-wide financed-demand fraction in this evidence set.

The strongest fragile mechanism is the concentration of investor, supplier, creditor and backstop exposure around a customer whose independent cash generation is incompletely disclosed. The strongest benign structural control is ASML's customer-funded development with broader market access and explicit commercial incentives. Meta and Talen provide additional operating-business controls. Government support can preserve capacity or lender recoverability while imposing dilution, restrictions or losses on equity.

## Evidence standard and selection

This is a purposive 15-case sample, not an estimate of prevalence. OpenAI and Anthropic recur across cases, so cases are not independent statistical observations. SEC filings, filed agreements, government documents and issuer announcements supply the primary record; the rating-agency description establishes its own financing assessment and is separately qualified. Public use/run-rate claims remain issuer reports, not independently audited cash.

First-public, agreement, close, draw, reporting-period and retrieval dates are different clocks. Where an exact clock is missing it stays unknown. The structured data are explicitly ineligible for point-in-time or production consumption pending owner admission. Later contract detail is never automatically backdated to an initial announcement. Sources were inspected in relevant sections; the return does not claim full-document byte retention, legal completeness or a comprehensive subsequent-event search for every unknown.

Amounts retain currency, scope and qualifiers. A cap is not cash; a contract is not revenue; revenue is not collection; delivered capacity is not utilization; utilization is not profitable independent demand. Unknown is not zero. The casebook is an economic evidence reconstruction, not an allegation of fraud or a credit/trading recommendation.

## C01 — Microsoft–OpenAI

## C05 — Amazon–Anthropic: funded vendor equity and a compute-delivery-linked financing facility
**Assessment:** Strongly documented vendor-capital / purchase relationship; real deployment evidence; independence of ultimate customer cash coverage remains unmeasured. No finding of fabricated revenue.

**Entities and roles:** Amazon.com, Inc.: capital provider; AWS parent; Anthropic minority investor. AWS: compute supplier; custom-chip developer; Claude distributor through Bedrock. Anthropic: model developer; compute buyer; equity/convertible issuer. Anthropic outside investors and customers: potential external capital and customer-cash ingress; distinct populations.

### Public information clocks

| Economic event | Earliest verified public clock for this evidence | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| 2023-09-25 | 2023-09-25 | Named partnership and up-to-USD4bn minority investment announced [AI_A01] |
| 2026-04-20 | 2026-04-20 | >USD100bn / 10-year AWS commitment; USD5bn current and up to USD20bn future capital announced [AI_A02] |
| 2026-05-28 | 2026-05-28 | Series H announcement includes USD5bn Amazon participation [AI_A04] |
| 2026-06-30 | 2026-07-30 | Quarter-end funded amounts and detailed delivery-linked facility disclosed in 10-Q accepted July 30 18:11:13; filing date July 31 [AI_A03] [AI_A05] |

### Structure and first public clocks

Amazon invests in the same firm that purchases AWS capacity and distributes Claude through Amazon Bedrock. The September 25, 2023 announcement established the named relationship: up to USD4bn of minority capital, AWS as primary cloud, and collaboration on Trainium/Inferentia. This creates two economic benefits for Amazon—investment returns and cloud/chip demand—without proving that any particular investment dollar later became AWS revenue. Keep that first public clock separate from the much larger April 20, 2026 expansion and the later SEC disclosure of its financing mechanics. [AI_A01] [AI_A02] [AI_A05]

### Financing, execution and equity rights

Amazon's June 2026 filing identifies USD8bn invested in convertible notes during Q3 2023–Q4 2025, followed by USD5bn Series G and USD5bn Series H preferred-stock investments in Q2 2026. Cumulative funded capital is therefore USD18bn, a calculation rather than a separate issuer headline. The new facility began with a USD20bn cap; Series H participation reduced the remaining cap to USD15bn. Availability depends on AWS delivery milestones, with Anthropic choosing draws. Funding purchases notes or, after a liquidity event, common stock subject to an ownership cap. The facility expires 30 months after an Anthropic liquidity event. Nonvoting equity and convertibles do not establish a control acquisition. [AI_A03]

### Commercial obligation and performance

Anthropic announced a commitment exceeding USD100bn over ten years for AWS technology, alongside capacity of up to 5GW. The announcement covers Trainium 2–4 and Graviton and includes future-silicon options; it should not be read as a fixed quantity of already delivered chips. Amazon's filing confirms contractual chip-performance obligations. The full schedule of enforceable minimum spend, rebates, cancellation damages, service credits, assignment rights, prepayment requirements and termination conditions is unavailable in inspected sources. This is a documented purchase commitment with performance conditions, not evidence that every announced dollar is unconditional or already recognized. [AI_A02] [AI_A03]

### Revenue, cash, delivery and utilization

In April, Anthropic reported using over one million Trainium2 chips to train and serve Claude; almost 1GW of additional Trainium2/3 capacity was expected during 2026. The first statement is operational evidence supplied by the customer; the second is forward capacity. Neither supplies utilization percentages. The same announcement reports over 100,000 Bedrock customers. Those customers can include free, trial or mixed-volume accounts, so account counts do not measure independent cash. Anthropic-specific AWS revenue, cash collections, receivables and deferred revenue are not separately reported in inspected filings. Amazon's investment remeasurement gains belong to investment accounting, not cloud sales or cash receipts. [AI_A02] [AI_A03]

### External capital, end customers and public support

Anthropic's May 28 Series H announcement reports USD65bn raised, including USD15bn of previously committed hyperscaler money. Amazon's USD5bn is inside that total. Named financial investors show that financing extends beyond AWS; named semiconductor investors also benefit from infrastructure growth, so the remainder cannot all be labeled economically disinterested capital. Anthropic also reports a USD47bn revenue run rate reached earlier in May. That is a current-rate extrapolation, not audited annual revenue or cash collection. No case-specific government grant, guarantee, procurement cash, warrant or subsidy amount was established in these sources. Absence of a disclosed amount is not proof that no public support exists. [AI_A04]

### Dependencies and economically benign explanation

The capital facility explicitly connects AWS delivery with financing availability. A delivery miss could slow both compute expansion and access to vendor capital, while sustained customer demand could require still more capacity. This is an execution dependency, not a demonstrated insolvency trigger. The benign explanation has economic support: co-development of specialized chips and an established distribution channel can reduce unit costs and speed customer access; existing training/serving deployments indicate productive equipment use. Multiple other capital providers weaken a claim that Anthropic is wholly captive to one financier. However, vendor variety does not establish immediate workload portability or prove profitable unit economics. [AI_A01] [AI_A02] [AI_A04]

### Falsifiers, unknowns and metric limits

A fragility thesis would weaken with audited third-party customer cash, durable positive contribution margins after full inference costs, capacity delivered on schedule, and ordinary financing raised without tied purchases. It would strengthen with persistent usage shortfalls, repeated vendor-funded renewals, large disputed receivables, or released contractual terms showing obligations that substantially exceed independent cash generation. Those are tests, not findings. Do not divide USD18bn funded equity by USD100bn of ten-year purchase commitments and call the quotient a financed-demand share: different periods, cash-flow categories and overlapping financing rounds defeat that denominator. Present funded cash, conditional capital, purchase commitments and utilization as separate observations.

**Explicit unknowns:** Anthropic-specific AWS recognized revenue, accounts receivable, deferred revenue and collections; Exact enforceable minimums, cancellation damages and chip-performance remedies; Actual drawn amount of remaining USD15bn facility after June 30; Independent end-customer cash and audited unit profitability; Complete equity governance/ownership cap terms; Case-specific government support amounts.

**Entities and roles:** Alphabet / Google: Anthropic investor; convertible creditor; compute supplier; Vertex AI distributor. Anthropic: model developer; compute customer; capital recipient. Broadcom: Google TPU and networking supplier; named intermediary for part of Anthropic capacity. Other cloud suppliers, investors and end customers: alternative channels/capital and potential external cash.

### Structure and public clocks

Google is both a capital provider and a seller/distributor of services used by Anthropic. The public cloud partnership began February 3, 2023. A later CMA decision describes an October 2022 side letter, but that historic contract date must not be used as the date the public could know its terms. The November 19, 2024 decision's full text became public December 24. It identifies nonvoting equity, convertible debt, a nonexclusive compute commitment, and nonexclusive Vertex AI distribution. The distinction matters for event studies: a previously confidential term can only enter a public information set when disclosed. [AI_G01] [AI_G02]

### Capital, rights and disclosure limits

The CMA redacts dollar amounts and parts of the supply timetable. It records no board representation rights, conversion into nonvoting shares, and no commercial-policy veto establishing material influence under the test applied. These are useful governance facts, not a finding that Anthropic's financing or demand is economically independent. Exact cash funded, current ownership, note maturities and any later revised governance terms remain unknown here. Alphabet's June 2026 filing includes an anonymous private-company future capital commitment of USD20bn, contingent on milestones through 2030. It does not name Anthropic; that amount is excluded from this case's named capital totals. [AI_G02] [AI_G06] [AI_G07]

### Commercial commitments and capacity

On October 23, 2025, Anthropic announced an expansion involving up to one million Google TPUs, valued imprecisely at tens of billions of dollars, with well over 1GW expected online during 2026. On April 6, 2026, it announced multiple gigawatts of additional next-generation TPU capacity expected to come online starting in 2027. Broadcom's same-day 8-K names approximately 3.5GW through Broadcom as part of the broader commitment. Anthropic's May Series H release later describes the Google–Broadcom arrangement as 5GW. The 3.5GW component and 5GW envelope must not be added to produce 8.5GW. [AI_G03] [AI_G04] [AI_G05] [AI_A04]

### Conditions, recognized economics and real consumption

Broadcom says consumption depends on Anthropic's continuing commercial success and that it is working with operational and financial partners. That is a meaningful risk qualification, but the public wording does not reveal an enforceable customer cancellation right. The full capacity contract, minimum cash payments, acceptance tests, service credits, equity incentives, termination rights and default waterfall were not obtained. Google-specific Anthropic revenue, customer collections, receivables and deferred balances are not quantified. TPU use is established qualitatively; utilization of the announced future gigawatts is not. A capacity reservation should remain a commitment edge until delivery and consumption are separately evidenced. [AI_G03] [AI_G05]

### Outside demand, alternatives and public support

Anthropic repeatedly describes a three-platform strategy spanning Google TPUs, AWS Trainium and Nvidia GPUs, while naming AWS its primary cloud/training partner. The CMA reviewed multiple financing sources and sales channels and found insufficient evidence of Google material influence at that time. This gives the benign alternative more support than a bare claim that a successful startup can refinance indefinitely. Anthropic's customer-growth and revenue-run-rate disclosures provide demand indicators, but they are affiliated statements and do not isolate independent cash collected or gross margins on Google compute. No case-specific government grant, procurement, public guarantee or tax-credit amount was established. [AI_G02] [AI_G03] [AI_G04]

### Dependency analysis and competing benign explanation

A supplier investing in a major customer can share development risk, secure demand for specialized silicon, and help a customer optimize workloads. Multi-vendor procurement can improve bargaining power and reduce exposure to a single technology roadmap. These are plausible productive reasons for the relationship, supported by named deployments across alternative platforms. The remaining fragility hypothesis concerns economics and timing: customer revenue might fail to grow fast enough to absorb reserved capacity, or migration and chip qualification could take longer than financing assumptions allow. Nonexclusive contracts permit alternatives but do not make alternative capacity instantly available. No cash round trip or fictitious revenue is established.

### Falsifiers and exclusions

The strongest falsifiers would be audited independent customer cash sufficient to service compute costs, verified broad TPU utilization, transparent cancellation/acceptance terms, and outside financing without commercial ties. Evidence of repeated vendor-supported extensions, unused reserved capacity, or obligations concentrated in one platform would instead strengthen dependency concerns. Do not import a media-reported USD40bn Google investment or USD200bn Google purchase headline into primary-verified totals without named primary documents. Likewise, Broadcom's later anonymous XPV financing, guarantee and convertible-note facility are excluded from the named Anthropic graph; a shared sector, technology or timing is insufficient counterparty identification. The appendix preserves that relevant evidence without manufacturing a link. [AI_B01]

**Explicit unknowns:** Named Google cash invested and current exact ownership; Convertible maturity, covenants, cash coupon and conversion details not in public CMA text; Exact dollar spend/minimums and termination remedies for TPU contracts; Actual delivered MW and utilization of announced expansions; Counterparty Google revenue/AR/deferred cash and independent end-customer collections; Identity of private company in Alphabet Q2 commitment and later anonymous Broadcom financing.

## C07 — Stargate / Oracle / SoftBank / OpenAI: overlapping infrastructure promises and actual outside financing

### Program, entities and initial promise

Stargate's January 21, 2025 announcement describes a new company intending to invest USD500bn over four years and begin deploying USD100bn immediately. It names SoftBank, OpenAI, Oracle and MGX as initial equity funders, with SoftBank responsible for finance and OpenAI for operations. It also says definitive agreements were still being finalized. The headline therefore belongs to an announced program envelope, not a verified USD500bn financing close. Microsoft, Nvidia and Arm's technical participation does not itself establish equal equity funding. A White House launch is not proof of a federal guarantee, grant or procurement payment. [AI_S01]

### Purchase commitments and overlapping capacity

OpenAI's September 23, 2025 update expressly states that its Oracle partnership exceeds USD300bn over five years. That is named primary evidence for the headline, although the complete enforceable contract is unavailable. The same page connects nearly 7GW of planned capacity with more than USD400bn of investment and identifies up to 4.5GW of additional Oracle capacity. These overlap the original Stargate program and must not be summed. The page reports first GB200 racks at Abilene in June and early training/inference. A later October site update should be distinguished from the September information clock. [AI_S02]

### Actual capital and equity

SoftBank's March 31, 2025 investment agreement, announced April 1, initially contemplated up to USD40bn with USD10bn syndicated and staged closing conditions linked to OpenAI's restructuring. Its convertible instruments included preferred/common conversion provisions. That historic conditional envelope is superseded for cash-status purposes by later executed disclosures. On October 1, 2026, SoftBank reported completing the third USD10bn tranche of its 2026 USD30bn follow-on investment. It put cumulative OpenAI investment at USD64.6bn and ownership near 13%. These are equity injections into OpenAI, not demonstrated spending on a particular Stargate campus. [AI_S04] [AI_S07]

### Debt, maturity and correction of the current risk picture

SoftBank executed a USD40bn unsecured bridge facility in March 2026, originally maturing March 25, 2027, with major banks as lenders. A facility limit does not prove that the full amount was drawn. Planned capacity is not observed battery output or consumer sales. [SEL-S26, SEL-S27, SEL-S28]

The May 2026 8-K specifies the original principal limit as $9,633,040,000 and cumulative advances as $7,835,540,000. Those advances are historical funding, while the later $3,805,040,000 assumption is an overlapping subset and creates no fresh cash. Ford initially guaranteed 50% of BOSK payment obligations. A JV disposition agreement was dated **December 9, 2025** and the transaction **closed May 20, 2026**, disclosed in a May 21 8-K. At the May 20 close, Ford was released from its prior sponsor-support obligations, including the **50% BOSK payment guarantee**. Ford's membership was redeemed, its remaining capital-contribution obligation ended, and its subsidiary obtained the two Kentucky plants subject to DOE liens. Ford assumed a **$3,805,040,000** note for advances to one Kentucky plant. That assumption transferred an existing debt; it was not fresh cash raised on May 20. The note pays **4.814%**, with interest-only payments through January 15, 2030 and final maturity July 15, 2040. [SEL-S28]

The new loan agreement identifies FFB as funding source and states the loan was **already fully advanced**, with no right to further advances under that note. It retains collateral, prepayment requirements and limits on the borrower. This does **not** establish what happened to every other original BOSK facility; any assertion that the entire undrawn remainder of $9.63 billion was canceled is left unverified. [SEL-S31]

Ford's Q2 2026 filing reports that, after DOE draws, BOSK had distributed **$3.1 billion to Ford as returns of capital**, including $1.7 billion in H1 2025. The completed disposition generated a **$3.6 billion pretax special-item charge**, including about $500 million of cash expenditures. Thus there is observed government-funded refinancing of previously invested sponsor capital, coupled with recognized private losses and changed capacity plans. These are reported accounting/cash facts; a claim of improper extraction would require additional evidence about permitted uses and the complete transaction. [SEL-S29]

### Protection mechanism and alternatives

**External cash and demand:** federal credit funded the JV's assets and allowed capital returns; prospective vehicle/battery sales remain a separate private demand stream. Ford's sponsor contribution, BOSK loan draw and BOSK return of that contribution cannot all be treated as independent final demand. Post-restructuring battery uses may broaden, so the control should be labeled by its EV-financing origination and the changing use documented separately.

**Protection target, inference:** credit-supported factories and secured loan recovery can persist even as the original JV dissolves. Existing shareholders face charges and the parent retains debt. The case directly contradicts a universal version of “strategic means the original equity/business plan is protected.”

**Benign interpretation, inference:** restructure ownership and manufacturing use to preserve recoverable industrial assets as customer demand changes.

**Fragile interpretation, inference:** the original market/capacity forecasts may fail despite favorable credit, and support can shift risk to the parent or taxpayer while the assets require further investment.

**Falsifiers / follow-up evidence:** production and utilization at each plant; independent battery/vehicle customers; actual sales margins; debt-service compliance; collateral disposition or revised loan agreements; clear evidence about the unused original facilities. Do not infer blanket debt forgiveness, 100% federal loss absorption, or guaranteed equity returns.

**Competing benign explanation, inference.** Customers pool development risk for enabling technology with broader industry access and explicit accounting of incentives.
