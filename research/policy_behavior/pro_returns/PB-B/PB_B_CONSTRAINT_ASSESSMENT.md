# PB-B CONSTRAINT_ASSESSMENT

**Research cutoff: 2026-10-07. Scope: U.S.–Japan monetary, foreign-exchange and dollar-liquidity interactions, with domestic-policy controls.** Source IDs in brackets map to this packet’s registry. Confidence labels describe the strength of evidence, not numerical hypothesis probabilities. This is research for PB-G adjudication; it creates no production signal, score, trade instruction or policy command.

## Judgment

**Conditional U.S. leverage over assisted FX cooperation and instrument selection survives. A contribution to BOJ timing is credible, but its incremental effect is not identified against domestic normalization.** The evidence supports a narrower external-constraint account than a general claim that Washington dictated Japan’s monetary or fiscal policy.

H1 and H2 are co-leading explanations across different endpoints. H1 is strongest where Washington controls its own participation or the terms of assistance. H2 leads for the aggregate BOJ rate path. H3 and H4 explain the shared purpose and demand for cooperation; they can coexist with conditional leverage. H5 constrains attribution to particular late public statements, but cannot establish that the entire path was priced before cumulative U.S. involvement.

| Claim | Judgment | Confidence / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| The U.S. sought Japanese normalization and yen stability | Supported by public statements and official readout | High for preferences; not an enforceable undertaking |
| Conditions influenced access to assisted FX support or instrument selection | Substantive surviving mechanism | Moderate causal confidence; exact accepted terms remain private |
| U.S. involvement changed BOJ meeting timing | Credible contributing hypothesis | Incremental effect unmeasured; pre-May intended path missing |
| Domestic evidence principally explains the normalization baseline | Strong competing account | Strong evidence of relevance; exclusive domination unproved |
| Sept 1 public words newly determined Sept 18 policy | Not established | Earlier information and pricing prevent clean attribution |
| FIMA enlargement or material borrowing financed intervention | Not verified | Advocacy is not approval or funding; public balances constrain use claims |
| Japan lost unilateral FX or fiscal discretion | Rejected in strong form | Actual unilateral intervention and continued spending contradict it |

## The strongest external-constraint evidence

The [Reuters reconstruction](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/how-bessent-americas-bond-salesman-cornered-japan-big-spending-2026-09-17/) reports three sources describing private May rate-hike urging, and a government source describing subsequent June/July consideration. It also reports Katayama’s June 22 request for assistance and Bessent’s fiscal/monetary-consistency conditions. Specific private conversations were not officially confirmed. The retrieved edition includes Sept 18’s outcome; fiscal spending nevertheless expanded. [RX_2026_09_PRIVATE]

This supplies a plausible causal channel beyond correlated rhetoric: a request, a resource controlled by the outside party, conditions, and later cooperation. The relevant constrained choice is the **U.S.-assisted option**. Japan could retain unilateral alternatives while finding that an assisted option carried a price. Solicitation therefore does not negate leverage, and leverage does not require hidden control, legal subordination or elimination of every alternative.

The [official Treasury readout](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0619) corroborates the preference for sound monetary policy and decisive steps supporting yen stability. Its Aug 30 dateline describes the meeting; the verified Sept 1 16:18 UTC news bound does not certify Aug 30 publication. This primary corroboration establishes the policy preference, not the detailed terms of earlier private negotiations. [TREASURY_2026_08_READOUT; RX_2026_09_READOUT]

Actual joint intervention was publicly confirmed, while a later account describes months of preparation and a separate Japanese operation preceding the joint leg. Actual U.S. size is unknown; an apparent plan cannot substitute for executed amounts. Reports about exchanging foreign-currency assets for yen support an instrument distinction from a direct dollar sale or loan. [RX_2026_08_JOINT; RX_2026_08_ORIGIN; RX_2026_08_LETTER]

The main inferential gap is the counterfactual. A negotiated option becoming available does not prove that Japan accepted every reported condition, that support would otherwise have been refused, or that a particular rate hike was the concession. The evidence establishes a credible bargaining relationship more firmly than a measured change in the monetary reaction function.

A newly checked pre-May baseline narrows the monetary claim further. An April 7–14 Reuters poll already found 46 of 71 economists expecting the rate to reach 1% by end-June. On April 28, three BOJ members sought 1% while the majority held 0.75%; contemporary reporting described June market anticipation. Those are survey and qualitative market observations, not a numeric OIS surprise, but they make it untenable to infer that the May conversation created June-hike expectations. They follow earlier U.S. involvement and therefore are not wholly untreated controls. [RX_2026_04_POLL; BOJ_2026_04; RX_2026_04_BOJ]

## Evidence against the strongest constraint narrative

Normalization preceded the specified pressure cycle: the BOJ exited its earlier framework in March 2024, raised its rate in July 2024, and reached 0.5% in January 2025. These controls reject a claim that the later U.S. interventions originated all tightening. They cannot fix the counterfactual dates of subsequent moves. [BOJ_2024_03; BOJ_2024_07; BOJ_2025_01]

The BOJ held 0.5% on Oct 30 2025 after Bessent’s call for policy space, then raised it to 0.75% in December. In 2026 it held in January and March, raised to 1% in June, held in July, and raised to 1.25% in September. This discontinuous path is inconsistent with a simple “U.S. demand implies the next meeting’s hike” rule. July’s hold alone does not refute broader directional conditions: the preceding hike or a prospective path could have satisfied them. [RX_2025_10_PRESSURE; BOJ_2025_10; BOJ_2025_12; BOJ_2026_01; BOJ_2026_03; BOJ_2026_06; BOJ_2026_07; BOJ_2026_09]

Japan independently executed ¥11,734.9 billion of intervention in the April 28–May 27 reporting period; official Q2 U.S. accounting records no U.S. intervention. This demonstrates an operative unilateral instrument. The later Japanese July 30–August 26 total of ¥15,399.3 billion is neither a July 31-only figure nor an American amount. [MOF_2026_05; MOF_2026_Q2; NYFED_2026_Q2; MOF_2026_08]

Fiscal autonomy requires a separate conclusion. Continued budget expansion weakens a claim that U.S. leverage prohibited spending growth. It does **not** establish that H2 explains all fiscal politics, or that there was no concession in composition, funding or expansion relative to a larger intended budget. Neither absent public concession detail nor a high spending level identifies full domestic domination.

## Domestic vintages: a genuine alternative, with mixed signals

The domestic alternative is a conditional wage/price/growth mechanism, not an assertion that every observed hike was inevitable. The May 2025 Bank’s View reduced projected FY2025/26 growth to 0.5%/0.7% and core inflation to 2.2%/1.7%; a hold was compatible with domestic uncertainty. By July, projected FY2025 core inflation was 2.7%, but food-driven increases and underlying inflation remained distinct. [OUTLOOK_2025_04; BOJ_2025_05; OUTLOOK_2025_07]

For the August 2025 remarks, the proper contemporaneous data were June CPI, the June preliminary wage release, and Q1’s second GDP estimate. Headline and ex-fresh-food inflation were both 3.3%; excluding fresh food and energy was 3.4%, while the broader food/energy exclusion was 1.6%. Preliminary total nominal earnings rose 2.5%, basic pay 2.1%, and standard real earnings fell 1.3%. Revised nominal 3.1% and real −0.8% figures arrived Aug 22 and cannot be inserted into an Aug 13 information set. Q1 GDP was −0.0% q/q after rounding, −0.2% annualized; Q2’s first estimate was not public until Aug 15. [CPI_2025_06; WAGES_2025_06_P; WAGES_2025_06_R; GDP_2025_Q1_2]

Negotiated wages supported normalization without measuring realized economywide income: March 2025’s first union tally was 5.46%; March 2026’s was 5.26%; the July 2026 final tally was 5.01%, with identifiable base-pay improvement 3.50%. These coverage and concept distinctions matter when connecting negotiations to consumption or sustained inflation. [RENGO_2025_FIRST; RENGO_2026_FIRST; RENGO_2026_FINAL]

Before the late-August 2026 meeting, July CPI was 1.9% headline, 1.8% ex fresh food, 1.9% ex fresh food/energy, and 1.4% on the broader food/energy exclusion. Revised June wages, public Aug 24, showed nominal earnings +4.0%, basic pay +3.5%, real earnings +2.2%. The Aug 17 GDP vintage showed Q2 +0.3% q/q, +1.1% annualized, but domestic demand −0.2%, investment −1.2%, and consumption −0.0% after rounding. The July BOJ outlook combined 0.6% FY2026 growth with 2.5% core inflation and upside inflation risk. This is a mixed domestic case, not a one-direction imperative. [CPI_2026_07; WAGES_2026_06_R; GDP_2026_Q2_1; OUTLOOK_2026_07]

Sept 8 releases strengthened the pre-Sept 18 information set: revised Q2 GDP was +0.4% q/q, +1.4% annualized; July preliminary wages were nominal +4.7%, real +2.4%, but same-establishment nominal growth was +2.8%. They were unavailable at the earlier meeting/readout cuts. August CPI was also public before the September 18 BOJ decision: the official 08:30 JST release corresponds to September 17 23:30 UTC, preceding the BOJ’s 02:54 UTC announcement. Headline inflation was 1.9%, ex-fresh-food 1.7%, and ex-fresh-food-and-energy 1.9%. The casebook includes that vintage. Oct 1’s Summary of Opinions supplies only ex-post rationale and government cautions. [GDP_2026_Q2_2; WAGES_2026_07_P; CPI_2026_08; BOJ_OPINIONS_2026_09]

## Expectations and numerical windows

The observations establish news salience and path uncertainty; they are descriptive windows, not causal coefficients:

- August 2025 JGB reports show five-year yields +3.5 bp to 1.10% and ten-year +3 bp to 1.545%; the 62% December-hike pricing is a post-remark level, without a same-contract pre-remark observation. Weak auction demand and thin liquidity also matter. [RX_2025_08_JGB]
- December 2025’s hike had roughly 90% reported pricing on Dec 10, yet the dollar gained 1.38% against the yen over Dec 19’s session. That session return cannot isolate announcement or conference effects. [RX_2025_12_PRE; RX_2025_12_POST]
- January 2026 reported New York USDJPY endpoints, about 157.50 to 155.66 and later 155.85, distinguish that leg from an earlier Tokyo move. Rate checks are indicative quote inquiries; subsequent official records show no funded January intervention. [RX_2026_01_CHECK; RX_2026_01_FX; MOF_2026_01; NYFED_2026_Q1]
- September-hike pricing rose from 24% on July 30 to 76% on Aug 13: **52 percentage points of composite repricing**, not a Bessent coefficient. Both endpoints follow earlier U.S. involvement. [RX_2026_08_PRICING]
- Aug 4’s approximate USDJPY 158 to 157.55 observation lacks synchronized ticks; the FIMA request was already reported Aug 3 from Aug 2 remarks. [RX_2026_08_SUPPORT; RX_2026_08_FIMA_FIRST]
- Sept 18’s 02:54 UTC announcement delivered 1.25% with two dissents. The early report showed yen weakness of about 0.7%, two-year yield −2.5 bp to 1.835% at 04:42 UTC, and ten-year −0.5 bp to 2.985%. The 06:30 UTC Ueda conference is a separate window; the syndication’s later posting clock cannot be assigned to all underlying quotes. [BOJ_2026_09; RX_2026_09_MARKET]

Near-full September pricing reported by Aug 31 limits an incremental late public-news shock. It is **post-treatment pricing for cumulative May–July involvement** and cannot disprove earlier influence. The 1.75% terminal figure is a broker forecast, not a downloaded OIS terminal rate. Missing raw OIS curves, synchronized UST controls and exact quote clocks prevent separation of policy-level, path, FX-support and global-dollar shocks. [RX_2026_08_31; RX_2026_08_PRICING]

## Instruments and operational correction

A BOJ policy-rate decision, MOF-directed yen buying, and Fed dollar-repo liquidity are separate instruments with different authorities and balance-sheet effects. Treasury concern for market functioning or reserve sales does not mechanically become a Japanese interest-rate instruction. The [Fed FIMA mechanism](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fima-repo-facility-faqs.htm) lends dollars against Treasury collateral. Governing rules specify $60 billion **total outstanding per counterparty**, not per day, and delegate adjustments to the Foreign Currency Subcommittee. No enlargement is verified. Rounded foreign-official asset repos were zero in relevant weekly H.4.1 observations; the liability-side foreign reverse-repo pool is different. These findings constrain material-use claims without proving zero to the cent or ruling out every intraday transaction. [FIMA_FAQ; FOMC_2026_DIRECTIVE; H41_2026_08_06; H41_2026_08_13]

## Endpoint rankings and falsifier

Overall: **H1 ≈ H2 > H4 ≈ H3 > H5**. This is an endpoint-aware synthesis, not a posterior distribution or measured total order.

| Endpoint | Explanatory ranking |
|---|---|
| Assisted FX access, terms and instruments | H1 > H4 > H3 > H2 > H5 |
| Why joint support occurred | H4 ≈ H3 > H1 > H2 > H5 |
| Cumulative BOJ path/timing | H2 > H1 > H4 ≈ H3 > H5 |
| Incremental Aug 31/Sept 1 public surprise | H5 > H2 > H1 > H3 > H4 |
| Fiscal expansion | Strong H1 weakened; remaining H1–H2 ordering unidentified |
| Realized FIMA funding | No positive causal ranking established |

**The single strongest new observation would be a contemporaneous record pairing the dated pre-May BOJ plan with its response to the external condition.** An unchanged already-fixed meeting path plus explicitly unconditional same-terms support would strongly falsify the incremental timing/bargaining account. A documented move from a later intended hike to an earlier meeting because assistance depended on it would strengthen H1. This joint observation discriminates the mechanism; another late-summer price comparison cannot supply the missing counterfactual.

## PB-G disposition and limits

**Preserve** conditional leverage over assisted cooperation and instruments, a credible cumulative timing channel, domestic normalization as a co-leading explanation, and stabilization/solicitation as compatible motives. **Weaken** attribution of the whole rate path to Washington, a newly decisive Sept 1 announcement, and arguments that later prepricing excludes earlier influence. **Reject** a permanent veto over Japanese policy, inability to intervene alone or expand spending, assumed executed FIMA enlargement/borrowing, and a mechanical expected-hike-to-stronger-yen rule.

The 15 constructed episodes share 10 BOJ target decisions and are not 15 independent causal experiments: several share the same summer bargaining process. Anonymous reconstruction, edition changes, incomplete primary access, sample revisions and sparse market observations remain explicit limitations. The retrospective first-public header is Sept 17 23:04 UTC, but the retrieved updated body cannot be certified wholesale as pre-Sept 18 content. Official domestic explanations are evidence, not proof that private pressure was absent. The surviving claim is material but bounded: **U.S. participation plausibly changed Japan’s assisted option and may have influenced timing; the evidence does not identify a generally dictated Japanese policy path.**
