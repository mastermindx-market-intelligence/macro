## Findings already established

The correct object is instrument-specific U.S. influence. Available evidence supports material U.S. participation in Japan's FX stabilization options. It does not identify a numerical effect on the timing of BOJ rate hikes.

1. **January was signaling.** The NYFed's Q1 report confirms no U.S. FX intervention during the quarter and describes January indicative quotes solely for Treasury in its fiscal-agent capacity. Japan's January monthly record reports zero intervention in the relevant reporting period. A rate check must not be relabeled a funded operation. [NYFed Q1](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/newsevents/news/markets/2026/q1-2026-fx-quarterly-report.pdf)

2. **Japan acted without U.S. trading support in April–May.** MOF's later daily release records April 30, May 4 and May 6 yen purchases totaling ¥11,734.9 billion. The daily rounded entries sum to ¥11,734.8 billion; preserve the published total and rounding difference. NYFed reports no U.S. intervention in Q2 and subsequent retracement of the yen response. [MOF Q2](https://www.mof.go.jp/english/policy/international_policy/reference/feio/quarter/2026_2Qe.html) · [NYFed Q2](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/newsevents/news/markets/2026/q2-2026-fx-quarterly-report.pdf)

3. **Joint July action is distinct from its funding and size.** Japan's August statement identifies July 31 U.S. Eastern time for joint action. The August monthly ¥15,399.3 billion figure covers Japan's July 30–August 26 period, not a single day or the U.S. amount. Actual U.S. execution size remains unknown. [MOF monthly](https://www.mof.go.jp/english/policy/international_policy/reference/feio/monthly/20260828e.html)

4. **A FIMA backstop is not a demonstrated draw.** H.4.1 reports foreign-official asset repos rounded to $0 million both as daily averages and Wednesday observations across the intervention week. A foreign reverse-repo investment pool is a different liability-side item. FOMC's January 27 directive permits a $60 billion total outstanding counterparty limit with specified delegated adjustments; do not substitute a per-day cap or assume every change requires a full new FOMC vote. [H.4.1 August 6](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h41/20260806/) · [FOMC directive](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/files/FOMC_AuthorizationsContinuingDirectivesOMOs.pdf)

5. **Domestic normalization predates the pressure cycle.** BOJ hikes in July 2024 and January 2025, tariff-related pauses and July 2025 opinions published August 8 create a substantial domestic counterfactual. BOJ's public July deliberations already contemplated further tightening before Bessent's August 13 remarks. [July opinions](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmsche_minu/opinion_2025/opi250731.htm)

6. **The strongest constraint evidence is private conditionality reported later.** Reuters' September reconstruction reports Japanese solicitation and U.S. fiscal/monetary conditions for help. It strengthens an external-leverage hypothesis without establishing a BOJ instruction or measuring acceleration. The article was initially published September 17 at 23:04 UTC and updated September 18 to include the decision: the retrieved full version cannot be admitted as an unaltered pre-decision input. [Reuters reconstruction](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/how-bessent-americas-bond-salesman-cornered-japan-big-spending-2026-09-17/)
