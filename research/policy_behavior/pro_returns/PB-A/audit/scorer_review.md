# PB-A scorer review

Reviewed completed `research/policy_behavior/pro_returns/PB-A/pb_a_analyze.py` at SHA-256 `590155910f811f29fd7230055387fb93f37cd83930d1ee01ef5db8f764774f27`, the frozen protocol, implementation clarifications, analysis contract, and the 23-change `outcome_inputs/policy_decision_changes.json` evidence ledger. No script edits, actual forecasts, actual outcome scores, or actual-case metric computations were performed. Nine independent checks used synthetic 2030–2032 inputs only.

## Concrete blockers

**None verified in the reviewed code/evidence.** Endpoint arithmetic, known-decision anchoring, paired coverage, descriptive cluster enumeration, and supplied CSV-header compatibility implement the frozen definitions coherently. This is a bounded code review, not proof of future generalization, full casebook certification, or approval of any downstream headline.

## Strongest verified safeguards

1. **Month endpoints use the cut's New York civil date, not UTC date or focal event date.** `horizon_date()` applies `utc(cut).astimezone(NY).date()` and `add_months()` clamps to the last valid calendar day. Independent synthetic tests covered leap-year UTC/New York date disagreement, year crossing, and May31→June30. This also correctly means an overnight conservative cut shifts the endpoint calendar relative to the prior day's focal announcement, as clarification C01 specifies.

2. **Already-public decisions enter the anchor even before effectiveness.** `known_anchor()` selects ledger decisions with `decision_utc <= cut`, takes the latest, and cross-checks the frozen declared focal range. It does not use the old effective range as anchor when a new range is already public. A synthetic non-rate midnight cut after a public announcement but before its effective day absorbed the announced new range. The supplied ledger separately records the real March3/4, March15/16, and May3/4 distinctions; no outcome labels were generated here.

3. **Secondary and net targets are separate and known changes cannot win the secondary target.** `policy_outcome()` uses `decision_utc > cut` and `effective_date <= horizon`, sorts by decision time, and chooses the first eligible change, while the primary target uses endpoint midpoint minus known anchor midpoint. `validate_changes()` enforces effective date on/after the public decision's New York date, so the horizon-effective filter cannot admit a post-horizon decision. No eligible change yields HOLD only within a covered ledger window; a synthetic out-of-window endpoint was correctly NOT_SCORED.

4. **Missing policy endpoints are quarantined.** Both target bounds must exist on the exact calendar horizon date; the scorer returns NOT_SCORED rather than silently carrying rates or replacing missing data with zero. The policy reconciliation validator also checks lower/upper observation-date equality, daily continuity inside the declared ledger window, original ledger/effective-range agreement, nonzero basis-point consistency, and equality of observed-versus-declared effective change dates. Synthetic absent-endpoint checks confirmed quarantine.

5. **Matched populations stay matched.** `metric_group()` separately constructs each model's own non-abstaining coverage, every pair's common coverage, and the M0/M1/M2 intersection. Paired contrasts use only their matching intersection. Counts, episode IDs, true-class frequencies, and regime counts accompany the comparisons; abstentions are never uniform probability forecasts or implicit misses. `build_metrics()` never pools PRIMARY with ADVERSARIAL_CHALLENGE or combines horizons. Split and regime reports are separate views of the same defined target.

6. **Uncertainty is descriptive and episode weighted.** `regime_uncertainty()` resamples whole available regime blocks, exactly enumerating K^K draws; K is the number of regimes represented in that comparison, at most four. Each draw recomputes episode-weighted means over its sampled blocks, preserving within-block dependence; leave-one-regime-out results use the same paired rows. Empty intersections and a one-regime leave-out produce null summaries rather than numerical confidence claims. The output explicitly disclaims significance/generalization. Four-cluster/overlap limitations remain substantive, but the implementation does not disguise them.

7. **CSV parsing matches supplied files and rejects dangerous numeric inputs.** Supplied headers are `observation_date,<series_id>`; the parser also supports `DATE`, UTF-8 BOM, standard missing markers, and finite float checks. Independent synthetic temporary CSVs confirmed BOM handling, skipped missing markers, duplicate finite-date rejection, and infinity rejection. Percent changes are converted to basis points with ×100. Policy series require exact dates; descriptive market series follow the separately frozen ≤7-day staleness rule.

8. **Market output remains descriptive.** The baseline observation must have a civil date strictly before the cut's New York date; horizon observations are on/before the horizon with reported actual dates/staleness. Nominal-real-breakeven decomposition is computed only when both baseline and endpoint dates align across all components, with rounding residual retained. Every market model forecast abstains. Missing DXY/expectations/surprise data remain named gaps rather than proxies or policy-score substitutions.

9. **Forecasts are verified before any outcome source is read.** `forecast` consumes only decision inputs, protocol, clarifications, and code; `predict()` accepts exactly four scalar feature keys and does not accept outcomes or arbitrary callbacks. `score_command()` calls `verify_freeze()` before loading CSVs/decision ledger. That guard verifies canonical frozen content, decision/protocol/clarification/code hashes, and regenerated predictions against the freeze. Outcome input digests are retained, and policy CSV digests are checked against the ledger's advertised identities. The receipt records observed hashes rather than claiming immutable history.

The review does not replace root's checks that the normalized narrow inputs contain the intended fixed cohort, accurate source bounds, correct `has_new_rate_decision` flags, and source-grounded M0 codes. Those values are authored outside the scorer. No additional blocker was inferred merely from the research model's disclosed limitations.
