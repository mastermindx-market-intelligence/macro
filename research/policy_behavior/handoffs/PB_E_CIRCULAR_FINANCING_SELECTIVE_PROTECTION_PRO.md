## Mission

Determine how much AI / strategic-industry demand and valuation support is linked to **capital provision from parties that benefit from the resulting demand**, and how much ultimately converts into independent external cash generation.

Also study how government procurement, guarantees, price floors, equity investment, offtake, tax incentives or regulatory preference can create a **two-speed cost-of-capital regime** even when aggregate financing conditions remain restrictive.

The target is not to label circular relationships “fake.” The target is to measure dependence, durability and failure modes.

## Research questions

1. What fraction of disclosed AI demand is linked to counterparties that also finance, invest in, guarantee or economically subsidize the buyer?
2. How much **new external cash** enters the network versus circulates among participants?
3. Which obligations are hard commitments versus optional / contingent?
4. Which contracts contain minimums, cancellation rights, warrants or performance conditions?
5. Does financing support precede reported demand acceleration?
6. Are receivables, deferred revenue or customer concentration changing in ways consistent with financing dependence?
7. How sensitive are the networks to tighter funding conditions?
8. Which government support mechanisms lower the effective cost of capital or reduce downside risk for strategic firms?
9. Do selectively protected firms show better financing access / revision durability than similarly levered controls?
10. Under what conditions does the loop become self-reinforcing versus fragile?

## Selective-protection hypothesis

Test whether restrictive aggregate rates coexist with targeted mechanisms that reduce effective financing burden or demand uncertainty for strategic sectors.

Separate:

- **aggregate monetary restriction**;
- **Treasury / market-structure relief**;
- **direct fiscal / procurement support**;
- **strategic guarantees / price floors / offtake**;
- **private financing loops**.

Do not infer that selective support proves the government intentionally wants high rates. It can, however, show that aggregate restriction and targeted protection can coexist.
